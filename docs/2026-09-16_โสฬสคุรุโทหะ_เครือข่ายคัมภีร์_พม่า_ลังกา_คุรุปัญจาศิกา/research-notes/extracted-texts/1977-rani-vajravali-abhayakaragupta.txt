The Vajrdvat nama mandalopayika is written by Abhayakaragupta of the
twelfth century. He was a prolific writer on Tantric dogmatics, liturgy and the
mandalas. Twentyfour works of his were translated into Tibetan, and are in-
cluded in the Tibetan Tanjur, He was an abbot of the Vikramaiila university
during the reign of the Pala king Ramapala who flourished in A, D. 1084-1130.
Tn the 30th regnal year of the same king Ramapala (A. D. 1114) he wrote the
Muni-mattlaikara. In Tibetan works several miracles are attributed to him.
Tibetan scholars risked the hazards of perilous journeys to visit him at Bodh-
gaya- Lce-btsun, the founder of the great monastery of Zha-lu, came ta Badh-
gaya to learn the monastic discipline (vinaya) and many other doctrines from
Abhayakara ‘the great actrya of Bodhgaya’, and he also brought back to Tibet
an image of Khasarpapa Avalokitesvara. Rgva lotsava, whose fame had
spread all over Tibet and even in India, studied under Abbayakara (GN.
Roerich, Blue Annals 2.796), Initiations into Abhayakara’s Vajravali and the
Amnaya-maiijari 4 commentary on the Samputa-tantra-raja were bestowed
by the precious great pandita Vanaratua on eminent Tibetan scholars. The
Panchen Lamas considered themselves ta be re-incarnations of Abhaytikara and
it is from this cycle that Tibetan painted scrolls (thaika) of Abhayakara stem.

The Blue Annals 2.1046 record the following legend of Vajrayogint: “The
zcarya Abbays was endowed with a mind free of illusions in regard to any of
the systems of the prajiaparamitz or tantra, from the lesser sciences (rig-gnas
phra-ma) to the anuttara-yoga tantras. Because he had recited the mantra of
Vajra-yogint in his former life, in this life, Vajra-yogint m the form of an
ordinary woman appeared before the acarya Abhaya. Because of his steadfast
attitude of a strict monk, he did not admit this woman. His great teachers,
such at Ku-so-ri-pa and others, told him that he had acted wrongly by not
availing himself of the method through which one could realise the sahaja
jfiana. On many occasions he prayed to Vajra-yogini. The goddess appeared
to him in a dream, and said: “Now, in this life you will not be united with me,
But, if you were to compose many commentaries on profound tantras and many
rites of mandalas, you would soon become a fortunate one”. Following her
instructions, he composed the Sri-samputa-tantra-raja-tikamnaya-maiijart
(Tanjur 1198), the $ri-Buddha-kapala-mahstantra-raja-tik@ abhaya-paddhati
nama (Tanjur 1654) and the Vajravalt (Tanjur 3140)”. é

The Vajravali is @ practical guide to all the preliminary rites preceding
initiation into the mandala from the very laying of the foundations of a

monastery where the mandala is to bedrawn. It is based on several tantras
like the Samputa-tantra, Dakint-vajra-pafijara, Vimalaprabha, Vajra-daka-
tantra, Mafijuvajra-mandala-tippani, Tattva-sahgraha, BhGta-damara, Kala-
eakra, Trailokyavijaya-tantra, Abhidhanottara-tantra, Vajramrta-tantra, Sardha-
tridatika, Amonya-mafijarl, Hevajra-tantra, Sarhvara-tantra, Yogint-saificnra-
tantra, Guhya-samsja (Srisamyja), Subahu-pariprccha, Padma-cupratisthita-
tantra, Sarnvar-oday-abhisamay-opayika and other authors, The extensively
scattered and complicated details about the mandalas and their ritual have
been systematised in a precise manner so that they can be drawn and visualised
in proper sequence (afetafag germ 4A: mead AMT). The author further
states that in earlier texts the subject is dealt with in an incomplete manner,
or without sequence, or is not clear enough in its enunciation, For the
attainment of merit and knowledge (qoqaurt sTa@arz) the preat acaryas have,
out of compassion, prescribed the performance of fifty vidhis so that beings may
cross over the vast ocean of existence, These are the subject-matter of the
present text. They are enumerated on page 3 of the facsimile edition.

The Vajravalt is basically devoted to mandalas of Sarhvara, Maiijuvajra,
Pindtkramokta, Samputa-iantra-okta Vajrasattva, Jiitina-dakint, Saptadaé-
atmaka Hevajra, Sodasabhuja Hevajra, Nairatma, Kurukulla, Vajramrta,
Mabamays, Buddbakapals, Vajra-hOinkara, Yogambara, Yamari, VajraTara,
Marici, Paficaraket, Vajradhatu, Durgati-pariiodhana, Bhita-damara, Sat~
cakravartin, Kalacakra. The structural layout, colours and attributes of the
deities in these mandalas are described at length from page 74 onwards.

The Vajravali was translated into Tibetan under the title Dkyil-Ahhor-
gyi cho~ga rdo-rje phren-ba by Hkhor-lo-grags* under the guidance of the author
himself. It was revised by Ses-rab-dpal; a second time by Ratnaraksita, Rahula-
Sribhadra, Chag lotsava Chos-rje-dpal (1197-1265)%, and on a third occasion

1481) says in his Blue Annals 2.1047-8 that "Chag lotsava* Chos-rje-dpal heard
