INSCRIPTION B 18: ARAHAGUTO DEVAPUTO
Location: On the Ajātaśatru Pillar, lower corner of the middle medallion representing the Descent of the Buddha from the Trayastriṃśa Heaven.
Language: Early Middle Indo-Aryan (Prakrit).
Script: Early Brāhmī of the Śuṅga period (circa 2nd century BCE).
Reading: Arahaguto devaputo dānaṃ [or: Arahaguto devaputo]
Translation: 'The divine being (or deity) Arahagutta' (or 'Gift of the devaputra Arahagutta').

ARCHAEOLOGICAL AND ICONOGRAPHICAL DESCRIPTION:
The relief represents the miraculous descent of the Buddha from the Trāyastriṃśa (Tāvatiṃsa) heaven at Saṅkāśya (Sankisa).
In the centre of the composition is a ladder (sopāna) divided into three distinct tracks, or three parallel staircases.
The ladder on the proper right is assigned to the gods (Śakra/Indra), the ladder on the proper left is for Brahmā, and the middle flight is for the Buddha.
Following early Buddhist aniconic convention, the Buddha is not depicted anthropomorphically. Instead, two sets of footprints (pādukā or buddhapāda) are shown:
one at the top of the ladder marking the departure from the heavenly world, and another set at the foot of the ladder under a shrine or tree, marking his arrival on earth.

EPIGRAPHICAL ANALYSIS AND TEXTUAL PARALLELS:
Lüders discusses the identification of 'Arahaguta devaputa'. In Pali sources, Arahagutta is not a prominent deity in early canonical texts, indicating that this name represents an early North Indian sectarian or popular Buddhist tradition preserved during the Śuṅga period.
Comparison with Literary Sources:
1. Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā (Vol. III, pp. 224-227): Describes the descent at Saṅkassa via three staircases: one of gold, one of silver, and one of jewels (maṇimaya).
2. Divyāvadāna (p. 150): Refers to the invariant necessity (avaśyakaraṇīya) of all Buddhas to perform the Devāvatāraṇa at Sāṃkāśya.
3. Faxian and Xuanzang: Both Chinese pilgrims describe the relics of the three ladders at Saṅkāśya, noting that King Aśoka excavated the steps and erected an elephant capital pillar.

HISTORICAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The Bharhut relief provides irrefutable physical proof that the legend of the triple stairway and the descent from the Trāyastriṃśa heaven was already fully formed in Indian Buddhist devotional culture by the 2nd century BCE, preceding the redaction of the extant Pali commentaries by several centuries.
