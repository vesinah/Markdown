# The Restoration Stela of Tutankhamun (Cairo CG 34183)

## Historical Preamble and Regal Titulary
Year 1 of the King of Upper and Lower Egypt, Lord of the Two Lands, Nebkheperure, Son of Ra, Tutankhamun, Ruler of Southern Heliopolis, beloved of Amun-Ra, King of the Gods.

<!-- Page 2 -->
## The State of Collapse under the Heresy
Now when his majesty arose as king, the temples and shrines of the gods and goddesses beginning from Elephantine down to the marshes of the Delta had fallen into neglect; their shrines had fallen into desolation and become ruins overgrown with weeds. Their sanctuaries were as if they had never come into being, and their courts were used as roads for footpaths.

<!-- Page 3 -->
The land was in severe affliction and sickness; the gods had turned their backs upon this land. If an army was sent to the Levant (Djahy) to broaden the borders of Egypt, no success came to their efforts. If men prayed to a god to ask for counsel, he came not at all; and if they petitioned any goddess in like manner, she would never attend to their petition. Their hearts were disgusted in their bodies, because the shrines were dismantled.

## The Royal Restoration and Renovation of the Shrines
Then his majesty deliberated in his heart, seeking every beneficial deed for his father Amun. His majesty fashioned his divine image in genuine fine gold from the highlands. He built all the sanctuaries anew as monuments for eternity; he endowed them with perpetual offerings and staffed them with purified priests, singers, and servants from the choicest youth of the land.

## The Usurpation by Horemheb and the King Lists
Following the death of Tutankhamun and Ay, the military commander Horemheb seized the throne and instituted a systematic Damnatio Memoriae against the entire Amarna line. The Restoration Stela itself was usurped by Horemheb: his cartouches were carved directly over those of Tutankhamun at Karnak.

In the monumental King Lists of the 19th Dynasty—most notably the Abydos King List in the Temple of Seti I and the Saqqara Tablet in the tomb of Tjuneroy—the names of Akhenaten, Smenkhkare, Neferneferuaten, Tutankhamun, and Ay were totally expunged from the royal canon. The official succession jumped directly from Amenhotep III (no. 73 in the Abydos list) to Horemheb (no. 74). The Amarna episode was officially treated as if it had never occurred, and the heretic king was referred to in Ramesside legal papyri only as "that enemy of Akhetaten" (pꜢ ḫrw n Ꜣḫ.t-jtn).
