# Ancestry and Pathology in King Tutankhamun's Family
By Zahi Hawass et al.

Context: The 18th Dynasty of the New Kingdom was one of the most powerful and transformative eras of ancient Egyptian history. The identity of Tutankhamun's parents and the circumstances surrounding his premature death at approximately 19 years of age have generated intense scientific debate. Various pathologies have been proposed, including Marfan syndrome, Wilson-Turner X-linked mental retardation syndrome, Fröhlich syndrome (adiposogenital dystrophy), and familial gynecomastia, largely based on the artistic representations of the Amarna period.

Objective: To determine familial relationships and search for pathological conditions, congenital disorders, and causes of death in Tutankhamun and 10 other royal mummies of the New Kingdom.

Design, Setting, and Participants: From September 2007 to October 2009, 11 royal mummies underwent detailed anthropological, radiological (multidetector computed tomography [CT]), and molecular genetic analyses (short tandem repeat [STR] markers and ancient DNA [aDNA] sequencing). In addition, 5 non-royal control mummies of the same period were analyzed to validate genetic data.

<!-- Page 639 -->
## Methods
Tissue Sampling: Bone biopsies were retrieved from deep internal osseous structures (femur, tibia, pelvis) of each mummy under strict aseptic, sterile conditions inside dedicated ancient DNA laboratories at the National Research Center and the Faculty of Medicine at Cairo University. Independent replications were performed in two separate laboratories with distinct personnel.

DNA Extraction and STR Analysis: DNA was extracted using phenol-chloroform and silica-based protocols. Nuclear STR markers on 16 polymorphic microsatellite loci (AmpFlSTR Identifiler and Yfiler systems) were amplified by multiplex PCR. Alleles were resolved by capillary electrophoresis. Mitochondrial DNA control region sequencing and Y-chromosomal STR typing were used to establish maternal and paternal lineages.

Diagnostic CT Scanning: Whole-body CT scans were performed using a multi-slice CT unit (Somatom Emotion 6; Siemens Medical Systems) installed on a mobile truck at the Valley of the Kings. Volumetric reconstructions and 3-dimensional multiplanar images were analyzed by international expert radiologists.

## Results: Genetic Kinship and Pedigree Reconstruction
A 5-generation pedigree of Tutankhamun's immediate ancestry was established with statistical certainty:
1. Amenhotep III (KV35) and Queen Tiye (the "Elder Lady", KV35EL) were identified as the grandparents of Tutankhamun. The Elder Lady matched the genetic profile of the hair lock inscribed with Queen Tiye's name found inside Tutankhamun's tomb (KV62).
2. The male skeleton discovered in tomb KV55 was determined to be a son of Amenhotep III and Queen Tiye (shared alleles and identical Y-chromosomal STR haplotype). Furthermore, KV55 was identified as the biological father of Tutankhamun (probability of paternity > 99.999999%).
3. The mummy known as the "Younger Lady" (KV35YL) was determined to be a daughter of Amenhotep III and Queen Tiye, and the biological mother of Tutankhamun. Tutankhamun's parents were therefore full brother and sister.
4. The two female fetuses buried in Tutankhamun's tomb (KV62) were genetically confirmed as the offspring of Tutankhamun and the KV21A mummy (probable Ankhesenamun, daughter of Akhenaten and Nefertiti).

Anthropological and Radiological Analysis of KV55:
The skeleton in KV55 exhibited mild degenerative changes, open or partially fused clavicular and pelvic epiphyses, and dental wear consistent with an age at death of roughly 35 to 45 years according to recent radiological re-evaluations, matching the biological age profile of Akhenaten rather than a very youthful prince.

## Pathology, Congenital Anomalies, and Cause of Death
Radiological examination of Tutankhamun, Amenhotep III, and KV55 revealed no evidence of gynecomastia, Marfan syndrome, or craniosynostosis. The distinctive elongated heads, prominent hips, and pendulous abdomens characteristic of Amarna art represent a stylized religious iconography rather than true physical dysmorphology.

However, significant pathologies were identified in Tutankhamun:
1. Kohler Disease II: Avascular bone necrosis of the second and third metatarsophalangeal joints of the left foot.
2. Equinovarus Deformity (Clubfoot): Hypoplasia of the second middle phalanx of the right foot and oligodactyly-like deformity causing pronounced walking impairment. This finding provides the physiological explanation for the 130 functional walking sticks and canes discovered in his tomb.
3. Severe Fracture of the Left Distal Femur: Exhibiting ragged edges and lipid inclusions indicating an acute antemortem trauma that occurred shortly before death.

## Infectious Diseases: Malaria Tropica
PCR amplification of specific genomic sequences of Plasmodium falciparum (parasite of malaria tropica) yielded positive results in Tutankhamun (KV62), Thuya, Yuya, and KV55. Tutankhamun harbored multiple strains of P. falciparum, indicating severe, recurrent malaria infections.

## Discussion: The Royal Lineage and Consanguinity
The identification of the KV55 mummy as Tutankhamun's father and KV35YL as his mother confirms a direct brother-sister mating. While such incestuous unions were common in ancient Egyptian royal ideology to mirror the divine sibling pairings of Osiris and Isis, the genetic consequence was severe consanguinity, increasing the expression of recessive pathological traits.

Because Nefertiti is nowhere recorded in historical inscriptions as a daughter of Amenhotep III and Queen Tiye, but rather is suspected of being of non-royal or court nobility origin (possibly related to Ay), the genetic identification of Tutankhamun's mother as a full sibling of KV55 makes it highly improbable that Nefertiti was Tutankhamun's biological mother. The Younger Lady is therefore either an otherwise poorly documented royal daughter (such as Nebetiah or Beketaten) or represents the biological identity of Kiya.

<!-- Page 645 -->
The age at death of the KV55 skeleton remains a focal point of debate. While earlier anatomical studies by Harrison (1966) and Strouhal (2010) suggested an age of 20 to 25 years (which would favor Smenkhkare), our modern multiplanar CT scans demonstrate that the medial clavicular epiphyses and spheno-occipital synchondrosis indicate an age of approximately 35 to 45 years, which perfectly fits Akhenaten's 17-year reign preceded by his youth.

<!-- Page 646 -->
Conclusions: A sudden severe traumatic injury (the compound distal femur fracture) occurring in a young king debilitated by avascular bone necrosis and recurrent virulent Plasmodium falciparum malaria represents the most medically plausible sequence of events leading to Tutankhamun's death at age 19.

<!-- Page 647 -->
The genetic mapping of the Amarna royal lineage provides an empirical baseline that resolves century-old controversies concerning the relationships among Amenhotep III, Akhenaten, the Younger Lady, and Tutankhamun, anchoring the historiography of the 18th Dynasty in rigorous biomolecular evidence.
