## Related findings

- Brill companion volume chapter (2017): "The second of Lindisfarne's texts is the Old English gloss which was added interlinearly, perhaps about 970, by Aldred, provost of the community." https://brill.com/display/book/edcoll/9789004337848/B9789004337848_010.xml
- digitalmedievalist.com (2025): c. 970 CE, when the community had moved to Chester-le-Street (later Durham), Aldred the provost added Old English glosses translating the Latin; "Aldred's cheat notes therefore are the oldest surviving version of the gospels in any form of English." Base text is Jerome's Vulgate in insular majuscule; Aldred's interlinear gloss is in Anglo-Saxon minuscule. Aldred's colophon (f. 259r) credits Eadfrith, Bishop of Lindisfarne (698–721) as scribe/artist, Bishop Aethilwold (c. 721–740) as binder, Billfrith the anchorite as metalwork artisan. Shelfmark BL Cotton Nero D.IV.
- Pons-Sanz (2016, open PDF at Cardiff ORCA https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/85937/1/Pons-Sanz_Multiple%20glosses.pdf): "Aldred, the glossator of the Lindisfarne Gospels, presents himself as carefully rendering the Latin lemmata. This paper explores glosses including different..." [multiple-gloss phenomenon]. Volume intro (dokumen.pub mirror of De Gruyter text): "Aldred often provides multiple grammatical translations by supplying double or even triple glosses (Curme 1912: 181; Kotake 2006a; Pons-Sanz, this volume)."
- Brookes (2016, Oxford ORA): examines Aldred's response to the gospel text through his choices of script. https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:20c8fdd2-1bd4-4d39-9019-932cfc6c9cd7
- Silva (2023, JSTOR 27278452): Aldred glossed Matthew, Mark, Luke and the beginning of John in a neat cursive hand using black ink; the Lindisfarne and Rushworth glosses were compared.

## MacRegol / Rushworth Gospels (companion corpus)
- Bodleian catalogue (medieval.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/catalog/manuscript_431): MS Auct. D.2.19, "The Macregol or Rushworth Gospels; Ireland (Birr), before 822"; Old English gloss added, 10th century; text and gloss ed. K. Tamoto 2013.
- Tamoto, Kenichi (ed.), The Macregol Gospels or The Rushworth Gospels: Edition of the Latin text with the Old English interlinear gloss transcribed from Oxford Bodleian Library MS Auctarium D.2.19, John Benjamins, 2013, DOI 10.1075/z.180 — first complete single edition of Latin text + interlinear OE gloss.
- University of Turin paper (iris.unito.it): the interlinear OE gloss to the Rushworth/MacRegol Gospel Book was written some time during the second half of the 10th century by two scribes identifying themselves as Farman and Owun in the colophons.
