## Content (harvested)

The Latin grammarian Priscian wrote his Ars grammatica (Institutiones grammaticae) at Constantinople around AD 527 — the most exhaustive compendium of information on Latin (and Greek) language and literature to survive from the ancient world.

The Stiftsbibliothek in St Gall contains manuscript no. 904, a copy of Priscian written by Irish scribes in AD 850–1, probably in Ireland. It was brought to the Continent between 855 and 863, reaching St Gall sometime after 888.

The manuscript contains a commentary of over 9,400 interlinear and marginal glosses, as well as c. 3,000 symbol glosses. About one-third of the verbal glosses are written in Old Irish, providing one of our earliest sources for the Irish language.

The digital edition provides the text of the glosses alongside Priscian's text, links to manuscript images, and linguistic information on the Old Irish glosses. Browsing is by book (Praefatio through Book 17); the manuscript breaks off in Book 17 and Book 18 is missing. A detailed typology of gloss types is offered (prosody, lexical glosses incl. translations into Old Irish, morphology, syntax with symbols, explanatory/etymological/encyclopedic/source glosses).

Citation: Rijcklof Hofman, Pádraic Moran, Bernhard Bauer, St Gall Priscian Glosses, version 2.1 (2023), http://stgallpriscian.ie/.
