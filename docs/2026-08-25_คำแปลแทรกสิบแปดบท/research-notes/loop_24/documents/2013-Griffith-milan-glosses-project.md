## Content (harvested)

In the study of the language history of Irish, the so-called Old Irish glosses (edited in W. Stokes & J. Strachan, Thesaurus Palaeohibernicus, Cambridge 1901–3) occupy a special position. They are almost the only testimonies of Old Irish from contemporaneous manuscripts; their language is not liable to interference from younger linguistic stages (Middle and Modern Irish).

The largest corpus within the glosses is formed by the Milan glosses (Thes. i 7–483), interlinear and marginal notes to and translations of a Latin commentary on the psalms in the manuscript Ambrosianus C301 inf., kept at the Biblioteca Ambrosiana in Milan.

Project aim: compile the entire Old Irish language material of the Milan glosses in one special dictionary, providing complete information on phonology, morphology and syntax of the lexemes. The model was Kavanagh's dictionary of the Würzburg glosses (Kavanagh/Wodtko 2001). Publication planned both in print and digitally; a clearer picture of the state of the language at the beginning of the 9th century is expected as a result.

Text basis: Griffith typed in the entire text as found in Thesaurus Palaeohibernicus, silently including corrections/additions from the ends of volumes 1–2; revisions were based primarily on the facsimile edition by Richard I. Best, The Commentary on the Psalms with glosses in Old-Irish preserved in the Ambrosian Library (MS. C 301 inf.), Royal Irish Academy 1936; difficult passages were checked against the manuscript itself during a visit to Milan in December 2010. Also consulted: Graziadio Isaia Ascoli, Il Codice irlandese dell'Ambrosiana (= Archivio glottologico 5–6), Rome 1878–9. Where TPH and this text diverge, the project version should be followed; errata are recorded in downloadable "ml_corrections" PDFs.

Downloads offered: Milan Glosses Database for Apple (73 MB) and Windows (56 MB), dated 12.09.2013, plus conversion conventions (2009).
