## Key findings

### RQ1. Sutra copying with kunten: Nanatsudera, Shōgozō, issaikyō vow projects
- Nanatsudera (Nagoya) whole-canon vow copying ran 1175-1179 under lay patron Ōnakatomi Yasunaga; 3,398 handscrolls and 1,556 accordion books survive; distinctive red top/bottom margin ruling (both directions only in the Daihannya scrolls). From 1990, Ochiai Toshinori identified previously lost Chinese works (incl. texts paralleling Dunhuang finds) among its volumes, prompting the ongoing series Nanatsudera koitsu kyōten kenkyū sōsho 七寺古逸經典叢書 (Daitō shuppansha). https://knmdb.kyohaku.go.jp/eng/8052.html ; https://dlbs.liberal.ntu.edu.tw/FULLTEXT/JR-MAG/mag609100.pdf ; https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Manuscripts_of_Nanatsu_dera.html?id=iuYQAQAAIAAJ
- Shōgozō 聖語蔵 (Tōdai-ji, next to Shōsōin): ca. 1,500 Nara-period scrolls plus Sui/Tang/Silla imports; 783 titles / 4,960 scrolls overall; Nara vow canons (Tenpyō 12 gogan kyō 740; Jingo Keiun 2 gogan kyō 770) were proofread several times and collated against other copies, hence high reliability; visible white reading marks hakuten 白点 make it key evidence for earliest Japanese reading practice; Iida Takehiko (2012) showed most scrolls labeled "Jingo keiun" actually belong to the Imakō ichibu canon sponsored by Emperor Kōnin. https://shosoin.princeton.edu/shogozo/
- Scale of Nara vow-copying: over twenty sets of Issaikyō were produced in the Nara period. https://journal.kci.go.kr/crbs/archive/articleView?artiId=ART002031000
- Digital access to old Japanese sutra manuscripts: Nihon koshakyō database 日本古写経データベース (ICABS): http://koshakyo-database.icabs.ac.jp/index.seam (linked from the Princeton Shōgozō page).

### RQ2. Kamakura-and-later printed editions with kunten
- Gozan-ban 五山版 defined as books published by Zen monks chiefly at the five Zen monasteries of Kamakura and the five of Kyoto, spanning mid-Kamakura to late Muromachi; largely reprints of Song/Yuan Chinese editions. https://www.jonathanahill.com/images/upload/buddhism-in-asia-pt-i.pdf ; https://brill.com/view/book/9789004749023/BP000014.xml
- Important nuance: Blum (2019) notes that Kasuga-ban, Kōyasan Kamakura printings, and Gozan printings were, as far as he knows, printed in kanbun (without kana glosses). Claims about gozan-ban carrying printed kunten/reading marks specifically were not confirmed in fetched sources (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน). https://journals.library.brandeis.edu/index.php/PAJLS/article/view/1688/1076
- Kōno Kimiko's open-access chapter treats kunten precisely in the transition "printed and digitized manuscripts," making it the best single bridge source between medieval print culture and modern digital corpora. https://waseda.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/japanophone-glosses-kunten-in-printed-and-digitized-manuscripts/
- Medieval readers actively annotated and repurposed printed sutras by hand (Dolce 2023). https://journals.publishing.umich.edu/ars/article/id/3987/print/

### RQ3. Reception of Chinese sound-gloss works (the transmission bridge)
Two distinct circuits, both verified:
- Xuanying 玄應 Yiqiejing yinyi (completed ca. 649-660): arrived in Japan in the Nara period (earliest copying record 729 CE); today it survives only in Japanese manuscripts (10 named holdings: Daiji 大治 copy, Kongōji, Hōryūji, Nanatsudera, Saihōji, Hiroshima Univ., UTokyo Historiographical Institute, Kyoto Univ., Tenri, Kōshōji) while China retains only printed editions; Dunhuang/Turfan fragments are in the British Library and BnF; the Song Sixi zang 思渓蔵 printed text was published in facsimile in 2019 jointly by ICABS and the National Library of China; Heian Japanese lexicography drew on it heavily (Zushoryō-bon Ruiju myōgi shō quotes ca. 1,300 entries; dependence on Shin senjimon studied by Li). Full-text database of the Japanese manuscripts built by Li Naiqi and Liu Guanwei (2018). https://profs.provost.nagoya-u.ac.jp/html/100013607_ja.html ; https://hdl.handle.net/2115/80863 ; https://ipsj.ixsq.nii.ac.jp/records/192469
- Huilin 慧琳 Yiqiejing yinyi (completed 807, 100 juan): lost in China after the Northern Song because later canon re-carvings omitted it; preserved because it entered the Liao/Qidan Tripitaka and thence Koryŏ, whose re-carved canon (Chaejo Taejanggyŏng 高麗再雕大藏經) contained it; Koryŏ had tried to obtain it as early as 954-960 (Zhou Xiande era) but no print existed then; the Koryŏ canon came to Japan on repeated requests (documented by Zuikei Shūhō's 1409 letter in Zenrin kokuhōki), copies kept at Kenninji (Kyoto) and Zōjōji (Tokyo); a print made from the Kenninji copy was acquired by Yang Shoujing 楊守敬, who reintroduced the work to China in the late Qing. Companion volume: Xilin 希麟's Xu yiqiejing yinyi (987, Liao) likewise went Korea -> Japan, was first printed in Japan in 1737, and returned to China in the 1912 Pinjia Jingshe printing; Qing scholars produced their own editions of Xuanying yinyi (Zhuang Xin 1786; Haishanxian Studio, Daoguang; Cao Zhou 1869). https://doi.org/10.1515/9783111322599-003 ; http://www.chinaknowledge.de/Literature/Science/yiqiejingyinyi.html
- Japanese-made yinyi-type dictionaries: the strongest verified case is reception/adaptation inside Japanese dictionaries (Ruiju myōgi shō's systematic quotation of Yiqiejing yinyi; Shin senjikon/新撰字鏡 dependence examined by Li 2021, https://icabs.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/582 ). A dedicated "Japanese imitation yinyi" genre was not separately verifiable in fetched sources (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน).

### RQ4. Edo-period kanbun kundoku pedagogy and terakoya textbooks
- Terakoya sequence: calligraphy from tehon model sheets, then ōraimono copybooks (lineage from Heian period, originally samurai education); many Edo-era copybooks switched to kana-majiri (kana interlinear with kanji) for accessibility; standard titles: Teikin ōrai (household precepts letters), shōbai ōrai (commerce), hyakushō ōrai (farming almanac), Jinkōki (abacus arithmetic), Senjimon, Myōji zukushi/Kuni zukushi, Toshisen, Hyakunin isshu. Official MEXT history: https://www.mext.go.jp/b_menu/hakusho/html/others/detail/1317227.htm
- By the end of the shogunate there were roughly thirty-to-forty thousand terakoya, with individualized curricula; textbook specimens (Teikin ōrai, Jinkōki, Ono no Takamura uta jizukushi) exhibited at the Edo Tokyo Digital Museum. https://www.library.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/portals/0/edo/tokyo_library/english/gakumon/page1-1.html
- Digitized oraimono corpus: Tokyo Gakugei University, Mochizuki Bunko Oraimono collection (late Heian-early Meiji textbooks, public domain scans). https://d-archive.u-gakugei.ac.jp/en/collection/orai
- Scholarship trend: early-modern kanbun kundoku wording studies show readings shifting from dignified to simplified styles readable by everyone - i.e., mass pedagogy reshaped kundoku itself. https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1040000782294202240

### RQ5. Modern databases and digital transcription
- NINJAL Kunten database (尚書古活字版第三種本加点情報データベース): searchable wokototen (including 音合符/訓合符 and 声点 tone marks) and word-order points (レ点, 一二点) across Shangshu vols. 1-9; simple search retrieves points by color/shape with frequency counts; detailed search combines position/appearance conditions; test-opened 2020-01-31; developed within NIHU 総合書物学 project and JSPS grant on international document-structure description and automatic kakikudashi generation. https://cid.ninjal.ac.jp/kunten-syousyo3/
- Automatic recognition research: Tsutsumi/Tajima/Kosukegawa/Takada (IPSJ Journal 63(2), 2022) classified wokototen diagrams into the traditional groups 1-8 with over 95% accuracy using nine positions of the ・ shaped point. https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390009683039645056
- Kanbun-LM (Waseda NLP; ACL 2023 Findings): first Classical-Chinese-to-Kanbun dataset; character-reordering and translation tasks; quantifies resource scarcity (~500 Tang poems available in kundoku form vs 48,900+ originals); code and dataset released. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12759 ; https://github.com/nlp-waseda/Kanbun-LM
- Related OCR/LLM work: "Beyond OCR: Enhancing Classical Japanese Transcription with Pre-Trained Language Models" (2024) refines OCR output of historical Japanese using six open-source LLMs trained on Minna de Honkoku crowdsourced transcriptions. https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1050865508277688832
- SAT Daizōkyō Text Database: complete Taishō vols. 1-85 with preserved location anchors; because plain text cannot represent Taishō critical apparatus (footnotes, font-size variants), scanned page images are provided side-by-side (SAT 2012); IIIF image server and Digital Hôbôgirin released 2021; parallel viewing with Tripitaka Koreana; image links to Waseda, UTokyo, British Library, NDL holdings. https://21dzk.l.u-tokyo.ac.jp/SAT/index_en.html ; http://www.acmuller.net/articles/2017-10-sat-taisho.pdf
- SAT-CBETA relationship: SAT representatives persuaded publisher Daizō Shuppan to relax copyright restrictions, enabling CBETA (Taiwan) to publish Taishō vols. 1-55 + 85 electronically; the two teams cooperate on rare-character representation. http://www.acmuller.net/articles/2017-10-sat-taisho.pdf
