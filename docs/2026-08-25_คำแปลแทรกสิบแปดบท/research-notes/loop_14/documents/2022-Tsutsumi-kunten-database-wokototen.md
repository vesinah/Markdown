## Verified content (from the CiNii abstract, JA/EN)

1. The paper discusses a Kunten Database that stores point diagrams (tenzu), which are the accumulated results of kunten scholarship.
2. Database functions: easy searching of kunten information and of the Wokototen Table; supports work such as moving points between positions (iten), producing interpretations (shakubun), and generating kakikudashi text; computerizes comparative study of kunten that was previously manual.
3. Experiment: automatic classification of point diagrams into the groups 1 through 8 established by earlier kunten research; focusing on the dot shape at nine important positions achieved an accuracy above 95 percent.
4. Implication: machine-assisted classification can now reproduce expert groupings, enabling large-scale comparison of glossing traditions across manuscripts.
