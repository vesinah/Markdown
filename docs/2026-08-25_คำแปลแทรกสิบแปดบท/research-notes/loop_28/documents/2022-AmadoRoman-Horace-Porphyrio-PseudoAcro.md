## Abstract points (from the article's Spanish summary)

The work examines the commentaries on Horace's Odes composed by Porphyrio, Pseudo-Acro, Cristoforo Landino and Antonio Mancinelli (Horatius cum quatuor commentariis, Venice 1492). After a brief overview of the Horatian exegetical tradition, it analyzes similarities and differences in content and form between ancient scholia and Renaissance commentaries, highlighting their function in an eminently school context.

## Key extracted content (translated from the Spanish by note-taker)

Chronology of the two ancient scholia corpora: "la primera interpretacion de la que se tiene noticia es la realizada por Porfirion en el siglo III d.C., seguida por la de Pseudo-Acron, obra cuyo nucleo casi con toda seguridad se conformo en el siglo V, aunque fue objeto de diversas alteraciones a lo largo de epoca medieval (Canto Llorca, 1994: 349)." — First known interpretation = Porphyrio, 3rd c. AD; Pseudo-Acro's core formed in the 5th c., with various alterations throughout the medieval period.

School context: "La aparicion de estos primeros escolios, escuetos por el caracter elemental de sus observaciones, se halla ligada a un contexto fundamentalmente academico: la lectura y el estudio de auctores -sobre todo Virgilio, pero tambien Terencio, Horacio, Persio, Lucano, Estacio o Juvenal- desempenaban un papel crucial en la escuela del grammaticus, dado su interes formativo tanto desde un punto de vista linguistico como exegetico (Pugliarello, 2009: 592)." — The first scholia arose in the grammaticus school through reading of the auctores: above all Virgil, but also Terence, Horace, Persius, Lucan, Statius and Juvenal.

Character of the notes: "...las interpretaciones de Porfirion y Pseudo-Acron, a pesar de incluir notas relacionadas con realia y aspectos retoricos, se caracterizan principalmente por prestar atencion a cuestiones lexicas, sintacticas o etimologicas, concibiendose de este modo los versos del Venusino como un modelo practico de ensenanza de la latinitas (Diederich, 1999: 344)."

Book order evidence of excerpting/rearrangement: "No resulta nada fortuito, de hecho, que en las anotaciones de Porfirion -tal y como han llegado hasta la actualidad- no se respete el orden cronologico, con los Carmina emplazados al comienzo..." — Porphyrio's annotations as transmitted do not respect chronological order, with the Carmina placed first.

Renaissance frame: the article then compares these ancient strata with the printed quadruple-commentary edition Horatius cum quatuor commentariis (Venice 1492), showing continuity of school use from antiquity to humanism.

## Relevance notes for the book chapter

- RQ2 anchor for both Horace scholia traditions with dates and character.
- The auctores list (Virgil, Terence, Horace, Persius, Lucan, Statius, Juvenal) is the single best quotable sentence linking Virgil commentary, Lucan/Juvenal scholia, and classroom practice across RQ1-RQ2-RQ5.
- Demonstrates that ancient scholia were still being repackaged for schools in print (Venice 1492), i.e. the glossary-commentary culture never died between late antiquity and the Middle Ages.
