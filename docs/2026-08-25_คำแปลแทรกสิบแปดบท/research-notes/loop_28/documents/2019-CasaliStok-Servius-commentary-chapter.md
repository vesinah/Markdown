## Abstract (verbatim, from publisher/Semantic Scholar)

"Servius (called Marius or Maurus Servius Honoratus in manuscripts from the ninth century onwards) was a grammarian active in Rome in the late fourth and fifth centuries AD. His celebrated commentary on Virgil is generally held to be based on a commentary (now lost) by the earlier fourth-century commentator Aelius Donatus (the teacher of St Jerome). Some manuscripts contain an enlarged version of Servius' commentary, the so-called Servius Danielis (Servius auctus, DServius or DS), published in 1600 by Pierre Daniel and thought to be a seventh-to eighth-century expansion made on the basis of material from Donatus' commentary not used by Servius himself. ## Facts secured for the book (each traceable to this abstract or to verified edition metadata)

1. The name form Marius/Maurus Servius Honoratus appears in manuscripts only from the ninth century onward — a datable onomastic datum.
2. Servius = grammarian active in Rome, late 4th - 5th c. AD.
3. The commentary is generally held to rest on Aelius Donatus' lost commentary (Donatus = teacher of St Jerome).
4. The enlarged recension "Servius Danielis"/DServius was first published by Pierre Daniel in 1600; current consensus view quoted here: a 7th-8th c. expansion incorporating Donatan material not used by Servius himself.
