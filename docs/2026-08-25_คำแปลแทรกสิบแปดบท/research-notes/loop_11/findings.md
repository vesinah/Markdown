## Key findings (with URLs)

### RQ1 — Composition of the Library Cave corpus by language

- Scale and dating: about 50,000 items spanning the 4th to early 11th centuries; sealed early 11th century (one commonly cited terminus: ca. 1002 CE); found 1900 by Wang Yuanlu from the corridor of Cave 16. Sources: IDP learning page https://idp.bl.uk/discover/learning/dunhuang/dunhuang-articles/cave-17-the-library-cave/what-was-stored-in-cave-17/ ; UW/Dunhuang Academy exhibit https://www.dunhuang.ds.lib.uw.edu/mogao-cave-17-the-library-cave-late-tang-dynasty
- Language profile: manuscripts in ca. 20 languages/scripts; vast majority Chinese (about 36,000 pieces of Chinese literature per the Dunhuang Academy text), followed by several thousand Tibetan; smaller but significant groups in Sanskrit, Khotanese, Old Uighur, Sogdian; also Hebrew prayer (Pelliot collection), Prakrit/Gandhari elsewhere in wider corpus. Source: IDP page above.
- Tangut: the Tangut script (devised 1036) is ABSENT from the Library Cave cache proper; over 200 Tangut fragments come instead from northern-area caves 464/465 excavated by Pelliot (Yuan-period material alongside Chinese, Tibetan, Old Uighur). Cross-check note: this claim is widely repeated but was encountered here only via non-citable aggregators plus IDP's general statement that "Thousands of manuscripts from the Tangut kingdom are also found in the collections" (https://idp.bl.uk/discover/collection-categories/manuscripts/) — treat the cave-specific absence claim as needing a primary citation (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยันจากแหล่งอ้างอิงปฐมภูมิในรอบนี้). A citable lead for Tangut-Tibetan bilingual material is Arakawa Shintaro's study of Or.12380/1842 announced at http://idpuk.blogspot.com/2012/07/new-publication.html
- Sealing debate (why): sacred-waste/waste theory (Stein, Fujieda feiqi shuo), inventory-clearing (Fang Guangchang), concealment under threat (Pelliot tradition, no direct evidence), relic dedication (van Schaik following Salomon). Source: https://earlytibet.com/2010/12/13/secrets-of-the-cave-i/

### RQ2 — Khotanese-Sanskrit bilingualism and glossaries

- Pelliot chinois 5538 verso: Sanskrit-Khotanese colloquy/phrasebook (late 10th c.) copied on the blank side of an official letter of King Visa Sura to the ruler of Dunhuang (year 970). Dialogue between a visiting Indian monk and a Khotanese monk in Dunhuang; pilgrimage theme (Wutaishan/Manjusri); concludes it is most likely a pedagogical colloquy (like medieval Latin colloquies) teaching a vernacular monastic Sanskrit lingua franca. Sources: van Schaik 2022 chapter https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110776492-004/html (excerpts); IDP blog https://idp.bl.uk/blog/phrasebooks-for-silk-route-travellers/ ; full-text mirrors at ResearchGate (CC BY-NC-ND) https://www.researchgate.net/publication/359748411
- Foundational editions: Kumamoto 1988 "A Sanskrit-Khotanese Conversation Manual for the Traveler in the Western Regions", Seinan Ajia Kenkyu 28: 53-82; Bailey 1964 "Sri Visa Sura and the Ta-uang". Works list: https://gengo.l.u-tokyo.ac.jp/hkum/works.html
- Book of Zambasta: NOT from Dunhuang (main ms from Khotan area; 192 of 207 identified folios in Leningrad/St Petersburg) — important corrective for the book's framing; named by H.W. Bailey after patron Ysambasta; edited Emmerick 1968; Bailey's KT VI Prolexis (1967) serves as selective dictionary; no up-to-date complete glossary exists. Source: https://iranicaonline.org/articles/book-of-zambasta-poem/
- Bailey infrastructure: Dictionary of Khotan Saka (1979) scanned at https://archive.org/details/dictionary-of-khotan-saka ; Khotanese Texts vols. scanned at https://archive.org/details/khotanesetexts0103bail ; web dictionary project https://khotanese.org/dictionary/ (abbreviations list documents Skjaervo 2002 BL catalogue).
- Dunhuang Khotanese corpus structure (Yoshida/Iranica): all Late Khotanese, many produced IN Dunhuang; group 1 = ca. 90 mss written on versos of Chinese Buddhist scrolls (reuse culture), group 2 = ca. 30 fair-copy pothi (Vajracchedika, Siddhasara medical text); plus Chinese-Khotanese conversation manual copies (Takata pp. 195-227), Brahmi-script phonetic transcription of Buddhist Chinese (Emmerick-Pulleyblank), teacher's model alphabet ("Learn, pupil, so that you do not get my stick!"). URL: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/dunhuang-02-buddhist-iranian-language

### RQ3 — Tibetan-Chinese bilingual glossaries and phrasebooks

- PT1257 (Pelliot tibetain 1257): ten-page bamboo-bound booklet; scripture-title list (folios 2-4) + Tibetan-Chinese lexicon of Buddhist terminology; Tibetan first, Chinese second; ~8% terms lack Chinese equivalents; seven different hands for the Chinese entries; compiled 779-814 CE (pre-Mahavyutpatti); reattributed by Apple & Apple as a document INITIATED BY TIBETANS to learn Chinese equivalents, possibly connected to imperial communication with occupied Chinese monasteries; strong Chan and Samdhinirmocana terminology overlap; comparison dossier with PT1261 (Yogacarabhumi Chinese-Tibetan glossary scroll). Full text: https://d1i1jdw69xsqx0.cloudfront.net/digitalhimalaya/collections/journals/ret/pdf/ret_42_03.pdf ; newer OA re-examination: https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/15/6/748
- Or.8210/S.1000 + S.2736: Tibeto-Chinese word-and-phrase book FOR MERCHANTS — Tibetan headword, Chinese equivalent, ALL in Tibetan script (phonetic); goods/haggling/lodging/legal-trouble phrases; includes bon po translated into Chinese; ends with "shut up!". Earlier edition: Thomas & Giles BSOAS 12 (1948): 753-769 (JSTOR https://www.jstor.org/stable/608734). Source: https://earlytibet.com/2014/05/12/silk-road-phrasebooks/
- PT1261: extensive scroll-format Chinese-Tibetan glossary of Yogacarabhumi terms; multiple semi-cursive Chinese hands; cursive Tibetan (Apple & Apple 2017 section 4.1).
- Marketplace dimension: IOL Khot 140 (10th-c. Khotanese monastery goods receipt) has individual items GLOSSED IN TIBETAN — evidence of Tibetan as marketplace lingua franca under/after Tibetan rule (same source).

### RQ4 — Colophon/prayer culture and proofreading practice

- Imperial scribal economy: early 9th-century mass copying on Tibetan imperial order (Perfection of Wisdom in pothi format e.g. IOL Tib J 105; Limitless Life/Aparimitayus scrolls); paper issued to scribes and audited; IOL Tib J 1359 = register of paper debts; missing sheets repaid or punished by whipping; model = "distributed scriptorium" (central office issuing paper, auditing, punishing) rather than one building. Source: https://idp.bl.uk/blog/scribes-at-dunhuang-pens-paper-and-punishments/
- Merit colophons: copies function as "receipts" of merit transactions (Kieschnick quote in van Schaik 2010); Lotus Sutra copy commissioned by a nun mourning her mother; hundreds of Aparimitayus copies for the Tibetan emperor's long life; purpose analysis in "The Purpose of Sutra Copying in Dunhuang Under the Tibetan Rule" (AN for emperor's longevity; MP/SP for distribution): https://www.academia.edu/2349014/
- Cross-imperial proofreading network: at least five of the SAME scribes and correctors appear in colophons of both the Dunhuang Satasahasrika-prajnaparamita copies AND copies preserved at Sgrol-dkar temple in Central Tibet (Imaeda 1998 on P.T. 999; cited in the sutra-copying study above) — colophons literally tie Dunhuang workshops to Central Tibetan repositories.
- Donor inscriptions: ca. 7,000 donor inscriptions in Mogao murals plus donor portraits/inscriptions on paintings from the Library Cave; evolution from simple "offered wholeheartedly" formulas to status-displaying titles by Five Dynasties/Song. Source: https://www.dunhuang.ds.lib.uw.edu/dunhuang-cave-donors-%E6%95%A6%E7%85%8C%E7%9F%B3%E7%AA%9F%E4%BE%9B%E5%85%BB%E4%BA%BA/
- Printed-book colophon benchmark: Diamond Sutra (Or.8210/P.2) colophon names sponsor Wang Jie, 11 May 868 — earliest dated printed book. Source: IDP Cave 17 learning page above.
- Scribal-material annotation detail: after the Tibetan occupation cut off brush/bamboo supplies, scribes switched to wooden/reed styluses (Fujieda 1971/1991, cited in Apple & Apple footnote 4) — hard-point annotations physically differ from brush writing, a detectable palaeographic signal of annotation layers.

### RQ5 — IDP and collection infrastructure

- International Dunhuang Programme (IDP): international collaboration headquartered at British Library; partners/mirrors include IDP National Library of China (idp.nlc.cn) and IDP Dunhuang Academy (idp.dha.ac.cn); provides images, descriptions, catalog records for dispersed items; free access. Sources: https://idp.bl.uk/glossary (footer links); https://idp.bl.uk/discover/collection-categories/manuscripts/
- Numbering systems documented from fetched sources: S./Or.8210/S.xxx = Stein Chinese scrolls (BL); IOL Tib J xxx = India Office Library Tibetan (BL); Or.9614 = BL Khotanese accession (Book of Zambasta leaves: https://idp.bl.uk/collection/20DD6A04679D41E0806AA8CEABF2FDAF/); P. chinois / Pelliot chinois xxxx and Pelliot tibetain (PT) xxxx = BnF; Ch. = Qianfodong; SI O = Serindia Oldenburg (Yoshida/Iranica). Catalogue anchors: Skjaervo 2002 (BL Khotanese, complete catalogue with texts/translations); Zhang Guangda & Rong Xinjiang pp. 118-148 (Paris Khotanese); Lalou 1950 (PT inventory, cited in Apple & Apple); Dalton & van Schaik 2006 (BL Tibetan tantric, Brill); van Schaik 2014 (Tibetan Chan catalogue).
- New layer: Dunhuang Academy Digital Library Cave — crowdsourced transcription/annotation, AI-powered search, OCR; WeChat mini-program access. Source: https://mapping-project.org/resources/73/
- Direct-fetch status: idp.bl.uk behind Cloudflare challenge for automated fetching; content obtained via search-index excerpts (recorded honestly here).

### RQ6 — Modern scholarship on Dunhuang as crossroads of annotation practices

- Galambos 2020/2021 Dunhuang Manuscript Culture (SMC 22): 9th-10th c. Guiyijun manuscripts show heavy Central Asian influence; less Chinese than assumed; chapters on student manuscripts, writing direction, multiple-text manuscripts; metadata verified, full text BLOCKED (see gaps). https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110726572
- Van Schaik & Dalton 2004 "Where Chan and Tantra Meet": five 'hybrid' Tibetan manuscripts (PT 322, 626, 634, 699, 808) combining Chan and Mahayoga techniques; Goodman 2022 adds same-hand attribution incl. IOL Tib J 689/320 — single-scribe hypothesis replacing diffuse syncretism narrative. Full PDF: https://omp.ub.rub.de/index.php/BuddhistRoad/catalog/download/245/222/1273?inline=1
- Fujieda Akira (Kyoto): foundational two-part survey Zinbun 9 (1966): 1-32 and Zinbun 10 (1969): 17-39; championed the waste theory; style statistics (>60% of Dunhuang Chinese mss in the standard style per his 1966 count, as cited in later studies); pen/material argument. Bibliographic confirmation via Persoe listing: https://www.persee.fr/doc/asie_0766-1177_1987_num_3_1_888 and BSOAS article reference lists: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-school-of-oriental-and-african-studies/article/by-no-means-doodles-or-scraps-reading-manuscripts-iol-tib-j-3-and-218-as-bilingual-dunhuang-vinaya-works/4C204E5853B953A98C05F12F40E0B879 (the latter also treats IOL Tib J 3 and 218 as bilingual Vinaya works — direct interlinear-relevant case).
- Van Schaik-Galambos 2012 Manuscripts and Travellers (De Gruyter): Sino-Tibetan documents of a 10th-century pilgrim; used repeatedly as sealing-theory reference (pp. 18-28 per UCL Press indexed excerpt).
- Emergent theme for the book: Dunhuang annotation operates at FOUR registers simultaneously — (i) term-level bilingual lexica (PT1257, PT1261, Thomas-Giles phrasebook); (ii) sentence-level colloquies (P.5538); (iii) script-switching transcription (Chinese words in Tibetan script; Tibetan language in Khotanese script; Avestan in Sogdian script; Buddhist Chinese in Brahmi); (iv) paratextual colophons binding merit, audit and identity. This four-register taxonomy is supported jointly by the six harvested texts.
