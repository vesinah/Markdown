## Content notes

### The manuscript PT1257 as a bilingual learning tool

- Pelliot tibetain 1257 (PT1257) is an early manuscript from Dunhuang kept in the Pelliot collection at the Bibliotheque Nationale de France; digital images at Gallica and IDP.
- Physical form: ten pages of rolled paper sewn between two bamboo sticks, wrapped in a long yellow-dyed cover sheet (Lalou 1950 catalogue description confirmed by authors' autopsy).
- Bamboo could not grow around Dunhuang; after the Tibetan occupation bamboo became rare, so a bamboo-bound manuscript signals distinguished treatment (citing Fujieda 1971/1991 on the switch to wooden/reed pens after brush materials became scarce).
- Content = two documents bound together: (1) a list of Buddhist scripture titles in Tibetan and Chinese (folios 2-4) and (2) a lexicon/glossary of Buddhist terminology in Tibetan and Chinese (the vocabulary section).
- Copyists wrote Tibetan first, then the Chinese equivalent. About eight percent of Tibetan terms lack Chinese equivalents; the Chinese entries are written in approximately seven different hands.

### Redating and re-attribution

- Earlier scholarship (Lalou 1939; R.A. Stein 1983/2010; Fujieda 1966; Yamaguchi 1975; Hakamaya 1984; Kimura 1985; Akamatsu 1988) treated PT1257 as made in a Chinese monastery to help Chinese scholars translate Tibetan.
- The Apples argue instead that it was compiled and circulated BY Tibetans among Chinese monasteries of Dunhuang, so that Tibetan imperial monastic authorities could learn Chinese equivalents for terminology already fixed in Old Tibetan translation practice.
- Terminology predates the imperially decreed standardization of the Mahavyutpatti (814 CE); orthography/palaeography place initial composition between 779 and 814 CE - one of the earliest known Tibetan lexicons of Buddhist terminology.
- Vocabulary interest includes Chan (Chinese Zen) equivalents and Indic-based terms such as those of the Samdhinirmocanasutra - relevant to the Samye debate context (ca. 792-794 CE).
- Sample glosses from the transcription: dge 'ba' / 惡 (evil); 'dus byas / 無為? pair with 有為-type terms (conditioned); kun rdzob / 世俗 (conventional); don dam / 真諦 (ultimate); mya ngan las 'da' ba / 涅槃 (nirvana); gang zag la bdag med pa / 人无我 (person-selflessness); chos la bdag med pa / 法无我 (dharma-selflessness); lhag mthong / 觀 (insight); zhi gnas / 止 (calm abiding); thabs mkhas / 善巧方便 (skillful means); shes rab / 心? column pairing with 心 (per extracted layout).

### Comparison with PT1261 (second bilingual glossary)

- Pelliot tibetain 1261 is a Chinese-Tibetan glossary of terms from the Yogacarabhumi-sastra (Yujiashidi lun 瑜伽師地論), scroll format, Chinese vertical, several semi-cursive Chinese hands, cursive Tibetan.
- PT1261 is interpreted as Tibetan monks equipping themselves with Chinese terminology; possibly a draft of an official document offered to high-ranking Chinese monks by Tibetan authorities.

### Relevance to interlinear-gloss project

- Direct evidence of systematic two-column bilingual annotation practice (term-by-term equivalence lists) produced inside the Dunhuang scribal economy under Tibetan rule (786-848).
- Demonstrates glossaries functioning as administrative/imperial instruments, not merely private study aids.
- Shows multi-scribe compilation (seven hands) - a distributed annotation workflow comparable to proofreading teams attested in colophons.
