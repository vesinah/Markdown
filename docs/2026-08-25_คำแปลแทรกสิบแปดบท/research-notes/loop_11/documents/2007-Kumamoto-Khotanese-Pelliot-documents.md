## Content notes

### The Pelliot Khotanese document corpus and its edition history

- Khotanese documents of the Pelliot collection were published in facsimile by R.E. Emmerick as Saka Documents V and VI (1971, 1973); Kumamoto is preparing the companion Text Volume II in the Corpus Inscriptionum Iranicarum series.
- Series context: Bailey edited Saka Documents I-IV with Text Volume (1968) for Hoernle, Stein, Hedin and some Pelliot manuscripts; Emmerick and Vorobyova-Desyatovskaya produced Saka Documents III (facsimile 1993; text volume 1995) for St Petersburg.

### P 2786 - longest Khotanese secular document (250 lines)

- Structure in thirds: (1) draft of a report to the Khotan royal court by a Khotanese envoy leading a delegation of Buddhist priests and laymen, halted at Dunhuang by order of the ruler of Dunhuang (the Linggong) because the road east was unsafe; (2) a revised, expanded version of the same report - draft-to-fair-copy layering inside one manuscript; (3) a different topic (sending products of Khotan).
- A Stein miscellaneous manuscript contains a parallel account written by the chief priest of the same delegation - independent description of the same events across collections and languages.
- Date: 10th century, more likely second half.

### Cross-collection joins as evidence of manuscript circulation

- P 2031 + P 2788 join into a single fragment (Chinese side and Khotanese side both continuous).
- P 2898 (BnF) joins with Ch. 00327 (= IOL Khot S.18, British Library): continuous text on BOTH sides - Chinese side is a copy of the Aparimitayus-sutra known to have been mass-produced under Tibetan rule at Dunhuang; unnoticed for decades because the pieces are deposited in different institutions.
- These documents may belong to the end of the ninth century.

### Official letters and direct evidence

- P 4091: genuine official letter from the Khotan royal court (not a draft scribbled on discarded Chinese scrolls), ornamental script like P 5538; uses the Chinese regnal year Kaiyun 2 = 945 (Later Jin) with a square Chinese seal.
- P 5538: official letter of the Khotanese king Visa Sura to the ruler of Dunhuang, year 970 (Khotanese regnal year in Chinese style); verso later used for the Sanskrit-Khotanese conversation manual.
- These two letters are the only Khotanese texts from Dunhuang certainly written IN Khotan and sent to Dunhuang - hence, given the lack of datable 10th-century Khotanese manuscripts from the Khotan area itself, they are the only direct evidence of the Khotanese language used in 10th-century Khotan.

### Literary fragments

- "Letters in verse" group; P 4649 has a Khotanese poem with a female narrator (humble maidservant, feminine past-tense verb forms) written on the back of a large sheet bearing a six-armed Bodhisattva drawing.
- P 2739 in bad shape already when Emmerick made his 1970s facsimile; Bailey's readings rest on pre-war photographs now in the Ancient India and Iran Trust - material instability of the corpus documented.
