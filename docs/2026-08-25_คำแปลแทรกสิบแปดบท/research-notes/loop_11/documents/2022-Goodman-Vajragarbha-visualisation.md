## Content notes

### Van Schaik and Dalton manuscript typology as starting point

- Core reference: van Schaik and Dalton, "Where Chan and Tantra Meet: Tibetan Syncretism in Dunhuang", in The Silk Road: Trade, Travel, War and Faith, ed. Susan Whitfield and Ursula Sims Williams (Serindia, 2004), pp. 61-71 (cited at p. 63).
- The five 'hybrid' Tibetan manuscripts combining Chan meditation techniques with tantric practice are Pelliot tibetain 322, 626, 634, 699 and 808 (Bibliotheque nationale de France).
- Additional manuscripts apparently written by the same hand: IOL Tib J 689 (British Library Stein collection); Jacob Dalton (Conjuring the Buddha) identifies the same uchen/cursive hand plus decorative marker in IOL Tib J 320.
- Goodman's contribution: links all five hybrid manuscripts to a single Dunhuang author or scribe, redirecting decades of speculation about a Chan-Tantra connection to one hand at one site - palaeography as attribution tool.

### Cross-language manuscript matrix at 10th-century Dunhuang

- The paper examines a Chinese sadhana (Jingangzang pusa sanzi guanxiang) alongside THREE Tibetan Mahayoga works from the same site: a commentary on a brief Chan meditation text and two ritual commentaries on the same root sadhana.
- Seven manuscripts total (four Chinese, three Tibetan) situated within the wider manuscript matrix of late Guiyijun-period (851-1036) Dunhuang - evidence that Chinese and Tibetan ritual annotation/commentary traditions ran in parallel and interacted.
- P.T. 626 and P.T. 634: anonymous ritual commentaries on an unidentified Mahayoga sadhana; Dalton surmises composition in Tibet or Tibetan-speaking areas around Dunhuang.
- P.T. 699 contains a commentary on the Lung chung (Small Treatise) meditation instructions; three copies of the Lung chung excavated from Dunhuang: IOL Tib J 689, IOL Tib J 1774, and P.T. 121.
- The three samadhi doctrine: most extensive treatment at Dunhuang in IOL Tib J 437/2; also IOL Tib J 552, 553, 554, 716; Or.8210/S.95/7; P.T. 42, P.T. 283, P.T. 634.
