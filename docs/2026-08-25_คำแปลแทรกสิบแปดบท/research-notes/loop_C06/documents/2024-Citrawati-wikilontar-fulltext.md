## Substantive content harvested (from publisher HTML full text)

1. Scale of the recording universe: Penyuluh Bahasa Bali (contract staff recruited since 2016 by the Bali provincial government for language preservation including community lontar conservation) recorded 25,106 lontars across Bali in 2016-2018 (citing Ardiyasa 2021, p. 81). Recording continues; the sheer number is named as the core design challenge of the rescue project.
2. Institutional origin: Komunitas Wikipedia Denpasar instigated WikiLontar 2021; programme goal stated as collecting metadata and cataloguing community lontars "to add additional references to Wikipedia, particularly Wikidata". Data flow: identities into Wikidata; front/end-page images and transcriptions into Wikimedia Commons; everything served through WikiPustaka (Balinese Wikisource), described as "a collaborative project between Panlex.org and the Wikimedia Foundation"; automatic translation offers three Latin rendering options for re-typed lontar letters. Geospatial integration: each lontar location point assigned ordinates connected to Wikidata queries mapping Balinese lontars.
3. Field results: of ten target lontar owners at project outset, FOUR DECLINED to participate despite repeated approaches; owners' belief in the sacredness of lontars is analysed as the major obstacle (with the fear-of-reading complex noted: owners maintain lontars without daring to read them).
4. Directly relevant find: the project "found a particular type of Lontars, i.e. Kakawin", specifically "Kakawin Bratha Yudha from Gria Kelodan Sawan (Buleleng), Jro Mangku Tojan (Klungkung), and Jro Mangku Sukawati (Gianyar)". Footnotes give the image URLs: Sawan = ban.wikisource.org/wiki/Kaca:Bali-lontar-Sawan-Bharata_Yudha_Maarti.jpg (the file NAME identifies the Sawan copy as a MAARTI copy - a third, community-owned Bharatayudha maarti witness, held by a brahmin residence in Buleleng); Tojan = ...Kaca:Bali-lontar-Tojan-Kakawin_Bharatayudha.jpg; Sukawati image on Wikimedia Commons (Bali-lontar-Sukawati-Kakawin_Bharata_Yuda-34.jpg). A Wikidata item Q104529414 is cited in the notes (item identity not inspected; Wikimedia = finding aid).
5. Background claims usable for the historical chapter: earliest attestation of the word "lontar" in the Trunyan A II inscription, 971 saka, which records villagers asking the king to have a damaged lontar inscription rewritten and redone on copper (Ardika and Beratha 1996); Kirtya founded 1892 (Hooykaas 1979: 348) in response to enthusiastic foreign buying of lontars; typology of physical formats: cakep/cakepan (bound, clamped volume; may contain several titles), embat-embatan (single-hole loose leaves, also used for notes/letters/genealogies), kropak (wooden box storage); pressing tool called pamlagbag; Sarasvati Day as the community commemoration of the descent of knowledge.
6. Framing useful for our argument: before WikiLontar, academic recording produced library-type catalogues while "data on community-owned Lontars remained unintegrated as freely accessible public information"; preliminary community tracking could even surface "new manuscripts that had never been studied" because researchers had relied almost solely on library-held data.
