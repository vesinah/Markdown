# Document harvest: the "Corbie vs Soissons" archetype/origin question - identification and position-stating study

Loop: C03 (Phase C), harvested 2026-08-26.

## What the question refers to

Identification result: "Corbie vs Soissons" is NOT a stemma dispute inside the Martianus Capella gloss tradition. It is the classic debate over WHERE AND BY WHOM the Liber glossarum (Liber glossarum / Glossarium Ansileubi, hereafter LG) - the largest Latin gloss collection of the early Middle Ages, glossing about 27,000 Latin words in a three-column elephant folio - was COMPILED around the late eighth century. The Martianus connection is indirect: the oldest Carolingian gloss tradition ON Martianus ("oldest gloss tradition", OGT) circulated through Corbie as its main distribution hub, and the LG question is routinely discussed alongside it because both concern how Carolingian gloss collections were produced and disseminated.

Anchor study found (open access, positions stated in its opening pages):

Michel Huglo, "Les arts liberaux dans le 'Liber glossarum'", Scriptorium 55.1 (2001), pp. 3-33; DOI https://doi.org/10.3406/scrip.2001.1914; full text on Persée: https://www.persee.fr/doc/scrip_0036-9772_2001_num_55_1_1914 (fetched this loop).

## Positions as stated by Huglo 2001 (opening page, verbatim render)

1. BERNARD BISCHOFF (cited to "Die Bibliothek im Dienste der Schule", 1972, in Mittelalterliche Studien III, 231): the ARCHETYPE of the LG was assembled during the first term of Abbot Adalhard's abbacy (780-814) at CORBIE; it is written in ab-minuscule supplemented by Maurdramne-type script, both scripts practiced at Corbie from the late 8th into the early 9th century.
2. DAVID GANZ (Corbie in the Carolingian Renaissance, Sigmaringen 1990, 48-55 and 143): the collation of sources and layout of thousands of alphabetically ordered slips would have been carried out by NUNS in a monastery AFFILIATED to Corbie - Chelles perhaps, or NOTRE-DAME DE SOISSONS - the house Ganz prudently calls "the ab scriptorium". This is the Soissons side of the debate.
3. M. B. PARKES (oral communication to Huglo, Austin TX, 13 April 2000): there must have existed SEVERAL ab scriptoria in northern France, Soissons for example.
4. GIGLIOLA BARBERO (per Huglo's survey of her conclusions): manuscript P (Paris BnF lat. 11529-11530) cannot be the archetype but is a COPY of the archetype made at Corbie, kept up to date into the early 9th century; Lowe, CLA 5 no. 611: "written doubtless at Corbie".
5. Huglo adds the reception datum: Martianus Capella's De nuptiis entered the arts curriculum under Louis the Pious and was diffused by Corbie; the LG quarried older glossaries (Placidus etc.) rather than Martianus directly for most rare words.

So the live alternatives are: composition AT Corbie itself (Bischoff, Lowe, Barbero) versus composition in a Corbie-affiliated NUNNERY, with Notre-Dame de Soissons (or Chelles) as candidates (Ganz), or multiple northern houses (Parkes). A further modern refinement shifts the OLDEST LAYER elsewhere entirely:

6. EVINA STEINOVA, "The List of Notae in the Liber Glossarum", Journal of Medieval Latin (Brepols), https://doi.org/10.1484/J.JML.5.112087 - abstract states that comparison of the LG sign list with Isidore's Etymologiae "provides evidence that the oldest core of this glossary came into being on the Iberian peninsula and includes material that had been used by Isidore of Seville", following Anne Grondeux.
7. The LibGloss project edition site (https://liber-glossarum.huma-num.fr/exist/apps/libgloss/TheLiberGlossarum_en.html) frames the work as compiled from heterogeneous materials originating in 7th-century Visigothic Spain, widely circulated by the end of the 8th century, present in Carolingian libraries including Corbie, Reichenau, Saint-Riquier, Fleury, Lorsch, Tours, Auxerre.

## Supporting technical study

Franck Cinato, "Que nous apprennent les ecritures des plus anciens temoins du Liber glossarum sur l'archetype?", HAL https://hal.science/hal-01421393v2/file/D10-HEL-CinatoV2.pdf (direct download blocked this loop by Anubis anti-bot protection; content taken from search-engine-rendered excerpts, flagged accordingly):
- The three oldest witnesses P (Paris), C (Cambrai), V (Vatican) are second-generation copies via lost antigraphs phi and gamma per Anne Grondeux's stemma (LibGloss project).
- T. A. M. Bishop, "The prototype of Liber glossarum" (1978), comparing P and C codicologically, explained the LG's famous alphabetical disorders as QUIRE-TURN accidents in copying - evidence that the archetype was a homogeneous book, while residual micro-disorders point to an earlier composite stage ("tare de naissance") in its composition.
- The paper promises an updated bilan des hypotheses relatives a l'origine du Liber glossarum.

## The Martianus side (for the book's Latin chapter)

- MMP database entry for "Glossae Aevi Carolini in libros I-II Martiani Capellae De Nuptiis Philologiae et Mercurii (825-950)" (https://mmp.acdh-dev.oeaw.ac.at/archiv/text/detail/4): the OGT was attributed once to Dunchad, then Martin of Laon, now to multiple scholars; "Corbie appears to have been an important distribution centre, together with Auxerre, Fleury, Reims, and Lorsch. The place of origin of the text is uncertain." Edition: CC CM 237 (S. O'Sullivan 2010).
- Sinead O'Sullivan (editor of CC CM 237, 2010): introduction excerpts via https://www.academia.edu/13372221: the manuscripts of Group B - Pb, R, Pd - are closely linked with Corbie; Bischoff locates Pb there on palaeographical grounds, Ganz notes the hand of Hadoard in some glosses, Corbin its notation, Teeuwen its neumes. Her case study "Problems in Editing Glosses: A Case Study of Carolingian Glosses on Martianus Capella" (QUB PDF above) documents lemma identification, accretion, cross-fertilization and multi-hand layering in twenty witnesses of the OGT (ca. 825-900).

## Usable formulation for the book

For interlinear-gloss historiography, cite the Corbie-vs-Soissons pair specifically as the PLACE-OF-COMPOSITION question of the Liber glossarum: Corbie (Bischoff-Lowe-Barbero: archetype written in Corbian ab-minuscule) against Soissons-or-Chelles nuns affiliated to Corbie (Ganz's "ab scriptorium"), now complicated by the Iberian-core thesis (Grondeux, Steinova). For the Martianus gloss tradition, use O'Sullivan/MMP wording: origin uncertain, Corbie the principal distribution centre.
