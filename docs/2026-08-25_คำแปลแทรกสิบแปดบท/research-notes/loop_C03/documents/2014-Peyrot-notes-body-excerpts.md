## Verbatim body content of Part I recovered through quotation

Source A: Tao Pan 2021, Acta Asiatica Varsoviensia 34: 263-294 (OA PDF: http://aav.iksiopan.pl/images/aav34_10_Pan.pdf; DOI https://doi.org/10.60018/AcAsVa.abdn5783). Downloaded and text-extracted locally this loop (36 pp.). Pan quotes Part I directly:

1. "This 'remarkable' circumstance is noted by Peyrot 2014: 163 as well: 'It is remarkable that all Tocharian glosses to this fragment are in Tocharian A, except for this one.'" (Pan p. 263-264, quoting Peyrot p. 163 on SHT 4438.)
2. "'There seems to be no palaeographical difference between the Tocharian A glosses and this gloss in Tocharian B' (Peyrot 2014: 163 fn. 40)." - i.e., the single TB gloss among TA glosses is CONTEMPORANEOUS by script, not a later addition.
3. Pan summarizes Peyrot's explanation of that TB gloss (saiṣṣe 'world' under Skt. jagat-) as motivated by brevity ("TB śaiṣṣe is shorter than TA ārkiśoṣi") and disputes it on spatial grounds ("on the folio there is enough space even for a word of 8 akṣaras").
4. Pan's footnote 2 cites the survey corpus as "See Malzahn 2007a: 301-319, Peyrot 2014, 2015" - confirming Peyrot 2014 treats the same SHT gloss corpus.

Analytical value: Part I p. 163 demonstrably contains a language-distribution observation per fragment (all-TA except one TB gloss) plus a palaeographic argument for single-workshop execution of mixed-language glossing - exactly the kind of typological statement B03 lacked.

Source B: Georges-Jean Pinault 2022, "Colophons in Tocharian Manuscripts", in N. Balbir and G. Ciotti (eds.), The Syntax of Colophons, De Gruyter (Studies in Manuscript Cultures 27), pp. 347-372. OA at https://hal.science/hal-03911250v1/document and https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110795271-012. Footnote 10, verbatim: "The searching function of CEToM yields 70 items. This number should be slightly revised. The colophons found in Sanskrit manuscripts edited by Peyrot 2014, 134-136 and 2015, 108-112 should also be added." - establishes that Part I pp. 134-136 and Part II pp. 108-112 are the colophon-edition sections of the respective articles, and that Sanskrit manuscripts carrying Tocharian colophons form a distinct stratum beside CEToM's ~70 native-Tocharian colophon items.

## Part II status

Author abstract (verified again from the Leiden repository record, identical wording to B03's Academia capture): "Many Sanskrit manuscripts from the Tocharian area stretching from the Kuča to the Turfan regions bear witness of use by speakers of Tocharian. Some contain glosses translating separate words or clarifying difficult notions and passages. In others, a Sanskrit text may be concluded with a colophon in Tocharian, or sometimes with later additions such as pious wishes or notes of ownership. In this article, material from the Paris, London and Berlin collections is presented."
