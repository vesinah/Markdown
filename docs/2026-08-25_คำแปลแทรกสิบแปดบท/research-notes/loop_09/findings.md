## Key findings (with URLs)

1. THE core corpus for RQ1 exists and is precisely catalogued: CEToM lists THT 527-540 as "Skt.; TB; Kocho; Doctrine; Bilingual list of doctrinal terms" and THT 541-544 (Kocho/Murtuk) as "Bilingual about doctrinal terminology". THT 527's record shows Sanskrit lemmata interwoven line-by-line with Tocharian B equivalents (identifiable Skt items: cakṣuranitā-, avadhatsva, srotra-, yathā samāhite, kalpayati, saṃpadyate, vividhāny arcīṣi...) in a pothī of 5 lines per side; scholarly chain = Couvreur 1968 ("Sanskrit-Kutschische Listen von Stichwörtern aus dem Catuṣpariṣatsūtra, Daśottarasūtra und Nidānasaṃyukta"), edition Sieg/Siegling 1953 (Tocharische Sprachreste B Heft 2), translations by K.T. Schmidt 1974. Source: https://cetom.univie.ac.at/?m-tht527 .

2. The Sanskrit-Tocharian BILINGUAL UDĀNAVARGA is an established edition series spanning a century (RQ1): first publication Sieg & Siegling 1932 BSOS 6: 483-499 ("Udānavarga-Uebersetzungen in 'Kucischer Sprache'"); Lévi-lineage Hoernle-collection volume by Werner Thomas 1971 (Bilinguale Udānavarga-Texte der Sammlung Hoernle); Thomas' two ZVS studies (1968: 183-213 on the translation; 1969: 290-322 on how Sanskrit verbs are rendered) directly address sense-vs-word rendering; modern continuation by Michaël Peyrot (IF 113 [2008]: 83-125, DOI https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110206630.83 ; TIES 17 [2016]: 153-211; BSOAS 79 [2016]: 305-327). For Tocharian A: Itkin & Malyshev 2019 publish THT 1846 as a NEW Skt-TA Udānavarga bilingual fragment; Malyshev 2019 adds a Skt-TA Cīvaravastu bilingual. Sources: https://cetom.univie.ac.at/?bibliography ; CEToM list entries A 217/218, A 391, A 425.a-f + THT 1846.

3. ATTESTED WORD-LEVEL GLOSSES (RQ5 - the core question) are real and individually catalogued: CEToM marks ~50 fragments with subgenre "Gloss", mostly Sanskrit manuscripts carrying Tocharian A or B glosses (Berlin SHT/THT series, plus Or 15009 London and PS Bl/PK NS/PS rouge Paris). Fetched examples: SHT 412.32 (Shorchuk) where TA ñom glosses Skt. adhivaca(na) 'name' and mā cäs glosses nemam 'not him', explicitly clarifying SANDHI (na imam > nemam); THT 4083.d-f (Murtuk) where TA verbs gloss Skt. verb forms and the glossator even misparses a Sanskrit compound tense; THT 4081 whose blank verso received TB clauses from two different hands next to a Skt Prātimokṣa recto; SHT 395 combines Skt Karmavācanā verso with TB writing exercise/gloss. Key surveys named by CEToM: Malzahn 2007b, "A preliminary survey of the Tocharian glosses in the Berlin Turfan Collection," Instrumenta Tocharica, 301-319; Peyrot 2014, "Notes on Tocharian glosses and colophons in Sanskrit manuscripts I," TIES 15: 131-179. Sources: https://cetom.univie.ac.at/?m-sht41232 ; https://cetom.univie.ac.at/?m-tht4083d ; https://cetom.univie.ac.at/?m-tht4081 ; https://cetom.univie.ac.at/?m-sht3951 .
   Caveat recorded honestly: the arrangement is described as glossing ON/AROUND Sanskrit leaves (interlinear/marginal/additional hands); none of the fetched pages uses the literal phrase "between the lines" for these, so the physical placement word "interlinear" should be used cautiously per-manuscript.

4. Column-parallel bilingualism also attested: besides glosses there are TRUE PARALLEL bilingual formats - the THT 527-544 doctrinal-term lists (Skt lemma + TB equivalent) and A 384/385 "Abhidharmakośa, Sanskrit terms with Tocharian explanation" (Bezeklik), whose CEToM translation shows Sanskrit technical terms (vicāra, pramāda, apramāda...) embedded in running TA explanatory sentences - i.e., a scholastic glossary/commentary genre. Plus A 387-389 labelled simply "Bilingual" (Bezeklik). Sources: https://cetom.univie.ac.at/?m-a385 ; manuscript list at https://www.univie.ac.at/tocharian/?manuscripts .

5. Tocharian B - OLD TURKIC bilingual with gloss-like alignment (RQ1/RQ4/RQ5 adjacent): the unique Manichaean hymn to Mani in TB (U 99 ff., Murtuk; leaves M8533, T II D 67) was edited by Werner Winter with Annemarie von Gabain (Berliner Turfantexte IX, 1958); Pinault (2008, full text fetched) states "the Uygur translator has conceived his glosses after the division of the text into metrical segments" (TB cola scheme 7-8-7), i.e., segment-by-segment vernacular rendering after a Tocharian model; re-treated in Clark's Turkic Pothi-Book (1982) and Peyrot/Pinault/Wilkens JA 307 (2019). Source: https://hdl.handle.net/11094/16397 .

6. SOGDIAN Buddhist translations were overwhelmingly made FROM CHINESE prototypes, not Sanskrit (RQ2): Yoshida 2019 (abstract fetched): "most Buddhist Sogdian texts are shown to have been translated from Chinese originals"; his three exceptions from Turfan (Sogdian Karmavibhaṅga; Prātihārya-sūtra = Divyāvadāna ch. 12; Kāñcanasāra legend from Daśakarmapathāvadānamālā) depend on Northern-Silk-Road originals circulating among Buddhists of Kucha/Karashahr/Turfan, and the last has TOCHARIAN AND UIGHUR versions - concrete Sogdian-Tocharian textual contact. Source: https://doi.org/10.1556/062.2019.72.2.1 . Supporting edition chain: MacKenzie 1970 (Sūtra of the Causes and Effects of Actions in Sogdian) with reviews by Gershevitch (IF 1970, https://doi.org/10.1515/if-1970-0132 ) and Schwartz (BSOAS 1971).

7. The SOGDIAN VESSANTARA JĀTAKA exists as an edited text: Benveniste, É. 1946. Vessantara Jataka: texte sogdien. Paris: Geuthner - verified through Chunakova 2022's printed reference list (full text not accessible this session). Source: https://doi.org/10.55512/WMO112406 .

8. Sino-Sogdian bilingual codicology at Dunhuang (RQ2): DHN 5966 inv. 6276 (IOM RAS) is a reused Chinese scroll: recto carries Lotus Sutra juan 1 columns PLUS six Sogdian lines, verso Sogdian prose + Manichaean hymn, same careless cursive hand; >300 Sogdian-script fragments sit in the Dunhuang fund, most actually in Uyghur language. This is manuscript-level bilingual PRACTICE, though not interlinear glossing. Old Turkish-Sogdian word-level glosses specifically: NO direct attestation found this session (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน); nearest evidence is the Sims-Williams/Hamilton Turco-Sogdian Dunhuang documents (existence verified via review records only) and the TB-Old Uyghur Mani-hymn alignment above.

9. BERLIN TURFAN COLLECTION infrastructure (RQ3): BBAW "Turfanforschung" edits the Turkic/Iranian parts, publishing in the series Berliner Turfantexte (BTT); the DFG-funded Digitales Turfan-Archiv with the Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin and IDP-Berlin has digitized ALL 40,000 fragments; project ran until 31.12.2022. Cataloguing systems: Sanskrit fragments in SHT = Sanskrithandschriften aus den Turfanfunden, 11 volumes, 13,530 catalogue numbers (Waldschmidt/Sander/Wille 1965-2012); Tocharian pressmarks per CEToM guidelines: IOL/Or (London), PK (Paris Pelliot), SI (St. Petersburg), THT (Berlin Turfan collection); CEToM database holds 8,612 TB + 1,832 TA fragments with images (courtesy IDP-Berlin/BBAW/SBB Orientabteilung). Sources: https://www.bbaw.de/forschung/turfanforschung ; https://www.univie.ac.at/tocharian/?edition ; Malzahn 2018 ( https://doi.org/10.17816/wmo35145 ).

10. Bilingual learning model along the Silk Road (RQ4 synthesis from fetched texts): (a) canonical/doctrinal literature was long USED IN SANSKRIT while para-canonical and scientific genres were translated into Tocharian (Malzahn 2018 statistics; quote: doctrinal texts "mostly still used in the Sanskrit original"); (b) for the Udānavarga specifically the Sanskrit original remained preferred and only the TB commentary Udānālaṅkāra became popular in Tocharian (Peyrot 2017 as quoted in Malzahn 2018); (c) TB literary language is modeled on the Buddho-Indian template including loan of nāṭak- and extensive calquing studied by Pinault (2015a); (d) the gloss fragments show active word-level learning ON Sanskrit books (findings 3-4). Sources: https://doi.org/10.17816/wmo35145 ; https://cetom.univie.ac.at/
