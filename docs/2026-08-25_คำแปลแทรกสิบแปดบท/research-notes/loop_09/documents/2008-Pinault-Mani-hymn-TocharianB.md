## What the text is

The unique Manichaean text known so far in Tocharian B: a hymn to Mani transmitted in a manuscript that carries BOTH the Tocharian B original parts AND an Old Turkic (Old Uyghur) rendering in Manichaean script. The leaves are from Murtuk (Berlin signatures U 99 etc.; one leaf T.M.177 = M8533, another T II D 67).

## Key passages (verbatim with minimal OCR normalization)

1. Prior edition by Werner Winter with Annemarie von Gabain: "This text has been edited and commented by Werner Winter in collaboration with Annemarie von Gabain in a booklet published in 1958 (TT IX)." [TT IX = Berliner Turfantexte IX.]

2. Larry Clark's treatment inside the Manichaean "Pothi-Book" edition: "It has been the basis of the treatment by Larry Clark, which is inserted in his edition of the so-called Manichaean 'Pothi-Book' (1982): I refer to the edition (pp. 174-175), followed by a translation (p. 188) and a commentary (pp. 203-204)."

3. The Old Uyghur side follows the Tocharian metrical segmentation like glosses — central quote for bilingual/glossing practice:
   "First, Winter has disentangled (TT IX, pp. 29-34) the metrical structure of the original poem in Tocharian B. It consists of three stanzas of four lines of 22 syllables each, showing the alternation of cola (segments) of 7 (4+3) and 8 (4+4) syllables, according to a scheme 7-8-7. This point is quite essential, since the Uygur translator has conceived his glosses after the division of the text into metrical segments. With some exceptions, he has followed carefully this segmentation."

4. Dialect character of the TB parts: "Winter has seen (TT IX, p. 23) that several phonological and morphological features of the Tocharian B text, as far as they can be deduced from the transposition into Manichaean script, point to a specific variety of this language, which he has described at that time as 'oriental' [...] most of these so-called 'eastern' features belong actually to a late and vulgar variety of the language, by comparison with archaic and standard varieties [...]. Further analysis confirms that many formal peculiarities of the Manichaean text concord with the features of the late manuscripts in Tocharian B from the Turfan oasis, where the orthography and language are no more strictly controlled."

5. Content structure: "this hymn to Mani follows the praising genre of the Buddhastotra [...] The three stanzas correspond clearly and successively to the three items of the 'triad of gems', that is Buddha, Law (dharma) and Community (sangha). It has been proven that the best way to understand or complete some desperate passages of this poem is to resort to the Buddhist phraseology."

6. Reviews consulted by Pinault for corrections: Werner Thomas (1960) and Walter Couvreur (1961), reviews of TT IX.

## Harvester assessment

This is the clearest published case of a Tocharian B - Old Turkic bilingual from the Berlin Turfan collection in which the Turkic version functions segment-by-segment after the Tocharian model ("conceived his glosses after the division of the text into metrical segments"). It also documents the Winter/von Gabain 1958 Berliner Turfantexte IX edition chain requested under RQ1 (Werner Winter).
