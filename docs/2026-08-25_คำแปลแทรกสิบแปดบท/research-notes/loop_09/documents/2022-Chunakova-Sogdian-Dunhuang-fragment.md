## Subject manuscript (all signatures from the article)

- Signature: ДХН 5966, инв. 6276 (old code Дх 6489) — Dunhuang fund of the IOM RAS.
- Size: 11.6 x 9.2 cm ("pocket-book" scroll format); Verso: 13 incomplete lines; Recto: 6 Chinese columns + 6 Sogdian lines.
- Content per side: Recto = fragment of juan 1 of the Chinese Lotus Sutra (identified by V.P. Zaitsev; LS juan 1, 7c02-06) PLUS Sogdian lines; Verso = Sogdian prose composition AND a Manichaean hymn on a Chinese scroll.
- Script/hand: "небрежный согдийский курсив" — careless Sogdian cursive; both sides probably by one scribe; corrections and erased signs by the scribe on the Verso.
- The connector rty is written out on Recto but replaced by ideogram ZY on Verso — explained as a feature of the underlying original text rather than scribe preference.

## Key statements (harvester's translations from Russian)

1. Collection size: "В Дуньхуанском фонде [...] имеется более 300 фрагментов, выполненных согдийским шрифтом. Среди них большая часть написана на уйгурском языке [...]. Согдийских фрагментов в фонде менее ста" — "The Dunhuang fund contains more than 300 fragments written in Sogdian script; most are in the Uyghur language (whose script derives from Sogdian), fewer than 100 fragments are in Sogdian proper."

2. On the bilingual codicology: the fragment is an unedited double-sided piece on a CHINESE scroll — Chinese Buddhist text on the recto with Sogdian writing over/beside it, Sogdian prose + Manichaean hymn on the verso. This documents practical bilingual reuse/re-inscription of Chinese Buddhist scrolls by Sogdian-speaking Manichaeans/Buddhists at Dunhuang, but NOT interlinear word-level glosses.

3. The author notes the difficulty that tiny fragments often cannot even be assigned to Sogdian vs Uyghur language.

## Harvester assessment

Useful for RQ2/RQ3 as concrete evidence of Sogdian-Chinese manuscript contact at Dunhuang; explicitly NOT evidence of interlinear glossing. Also the only fetched source this session confirming the bibliographic reality of Benveniste 1946 (Sogdian Vessantara).
