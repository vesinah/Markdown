## Translation direction - the key statements (verbatim)

- "Most of the Buddhist Sogdian texts are more or less correct translations based on Chinese originals; only a few seem to have been based on prototypes in either Sanskrit or Tocharian, although it has not been possible to trace their originals."
- "A colophon found in one Turfan text states that it had been translated from the Kuchean language or Tocharian B, (Henning, pp. 59-62; Kudara and Sundermann, 1987, pp. 347-48)."
- Places of translation noted in colophons of Chinese originals used by Sogdians: Luoyang (MacKenzie 1976, p. 8), Changan (P2), Dunhuang (P8); translators named include Xuanzang, Śikṣānanda, Yijing, Amoghavajra.
- Terminology practice: Sogdian Buddhist terms often calque CHINESE wording, e.g. wytxwy ('t) sryβt'm = Chinese fannao 煩悩 ~ Skt. kleśa; pk'β'm ~ bhagavān via Chinese baoqiefan 薄伽梵 (Sims-Williams 1983: 138).
- Sociological frame: Buddhism never became a state religion among the Sogdians; no state saṃghas; "Lack of organized community may have prevented the establishment of standard Buddhist terminology in Sogdian."
- Vinaya terms discovered recently render Indian forms directly (pʾytyk etc., Yoshida 2010: 92 n.13).

## Individual works and their prototypes (verbatim fragments)

- Vessantara Jātaka: "perhaps a native Sogdian composition, a retelling rather than a translation (Sims-Williams, 1989, p. 175)"; Durkin-Meisterernst (2009) claims a non-Chinese original, while Yoshida (2013a) argues adaptation from Taizixudanajing (T 171).
- Araṇemi-jātaka: popular in the Northern Tarim Basin with Tocharian A, Tocharian B, Tumshuqese and Uighur versions known; the Sogdian pothi is illustrated on every folio.
- King Kāñcanasāra story (ch. 5 of Daśakarmapathavadānamālā): one pothi folio; "the Sogdian seems to represent an independent recension based on a lost Tocharian prototype (Sundermann, 2006)."
- Saṅghāṭa-sūtra: six manuscripts identified, especially liked by Buddhist Sogdians.
- Mahāyāna-mahāparinirvāṇa-sūtra: about eighty fragments of one pothi book in the German Turfan collection; identified pieces spread across the 40 fascicles of T 374, so a complete translation once existed; four manuscripts total. Also the "Latter part" T 377 (Yoshida 1995); two recensions of Vimalakīrtinirdeśa (Dunhuang MacKenzie 1976; Turfan Reck 2012).
- "Sūtra of the Causes and Effects of Actions": translated from a CHINESE APOCRYPHAL sūtra, as was Dafangguanghuayanshiepinjing; Chan-favored apocrypha recur (Fawangjing T 2883; Dhūta-text = T 2886; Jiujingdabeijing T 2880 cited in P9).
- Prātimokṣa/vinaya: Caturvargavinaya-prātimokṣa (T 1430) fragments.
- Prajñāpāramitā group: Aṣṭadaśasāhasrikā (?STii text 8), Pañcaviṃśatisāhasrikā (T 223, two mss), Hṛdaya (T 251); commentaries on the Chinese Vajracchedikā ("Treatise on the Wisdom of Vajra", "Treatise on Vajra").
- Dhyāna-text = Buddhānusmṛtisamādhisāgara-sūtra (T 643), scroll of 404 lines.

## Chinese texts read/copied BY Sogdians (verbatim section header "Employment of Chinese texts")

- "That Buddhist Sogdians read and copied not only Sogdian but also Chinese texts is known from a few Buddhist Chinese texts that are phonetically transcribed in Sogdian script." Examples: passage of Mahāyānaśraddhotpāda-śāstra (T 1666) transcribed character-by-character (Yoshida 2013b; pronunciation points to first half of 8th c.); transcription of Jingangwuliwen "Five salutations to the Vajracchedikā-sūtra" (10th c. Dunhuang; Yoshida 1994b); a Sogdian title written on the back of a Chinese text (Yoshida 2009a: 326); Chinese colophon by the Sogdian copyist Shi Lushan.
- Turco-Sogdian documentary material datable to the 10th century is referenced to Sims-Williams and Hamilton, p. 40.

Relevance for the project: this synthesizing survey independently confirms and extends Yoshida 2019 - the Sogdian Buddhist corpus is overwhelmingly Chinese-derived; Sanskrit/Tocharian prototypes are the rare exception concentrated at Turfan; and Sogdian script was even used to transcribe Chinese pronunciation (a word-level bilingual practice adjacent to glossing).
