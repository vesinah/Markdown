## Core thesis (verbatim from abstract)

"In this paper three Buddhist Sogdian texts discovered from Turfan are identified and edited. These are unique among the Buddhist Sogdian texts in that they are not translated from the Chinese prototypes, but are dependent on, if not translated from, the originals which were popular among the Buddhists resident in Kucha, Karashahr or Turfan, i.e. the area along the Northern Silk Road, whereas most Buddhist Sogdian texts are shown to have been translated from Chinese originals. The three are the Sogdian versions of (1) the Karmavibhaṅga, (2) the so-called Prātihārya-sūtra or chapter twelve of the Divyāvadāna, and (3) the legend of King Kāñcanasāra."

## Manuscript signatures named in the full text (all from the fetched PDF)

1. Sogdian KARMVIBHAGA: So 14700 (22) and So 14700 (23), two fragments of one scroll made of ruled sheets prepared for Buddhist Chinese texts; written in formal (formerly "sutra-") script; old expedition signature of the pair given as T II Toyoq A 24. Text corresponds to karma no. 65 of the developed Karmavibhaṅga tradition (Lévi's Mahākarmavibhaṅga; Chinese Śuka-sūtra T01n0080). Possibly related: So 13901 (old signature T II D 63), prudently NOT assigned to the same manuscript by Yoshida.
   Cross-reference: Tocharian B version translated by Tamai 2015 ("The Tocharian Karmavibhaṅga," Sōka Daigaku Kokusaibukkyōgaku Kōtōkenkyūjo Nenpō).
2. Sogdian PRATIHARYA-SUTRA (= Miracle sūtra, Divyāvadāna ch. 12): fragments L81, L52, L89, L40, L5238(?), plus Kr IV/879 (published by IvIC/Vladimir Livshic 1996). Yoshida: "the group of the Sogdian fragments so far discussed contains a unique Sogdian recension of the Miracle sūtra ... likely to have been based on the original, either in Sanskrit or Tocharian, popular along the Northern Silk Road." The Miracle story itself has NOT yet been found in Sanskrit/Tocharian among Northern Silk Road finds, but it was depicted in murals of Qizil grotto No. 80 (per Zhao Li 2006, via Hiyama), so known in Kucha. Fragment L40 contains a Buddha-Upaka Maṇḍikaputta dialogue closest to Pali AN 188 - a motif with no Chinese counterpart and unattested in studied Sanskrit/Tocharian texts; conclusion: "there once existed a Sanskrit or Tocharian text along the Northern Silk Road that served as the prototype of our Sogdian text."
   Linguistic contact datum: pβ'n in L52 line 9 = loanword from Sanskrit bhavana 'dwelling' "possibly via Tocharian B bhavaṃ 'id.'".
3. Sogdian KANCANASARA legend (ch. 5 of Daśakarmapathavadānamālā): Berlin fragment bearing old signature T I α (announced by Henning 1949: 160 n.2), joined by Sundermann with So 10132 into an almost complete short-lined pothi folio; MARGINAL TITLE reads (δs)' šyr'ktyh pn(cmy)k (prwrt X) 'ten good deeds, fifth chapter'. Uighur parallel published by Müller, Uigurica III (1920: 31); fresh comparison by Wilkens 2015/2016. Yoshida's verdict: the Sogdian and Uighur versions are TWO INDEPENDENT TRANSLATIONS, possibly of the same prototype; footnote 44: "On the Uighur colophon mentioning the text in the Twγry language as its direct prototype, which in turn was translated from the version in the language of Küsän or Kucha, see Yoshida 2018" ('Farewell to the "Teacher of Four Twγrystān"'). Sundermann preferred the Tocharian version as direct original of the Sogdian (name form kncns'r vs TB kañcansāre, newly discovered per Wilkens 2015: 246 n.1).
   This folio also appears as the illustration "So 10132 + ohne Signatur (T I ?)" on the BBAW project page for Reck's vocabulary project.

## Additional direction-of-translation evidence inside the same article

- Sogdian version of the Lengqieshiziji 楞伽師資記 identified (Yoshida 2017): a Chan/Zen text composed by the Chinese monk Jingjue in the early 8th c.; gives a post quem date and shows Sogdians engaged with Sinicised Buddhism. A small Chinese fragment of it surfaced in the German Turfan collection as Ch 0365 (Mitani 2018: 675).
- Chinese fragment So 14830 cites the Dasheng qixin lun but is otherwise an unknown Chinese text; unidentified Sogdian fragments may eventually be matched with such Chinese material.
- Catalogue basis: Reck 2016 catalogue of the Buddhist Sogdian fragments of the German Turfan collection ("marks the new epoch"); many Berlin fragments still unpublished.
- Pressmark key printed in the article: Mainz = Sogdian/Uighur texts housed in the Staatsbibliothek Berlin; MIK = texts of the former Museum für Indische Kunst, Berlin; So/L/Kr numbers for other holdings.
- Araṇemi-jātaka section: Sogdian version with TA, TB, Tumshuqese and Uighur parallels; illustrated pothi fragments So 18240, 18242; further MIK III 4932; unique Vinaya text in So 10921, 19530a-b, Mainz 155; fresh TB translation of Araṇemi in Tamai 2018.

Assessment for the book project: this single OA article now supplies the exact signatures, recension history and translation-direction argument that Phase A loop 09 lacked; it anchors the claim "Sogdian Buddhist literature = mostly Chinese-derived, with a small Turfan-side stratum dependent on Sanskrit-or-Tocharian originals".
