## Part I - concrete content recoverable from fetched CEToM editions that cite it by page

CEToM cites Peyrot 2014 with page precision on three fully-edited gloss fragments:
- SHT 2054.b (Buddhacarita 2.48a-54d, Sengim): cited as Peyrot 2014: 154-6. The edition shows TB glosses placed UNDER the Sanskrit akṣaras of the poṭhī leaf; commentary notes a gloss "written a little higher than tāka ... apparently does not belong" to it, a paper patch covering part of an akṣara, and further fainter glosses to the right.
- SHT 2250 and 2254.a (Buddhacarita 13.66d-14.1a, Sengim): cited as Peyrot 2014: 133, 156-9. Edition documents TWO LAYERS of glossing: "this gloss was written in blacker ink with a thicker line through an original gloss in fainter ink with a thinner line"; also glosses written separately word-by-word under single Sanskrit compounds, a gloss preceded by a faint reading sahā·[ā] showing the glossator preferred a different Sanskrit variant than the transmitted one, and a later-added vertical caesura line.
- SHT 4477 (Vasubandhu, Abhidharmakośabhāṣya 349.8-22): cited as Peyrot 2014: 165. TB gloss kramtsa santänäntse uses two calques (kram*, santäm*); CEToM comment: "perhaps the glossator only wanted to indicate the case of the Sanskrit words"; another gloss mokṣabhāgi mäskeṃträ adds explanatory specification ("[these] are mokṣabhāgīyas") absent from the Sanskrit lemma.

## Part II - verified record and real abstract

Peyrot, Michaël. 2015b. "Notes on Tocharian glosses and colophons in Sanskrit manuscripts II." Tocharian and Indo-European Studies 16: 107-30.
- Verified from CEToM bibliography and from the CEToM PS Bl 13 page (cited there as Peyrot 2015b: 112-113).
- TIES 16 table of contents (https://chicagoreference.com/ucp/books/book/distributed/T/bo25395532.html) lists it.
- Author's own description as displayed with the paper on Academia.edu (https://www.academia.edu/22217928), verbatim: "Many Sanskrit manuscripts from the Tocharian area stretching from the Kuča to the Turfan regions bear witness of use by speakers of Tocharian. Some contain glosses translating separate words or clarifying difficult notions and passages. In others, a Sanskrit text may be concluded with a colophon in Tocharian, or sometimes with later additions such as pious wishes or notes of ownership. In this article, material from the Paris, London and Berlin collections is presented."

## Part II - concrete content from CEToM PS Bl 13 (Paris, BnF Pelliot Koutchéen)

PS Bl 13 (also "Pelliot Sanskrit numéro bleu 13"; expedition codes 635, 616): poṭhī containing end of Pravāraṇa-sūtra + start of Dhvajāgra-sūtra; CEToM edition by Michaël Peyrot himself. Philological commentary, verbatim key sentence: "This scribbling is found under Skt. hartāraṃ sarvaśalyānāṃ vande 'I greet the remover of all thorns [of lust]' ... The interpretation of the scribbling is problematic; in any case, it can be excluded that it is a gloss translating the Sanskrit text directly above."
Typological value: proves that Tocharian writing placed UNDER a Sanskrit line is not automatically a translation gloss - placement alone does not determine function; some instances are scribblings/exercises. Cited literature: Couvreur 1970: 181-182 (Boeddhistische Sanskritfragmenten in Koetsjische handschriften-verzamelingen); Hartmann/Wille 1997 catalogue of Pelliot Sanskrit manuscripts.

## Placement typology synthesis supported by fetched sources (Phase-B working result)

1. Dominant physical arrangement in the Berlin Buddhacarita and Kośabhāṣya gloss leaves: Tocharian equivalents written beneath/between the Sanskrit akṣaras they explain, aligned word-for-word or phrase-for-phrase (SHT 2054.b; SHT 2250+2254.a; cf. Phase A: SHT 412.32 sandhi clarification).
2. Multi-layer glossing exists: a fainter earlier gloss overwritten by a darker second hand (SHT 2250+2254.a n4).
3. Gloss behavior ranges from pure lexical substitution to case-marking help, variant selection, and free explanatory expansion (SHT 4477); sense translation may override Sanskrit compound structure (CEToM ad loc.: "The Sanskrit compound is not followed literally in the Tocharian gloss").
4. Not all under-line Tocharian writing is glossing (PS Bl 13 scribbling).
5. Colophons, ownership notes and pious wishes in Tocharian occur in the same Sanskrit manuscript population (Peyrot 2015b abstract).
