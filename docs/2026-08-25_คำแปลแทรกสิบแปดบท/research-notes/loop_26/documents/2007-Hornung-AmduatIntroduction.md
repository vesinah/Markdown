## Content notes (verified from the sample)

1. The Amduat ("What is in the Netherworld"), original title "Treatise of the Hidden Chamber", is attested from the time of Hatshepsut (ca. 1470 BC); in the 18th dynasty it exclusively decorates royal burial chambers (KV34 of Thutmose III is the classic witness).
2. Structure: twelve hours of the night; text and pictures form a unity - word-image commentary format; each hour (except the first) divided into three registers with the sun bark at the center.
3. Two recensional formats documented: a long illustrated version and a SHORT version without illustrations - an abstract/catalogue listing important names plus remarks on the usefulness of the book; Thutmose III added 741 deities to this catalogue on the upper pillared hall of his tomb.
4. Annotation practice inside the New Kingdom copies: introductions and instructions written in red (rubra) against black main text for quick orientation; red parts mostly identical between tombs of Thutmose III and Amenhotep II.
5. After the New Kingdom the Amduat spread to papyri and coffins for priests and their relatives; prominent in Late Period tombs and sarcophagi until the Ptolemaic Period; quotations still in Roman times.
6. Reference edition: Erik Hornung, "Texte zum Amduat" (complete edition of all New Kingdom versions); continuous deity numbering goes back to Hornung's German edition with translation and commentary of 1963.

## Relevance to interlinear-gloss/scholia topic

- Documents the oldest Egyptian layer of explanatory apparatus attached to funerary texts: rubrication, catalogue abstracts, image-caption-text unities. However, this sample does not discuss formal "scholia" (short explanatory notes) as a defined phenomenon; that discussion must be sourced elsewhere, e.g., von Lieven's classification of commentaries (Grundriss des Laufes der Sterne, 2007, 263-264, primary vs secondary comments) and Roessler-Koehler, "Text oder Kommentar", in Assmann & Gladigow (eds.), Text und Kommentar (1995), 111-139 - both cited in the Joergensen thesis document in this folder.

## Selected verbatim passages

- "Whereas the Book of the Dead is composed of individual spells with no fixed sequence, the Books of the Netherworld follow a strict pattern."
- "In addition to the long, illustrated version of the Amduat, a short version, without illustrations, is found in the tombs and on papyri. It is a sort of abstract or summary, listing important names and adding further remarks on the usefulness of the book."
- "For quick orientation, introductions and instructions are written in red, the rest black."
