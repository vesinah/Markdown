## Primary texts covered

1. Tebtunis Mythological Manual = PSI inv. 1.72 [Trismegistos TM 218348], provenance Tebtynis, 2nd century AD, Hieratic script; a compilation of learned religious lore for priestly instruction covering Egypt nome by nome (the translated portion covers Upper Egyptian nomes).
2. The Manual of the Delta (edited by Dimitri Meeks, Mythes et legendes du Delta).
3. Related priestly manuals and commentary texts discussed: pJumilhac; Book of the Dead ch. 17 (with CT 335 precursor); the Ritual for Averting the Raging One; Book of the Temple; Tebtunis priestly onomasticon; von Lieven's Grundriss des Laufes der Sterne material.

## Key verified passages relevant to glossing/commentary traditions

1. INTERLINEAR TRANSLATIONS in pharaonic ritual texts: "The translations that have survived are all from Middle Egyptian to Demotic, apart from a single Middle Egyptian text translated into Late Egyptian or Proto Demotic. The latter text, the Ritual for Averting the Raging One, has interlinear translations and is given the title 'The Interpretations of the inaccessible (language) of the Ritual for Averting the Raging One'." (footnote: Urk. VI, 61,10; cf. Schott, Die Deutung der Geheimnisse des Rituals fuer die Abwehr des Boesen, 1954, 143-237.)
2. SUPRALINEAR GLOSSES in the Tebtunis temple library: the phrase wHa itnw is also used for the priestly onomasticon from Tebtunis (Osing, The Carlsberg Papyri 2: Hieratische Papyri aus Tebtunis I, 1998, p. 68); "it remains uncertain whether it refers to the general text that orders and classifies a wide range of phenomena, or to the running supralinear additions in demotic and proto Coptic that provided the reader with both translation and a pronunciation aid."
3. Typology of commentaries: "According to von Lieven (Grundriss des Laufes der Sterne (2007), 263-264) commentaries should be divided into primary and secondary comments, in which the first are those comments that are already found in the original text, and secondary those added in the course of the text's transmission."
4. Philological commentary markers: ky-Dd / ky-sp ("another saying", "variant") introduced collation variants and divergent interpretations, e.g., in pJumilhac on the Imiut-relic; most extreme case is Book of the Dead chapter 17, where main text is interspersed with questions and answers plus variants introduced by ky-Dd.
5. BD 17 as the classic commented funerary spell: found both with and without commentary; trend toward larger, more fixed commentary culminating in the BD version; interpretations revolve around the Heliopolitan Ennead; Assmann characterized these glosses as operating "on exactly the same level as the 'main text'" producing possible references rather than explanations; for the commentary literature see Roessler-Koehler, "Text oder Kommentar", in Assmann & Gladigow (eds.), Text und Kommentar (1995), 111-139.
6. Education link: "Since The Book of the Temple includes interpretation of ritual texts as one of the mandatory subjects for the education of higher priesthood, we may infer that commentaries or interpretations must have existed for a number of religious texts."
7. Thesis conclusion: mythological manuals use key myths built on the Heliopolitan Ennead to structure local traditions (paradigmatic/syntagmatic approaches); the Delta Manual adds an extra Ennead deity, a female Horus, practically unknown outside priestly manuals; manual techniques are compatible with commentary techniques on temple walls and ritual texts.

## Relevance

This thesis is a single-access survey tying together Egyptian interlinear translation practice (Ritual for Averting the Raging One), supralinear Demotic/proto-Coptic glosses (Tebtunis onomasticon), formal commentary typology (von Lieven), and scholion-like Q&A apparatus (BD 17) - directly serving research question 4 of Loop 26.
