## Extract — p. 240: obelus of Zenodotus; signs of Aristophanes and Aristarchus

"Obeli ipsum iam signum Zenodoti editioni appositum fuisse non dubitandum. Aristophanes praeterea in Homericis habuit ceraunion (ad plures ut videtur continuos versus spurios uno signo notandos, quod Aristarcho non commodum visum nec assumtum), sigma et antisigma, asteriseum 'ad notandos locos quibus sensus deesset' (Anecd. Paris. Reifferscheid p. 139). Nec ad hanc rem Aristarcho, cui ad hoc etiam diple sufficiebat, singulare signum opus videbatur. Sed praeter diplen periestigmenen ad Zenodotea notanda commodum Aristarcho visum singulari signo notare locos recurrentes nunc recte nunc falso positos; et ad hoc potius ipse asterisco usus est et contra posuit asteriseum obelatum. De his vide Nauckium Arist. p. 16 et Sengebuschium diss. Hom. I p. 50."

English gloss by the harvester (not part of the source): "That the very sign of the obelus was appended in Zenodotus' edition is not to be doubted. Aristophanes moreover used in Homer the keraunion (to mark several consecutive spurious lines with one sign, which did not seem useful to Aristarchus nor adopted by him), sigma and antisigma, and the asterisk 'for marking passages where sense is lacking.' Nor for this purpose did Aristarchus need a special sign, since the diple served him. But besides the dotted diple for marking Zenodotean readings, it seemed good to Aristarchus to mark with a single sign recurrent passages placed now rightly now wrongly; for this he rather used the asterisk, and conversely set an obelized asterisk..."

## Extract — p. 240 cont.: the diple before Aristarchus (Leogoras legend refuted)

"'Diplen aperisticton primus Leogoras Syracusanus apposuit Homericis versibus ad separationem Olympi a caelo...' ... usus est ea [diplen] in multis Aristarchus, nunc ea quae praeter consuetudinem tam vitae nostrae quam ipsius poetae apud eum invenirentur adnotans, nunc proprias ipsius figuras, interdum ea in quibus copiosus est, rursus quae semel apud illum ponerentur. Aristarchum diplen, si iam ante aliquis eius usus erat, ad multo plura adhibuisse... Fuisse iam ante eum aliquem diples usum id potest verum esse: sed illi testimonio nulla fides. Fuisse aliquam ante Aristarchum editionem Homericam, et quidem iam diple usam, ignoti cuiusdam Leogorae, cuius editionis nullum praeterea vestigium, certe non admodum probabile."

English gloss by the harvester: Lehrs quotes (and demolishes) a claim that one Leogoras of Syracuse first used the "unadorned" diple in Homeric verses to distinguish Olympus (the mountain) from heaven; he grants that some pre-Aristarchan use of the diple is possible but denies credibility to the testimony and doubts the existence of any such pre-Aristarchan critical edition.
