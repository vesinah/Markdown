## Key findings (with URLs)

1. Typology is now standardized on two axes (verified across AKS E0005629/E0071893/E0078701): by reading method - 석독구결 (interpretative, re-orders hanmun into Korean sentences, uses 훈) vs 음독구결=순독구결 (sequential phonetic reading with inserted particles); by writing medium/sign type - 자토석독구결 (simplified gugyeolja letters) vs 점토석독구결 (dots/lines, usually incised 각필, also called 부호구결/부점구결). Seokdokgugyeol reflects 10th-13th-century Korean word order, grammar and part of morphology, hence "Korea's oldest translation" (https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0071893).
2. Placement mechanics between/beside lines are precisely reconstructible: jato to sit right AND left of the column; right-side read first, left deferred; 역독점 (reverse dots) and 역독선 send the reader back up so output emerges in pure Korean order; 합부 joins constituents (https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0005629; https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0071893). In jeomto texts, signs occupy up to 25 defined positions on the character square, each position x sign shape mapping to one gugyeolja or letter-group (Lee Yong 2011: https://journals.uni-lj.si/ala/article/view/16).
3. Attribution tradition: 삼국유사 preserves the claim that 설총 (650-730) read the Nine Classics in Korean, i.e., created interpretative-reading gugyeol, a practice said to continue to the late 1200s; the AKS entry adds Uicheon's testimony that Uisang's lecture records already mixed vernacular one generation before Seol Chong, and situates origins in Goguryeo Taehak (372) and Silla Gukhak (651) (https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0005629).
4. Key manuscripts, verified list: oldest = 화엄경사경 vols. 12-20 (740s, Tōdaiji library, Silla-language stylus seokdokgugyeol); 균여 『석화엄교분기원통초』 960s; five Goryeo blockprints with jato seokdokgugyeol - 화엄경소 권35 (late 11th c.), 화엄경 권14 (early-mid 12th c.), 합부금광명경 권3 (13th c., only double-marked text), 구역인왕경 (Munsusa discovery 1973), 유가사지론 권20 (mid-13th c.) (https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0005629; Lee 2011); jeomto corpus first found July 2000 (유가사지론 권8), now 14 sources including 법화경 권1 c. 11th c. and 진본화엄경 권20 c. 10th c.; about 15 stylus-marked volumes enumerated with museum holdings (https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0071893; Lee 2011). The Lotus Sutra is uniquely represented in all four combinations (https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0078701).
5. Kunten mutual-influence debate: the 1973 Munsusa find overturned the belief that vernacular reading of Chinese was a uniquely Japanese invention; post-2000 joint Japan-Korea research led several scholars to propose that 8th-century Korean Huayan-school reading practice influenced Nara kanbun kundoku (Ito excerpt: https://www.waseda.jp/fpse/winpec/assets/uploads/2017/01/No.E1614Ito.pdf). Kobayashi Yoshinori argues the points on Silla-era 화엄문의요결 (whose reverse carries 동대사후몽문집) must be read in Korean, based on point-map similarity with 12th-century Korean Hwaeom materials and absence of subject markers in pre-9th-c. Japanese annotation (Lee 2011). The term 현문석독구결 from the brief did not surface in any fetched source (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน).
6. Hyangchal vs idu vs gugyeol: same ideogram+phonogram building block; differences are function and completeness - idu mixes hanmun/Korean order for documents, hyangchal writes whole Korean sentences (26 surviving songs) with near-complete particles, gugyeol glosses an unchanged hanmun source; proposed emergence order proper nouns -> idu -> gugyeol -> hyangchal, and arranging any seokdokgugyeol text per its to yields exactly hyangchal-style notation (헌화가 example) (https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0062985; Yoon 2010 RISS abstract).
7. Joseon transition: sun-dok/eumdok gugyeol standard from c. 12th-14th c.; state projects made gugyeol-fixing equal interpretation-fixing (세조's three-year 주역구결 debate; 정몽주, 권근, 율곡/퇴계 lineages; 교정청본 경서언해) (https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0005629). For printing, gugyeol was a distinct pre-translation step: 능엄경언해/금강경언해 colophons say translate followed fixed gugyeol; 법화경언해 1463 bears Sejo's own gugyeol; 목우자수심결언해 1467 credits 비현합 for gugyeol and 신미 for translation (https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0036188; .../E0022767; http://db.sejongkorea.org/front/contentlist.do?bkCode=P34_SB_v001). 두시언해 (1481) marks an orthographic watershed (same source).
8. Modern scholarship verified: 오구라 신페이 『鄕歌及び吏讀の硏究』 1929; 양주동 『고가연구』 1942; 김완진 『향가해독법연구』 1980 (four decipherment criteria; his fame rests on hyangga decoding rather than seokdokgugyeol proper - the prompt's attribution needs this nuance); 남풍현 『차자표기법연구』 1981, 『국어사를 위한 구결 연구』 1999, Dankook-based 구결학회 (founded as 구결연구회 1988); 정재영 stylus/symbol-gugyeol vs kunten monographs (2003); 이승재 외 『각필구결의 해독과 번역 1-5』 2005-2009; 황선엽 외 『석독구결사전』 2009; 백두현 『석독구결의 문자 체계와 기능』 2005 (all listed in https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0071893; research history: https://www.korean.go.kr/nkview/nklife/1997_4/1997_0402.pdf). Digital humanities: 한국고전종합DB db.itkc.or.kr since 2001 with 3,275 works and MT services (https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART002780349); the five jato seokdokgugyeol texts are fully computerized with collation and dictionaries (https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0071893).
9. Comparative note: Korea-Japan comparison is mature (Kobayashi 2002 relation of Korean kakuhitsu marks and old kunten; joint symposia 2001-2013; Cambridge Handbook 2022 ch. 6 framing vernacular reading as a widespread phenomenon). Whitman's NINJAL project extended comparison to Anglo-Saxon Latin glosses, and a 2014 workshop reported Vietnamese kundoku cases (Ito excerpt). No dedicated study comparing gugyeol specifically with Vietnamese chư nho reading marks was found; Vietnamese scholarship centers on chữ nôm script creation rather than interlinear glosses (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน for a direct three-way gloss-mark comparison).
