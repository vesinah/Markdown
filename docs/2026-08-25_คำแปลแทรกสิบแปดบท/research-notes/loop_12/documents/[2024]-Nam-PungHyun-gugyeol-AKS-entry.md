## Key content (faithful digest)

### Definition and terminology
- Gugyeol (口訣) = notation added below each phrase when reading Chinese texts, for grasping meaning or for chanting. Aliases recorded in the entry: 토 (to), 입겿 (ipgyeot), 현결 (hyeonbyeol rendered 현결 懸訣), 현토 (懸吐).
- The term gugyeol is thought to derive from 구수비결 (口授秘訣), oral-secret transmission from master to disciple; 토 derives from 구두 (句讀), the phrasing-points of Chinese prose.
- To are mostly Korean particles and endings, sometimes auxiliary verbs or final-sound additions (말음첨기).
- Joseon usage: adding to called 현토 or 현결; gugyeol also called 입겿 ('입' renders 口, '겿' means 어조사). Under Sejo (세조), officials were split into two teams to debate the correctness of 주역구결 (周易口訣) every palace-examination day for three years — evidence that fixing a classic's gugyeol was equated with fixing its interpretation.

### Origin and development
- Gugyeol presumably arose once systematic study of Chinese texts began: Goguryeo 태학 (Taehak, 372), Baekje 박사 institution, Silla 국학 (Gukhak, 651). Early gugyeol was oral; to-graphy not yet fixed (Three-Kingdoms idu documents show mixed or fully Korean word order without written to).
- To-graphy developed around the Silla unification period. The 삼국유사 (Samguk yusa) tradition that 설총 (Seol Chong) read the 구경 (Nine Classics) and 문학 in Korean, continuing down to the late 13th century, indicates Seol Chong created interpretative-reading gugyeol (토를 달아 우리말로 석독하는 구결).
- The entry notes 대각국사 의천 (Uicheon) reported that Uisang's Hwaeom lecture records 요의문답 and 일승문답 mixed 방언 (vernacular) — suggesting to-graphy existed a generation before Seol Chong.
- Decisive material evidence: a mid-8th-century Silla copy of the Hwaeom sutra (화엄경사경) kept in the Tōdaiji library, Nara, bears Silla-language seokdok gugyeol written with 각필 (stylus); the seokdok line continues to the mid-13th century.
- 순독구결 (sun-dok, sequential phonetic reading): earliest materials early 14th century; plausibly developed in the 12th century with the examination system (제술업 emphasis) and the twelve private schools (12공도). From the 15th century seokdokgugyeol handed its function to 언해 (eonhae) and gugyeol came to mean sun-dok gugyeol.

### Two sub-systems of seokdokgugyeol
- 자토석독구결 (字吐釋讀口訣): to written with simplified gugyeolja signs derived from Chinese characters.
- 점토석독구결 (點吐釋讀口訣): to written with dots and lines, mostly incised with a stylus (각필), hence also 각필구결 / 부호구결 / 부점구결.

### Census of jato seokdokgugyeol sources (7 items, with dates given in entry)
1. 화엄경사경 vols. 12–20, Tōdaiji library: 740s; stylus; mostly regular script with some cursive; earliest known hanmun seokdok material in Korea and Japan combined.
2. 균여(均如) 『석화엄교분기원통초』 (釋華嚴敎分記圓通抄): 960s; under two lines only, but securely dated and continuous with Gyuyeon's hyangga orthography.
3. 『화엄경소』 (華嚴經疏) vol. 35: late 11th c.
4. 『화엄경』 vol. 14: first half of the 12th c.
5. 『합부금광명경』 (合部金光明經) vol. 3: first half of 13th c.
6. 『구역인왕경상』 (舊譯仁王經上) 5 detached leaves: mid-13th c. (discovered at Munsusa, 1973, per related literature).
7. 『유가사지론』 (瑜伽師地論) vol. 20: mid-13th c.; latest language among seokdok materials.
- Reading mechanics demonstrated on 復有他方不可量衆: right-side to read first; left-side to deferred; 역독점 (reverse-reading dot) sends the reader back up; result is pure Korean word order. When re-written with regular characters the sequence equals hyangchal-type notation; the 헌화가 (獻花歌) line shows the same ideogram+phonogram structure.
- About 55 gugyeolja per single text across the Goryeo corpus; sign inventory tabulated in the entry (只/기, 隱/ㄴ, 乙/ㄹ, 冬/ᄃᆞᆯ, 之/다 etc.).

### Jeomto system
- 25 positions defined on the square of a character (inner thirds, outer thirds, four corners) x sign shapes (single dot, double dot, lines, combinations, orientation distinctive) yield up to 625 possible to-values; 점도 (point maps) reconstructed per text.
- Census: 유가사지론 vol. 5 (early 11th c.), 주본화엄경 vol. 6 (12th c.), 법화경 vol. 1 (c. 11th c.), 진본화엄경 vol. 1 (c. 10th c.), 합부금광명경 vol. 1 (13th c.) - 5 kinds / 14 volumes; almost all incised by stylus, some in ink. 합부금광명경 vol. 3 uses jato AND jeomto side by side but never mixed within one system; 수덕사 소장 『법화경』 vol. 7 mixes them unsystematically (private memo-like).
- Sect difference: 주본화엄경 and 유가사지론 point maps stand in systematic opposition (school-lineages), unlike jato materials.

### Sun-dok gugyeol and Joseon state projects
- Early sun-dok retains seokdok traces (e.g., 者 read 훈독 as 란 split around the character); such usages vanish by the 15th century.
- Joseon: 정몽주 주역구결 recorded in 조선왕조실록; 권근 composed gugyeol for 시·서·역; Sejo compared his own 어정주역구결 with Kwon Geun's in the 3-year court debate; later 율곡 이이 and 퇴계 이황's classic gugyeols became normative, differences feeding later 이기론 controversy; concluded with 선조-era 교정청본 경서언해 printing. 15th-century Buddhist eonhae were also translated following prior gugyeol.
