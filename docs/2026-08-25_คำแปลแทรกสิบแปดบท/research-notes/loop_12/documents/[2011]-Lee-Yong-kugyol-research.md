## Substance (from full text)

### Typology with worked examples
- Kugyol has two senses: the added Korean markers themselves, and texts bearing them. Gugyeolja are formed by taking part of a Chinese character and modifying it - the article explicitly compares this principle to Japanese kana formation.
- eumdok kugyol (transliteration kugyol): letters placed at phrase/sentence ends giving case particles and endings; example from the Analects 學而時習之면 不亦悅乎아; used late Goryeo (13th c.) through Joseon; studied by few.
- seokdok kugyol (translation kugyol): older than eumdok; characteristics listed: (1) gugyeol letters on BOTH right and left sides of columns; (2) purely syntactic unpronounced operators meaning read / skip / return-and-read; (3) semantic glossing plus instruction to read phonetically in Korean; (4) extra markers for modality and aspect.
- Reading rules stated: right-side letters first pass; left-side letters deferred; reversal mark returns reader to a left-side letter - demonstrated on 구역인왕경 (舊譯仁王經) passages 有五道一切衆生 and 復有他方不可量衆, reconstructed as 잇겨며 etc.
- jeomto seokdok-kugyol: point marks ("holes and scratches" incised with a stylus) playing the same role as gugyeol letters; each jeomto corresponds to one letter or a group of letters (citing Chang Kyung-joon 2002). Requires scopes/raking light to detect (Kobayashi 2002).

### Five key jato seokdok-kugyol xylographs (article's list)
1. 대방광불화엄경소 vol. 35 - printed c. 12th century.
2. 대방광불화엄경 vol. 14 - late 12th-early 13th c.
3. 금광명경 vol. 3 - 13th c.; first and last two leaves copied by another hand; only text containing both letters AND jeomto marks (double-marked).
4. 구역인왕경 vol. 1 - only 5 leaves survive; 25 lines x 17 characters; 13th c.
5. 유가사지론 (Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra) vol. 20 - latest linguistic features among seokdok materials.

### Discovery history
- 1973: Kuyeok-inwanggyeong found at Munsusa temple, Seosan, South Chungcheong - changed the field (more complex than eumdok kugyol).
- Year 2000: Kobayashi Yoshinori discovered stylus-made jeomto in Yugasajiron vols. 5 and 8.
- ~15 additional stylus-marked texts identified; enumerated with locations/dates: 진본화엄경 vol. 20 (late 9th-early 10th c., Seongam/Seomam museum); 유가사지론 vol. 3 (first half 11th c., Horim Museum); 유가사지론 vols. 5·8 (first half 11th c., Seomam); 주본화엄경 vols. 6, 22, 36, 57 (late 11th-early 12th c., Seomam); 주본화엄경 vols. 31, 34 (Horim).

### Korea-Japan kunten problem
- 동대사후몽문집 (Tōdaiji fujumonkō, early 9th c.) was written ON THE REVERSE of 표훈 (Pyeonwon)'s Silla-era 화엄문의요결 (Hwaeommuneuiyogyeol, early 8th c.), which carries brush-drawn points/lines readable as late-8th-century jeomto.
- Open questions stated verbatim in substance: who made these points and when; in what language were the texts read. Because Korean and Japanese syntax are similar, attribution is contested.
- Kobayashi's arguments for Korean reading: point map similar to the 12th-century Korean Hwaeomkyeong maps; subject/topic markers exist here but no subject markers appear in pre-9th-century Japanese texts.
