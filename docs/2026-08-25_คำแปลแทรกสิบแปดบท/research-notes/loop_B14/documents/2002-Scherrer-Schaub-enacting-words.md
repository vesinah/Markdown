## Content notes (verbatim quotes from extracted PDF text)

### Thesis and method

Opens by framing manuscript study as a search for authenticity ("vérito-machie"): "in the study of a manuscript... one of the motivations if not the motivation is a kind of 'vérito-machie' ['truth-o-machy'], as we are overtly engaging in a search of 'authenticity'". Diplomatic analysis (Lat. diplomatica) and philology are the instruments; stemmata establish document genesis, text-stratigraphy its layers.

### The three decrees (bkas bcad) and their textual products

Summary section (pp. ~322-323 of article):
- "In 763 → the 'small repertory' (Svalpavyutpatti) was composed on the basis of a previous prototype. This 'small list' subsequently merged into the 'large repertory' (Mahavyutpatti)..."
- "In 783 the sGra sbyor was written down."
- "Finally in 814 the terminology, i.e. those entries so far included in the register of terms, was fixed ne varietur. The register of terms itself, however, remained open to additions and modifications."

On the legal nature of the documents: "the texts/repertories (vyutpatti) are collections of writing while the bkas bcad are edicts, or decrees, having force of law." A passage of Ñan ral's Chos 'byun me tog sñin po is cited as an echo: "...the large and small [registers? vyutpatti?] and the decrees (bkas bcad)... three texts of [these?] register(s) (dkar chag gi yi ge gsum) were made".

### Correction and revision culture (key for annotation/correction studies)

- The 812/814-era office structure: "The chancery procedure is more refined and the offices and officers are subject to various changes. The Bhagavat's representative is flanked by a committee (mdun sa) and assisted by the college for proposals of great revision (zu chen) of Buddhist texts (dha rmma zu chen 'tshal ba'i grvar), that has its seat at the Imperial palace."
- "The fact that the text of the bkas bcad of 814 speaks of 'great revision' (zu chen) means that emendation and revision (zus) existed before, this being confirmed by colophons."
- Colophon formula quoted from Simonsson's Lalitavistara material: "|| rgya gar gyi mkhan po ji na mi tra dan | da na si la dan | mu ne bar ma dan | zu chen gyi lo tsa ba ban de ye ses sdes bsgyur cin zus te | skad gsar bcad kyis kyan bcos nas gtan la phab pa ||" translated: "The Indian Masters (mkhan po, upadhyaya) Jinamitra, Danasila and Munivarman and the Translator (lo tsa ba) [in charge of] Great Revision (zu chen) Venerable (ban de) Ye ses sde translated, corrected and fixed/ratified [the ne varietur version] after having made [the Tibetan translation] in conformity with the decision relative to the new language".
- "That former translations were corrected and revised according to terminology and principles fixed and established for the new language (skad gsar) is a fact known also through the record of texts' colophons... The expression skad gsar bcad... attested in colophons of Dunhuang manuscripts, was certainly taken from a passage of the sGra sbyor bam po gñis pa kept in all versions and apparently unnoticed up until now."

### Dunhuang manuscripts mentioned

- Pelliot tibétain 609: "a bilingual commentary to the Lañkāvatāra" (n.108).
- Pelliot tibétain 608 (n.110): "possibly belongs to the first type of Dunhuang Tibetan manuscript, making use of ancient system of pagination (Scherrer-Schaub 1999a: 20-22) to which a new system of pagination has been subsequently added... It might be interesting to note that the persons having corrected and copied this manuscript bear patronyms well attested in the region of Khotan."
- On dPal dbyans in a colophon (n.111, quoting Stein): "Celui du colophon n'était peut-être pas l'auteur de la liste mais un simple copiste" (the colophon figure may have been a mere copyist).
