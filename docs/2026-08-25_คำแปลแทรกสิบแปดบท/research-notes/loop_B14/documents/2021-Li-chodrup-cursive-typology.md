## Content notes (verbatim quotes from extracted PDF text)

### Chödrup the zhu chen translator-editor

Chödrup (Facheng) was the great 9th-century translator from Chinese. Colophons quoted in Table 1 repeatedly read: "zhu chen gyi mkhan po dang/ lo tsa pa ban de chos grub kyis rgya'i dpe las bsgyur cing zhus te/ gtan la phab pa" (IOL Tib J 625 colophon; variants at IOL Tib J 588, 1v1 / 21v4 / 24v7 / 44r6).

### Interlinear annotations on named manuscripts

- IOL Tib J 625: classified as "Interlinear sentences"; text = "Exposé in Verse Form (of) the Set of Eight Nominal Cases (as they) Occur (in Use)", one of Chödrup's translations from Chinese. Footnote: "The main body contains one folio (5 lines on the recto; 3 lines on the verso). Interlinear commentaries appear below the fifth and sixth lines on the recto and the first line on the verso."
- P.T. 783 verso: "Interlinear sentences in red" — "These are written on the verso of the Lecture Notes of the Yogācārabhūmi possessed by Fajing."
- IOL Tib J 588 I-III and IV: "Interlinear sentences"; contains three/four texts translated by Chödrup (Verses of the Treatise Entitled the 100 Syllables; its Exposition; Thirty Verses on Dependent Arising and its Exposition). For typological purposes the author uses only text IV since it "has two other witnesses in Dunhuang".
- IOL Tib J 619 + P.T. 770 "seem to have originally been part of the same manuscript" (folios ka go bzhi (94) to kha gcig vs. kha lnga (5)).

### Marginal editorial records (discarded-folio notes)

- "a discarded folio from the Śatasāhasrikāprajñāpāramitāsūtra... As noted in the marginal records, Chödrup, the inspector and proofreader of this copying project, discarded this folio, copied by Leng ho zhir hing (bottom margin: Leng ho zhir hing gi ro; left margin: +++ hing gyi ro chos grub gis phyungo//)".
- Parallel discard records quoted: Db. T. 487 ("chos grub gyi grar phyungs; an tsheng tshe'i ro"); Db. T. 2910 ("kim kang ro dang zhus pha chos grub phyungo"); Db. T. 2920 ("legs bzang ro chos grub phyungo"); Db. T. 2921 ("se'u hvan ro dang zhus pha chos grub phyungo"); Db. T. 2932 ("ke'u ke'u ro dang zhus pha chos grub phyungo").
- "The handwritings of all these editorial records closely resemble that of IOL Tib J 686 and 687. Therefore, there are good grounds to hypothesise that in all these manuscripts, it is Chödrup's hand." Also noted: "in these editorial records, bris is consistently used to express 'to copy'", whereas IOL Tib J 686/687 use bgyid.

### Typology of the dbu med hand

Section title verbatim: "4. A Typology of Chödrup's Cursive (Tib. dbu med) Handwriting".

- Method frame: van Schaik's five-style classification; Dotson & Helman-Ważny (2016) ductus analysis of index letters (ka, ga, nga, ca, pha, ra, sa) in four styles each; stroke terminology (heads, bellies, loops/lobes, limbs, bowls, shoulders/minims) illustrated against "Uchen (Tib. dbu can) script appearing in printed form".
- Characterization of the hand: "Chödrup's cursive (Tib. dbu med) handwriting contains a considerable number of idiosyncratic features. First and foremost, this style is ornately cursive and small. The ductus... was modified for swifter execution: strokes are elided or joined unless they are essential for recognition. For instance, the head of a syllable is frequently omitted or reduced to a tick or a small line. Most syllables are executed in a single stroke."
- Structure: "letters tend to extend further horizontally than vertically. Descenders/ascenders are generally shortened and the horizontal strokes are lengthened... vertically compact but horizontally loose."
