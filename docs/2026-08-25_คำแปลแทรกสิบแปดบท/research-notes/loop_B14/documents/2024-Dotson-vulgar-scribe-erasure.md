## Content notes (verbatim quotes from extracted PDF text)

### Thesis: erasure of the scribe within a merit economy

- Purity anecdotes (e.g., the Mingbao ji breathing-tube story) "ritually cloaking a scribe's humanity" functioned to counteract the threat that the scribe might pollute the sacred text: "the Buddhist scribe occupied a profoundly ambiguous position". Writing the scribe's name in the colophon granted him "a part of the religious merits" but also made him "a third party with a claim to the merit".
- Against this background: "alighted upon profane insults and drawings that cast sutra scribes in a [vulgar light]... these vulgar and comic jottings and marginalia are affirmations of scribes' continued, unvarnished presence."

### Sutra economy under Tibetan rule (820s-840s)

- The Sutra of Limitless Life (Tshe dpag du myed pa zhes bya ba'i theg pa chen po'i mdo) was "copied thousands of times by Dunhuang's scribes in the 820s"; quoted from ITJ 310.645, d14-156: "sa pyog gang na mdo 'dI yi ger 'dri 'aM yi ger 'drIr 'jug pa'I sa pyogs de yang mchod rten dang 'dra bar 'gyuro / pyag bya bar 'os par yang 'gyuro" — "As for whatever place where this sutra is written... that place shall indeed become a locus of offering."
- Scribes were mostly Chinese, but also Tibetans and Tuyuhun; many were monks (titles ban de, dge slong). They also drafted letters and contracts (PT 1098 hire contract; PT 1115, PT 1095, PT 1297 sale contracts; ITJ 844 loan contract; beer-brewing circular on PT 1648v).

### glegs tshas — scribes' writing surfaces

- Definition: "letters on their writing surfaces (glegs tshas), which were essentially oversized pothī-folia that served as their desks or work surfaces in the scriptorium. Scribes usually wrote their names on these, and also recorded loans of small items like knives, paper, and ink. Many are also full of doodles and jottings, and even curses directed at those who might steal these writing boards/surfaces."
- Ownership curse pair on writing board PT 1196: "This is Li Lug lug's writing surface" (li lug lug gI glegs tshas lags so') alongside "This is not Li Lug Lug's writing surface" (li lug lug gyi glegs tshas ma lagso).
- Jotting about another's board, PT 1155 (fig. 4): "...yar zo shig//" — unclear whether an accompanying "picture" caption.
- Pornographic drawing with caption traced "in massive letters", four times repeated, on the verso of Song Stag skyes' writing surface PT 1176 (figs. 5-6).

### Insults and editorial jokes on discarded folia

- Top-margin admonition on a discarded Prajñāpāramitā folio: "you, boy! Do not destroy the Prajñāpāramitā!" (bu khyod gyis shes rab gyI pha rol du phyIn pa['i md]o mas shIg).
- Upside-down top-margin jotting, PT 1451r: "Hing 'do wrote one 'chapter' of Jin Jin's sutra copy. Later, Hing 'do said, 'remove it as a discard'" (hing'do nI jin jin gyi dar ma bam po gcig nI bris lags so hing 'do nI slad ro du chung zhe mchis). Comment: "Hing 'do was employed to write a section of the SP that was to be credited to his client Jin Jin, but in his capacity as editor Hing 'do has rejected his own work."
- Comic note on rejected folio GL.t. 0326 by scribe Jin Hig tshe: "there being no second sutra folio as marvelous as this one, it was removed as a discard" (dar ma glegs bu 'di gcig la mtshar po che nyis pa ma mcwhis pas/ ror phyung ba/; Ma 2011, 99).
- Vulgar marginal jotting on discarded SP folio PT 1466: "Legs bzang has fucked a whole lotta Chinese women" (legs bzang gIs nI rgya mo du ma zhig brgyos so).
- Insult married to Buddhist philosophy, top margin of PT 1480: "Being someone who [practises] non-rejection, Ling ho Wen men fucked his mother" (dor ba ma myed pa ste la ling ho wen men ma la rgyos).
- Caution: "Most discarded folia have no jottings at all. Those that do are mostly devotional or practical, like the draft of a letter." Boredom factor: MP had 600 juan; SP four tomes of seventy-six bam po each; "a given scribe often copied the same chapter or section of the sutra over and over again".
