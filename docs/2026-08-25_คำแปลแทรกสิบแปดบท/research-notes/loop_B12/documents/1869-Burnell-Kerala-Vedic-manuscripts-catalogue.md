## Part 1 — A. C. Burnell, Catalogue of a Collection of Sanskrit Manuscripts (India Office), 1869

- Scan: https://archive.org/details/b30094288 (djvu.txt 150 KB, downloaded and grepped this loop).
- Author: Arthur Coke Burnell (1840-1882), with India Office Library; preface signed Sidmouth, August 3, 1869.

### Kerala / Malabar context statements (preface)

> "Medical, Astronomical, and Astrological works are more studied in Malabar than in any other part of India, and the Vedic sacrifices were formerly kept up in that province better than anywhere else."
>
> "The Brahmans of Malabar read the Vedas in a way that is unintelligible to those of the Coromandel Coast..."
>
> Footnote: "On the Coromandel Coast, of 100 Brahmans, about 75 are followers of the Black Yajur Veda, 20 of the Sama Veda, and 5 of the Rig Veda. In Malabar, of the same number, 80 will be followers of the Rig Veda, 19 of the Black Yajur Veda, and 1 of the Sama Veda."
>
> "The Brahmans of South India do not consider paper to be pure, and therefore generally use the leaves of the talipat and palmyra palms instead. With one or two exceptions all the palm leaf (or 'olai') MSS. in my collection were purchased... It is seldom that the date when the MS. was copied is entered in palm-leaf MSS., but from the character of the handwriting one may safely infer it."

### Vedic manuscript entries (Part I)

Item I.1 — Rigveda Saṃhitā PADA-PĀṬHA:

> "Rig-veda-samhitā — Pada-pātha. I-vi.3. Accented. In the Grantha character, on 219 palmyra leaves. No date, but apparently of the 17th century, as most of the leaves are numbered by letters, and this system became obsolete about the beginning of the 18th century. The text appears to agree closely with that published by Dr. Max Müller."

Item II — RV-prātiśākhya:

> "Rig-veda-prātiśākhya. 18th century. In the Grantha character, on palmyra leaves; 57 leaves. (No. 205, c.) The division into patalas is here different from the usual one, there being altogether 20 patalas."

Item III — Anuvākānukramaṇī attributed to Śaunaka: "In the Grantha character, on palmyra leaves" (within MS No. 205).

Black Yajurveda items XX-XXIII (same Grantha/palmyra conventions): XX Saṃhitā-pāṭha unaccented on talipat leaves 19th c.; XXI-XXII Padapāṭha "partly accented", Grantha, talipat; XXIII Tribhāṣyaratna (= Prātiśākhyavivaraṇa, commentary on the Taittirīya-prātiśākhya) "on 153 talipat leaves; 19th century; in 2 prapathakas" — note that COMMENTARIES circulate as separate palm-leaf volumes.

## Part 2 — M. Seshagiri Sastri, Descriptive Catalogue of the Sanskrit MSS of the Government Oriental Manuscripts Library, Madras, vol. I pt. 1

- Scan: https://archive.org/details/p1descriptivecat01madruoft (djvu.txt 97 KB, downloaded this loop).
- The Veda volume contains entries for manuscripts of C. W. Whish's "Calicut series" — collected at CALICUT (Kozhikode, Malabar), January 1825.

No. 9 — RIGVEDA-PADA-PĀṬHA (Malayalam):

> "Substance, palm-leaf (sritala). Size, 16 x 1¾ inches. Pages, 460. Lines, 8 on a page. Character, Malayalam. Appearance, new. In excellent order. ... Contains the first four Astakas complete. Accented."
>
> Whish's ink note on the first folio: "Vol. I. This volume contains the 1st, 2nd, 3rd and 4th Astakas of the Samhita of the Rigvedam, Malayalam, Arya character. CALICUT, January 1825, C. W. WHISH."
>
> Accent convention: "The perpendicular stroke stands for the Udatta in this and the following manuscripts except in Nos. 12 and 15."

No. 10 — RIGVEDA-PADA-PĀṬHA (Malayalam):

> "Substance, palm-leaf (sritala). Size, 17 x 1¾ inches. Character, Malayalam. Pages, 336. Lines, 9 on a page. ... Contains Astakas 5-8 complete. Accented." [Whish: "the 5th, 6th, 7th and 8th Astakas of the Samhita (Pada text) of the Rigvedah."]

No. 11 — RIGVEDA-PADA-PĀṬHA (GRANTHA):

> "Substance, palm-leaf (sritala). Size, 17½ x 1¾ inches. Pages, 450. Lines, 9 on a page. Character, Grantha. Appearance, more than 60 years old. Slightly injured. Contains the Padapatha of Astakas 5-8 complete. Accented. The upper stroke stands for the Udatta." [Whish notes: "The Samhita of the Rigvedah. In three volumes. Grantha character. CALICUT, January 1825."]

No. 13 — RIGVEDA-PADA-PĀṬHA (Grantha):

> "Substance, (Sritala) palm-leaf. Size, 15¾ x 1¾ inches. Pages, 158. Lines, 7 on a page. Character, Grantha. Appearance, new. ... Contains the Padapatha of the 1st Astaka complete. Accented."

(No. 12, same series: padapāṭha portions in TELUGU character, palm-leaf, showing multi-script supply routes.)

## Significance for the book

1. Kerala (Calicut/Malabar) preserved the RV PADA-PĀṬHA itself on palm leaf in both Malayalam and Grantha scripts, with accent marks (udātta strokes) — contrasting with Witzel's loop_20 finding that the earliest surviving NORTH Indian Vedic mss (Nepal, c. 1040 CE) lack accent marks.
2. A dated acquisition chain exists: collected by C. W. Whish at Calicut 1825 -> entered Madras GOML -> catalogued by Seshagiri Sastri; and independently: Malabar Grantha RV-pada MS -> India Office -> Burnell 1869 (17th-c. dating by palaeography).
3. Burnell's demographic footnote (80% of Malabar Brahmans = Rig Veda followers) explains WHY pada/prātiśākhya/anukramaṇī apparatus mss cluster in Kerala collections.
4. Complements Staal 1961 (loop_20): his grantha-script palm-leaf mss (Burnell B61/B62) were Jaiminiya texts; the present documents add the RIGVEDA side of the same Kerala palm-leaf culture.
