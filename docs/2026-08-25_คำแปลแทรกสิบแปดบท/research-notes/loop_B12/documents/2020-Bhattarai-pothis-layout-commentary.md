## Work 1 — Bidur Bhattarai, *Dividing Texts: Conventions of Visual Text-organisation in Nepalese Manuscripts*

- Publisher/series: De Gruyter, 2020. Fully open access via OAPEN: handle 20.500.12657/23234
  - https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/23234
  - Full text bitstream used this loop: https://library.oapen.org/rest/bitstreams/a82b9d4a-c455-4502-b8db-b80830c0956f/retrieve (.pdf.txt, 700 KB)
- Author's abstract: "The visual organization of texts in North Indian and Nepalese manuscripts from 800 to 1300 CE is at the centre of the present study. It sheds light on both the ways of manuscripts production and the employment of the manuscripts in ritual contexts in different areas of India and Nepal."

### Verified content relevant to tika/pothi layout

Scope and method:

> "In these manuscripts ... symbols, blank spaces, gaps, colours etc." are recorded manuscript by manuscript; "The comparison between different kinds of symbols, blank spaces, sub-..." [study organizes pothi anatomy into: string-holes, margins/foliation placement, divider symbols, blank spaces, colours, hands].

Corpus includes root texts AND commentaries:

> "Ten contain commentaries on Buddhist tantric texts (GV, HAP, LT, ST, VP, AP, CPN, HP, HT and AT). PR contains a dhāraṇī and AA contains a commentary to a Buddhist mahāyānic text." (p. 68)
>
> West Indian group: "I have examined seven manuscripts from West India. One manuscript contains a commentary of the Haravijayamahākāvya... The other manuscripts largely contain texts (both mūla ('root') texts and commentaries) related to the Jaina tradition. The manuscripts can be dated 12th to 13th c." 
>
> Example siglum TSPV = Tattvasaṃgrahapañjikāvṛtti manuscript dated 1143 CE; symbols cited per folio/side/string-hole ([TSPV_36v_LSH_1143]).
>
> Also treated via Pant 2000: Jatarūpa's Commentary on the Amarakośa — "He records and describes some of the symbols and signs of the manuscript he used for his edition of Jātarūpa's Commentary on the Amarakośa precisely."

Layout conventions documented (selection):

> West Indian Jaina pothi TSa: "TSa presents one string-hole per folio, each with four to six lines. ... The text has been divided into two text-sections on each folio. The left text-section is narrower than the right text-section. Both text-sections are demarcated by vertical ruling-lines."
>
> Foliation norms recur throughout the catalogue entries: "Letter-numerals on the left-hand margin on verso" / "figure-numerical foliation on the right-hand margin"; supplementary material added later appears in margins ("The manuscript also contains some extra 'supplementary' ..." folios; dates added later in different hands).
>
> Multi-text manuscripts (MTM) bundle related works in one pothi (e.g., KL 45 / NGMPP C 4/11 containing Bālārkastuti, Daṇḍakrodhāgnisalokasaṃgraha, Pañcatathāgatastuti, NS 545 = 1415 CE).

## Work 2 — Gérard Colas, "Palm-leaf Manuscript Libraries in Southern India Around the Thirteenth Century: The Sarasvatī Library in Chidambaram"

- In: Michael Friedrich & Cosima Schwarke (eds.), *Libraries in the Manuscript Age*, De Gruyter 2023, pp. 135 ff. Fully open access via OAPEN: handle 20.500.12657/91071; chapter DOI 10.1515/9783110779653-007.

Verified extracts (from OAPEN .pdf.txt):

> "Sarasvatī Library's manuscripts were intended to be read and consulted by scholars. The fragility of palm-leaf meant that a workshop of regular copying and restoration of texts had to be closely associated with conservation. Making new copies of manuscripts leaves, whether some of them or their entirety, was probably done with the assistance of people reading out loud. ... In principle, this was followed by a new reading to check the copying and make corrections. It was thus an editorial process in which the editor's mnemonic library played the role of the authoritative reference..."
>
> "...it was most likely there ... that scholars came to consult texts — with the assistance of employees who unfastened the bunches of palm leaves and tied them once again — and copied or ordered the copying of the texts that they needed."
>
> On temple archives vs scholarly library division at Chidambaram (Naṭarāja temple), inscriptions recording librarian staff (sarasvatipaṇḍāra), and the later transfer tradition of temple mss toward the Tanjore Maharaja Serfoji's Saraswati Mahal Library.

Footnotes point to Colas's earlier codicological works (Colas 1994, 2001, 2011) for copying/correction practices.

## Work 3 — Gérard Colas, "'Variant', variation and pāṭha in Sanskrit" (complementary, OA)

- In: Glenn W. Most (ed.), *Variants and Variance in Classical Textual Cultures: Errors, Innovations, Proliferation, Reception?*, De Gruyter 2024, pp. 37-84. OAPEN handle 20.500.12657/94302; chapter DOI 10.1515/9783111054360-002.

Verified extract (pp. 56-57 of volume OCR):

> "Scholarly editor-compilers codified and phonically adapted the collection in the eighth or sixth century BCE. ... In order to consolidate the oral invariance, the editors-compilers elaborated, from the eighth century BCE onwards probably, a succession of pāṭha-s, modes of recitation/singing... As many as eleven modes of recitation (pāṭha-s) were thus defined. Three of them (called prakṛti-s) were said to be fundamental, the first two being the earliest: the simple continuous recitation (saṃhitā-pāṭha), the word-by-word recitation (pada-pāṭha) (said to be due to Śākalya, perhaps 6th century BCE) and the krama-pāṭha, that is, 'by steps' (attributed to the grammarian Bābhravya)... To these ... were later added eight secondary modes (the 'derived' vikṛti-s)... For example, the 'braided' mode of recitation (jaṭā-pāṭha) presents a pair of words, its repetition in reverse order, then its repetition in the regular order... AB BA AB."
>
> "The complementary use of these eleven pāṭha-s made it possible to preserve the text of the Ṛksaṃhitā faithfully. It helped to avoid the intrusion of variance..."

## What remains unverified

An explicit published statement that tīkās were copied onto interleaved blank folios WITHIN the same pothi as their root text has NOT been found in any accessible source this loop — "(ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน)". What IS verified: (a) commentaries circulating as separate palm-leaf volumes (Burnell XXIII Tribhāṣyaratna, 153 talipat leaves; Sāyaṇa commentary volumes in Madras GOML); (b) two-text-section ruled folios in West Indian Jaina pothis; (c) marginal additions/foliation/supplementary-folio practices in Nepalese pothis (Bhattarai); (d) the standing copy-and-check workshop economy around palm-leaf libraries (Colas 2023). Candidate paywalled source for the interleaving claim: E. Wilden, "Writing a Commentary on Palm-Leaf", Mesótēs (De Gruyter 2025), DOI 10.1515/9783111591025-022.
