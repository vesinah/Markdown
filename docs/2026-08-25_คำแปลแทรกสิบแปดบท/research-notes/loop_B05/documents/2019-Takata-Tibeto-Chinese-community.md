## Key claims

1. Concept: after Tibetan rule (787-848), a "Tibeto-Chinese community" formed at Dunhuang - Chinese inhabitants so influenced by Tibetan that they habitually used Tibetan script to write their native Chinese language.
2. Formation mechanisms argued: (a) working knowledge of Tibetan was essential for Chinese under Tibetan administration; higher competence needed for cooperation with authorities; (b) empire-scale sutra-copying projects mobilised many Chinese, who thereby acquired Tibetan-script writing skill - including people not fully literate in Chinese characters, for whom Tibetan script substituted for Chinese characters; (c) generally low literacy of medieval Dunhuang society as enabling condition.
3. Persistence: the Tibeto-Chinese community remained in existence into the Guiyijun period (848-early 11th c.); much cited material is Guiyijun-dated on phonetic grounds.
4. Dating tool (important for annotation-layer analysis): Tibetan transcriptions of Chinese fall into two systems - one reflecting the Tang capital Chang'an koine (with local corruption) used in the Tibetan period; the other showing the local Dunhuang dialect (North-Western Chinese variant) in the Guiyijun period. Phonetic profile of an interlinear or marginal transcription can therefore date the layer.
5. New identification announced: P.T.1254 (Chinese text in Tibetan script) identified by Takata as a wedding-ceremony recitation (introductory address, prayer for bride, prayer for groom, closing address), a popular medieval Dunhuang practice; evidence for continued Tibeto-Chinese usage in the late Guiyijun 10th c.
6. Other documents discussed include P.T.1256 and P.T.1102 verso (sheyi circular with expenditure record dated to early Guiyijun), and pentatonic-scale notation material P.T.… (Chinese surnames assigned to scale notes).
7. Self-reference confirming the classic piece: n. 18 cites Takata, 'Multilingualism in Tun-huang', Acta Asiatica 78 (2000): 60, 69-70.

## Relevance to interlinear-gloss project
- Sino-Tibetan transcription layers (Chang'an vs Dunhuang dialect phonology) provide an internal chronology for glosses/transcriptions added in Tibetan script beside or above Chinese text.
- Supports treating hard-point pen + Tibetan script annotations as distinct social practices rather than random marginalia.
