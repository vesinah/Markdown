# Source Notes: Dunhuang Schoolroom / Student-Manuscript Studies (Mair 1981 cluster)

- File: 1981-Mair-schoolroom-manuscripts-cluster.md
- Scope: bibliography + evidence summary for didactic annotation practice at Dunhuang (Thousand Character Classic drills, Taigong jiajiao, student colophons, verso scribbles), as verified in Loop B05.
- Primary verification vehicle: Galambos 2020 (De Gruyter, OA), ch. 2 "Manuscripts Written by Students" and its References - full text harvested this loop (see companion file 2020-Galambos-Dunhuang-Manuscript-Culture-fulltext.md). Direct access to Mair 1981 itself was not possible this loop (see status).

## Anchor study
- Mair, Victor H. (1981), 'Lay Students and the Making of Vernacular Narrative: An Inventory of Tunhuang Manuscripts', Chinoperl Papers 10: 5-96.
  - Per Galambos 2020 ch.2: a catalogue of nearly 600 manuscripts judged to be student work; transcribes/translates colophons; comments on handwriting quality, corrections, punctuation and other palaeographic peculiarities. (This is the classic English-language entry point for correction/punctuation habits in schoolroom copies.)
  - Per Galambos n.187: a digital version is available through IDP (idp.bl.uk) - currently not machine-accessible from this environment (403/bot-check).

## Japanese monograph line (Ito Mieko)
- Ito Mieko (2008), Tonko monjo ni miru gakko kyoiku [School education seen in Dunhuang documents], Tokyo: Kyuko shoin. (In Japanese; the standard monograph on Dunhuang school education from manuscripts.)
- Ito Mieko (2001), 'Tonko shahon "Taiko kakyō" to gakko' [The manuscript Taigong jiajiao and schools], Ochanomizu joshi daigaku Chugoku bungaku kaiho 20: 69-89.
- Ito Mieko (2007b), 'Tonko no tsuzokushi "gakuro shi" ni tsuite' [Popular 'schoolboy poems' of Dunhuang], Ochanomizu joshi daigaku Chugoku bungaku kaiho 26: 1-21.

## Chinese scholarship
- Li Zhengyu (1987), 'Dunhuang xuelang tiji jizhu' [Annotated collection of Dunhuang student colophons], Dunhuangxue jikan 1: 26-40 - 144 colophons gathered, 72 firmly dated; some judged inauthentic.
- Li Zhengyu (1986a) 'Tang Song shidai de Dunhuang xuexiao', Dunhuang yanjiu 1: 39-47; (1986b) note on a Tang pupil's practice sheet, Wenwu tiandi 6: 15; (2005) hard-pen calligraphy of Dunhuang reserves, Taipei: Xinwenfeng.
- Gao Mingshi (1986), 'Tangdai Dunhuang de jiaoyu', Hanxue yanjiu 4.2: 231-270 (three-period division; monastic private schools in late Tang).
- Zhang Nali (2001; 2002a) on Dunhuang Qianziwen recensions and the annotated Zhu Qianziwen (Dunhuang yanjiu 3: 100-105; Dunhuangxue jikan 1: 45-59).
- Zheng Binglin 2001 cited by Galambos for a scenario about student use (n. 363).

## Western scholarship
- Zuercher, Erik (1989), 'Buddhism and Education in T'ang Times', in de Bary ed., Neo-Confucian Education: The Formative Stage, Berkeley: UC Press, 19-56 (repr. in Silk ed., Buddhism in China, Brill 2013, 295-337): hypothesised pupils as young as five or six; described the drill layout where the master wrote primer characters horizontally across the top and pupils copied each down full vertical columns.
- Galambos 2016, 'Scribbles on the Verso of Manuscripts Written by Lay Students in Dunhuang', Tonko shahon kenkyu nenpo 10: 497-522.

## Concrete evidence points (via Galambos 2020 ch.2, read this loop)
- S.2703: Qianziwen practice rows of identical characters with day counts; workload ca. 150-230 characters/day (12-13 lines x 12-18 chars).
- P.2825 (850) and S.705 (851): paired colophons of Song Wenxian (read aloud/du) and An Wende (copied) - dictation/copying pedagogy; second colophon segment possibly later addition in larger hand (annotation-layer case).
- P.3698/S.728/S.707 versos: disconnected titles and quotes (Xiaojing; Baixing zhang chs. 18-19), circular fragments in unskilled hands with errors; interpreted as reminders of assigned exercises; years-long collaborative reuse.
- Red punctuation marks limited to first 17 lines of the Xiaojing preface/text on one scroll - segmentation applied only where teaching reached.
- Copyists' poem about intentionally leaving mistakes for successors in four copies (P.2621, BD08668, P.3305, P.2937[884]) with phonetic-substitution variants.
