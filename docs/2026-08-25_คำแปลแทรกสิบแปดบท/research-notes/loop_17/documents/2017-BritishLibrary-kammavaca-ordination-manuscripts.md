== CONTENT SUMMARY (condensed transcription) ==

Kammavaca manuscripts contain Pali texts used at ordination ceremonies. The most important were
prepared for upasampada (higher ordination). A novice may remain a monk as long as he wishes.

Materials: kammavaca manuscripts are written on a variety of materials - primarily palm leaf, but
also stiffened cloth, or gold, silver, metal or ivory sheets in the shape of palm leaf. Thick
lacquer or gilded decoration appears on leaves and cover boards. The Pali text is written in black
lacquer in ornate Burmese characters known as "tamarind-seed" script (also called "square" script),
which differs from usual round Burmese writing. Some unusual kammavaca were made from discarded
monastic robes thickly covered with black lacquer with inlaid mother-of-pearl letters.

Examples held at the British Library (with dates given in the post):
- Add. 15289: kammavaca in Pali in Burmese square script on gilded and lacquered palm leaf,
  18th century.
- Or. 12010H: on ivory, 18th century.
- Or. 12010E: written on red palm leaf, black lacquer text on red lacquer ground, gilt boards,
  19th century.
- Or. 13896: on metal, gilded and lacquered in red, 19th century, with fine gold-and-red scenes
  from the Buddha's life on outer sides of first/last leaves.
- Or. 12010A: lacquered cloth leaves, gilded boards, donor's name on inner front board, 19th c.

Historical frame given in the post: In the 12th century the Sihala Ordination was introduced into
Burma by Chappata [spelled Chappaṭa] who had studied canon and commentaries in Sri Lanka. In the
15th century Sri Lanka was again turned to as source of orthodoxy; in 1476 twenty-two monks were
sent by ship and ordained by Mahavihara monks at the sima on the Kalyani River; King Dhammaceti
(1471-1492) built the Kalyani Sima in Pegu (Bago).

Ordination structure: two ceremonies held in sima - pabbajja (novices) and upasampada (monks).
Upasampada requires five perfections: person, assembly, sima, motion, and kammavaca; selected monks
recite the kammavaca with great care for articulation and pronunciation.

== SUPPLEMENTARY SOURCE (fetched same session): Smithsonian NMAA blog ==

"Ornate and Sacred: Burmese Kammavaca Manuscripts" (asia.si.edu, published 2026-05-04):
describes production technique - yellow ink ground + gold leaf wash-off gilding; text written in
tamarind-seed script with thick black lacquer sap (thitsi); squirrel-tail and cow-ear-hair brushes
(17th-19th c.); dated donor colophons in sakkaraj era (e.g., sakkaraj 1282 = 1920 CE, U Sa U and
Daw Shan of Thet-ke village, Wakema town). Examples dated to reign of King Sanay (1698-1714)
compared with Royal Asiatic Society and Bodleian collections. URL:
https://asia.si.edu/whats-on/blog/posts/ornate-and-sacred-burmese-kammavaca-manuscripts/
