## Abstract (verbatim)
"In the Aljamiado texts, we see a consistent system used for the transliteration of the Romance language in Arabic script. From the oldest copies preserved (15th century) until the last ones produced by the Moriscos at the beginning of the 17th century, a standardised graphic and orthographic system has been applied to these Spanish texts written in Arabic script."

## Key content extracted
1. Historical frame: after 1492 (fall of Granada) and Cisneros's campaign from 1499, Muslims of Castile (1502) and Aragon (1526) forced to baptise; Aljamiado production flourished among mudajares/moriscos in Aragon and Castile into the early 17th century.
2. A standardised graphic and orthographic system for Romance in Arabic script persists across the whole corpus; svarabhakti (epenthetic) vowel letters had phonetic reality under Arabic interference (following Corriente).
3. Troves and dispersal: manuscripts hidden behind walls in houses (e.g., Almonacid de la Sierra trove now mainly in Tomas Navarro Tomas Library, CCHS-CSIC Madrid, and Escuelas Pias of Saragossa; Calanda mss in Library of the Cortes de Aragon); other codices bought by bibliophiles in the 17th c., e.g. two Aljamiado manuscripts in the Bibliotheque nationale de France acquired by Antoine Galland, entering the King's library in Paris in 1680s.

## Companion article by same author (verified metadata)
Nuria de Castilla, "An Aljamiado Translation of the 'Morisco Qur'an' and its Arabic Text (c. 1609)," Journal of Qur'anic Studies 22.3 (2020): 35-62, doi:10.3366/jqs.2020.0439.
Abstract (via Crossref): strong links between three copies of the "Morisco Qur'an" - an Arabic copy (Aix 1367), an Aljamiado copy (BRAH T5), and a bilingual Arabo-Aljamiado version (BRAH T19, Real Academia de la Historia, Madrid); a single Aljamiado translation transmitted by the same copyist in two codices; all three produced around 1609 CE, when the final expulsion of the Moriscos began; enables a stemma codicum.

## Notes for the book chapter
- The bilingual Arabo-Aljamiado Morisco Qur'an (BRAH T19) is Iberia's closest analogue to interlinear Qur'an layout: Castilian rendering aligned to the Arabic for a community losing Arabic competence.
- Creating Standards volume also covers Persian (Orsatti), Judaeo-Arabic (Wagner), Ottoman Turkish orthography (Schmidt), Sino-Arabic xiaojing (Sobieroj), Jawi (van der Putten), Kanuri/Hausa Ajami (Bondarev/Dobronravin) - i.e., the Arabic-script vernacularisation complex within which interlinear glossing sits.
