# The "Zaferani Qur'an" — oldest dated complete Persian-interlinear Qur'an (546 AH / 1151-52 CE)

## Metadata
- Manuscript: copied by Abolfazl Zaferani in the city of Rey, 546 AH (1151-52 CE); preserved at the Reza Abbasi Museum, Tehran
- Event: high-quality facsimile unveiled at the National Library and Archives of Iran (NLAI), July 2026; joint project of NLAI and Farhang-e Siadat Publications; c. eight years' work with colour calibration
- Sources (both fetched in full this session):

## Verified content
1. "A high-quality facsimile edition of the 'Zaferani Quran', recognized by scholars as the oldest known complete and dated manuscript of the Holy Quran accompanied by a Persian translation, was unveiled..." (IQNA).
2. Copied 546 AH (1151-52 CE) by Abolfazl Zaferani in Rey; held at Tehran's Reza Abbasi Museum; "the earliest surviving complete and precisely dated Persian-translated Quran identified to date."
3. Layout (key for this project): "Another notable feature of the manuscript is its interlinear format, with the Persian translation written in smaller script between the lines of the Arabic text." (IQNA)
4. Translation character: closely associated with the Qur'anic commentary of the 11th-century scholar Abu Bakr Atiq Nishapuri (Surabadi); the copyist was Zaferani.
5. Speakers at unveiling: manuscript scholar Morteza Kariminia (project lead), linguist Ali Ashraf Sadeqi (linguistic value for classical Persian; no perfect one-to-one equivalence between Arabic and Persian), Seyyed Mohammad Emadi Haeri (translation rooted in early Iranian Quranic exegesis tradition; early legitimisation of Persian Qur'an translation), Mohammad Jafar Yahaqi (6th century AH as golden age of Persian Qur'an translation/exegesis), Masjed-Jamei.

## Notes for the book chapter
- This is now the anchor primary witness for Persian interlinear tarjuma: a dated Seljuk-era codex demonstrating the classic layout convention (smaller script between Arabic lines) two centuries before the famous Ilkhanid/Muzaffarid luxury interlinear Qur'ans.
- Caution: news-level reporting; codicological detail should be checked against Kariminia's facsimile edition when accessible.
- NOT verified this session: the "Qur'an of Sultan Abu Ishaq Inju" (757 AH) often repeated in popular accounts; and Tashkent/Isfahan codices as early PERSIAN interlinear witnesses (the Tashkent ms verified this session belongs to the EASTERN TURKIC trilingual tradition per Peacock 2025).
