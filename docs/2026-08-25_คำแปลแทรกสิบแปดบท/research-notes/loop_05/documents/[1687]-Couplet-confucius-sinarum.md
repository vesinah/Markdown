# Confucius Sinarum Philosophus (1687) — Jesuit translation of Confucius

## Metadata
- Title: Confucius Sinarum Philosophus, sive Scientia Sinensis Latine exposita ("Confucius, philosopher of the Chinese, or the Chinese knowledge explained in Latin")
- Editors/translators: Jesuit missionaries Prospero Intorcetta (1626-1696), Christian Herdtrich (1625-1684), François de Rougemont (1624-1674), Philippe Couplet (1623-1692); with François de Louvemont
- Publication: Parisiis, apud Danielem Horthemels, 1687 (also Cramoisy imprint)
- Digitized copy consulted: https://archive.org/details/confuciussinarum00conf ; partial digital edition of front matter: https://archive.org/details/CSP-1-front-matter-epistola
- URL: https://archive.org/details/confuciussinarum00conf

## Primary-text sample (Couplet's Epistola to Louis XIV; OCR)
Couplet describes his return from China and the king's favor toward the work:
> "Ego vero hujus unius rei & victoriae tam incredibili fama praecipue perculsus huc ab ultimis Sinarum oris adveni... Quibus porro incedent utitiis, cum accipient suum illum Confucium tanto a Te in pretio & honore habitum fuisse, ut ei ceteros inter Bibliotheca Regia libros locum esse volueris? eumdem Latino sermone donatum... non ligneis tantum illis, quibus Sina utuntur, tabulis, sed areis & elegantissimis excusos... eum denique, qui Sinico tantum in Imperio hactenus erat clausus, jam per Galliam atque ex Gallia per omnem late Europam brevi spargendum."
(OCR normalized; archive.org/details/confuciussinarum00conf)

In plain terms: Couplet thanks the King for placing the translated Confucius among the books of the Royal Library and promises its rapid spread through France to all Europe.

## Context
- The volume contains translations of three of the Four Books (Daxue, Zhongyong, Lunyu) with the Analects, preceded by a prolegomena on Chinese learning and Couplet's Tabula chronologica monarchiae Sinicae (Chinese dynastic chronology synchronized with biblical chronology) — visible in the archive.org item structure (CSP-1-front-matter-epistola, CSP-4-liber-primus, CSP-5-liber-secundus, CSP-7-tabula-chronologica).
- Significance for early Orientalism: first major Latin rendering of Confucius for European readers; presented Chinese moral philosophy as compatible with natural theology; its chronology provoked European debates about biblical chronology.
- William Jones's Third Anniversary Discourse (1786) still cites "Couplet" as an authority for the epoch of Buddha — evidence of the work's long scholarly afterlife (see eliohs text harvested at https://www.eliohs.unifi.it/testi/700/jones/Jones_Discourse_3.html).

## Notes for the book chapter
- Institutional channel: Society of Jesus mission network (Jesuit residences in China) plus royal patronage in Paris (Bibliothèque du Roi). The manuscript-to-print pipeline ran through collation of Chinese texts by missionary teams over decades.
- The Tabula chronologica exemplifies comparative chronology: aligning Chinese history with European/biblical time via manuscripts and printed tables.
