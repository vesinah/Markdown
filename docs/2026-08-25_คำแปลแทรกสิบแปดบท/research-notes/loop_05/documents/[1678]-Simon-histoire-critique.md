# Richard Simon — Histoire critique du Vieux Testament

## Metadata
- Author: Richard Simon (1638-1712), Oratorian priest
- Work: Histoire critique du Vieux Testament
- First edition: Paris, 1678 (printed by Billaine; almost the whole printing was confiscated and destroyed after Bossuet's intervention); reissued Rotterdam, chez Reinier Leers, 1680 (with false imprint on some copies); new "first" edition Rotterdam 1685
- Digitized copy consulted: Internet Archive identifier histoirecritique01simo (Rotterdam, R. Leers, 1685), vol. 1; also ned-kbn-all-00003353-001 (1680, DBNL scan)
- URLs:

  - English translation of his NT companion work: Richard Simon, Critical History of the Text of the New Testament (1715 ed. translated), ed. Bertrand Krans, The Critical Review 19 (2014), DOI https://doi.org/10.15699/tc.19.2014.14

## Primary-text sample (Preface; OCR from 1685 Leers edition)
The preface's programmatic statement on criticism of Scripture:
> "Enfin le profond respect que nous devons avoir pour les Livres Sacrés, ne doit pas nous empêcher de nous appliquer avec soin à la Critique de ces mêmes Livres. Il seroit honteux, que des Protestans qui font une profession particuliere de cette étude, fussent inférieurs à ceux de l'Eglise Romaine... Le profond respect que nous devons avoir pour les Livres Sacrés ne doit donc pas nous dispenser d'examiner avec liberté ce qu'ils contiennent..."
(OCR normalized; archive.org/details/histoirecritique01simo)

Earlier in the same preface he argues that judgment must rest on reasons rather than persons:
> "N'ayons donc point tant d'égard aux qualités personnelles ni à la Religion des Auteurs qui écrivent, qu'à la solidité de leurs raisons."
(OCR normalized)

## Notes for the book chapter
- Simon's method: systematic comparison of Hebrew manuscripts, ancient versions (Septuagint, Targums, Vulgate), patristic citations, and variant readings; he argued that no single text tradition is free of human transmission history — hence "critical history" of the text.
- Institutional dimension: Oratory; then censorship — the 1678 edition was suppressed (Bossuet and Harlay), pushing publication to Rotterdam with Leers. (Suppression details widely reported in secondary literature; Bernus 1869 documents the episode.)
- Connection to the era's manuscript culture: Simon treats manuscripts and versions as evidence requiring collation and classification, effectively applying Maurist-style erudition to the Bible itself.
