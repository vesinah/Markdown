# George Sale — The Koran (1734), Preliminary Discourse and explanatory notes

## Metadata
- Translator: George Sale (1697?-1736), English lawyer and orientalist
- Work: The Koran, commonly called the Alcoran of Mohammed, translated into English immediately from the original Arabic; with explanatory notes, taken from the most approved commentators. To which is prefixed a Preliminary Discourse.
- First edition: London, printed by C. Ackers, for J. Wilcox, 1734; dedicated to John Lord Carteret
- Digitized copies consulted:
  - 1882 Wherry edition containing Sale's Preliminary Discourse with commentary, vol. I: https://archive.org/details/comprehensivecom01sale (Trübner, 1882; OCR used for excerpts below)
  - 1734 first-edition scan (BIM/ECCO): https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_the-koran-commonly-call_1734
  - Later editions listed via Online Books Page: https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Sale%2C%20George%2C%201697%3F-1736

## Primary-text samples (Preliminary Discourse; OCR from Wherry vol. I)
Sale's survey of predecessor translations and his assessment of Marracci:
> "In 1698 a Latin translation of the Quran, made by Father Lewis Marracci, who had been confessor to Pope Innocent XI., was published at Padua, together with the original text, accompanied by explanatory notes and a refutation. This translation of Marracci's, generally speaking, is very exact; but adheres to the Arabic idiom too literally to be easily understood... The notes he has added are indeed of great use..."
And Sale's programmatic claim of impartiality:
> "Having therefore undertaken a new translation, I have endeavoured to do the original impartial justice, not having, to the best of my knowledge, represented it, in any one instance, either better or worse than it really is."
(OCR normalized; archive.org/details/comprehensivecom01sale)

The Discourse opens with Arabian geography and ethnology (limits of Arabia, its five provinces Yaman, Hijaz, Tahama, Najd, Yamama), citing Strabo, Pocock, Golius ad Alfraganus — i.e., built on European Arabist erudition.

## Context (secondary)
- Bevilacqua's study (Journal of the Warburg and Courtauld Institutes 79, 2016), "The Qur'an Translations of Marracci and Sale," JSTOR https://www.jstor.org/stable/24395514 : documents Sale's dependence on Marracci's Padua edition and identifies the Dutch Church, Austin Friars' 14th-century manuscript of al-Baydawi's commentary as a key source behind Sale's notes.
- quran-archive.org explorer for the 1734 first edition: https://quran-archive.org/explorer/george-sale — records over 120 editions to 1975 and the claim (unverified here) that Jefferson bought a copy in 1765.

## Notes for the book chapter
- Sale's notes are a version-comparison enterprise in miniature: he cites approved Arabic commentaries (al-Baydawi especially), compares readings, translates literal senses vs idiomatic renderings, and supplies historical context — the same apparatus culture visible in biblical criticism applied to the Qur'an.
- His Preliminary Discourse functioned as Europe's standard introduction to Islam for nearly two centuries.
- Method chain: Marracci (Latin, 1698, with refutation) -> Sale (English, 1734, with notes but claiming impartiality) -> German/Russian/Bulgarian translations derived from Sale (per Bevilacqua/JSTOR snippet).
