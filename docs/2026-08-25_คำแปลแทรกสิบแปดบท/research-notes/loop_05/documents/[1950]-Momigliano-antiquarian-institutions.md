# Antiquarianism institutionalized — Society of Antiquaries, Ashmole, Cotton and Harley libraries, Momigliano's thesis

## Metadata
- Topic: English/European antiquarian institutions of the 17th-18th centuries that made old documents a public, curated object of study

## Findings by institution

### Society of Antiquaries of London
- Source page: https://www.sal.org.uk/about-us/our-history/
- Claimed lineage to the College of Antiquaries (1586); James I's "mislike" stopped meetings in 1614.
- Founded 5 December 1707 at the Bear Tavern in the Strand ("refounded" 1717 per some documents); first President Peter Le Neve, Norroy King of Arms; business = "the subject of antiquities, particularly as they related to the history of Great Britain"; period of study pre-dating James I.
- Continuous minutes from 1717 (Mitre Tavern, Fleet Street); Royal Charter 1751; role defined as "the encouragement, advancement and furtherance of the study and knowledge of antiquities and history in this and other countries".
- Publication venue: Archaeologia (the Society's serial), referenced in Momigliano-related literature.

### Elias Ashmole and the Ashmolean
- Source pages: https://www.ashmolean.org/history-ashmolean ; Bodleian digital collection note: https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/collections/ashmole
- Ashmole (1617-1692), Windsor herald and antiquary; acquired the Tradescant collection ('The Ark', Lambeth, from 1634) and gifted it to Oxford in 1682; the Ashmolean opened 1683 on Broad Street as Britain's first public museum and world's first university museum, admitting the public from the outset.
- His manuscript bequest (2,000+ medieval and early modern texts; medicine, astrology, alchemy, heraldry) housed in the museum until transfer to the Bodleian in 1860.

### Cotton library
- Sources: https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=1488 ; https://www.dhi.ac.uk/projects/cotton-manuscripts/
- Sir Robert Bruce Cotton (1586-1631) collected original manuscripts from 1588 until his death; 958 manuscripts; called the most important private manuscript collection ever assembled in Britain; includes Magna Carta letters patent, unique medieval copies of Beowulf and Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, Lindisfarne Gospels, Tudor state papers.
- Bequeathed to the nation by his grandson in 1700; badly damaged by fire in 1731 (Ashburnham House); one of the foundation collections of the British Museum in 1753; Planta catalogue published 1801.

### Harley library
- Sources: https://www.rct.uk/collection/1071987/a-catalogue-of-the-harleian-manuscripts-in-the-british-museum-v-1 ; BL catalogue records e.g. https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002046380
- Assembled by Robert Harley, 1st Earl of Oxford (1661-1724), statesman, and Edward Harley, 2nd Earl (1689-1741); began with purchase of the antiquary Sir Simonds d'Ewes's ~600 manuscripts (1704/05).
- Contents: 7,660 manuscripts incl. 2,200 illuminated, 14,000+ original legal documents, 500+ rolls; sold to the nation in 1753 for £10,000 under the same Act of Parliament establishing the British Museum; librarian Humfrey Wanley catalogued it (Bibliotheca Harleiana).

### British Museum Act 1753
- Foundation collections: Sloane's collection + Cottonian Library + Harleian Library (joined by Old Royal Library 1757); first national public museum belonging to neither church nor king. Source summary: https://ipfs.io/ipfs/QmXoypizjW3WknFiJnKLwHCnL72vedxjQkDDP1mXWo6uco/wiki/The_British_Museum.html (mirror of encyclopedic text used for keywords only; core facts cross-checked against RCT/BL/dhi.ac.uk pages above).

## Momigliano's thesis (for method discussion)
- Core claim (as summarized in scholarship surfaced this loop): antiquarians studied the ancient past systematically through non-narrative evidence (inscriptions, coins, charters, monuments), while historians wrote narrative; in the 18th century their methods converged, and the antiquarian's evidentiary apparatus was absorbed into modern historical method — the antiquarian "gave shape to the basic principles of the modern historical method" (SciSpace abstract of the 1950 paper: https://scispace.com/papers/ancient-history-and-the-antiquarian-5g2jzo7xa3).
- Related modern study found: "David Hume, the Académie des Inscriptions and the Nature of Historical Evidence in the Early Eighteenth Century", Modern Intellectual History (CUP): https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/F075D92BD0F5139E170CA742240190CD/S1479244319000404a.pdf/david-hume-the-academie-des-inscriptions-and-the-nature-of-historical-evidence-in-the-early-eighteenth-century.pdf

## Notes for the book chapter
- The libraries of Cotton/Harley are directly relevant to manuscript studies of glosses/scholia: they preserved Anglo-Saxon and medieval witnesses whose marginal and interlinear annotations became objects of cataloguing and edition.
- Institutional chain: private cabinet -> chartered society or national museum -> printed catalogue (Planta 1801; Harleian Catalogue 1808-12; Bibliotheca Harleiana) — the documentary infrastructure that later philology presupposes.
