# Sir William Jones — Third Anniversary Discourse (1786) and the Asiatic Society of Bengal

## Metadata
- Author: Sir William Jones (1746-1794), judge of the Supreme Court at Calcutta, founder-president of the Asiatic Society ("Asiatick Society") of Bengal
- Work: The Third Anniversary Discourse, delivered 2 February 1786, by the President, at the Asiatick Society: On the Hindus

## Primary-text sample (the famous Sanskrit passage; from the Eliohs edition of Works III)
> "The Sanscrit language, whatever be its antiquity, is of a wonderful structure; more perfect than the Greek, more copious than the Latin, and more exquisitely refined than either, yet bearing to both of them a stronger affinity, both in the roots of verbs and in the forms of grammar, than could possibly have been produced by accident; so strong indeed, that no philologer could examine them all three, without believing them to have sprung from some common source, which, perhaps, no longer exists: there is a similar reason, though not quite so forcible, for supposing that both the Gothick and the Celtick, though blended with a very different idiom, had the same origin with the Sanscrit; and the old Persian might be added to the same family..."

Also methodologically notable in the same discourse:
> "Etymology has, no doubt, some use in historical researches; but it is a medium of proof so very fallacious, that, where it elucidates one fact, it obscures a thousand..."
Jones lists four "media" of inquiry into Hindu history: "first, their Languages and Letters; secondly, their Philosophy and Religion; thirdly, the actual remains of their old Sculpture and Architecture; and fourthly, the written memorials of their Sciences and Arts."

## Context (secondary)
- Britannica, "Asiatic Society of Bengal": founded Jan. 15, 1784 by Sir William Jones to encourage Oriental studies; Jones arrived in Calcutta Sept. 25, 1783 as supreme court judge; supported by Warren Hastings who declined the presidency; Indians first admitted 1829. https://www.britannica.com/topic/Asiatic-Society-of-Bengal
- Britannica, "Sir William Jones": learned 28 languages incl. Chinese; Grammar of the Persian Language (1771); Moallakât (1782); knighted and sailed to Calcutta 1783; Institutes of Hindu Law 1794; his 1786 presidential discourse postulated common ancestry of Sanskrit, Latin, Greek, providing impetus for comparative linguistics. https://www.britannica.com/biography/William-Jones-British-orientalist-and-jurist
- Nature 133, 21 (1934) on the Society's 150th anniversary: founded under the name "Asiatick Society", Jan 15, 1784, "to inquire into the history, civil and natural, the antiquities, laws, arts, sciences and literature of Asia". https://www.nature.com/articles/133021b0
- Royal Asiatic Society blog note on founding meeting presided over by Justice Robert Chambers: https://royalasiaticsociety.org/the-asiatic-society-and-william-jones

## Notes for the book chapter
- Institutionalized orientalism: the Society's journal Asiatick Researches (from 1788) created a periodical venue for manuscript collection, translation, and comparison across Asian literatures.
- Jones's comparative argument is philological stemmatics applied to languages: shared root/form systems imply a common ancestor source — structurally parallel to Bentley-style textual genealogy.
- Irony noted by Encyclopaedia Iranica: the same Jones who attacked Anquetil-Duperron's Avesta as forgery became the era's great comparativist.
