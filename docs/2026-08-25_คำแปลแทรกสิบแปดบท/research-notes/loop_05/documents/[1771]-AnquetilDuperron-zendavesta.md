# Abraham-Hyacinthe Anquetil-Duperron — Zend-Avesta (1771)

## Metadata
- Author/translator: Abraham-Hyacinthe Anquetil-Duperron (1731-1805), member of the Académie Royale des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres; Interprète du Roi pour les Langues Orientales
- Work: Zend-Avesta, ouvrage de Zoroastre, contenant les idées théologiques, physiques & morales de ce législateur, les cérémonies du culte religieux qu'il a établi... Traduit en français sur l'original Zend, avec des remarques...
- Publication: Paris, N. M. Tilliard, 1771 (3 volumes, including Journal du voyage, Notice des manuscrits, Vie de Zoroastre)
- Digitized copy consulted: https://archive.org/details/zendavestaouvrag03anqu (1771; scan labeled vol. 3 but internally titled TOME SECOND, containing Ieschts Sades, Boun-dehesch [Bundahishn], two vocabularies Zend-Pehlvi-Francois etc.) and ouvragedezoroastre02anqu (1781 printing)

## Context (secondary)
- Encyclopaedia Iranica, "ANQUETIL-DUPERRON, ABRAHAM HYACINTHE": In 1754 he saw Avesta lines from a Bodleian fragment brought by Richard Cobbe (1723) and resolved to retrieve the sacred book from India; enlisted as a soldier to reach Pondicherry (1755); studied Persian/Tamil/Telugu; at Surat (1758-1760) obtained the Vendidad from dasturs Kaus and Darab, learned Avestan via Persian, acquired 180 manuscripts including samples of nearly all Indian languages; deposited them in the Bibliothèque du Roi on 15 March 1762; published the three-volume translation in 1771. He was immediately accused of forgery by William Jones; vindicated only in 1791-94 by Tychsen and Heeren. "Anquetil was nonetheless the first to bring a manuscript of an ancient oriental sacred text other than the Bible to the attention of European scholars." The right approach came with Sanskrit (Eugène Burnouf, sixty years later). https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/anquetil-duperron
- German adaptation: Johann Friedrich Kleuker's Anhang zum Zend-Avesta (Riga, 1776-1783): https://archive.org/details/11731578bsb , https://archive.org/details/10219972bsb
- Later project: Anquetil-Duperron's Latin Oupnek'hat (1801-1802), translation of the Upanishads from Persian: https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_ulxNgvzEWggC (1802)

## Notes for the book chapter
- Methodological significance: Anquetil worked from living informants (Zoroastrian priests) and manuscripts together, documented his sources (Notice des manuscrits), and published philological apparatus (glossaries) — a new evidentiary standard for non-biblical sacred texts.
- The forgery controversy shows that authenticity criticism had become the gatekeeping question of the republic of letters when judging oriental texts.
