## Primary-text sample (Preface to the 1699 Dissertation; OCR)
Bentley opens by answering the Oxford editors' insinuation about Royal Library manuscripts:
> "The late Editors of Phalaris have told the World in their Preface, That among other Specimens of their diligence, they collated the MSS, as far as the XL Epistle; and would have done so throughout, but that the Library-keeper out of his singular Humanity denied them the further use. This was meant as a lash for me, who had the Honour then and since to serve His Majesty in that Office."
(OCR normalized; archive.org/details/dissertationupon00bent)

The Dissertation's argumentative core (per Britannica): Bentley "attacked the authenticity of the epistles, bringing all his learning and critical powers to bear in proof of their spuriousness," dating them linguistically and historically long after Phalaris (6th c. BC).

## Context (secondary)
- Britannica, "Richard Bentley": born Jan. 27, 1662, Oulton, Yorkshire; died July 14, 1742, Cambridge. Epistola ad Joannem Millium (1691) displayed "skill in textual emendation and... knowledge of ancient metre". Boyle lectures 1692; keeper of the Royal Library and FRS 1694; Phalaris dissertation 1699; master of Trinity 1700; regius professor of divinity 1717; Horace edition 1711; Terence 1726; Manilius 1739. His digamma discovery showed that a lost sound was present in certain Homeric Greek words though unrepresented in writing. https://www.britannica.com/biography/Richard-Bentley
- Mill connection: Bentley reviewed proof sheets of John Mill's edition of Malalas (1689 encounter), which led to the Epistola ad Millium. John Mill's Novum Testamentum (Oxford 1707), with its apparatus of ~30,000 variant readings, is documented by later reprints "ad exemplar Millianum": https://archive.org/details/novumtestamentu00baskgoog (1763 Baskerville reprint).

## Proposed New Testament edition — Proposals (1720) [VERIFIED from primary text]
- Primary source consulted (OCR): Dr. Richard Bentley's proposals for printing a new edition of the Greek Testament, and St. Hierom's Latin version. With a full answer to all the remarks of a late pamphleteer, London, J. Knapton, 1721 (44 p.; the Proposals text is reprinted here with Bentley's reply to Conyers Middleton's Remarks):
  - https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_dr-bentleys-proposals-_bentley-richard_1721
  - HathiTrust full view of same: https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=uc1.31175035489742 ; catalog https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/012434870
  - Dyce, Works of Richard Bentley, vol. III (1836) contains the Proposals: https://archive.org/details/worksrichardben02newtgoog
- Verified quotations (OCR normalized from the 1721 pamphlet):
  - On manuscript age: "...there are MSS in Europe, (accessible, though with great Charge) above a Thousand Years old in both Languages" — hence a new edition of the Greek and Latin "not according to the recent and interpolated Copies, but as represented in the most antient and venerable MSS, in Greek and Roman Capital Letters."
  - On Jerome as witness: Bentley cites passages of St. Jerome declaring "that he adusted and reform'd the whole Latin Vulgate to the best Greek Exemplars, that is, to those of the famous Origen"; his conjecture: if the oldest copies of the Greek and of Jerome's Latin were compared, "perhaps they would be still found to agree both in Words and Order of Words."
  - Aim: to retrieve "the true Exemplar of Origen, which was the Standard to the most Learned of the Fathers, at the time of the Council of Nice and two Centuries after."
  - On variant reduction: "out of a Labyrinth of Thirty Thousand Various Readings, that crowd the Pages of our present Best Editions... there will scarce be two Hundred out of so many Thousands that can deserve the least Consideration."
  - Apparatus planned: "the old Versions, Syriac, Coptic, Gothic, and Aethiopic, and of all the Fathers, Greeks and Latins, within the first Five Centuries," with all known variant readings from those five centuries printed in notes.
- Context (secondary): In 1716 Bentley announced the plan in a letter to Archbishop William Wake; assisted by J. J. Wetstein; John Walker collated manuscripts in Paris "with the help of the Maurists"; lead manuscript Codex Alexandrinus, which he called "the oldest and best in the world"; Bentley rescued it from the Ashburnham House fire of 1731 that damaged the Cotton library. The edition never appeared; Bentleii Critica Sacra (ed. A. A. Ellis, 1862) prints remnants from an interleaved Greek-Latin Vulgate at Trinity. (Sources: encyclopedic biography cross-checked against Britannica bio and HathiTrust/IA records listed above.)
- Stemmatic significance: Bentley reasons genealogically — old versions (Jerome's Vulgate, Syriac, Coptic, Gothic, Ethiopic) as independent witnesses to a lost archetype (Origen's exemplar / pre-Nicene text), mutual correction of Greek and Latin traditions, and drastic reduction of the variant mass. This is stemmatic reasoning before the formal stemma codicum.

## Notes for the book chapter
- The Phalaris controversy (Bentley vs Charles Boyle and the Christ Church Oxford circle) became the emblematic quarrel between philological criticism and polite letters; Swift's Battle of Books satirized it.
- Bentley's method: date a text by linguistic features (dialect forms, anachronisms, meter), test internal consistency, and weigh manuscript authority — applied here to secular antiquity just as Mabillon applied formal criteria to charters and Simon to biblical texts.
