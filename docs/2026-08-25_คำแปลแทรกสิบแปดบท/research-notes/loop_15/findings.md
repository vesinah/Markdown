## Key findings

1. Term verified: "maarti" is real and current. Kamiishi (2025, Textual Microcosms) defines it: derives from Balinese arti ("meaning"); a format placing translation equivalents directly above or below the OJ source text on lontar, with dotted lines connecting corresponding segments; word-/phrase-level alignment. Attested spellings in sources: maarti, Maarti (titles), also "Kekawin ... Maarti". Diacritized variants mārti/mārtti not attested in fetched sources (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน). "Pamurtian" likewise unverified anywhere this loop (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน). URL: https://medium.com/@Textual.Microcosms/sensuality-in-the-margins-translation-in-a-balinese-maarti-manuscript-3567b024c812

2. Function confirmed as didactic-performance aid: Indonesian philology journal (Widiantana 2024) states a maarti kakawin is complete with its Balinese translation so that the penembang (singer) can gloss each stanza during performance, since most Balinese lack good Old Javanese; it directly supports the peneges (oral interpreter) in mabebasan sessions and is regarded as the ancestor of printed kakawin editions carrying arti/interpretation. URL: https://www.manuskripta.manassa.id/index.php/journal/article/download/46/8

3. Layout constraints of lontar shape the format: narrow leaves (e.g., Bharatayuda Maarti leaves 51.5 x 3.5 cm; Ariwangsa Maarti ca. 54.5 x 3.5 cm; 246-327+96 leaves) leave no room for wide margins, hence equivalents set above/below with dotted connectors rather than true between-line insertion; Kamiishi explicitly notes maarti differs from narrow-sense interlinear translation while sharing proximity and alignment features.

4. Named surviving maarti manuscripts (Q2): (a) Kekawin Bharatayuda Maarti at Balai Bahasa Provinsi Bali, scribe Nengah Serangan, colophon saka 1814 (= 1892 CE), 23 sargah Jagaddhita-Tebusol, classed Itihasa in the Gedong Kirtya scheme, Wikidata Q109381051 (https://ban.wikisource.org/wiki/Kekawin_Bharatayuda_Maarti_(Balai_Bahasa_Prov._Bali)); village copies also imaged (Sawan Bharata Yudha Maarti). (b) Kekawin Arjuna Wiwaha Maarti, shelfmark 007/BPB/Vb/91, Balai Bahasa Provinsi Bali, 174 folios up to canto 36 (Kamiishi 2025). (c) Kakawin Ari Wangsa Maarti (Arjunawiwaha), Pusdok Dinas Kebudayaan Provinsi Bali, scanned on archive.org (https://archive.org/details/kakawin-ariwangsa-maarti). Thus both most-popular classics per Creese (Bharatayuddha, Arjunawiwaha) are attested in maarti form; Sutasoma maarti specifically not yet located this loop.

5. Holdings map (Q2): Gedong Kirtya, Singaraja (founded 1928; thousands of lontar; preservation study DOI 10.20473/rlj.v7i1.115); Leiden LOr collections (van der Tuuk legacy; cited throughout Creese 1999 inventory); British Library - 69 Javanese/OJ/Balinese palm-leaf MSS digitized within 277 total Indonesian MSS, metadata dataset DOI 10.23636/ghz6-vt54, DHARMA Java-Bali Palmleaf project; ANU Hooykaas-Ketut Sangka ~6,000 transcriptions (HKS numbers cross-referenced in Creese's inventory; copies also at Leiden, Cornell, BL, Perpustakaan Nasional), https://anulib.anu.edu.au/balinese-manuscripts ; Balai Bahasa Provinsi Bali (142 cakep catalogued by WikiLontar 2021); Pusdok Denpasar (ca. 3,000 lontar images online per Acri 2015).

6. Scholarship landscape (Q3): Creese 1999 BKI inventory is the foundational census (open access CC BY-NC); Creese 1996 Archipel 52 dates several Balinese/Lombok kakawin (authorship correction: CRESE, not Worsley); Rubinstein - Beyond the Realm of the Senses (composition as ritual) plus her mabasan study referenced by Creese as Rubinstein 1993 and colophons article Archipel 52 1996; Hunter contributed vyakhya-style commentary analyses and the Javano-Balinese poetics work, and co-authored the Sumanasāntaka volume with Worsley/Supomo/Fletcher. Book titled "In Search of Language of the Gods" attributed to Creese in the brief could not be verified in any bibliographic source (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน).

7. Textual Microcosms coverage (Q3): ERC Advanced-type project 101001731 at Hebrew University of Jerusalem, PI Ronit Ricci, 2021-2026, studies interlinear translation across the Indonesian-Malay world (late 16th-20th c.). Its corpus emphasis is Islamic Arabic-Malay/Javanese interlinear texts (Maulid syaraf al-anam, kitab makna gandhul, surau texts), but it now includes Old Javanese-Balinese material through postdoc Keiko Kamiishi (Wacana 2025 article; August 2025 blog post on Balinese maarti; "Interlinear Translation of the Month" series). URLs: https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101001731 , https://textualmicrocosms.huji.ac.il/

8. Pasantian school context verified (Q4): sekaa pasantian (from santi, peace - a place to obtain tranquility) equals sekaa mabebasan, literary appreciation groups that perform (menembang) and interpret OJ kakawin for Panca Yadnya rites; the paneges renders meaning into Balinese orally. Creese 1999 documents mabasan continuity into the 20th century and credits Rubinstein 1993 for its modern development; contemporary reports note declining participation post-COVID ("Strengthening Ideological Values through Pasantian Singing", https://www.academia.edu/125225829/ ).

9. Related Javanese tradition (Q5): word-level interlinear rendering existed in Java too - BL Add MS 12279 (Pakualaman, 1814) pairs OJ Bharatayuddha with Modern Javanese word-for-word translation (Kamiishi, Wacana 26(3), 2025); Islamic-era makna gandhul hanging glosses provide the formal contrast; the older prose paraphrase stratum comprises the OJ parwa corpus (late 10th c., e.g., Wirataparwa dated 996) and Balinese paparikan prose summaries noted by Creese 1999; language mixing appears as Javano-Balinese idiom in Creese's genre scheme and as deliberate register management inside maarti translations (high sari vs low nyonyo etc.).

10. Digital projects (Q6): WikiLontar 2021/WikiPustaka on ban.wikisource (metadata in Wikidata, images in Commons, transliteration workflows; includes named Maarti items; article DOI 10.1080/13614568.2024.2345182); Balinese Digital Library on archive.org (Pusdok, ~3,000 lontar incl. Ariwangsa Maarti); British Library digitised Indonesian MSS dataset (DOI 10.23636/ghz6-vt54) with DHARMA Java-Bali palmleaf project; EFEO Java-Bali Palmleaf Digitisation Project (BL blog 2023, https://bl.iro.bl.uk/concern/generic_works/ca617738-a85e-4e27-89e6-a01fc4109a39 ); LIACS Leiden Lontar Project applying AI recognition/transliteration (https://lontar.liacs.nl/). NLB Singapore's role found so far concerns Bollinger-project Malay MSS digitization, not lontar maarti.
