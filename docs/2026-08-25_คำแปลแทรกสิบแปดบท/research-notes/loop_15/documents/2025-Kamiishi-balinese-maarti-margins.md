## Key passages (paraphrased closely from fetched text)

### Definition of maarti
"The term maarti derives from the Balinese word arti ('meaning') and refers to a format in which translation equivalents are placed directly above or below the source text, with dotted lines connecting corresponding segments."

Maarti manuscripts are typically written on lontar (dried palm leaves) and contain Old Javanese kakawin accompanied by Balinese translations.

### Layout and relation to interlinear translation
The layout allows source and target texts to correspond at word or phrase level, enabling readers to engage with both simultaneously. Maarti represents a uniquely Balinese form of interpretation providing access to the meaning and poetic sensibility of kakawin through Balinese.

"While maarti differs from the narrow definition of interlinear translation - where the translation is inserted between lines - it shares essential features such as the visual proximity to the source text and word- or phrase-level alignment." Its primary function is communicating the poetic and semantic content of the original directly in a localized language; maarti stays near its source "both visually and interpretively".

### Manuscript case study
- Kekawin Arjuna Wiwaha Maarti, shelfmark 007/BPB/Vb/91, Balai Bahasa Provinsi Bali (Penatih, Denpasar).
- 174 folios; Old Javanese Arjuna Wiwaha up to Canto 36 with Balinese translation in maarti format.
- Source poem: Arjunawiwaha, 11th century, Mpu Kanwa; Tilottama erotic episode in Cantos 31-32.

### Register shift as translation strategy (Balinese resource absent in Old Javanese)
- High-register euphemism: OJ payodhara rendered as sari ("essence/pollen/flower").
- Register drop for intimacy: OJ susu rendered as nyonyo (everyday low-register Balinese), staging the erotic approach step by step.
- Reframing: OJ smaragama ("the art of love") rendered smaran tantranne ("the doctrine of love"), recasting eros as ritual knowledge.
- OJ ketek-keteg ("signs of heightened emotion/desire") rendered raras (beauty, charm, emotional resonance).
- Closing claim: the maarti translator "interprets, reshapes, and recontextualizes the narrative."
