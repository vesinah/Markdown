## Key content from retrieved excerpts

- Object: British Library Add MS 12279, Bratayuda (Modern Javanese spelling of Bharatayuddha), copied at the Pakualaman court, Yogyakarta, 1814; a word-for-word, line-for-line Modern Javanese rendering alongside the Old Javanese of parts of the poem.
- Framework: uses "interlinear translation" defined as dividing a source text into smallest possible units, arranging translation words in source order; notes the Javanese religious-study convention makna gandhul ("hanging glosses") used for Quranic/kitab study, where the vernacular gloss hangs diagonally under Arabic lines - contrasted with Add MS 12279's side-by-side arrangement.
- Hypotheses for why interlinear form was chosen for a narrative text: (1) foundation text for later freer adaptations; (2) instrument for learning Old Javanese through word-level segmentation.
- Possible link to Raffles-era circles: interest in Balinese/Javanese self-immolation (bela) during British administration (Crawfurd) may have motivated commissioning the translation.
- Comparative references: Ricci 2023a on interlinear Maulid Syaraf al-Anam across the Indonesian-Malay world; Ricci 2023b keywords for Javanese Islamic interlinear texts; Ricklefs-Voorhoeve-Gallop 2014 catalogue; Robson 2016 Ghatotkacasraya edition.
- Discussion of localization strategies: adding honorifics and aksara murda conventions absent in Old Javanese; handling long/short vowels.
