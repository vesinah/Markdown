## Key content extracted

### Canon of Javanese kakawin known in Bali (Creese's table, pp. 48-49)
Ramayana (9th c.), Arjunawiwaha (11th c., mpu Kanwa), Hariwangsa (12th c., mpu Panuluh), Bharatayuddha (1157 AD, mpu Sedah/Panuluh), Ghatotkacasraya (12th c., mpu Panuluh), Smaradahana (13th c., mpu Dharmaja), Sumanasantaka (13th c., mpu Monaguna), Krsnayana (13th c., mpu Triguna), Bhomakawya (13th c.?), Nagarakrtagama (1365, mpu Prapanca), Arjunawijaya (14th c., mpu Tantular), Sutasoma (14th c., mpu Tantular), Kunjarakarna, Parthayajna, Siwaratrikalpa, Wrttasancaya, Dharmasunya, Dharmaputus, Nirarthaprakrta, Jinarthiprakrti, Nitisastra, Nitisara.

### On glosses and transmission (pp. 50-51)
- "Active interest in the Javanese classics is evident from the number of copies that have been made over the centuries, many provided with Balinese glosses, as well as from the frequent writing and rewriting of new versions of older works in the form of prose summaries (paparikan) or in new poetic forms such as geguritan and kidung."
- Friederich (mid-19th c., Badung): most revered texts were a handful of Javanese classics: Ramayana, Arjunawiwaha, Bharatayuddha, Hariwangsa, Smaradahana, Sumanasantaka, Bhomakawya, Arjunawijaya, Sutasoma.
- Ramayana, Arjunawiwaha, Bharatayuddha, Sumanasantaka "appear to have enjoyed great popularity"; excerpts integral to rites of passage (marriage, death) and wayang.

### Mabasan reading groups (pp. 51-52, 55)
- "The vocalization and interpretation of kakawin in reading groups (mabasan), particularly of the Javanese classics that are believed to encapsulate the nature of Balinese ethics, religion and philosophy, has also continued into the twentieth century." Footnote: "For a detailed account of the practice and development of mabasan in twentieth-century Bali, see Rubinstein 1993."
- "Although there appears to be no parallel interest in the study of Balinese kakawin [in mabasan groups], kakawin composition has continued well into the twentieth century."

### Collections
- Gedong Kirtya collection: "the first major Balinese collection, set up in 1928 in Singaraja ... started under Dutch control and sponsorship" (siglum K; also MKLVT = Mededeelingen Kirtya Liefrinck-van der Tuuk series).
- Inventory entries explicitly noting interlinear glosses, e.g.:
  - "Candrasangkalan ('Chronograms') - An explanation of chronograms with interlinear gloss." LOr 16.774 (= HKS 3630).
  - "Malawi Jayendriya - One section of the Malawi Kasturi ... with Balinese interlinear gloss. The two copies are from Puri Kawan and Puri Gobraja ..." LOr 21.358 (= HKS 5213).
- Entry "Pawatikan ('Dating')": contains authors' names and chronogram dates for most extant Javanese kakawin including Ramayana, Arjunawiwaha, Bharatayuddha, Bhomakawya, Parthayajna, Ghatotkacasraya, Lubdhaka, Krsnayana, Arjunawijaya, Sutasoma and Nusa Bali (LOr 21.708 = HKS 5561).
- Sigla used throughout: K (Gedong Kirtya), LOr (Leiden oriental MSS), HKS (ANU Hooykaas-Ketut Sangka transcriptions), LOJ (Pigeaud, Literature of Java), JBMG (Javanese and Balinese Manuscripts in Germany).
