# Harvest: De Simini - Śivadharma manuscripts from Nepal (codicology of Nepalese MTMs)

Full EPUB of the volume downloaded from OAPEN this loop; the Nepal chapter text extracted verbatim.
The chapter is the standard codicological study of NEPALESE MULTIPLE-TEXT MANUSCRIPTS (MTMs) - the
pothi format in which several works are copied in sequence within one pothi. It complements the
annotation-layout evidence of Formigatti 2015 with the layout rules for multi-text pothis.

## Corpus claim

More than sixty manuscripts of the Śivadharma corpus (śāstra, dharmottara, saṃgraha, Umāmaheśvara-
saṃvāda, Uttarottaramahāsaṃvāda etc.), ranging from early palm-leaf to recent paper copies; the
formation of the fixed eight/nine-work corpus is "an invention of Nepal", attested ONLY in Nepalese MTMs.

## Layout conventions documented verbatim (key passages)

1. Work separation by caesura: "the different works are separated by means of caesuras consisting of
   a blank space left at the end of each work and one blank page (typically the recto side of the
   following folio) separating it from the following work."
2. Continuous vs restarted foliation: some palm-leaf MTMs run the foliation uninterrupted through all
   works ("kernel" + possibly appended units); others restart numbering with every work - "a circumstance
   that could have facilitated the removal of a work from the manuscript in order to be read or copied".
3. Double foliation systems: "there is in fact a double system of foliation, one in the left margin
   (verso side) reporting the page number in relation to the whole manuscript, and another one in the
   right margin (verso side), counting the pages of each single work" - typical of later PAPER Śivadharma
   MTMs; also diagnostic for single-text mss that were EXTRACTED from an MTM (left-margin numbers
   continuing a parent series, e.g. 279-290, while right-margin numbers restart at 1).
4. Enriched units: "the presence of a second layer of annotations and corrections added in margine by
   a different hand makes it an enriched unit." (Direct Nepalese evidence for later marginal annotation
   layers on pothis.)
5. Page economy on palm leaf: five lines per page as the norm; deviations signal interventions (e.g. a
   page reduced to three-and-a-half lines after an end colophon, remainder left blank; four lines where
   four lines were erased/cut elsewhere - physical evidence of reorganization).
6. Dated example: colophon in the expired Nepala year named 'ether-planet-hand' = NS 290, month Pauṣa,
   bright fortnight 15th lunar day, king Rudradeva, scribe Rāma - i.e. late 12th c.; unit boundaries
   confirmed by colophons ("they were seen and produced as a single unit").
7. Method: distinction of codicological units via akṣara size/cluster shape, number of akṣaras per line,
   orthography, page layout, scribal habits; Gumbert-style stratigraphy.

## Interpretation for the book project

Gives the Thai book a precise description of how Newar scribes organized MULTI-WORK pothis (blank-page
caesuras, dual foliation margins), against which the marginal-commentary strategies of Formigatti 2015
(KĀ ms B, N1) can be framed: the same page architecture that separates works also hosts marginal
annotation layers added by later hands.
