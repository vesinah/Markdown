## Key findings (with URLs)

1. GAP 5 RESOLVED — "In the margins of the text" IS REAL, but its frame is NOT "FOR 963". Formigatti's dissertation states verbatim that the study "is the outcome of a research project sponsored by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft and carried out at the University of Hamburg during the period 2008-11, in the context of the research group Manuscript Cultures in Asia and Africa. The title of the project was 'In the margins of the text: annotated manuscripts from Northern India and Nepal.'" The archived group page confirms the project list (Laufzeit 2008-2011) with no grant number displayed. The numeral "963" appears nowhere in the dissertation PDF nor on the archived page; grep for "Forschergruppe"/"FOR" found nothing; GEPRIS could not be queried (JS-only; zero Wayback captures of margin-related GEPRIS searches). Correct citation form for the book: DFG-sponsored subproject "In the Margins of the Text" within the research group Manuskriptkulturen in Asien, Afrika und Europa (AAI Hamburg, 2008-2011), PI-context Harunaga Isaacson (dissertation supervisor); output = Formigatti 2015. The label "DFG FOR 963" must be dropped as unverified (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน). https://ediss.sub.uni-hamburg.de/handle/ediss/6463 ; http://web.archive.org/web/20191022211859/https://www.manuscript-cultures.uni-hamburg.de/archive/Projekte.html

2. GAP 1 CLOSED — THE dedicated study exists and is open access: Formigatti 2015 (272 pp.) analyzes exactly how commentarial text is placed on North Indian/Nepalese manuscripts. Core results for the book: (a) indigenous layout terms tripata (annotations above AND below the basic text) and pancapatha (on all four margins); (b) annotation typology classified on the five laksanas of running commentaries (padaccheda; anvayokti; samasadivecana = laukika/alaukikavigraha; padarthabodha = authorial gloss/quotation; tatparya = authorial explanation/quotation); (c) FORMAT ECONOMICS: palm-leaf dimensions are fixed by nature, so the Kavyadarsa scribe deliberately minimized his ruling unit to leave margin space for an entire anonymous running commentary written in the margins WITH PRATIKAS, linked by reference-sign-above-word + digit-at-annotation-end marking the line; paper pothis stay oblong by tradition, so Nepalese paper ms N1 shows the same layout as palm-leaf Kavyadarsa ms B; true dense INTERLINEAR glossing flourishes only in the Kashmirian codex format (IPV mss J, P, Be quoting IPVV/Raghupancika as teachers' mnemonic supports); (d) transmission: the IPV annotations behave as an independent exegetical apparatus copied together with the root text from manuscript to manuscript. https://ediss.sub.uni-hamburg.de/bitstream/ediss/6463/1/Dissertation.pdf

3. NEW NEPALESE ANNOTATED WITNESSES WITH SHELF MARKS AND REEL NUMBERS (from Formigatti 2015): RaghuV N1 = NAK Acc. 1/1272, inventory 43783, NGMPP reel A 391/15 (filming line also prints "A 39/15"), filmed 14-07-1972, 161 exposures; paper pothi, hypotactic composite of FOUR units (kernel unit 2 in NEWARI script), 153 folios, 5 lines/folio, ~44 aksaras/line, carrying an anonymous kathambhutini-style commentary entirely in margins AND between lines. RaghuV N5+N6+N7 were filmed by NGMPP as ONE reel (A 397/18) and catalogued as one manuscript though codicologically distinct objects — a caution against equating reel numbers with physical mss. The annotated palm-leaf Kavyadarsa ms = siglum B of Dimitrov 2002a/b (Marburg: Indica et Tibetica Verlag; Dimitrov 2002b = "Tables of the old bengali script (on the basis of a nepalese manuscript of dandin's Kavyadarsa)", pp. 27-78). https://ediss.sub.uni-hamburg.de/handle/ediss/6463

4. GAP 1 SUPPLEMENTED — multi-work pothi architecture (De Simini 2016): Nepalese MTMs separate works by blank-space caesura plus a full blank page; foliation either runs uninterrupted or restarts per work (the latter facilitating extraction); later paper Sivadharma MTMs use DOUBLE foliation systems (left verso margin counting the whole MS, right verso margin the individual work) which also betray works extracted from parents; some mss carry "a second layer of annotations and corrections added in margine by a different hand" ("enriched unit"). Together with finding 2 this yields a defensible tripartite typology of commentarial-text placement on Newari pothis: (i) separate tika-pothi; (ii) satika/root-commentary sequential copying in one production; (iii) marginal/interlinear annotation layers added during use. https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/25341

5. GAP 2 CLOSED — two concrete dated Ayurvedic witnesses with NGMPP documentation: (a) Suśrutasaṃhitā + nighantu compendium, NAK 5/333, reel B 29-19: palm-leaf, Newari script, COMPLETE, 435 folios, 6 lines/page, scribe Amarasiṃhaka son of Kamaladatta, dated Sunday 29 July 1465; dual letter/figure foliation restarting per sthana (kalpa-/uttara- prefixes); contains the Gandhakakalpa of Nagarjuna on a single folio; its catalogue transcription explicitly encodes an interlinear insertion ("[[bhavet]] is added UNDER bhavati and the latter is not deleted") — documentary proof of interlinear correction practice in a dated Nepalese medical pothi. (b) Carakasamhita (jvaracikitsita section), NAK 2/189, reel A 213-10: Devanagari paper pothi, 26 folios, 9 lines/folio, marginal title carak, filmed 1971, category "Digitized manuscripts". Plus the NGMCP-official note on the very old (9th c.) Buddhist-scribe Suśrutasaṃhitā in NAK opening "oṃ namaḥ sarvvajñāyānuttaravaidyarājāya" with significant variants from printed editions. http://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/ngmcp/B_29-19_Su%25C5%259Brutasa%25E1%25B9%2583hit%25C4%2581.html ; .../A_213-10_Carakasa%25E1%25B9%2583hit%25C4%2581.html ; https://www.aai.uni-hamburg.de/en/forschung/ngmcp/news/manuscript-of-the-week.html

BONUS — NGMCP catalogue rubric evidence for annotation conventions: the static wiki entries carry structured fields (Lines per Folio, Foliation positions per margin, Binding Hole, Remarks) whose values routinely encode annotation-relevant facts (marginal titles like carak; foliation prefixes kalpa-/uttara-; abbreviations in upper-left margins such as prajñapāra./abhisamayā.; explicit interlinear-insertion notes as in B 29-19). Combined with Hidas's Phase-A rubric "No marginal or interlinear corrections" and Formigatti 2022's transcription symbols for interlinear/marginal insertions, this documents that presence/absence and placement of annotations is a STANDARD descriptive feature of Nepalese manuscript cataloguing. http://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/ngmcp/index.html
