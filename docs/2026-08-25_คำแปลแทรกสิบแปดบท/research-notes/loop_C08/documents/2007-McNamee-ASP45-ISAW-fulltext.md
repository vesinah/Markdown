## THE SCALE FIGURE - verbatim from McNamee's Introduction, p. 2

"The present corpus consists of marginal and interlinear notes from 293 ancient manuscripts found certainly or possibly in Egypt and dating from the third century B.C.E. to the seventh century C.E.. They are listed along with their salient features in the Comprehensive List."

Composition breakdown (same page):
- "Two-thirds (201) of the manuscripts treated here are papyrus rolls or parts of rolls, 69 are papyrus codices, 19 are codices of parchment or vellum, and four are wooden tablets."

Denominator statement (same page):
- "What remains is roughly 5% of the 5,431 classical literary papyri listed in LDAB as of August 2005."
- Footnote 4: "Of published literary papyri, roughly 350 - perhaps 10% - contain variants or possible variants. A little more than 100 of these also contain marginal comments of some kind."

## Further quantified statements harvested from the Introduction/Part One (usable in drafts)

- Provenance: "Oxyrhynchus has produced about 160 so far" annotated texts; the Arsinoite nome and Antinoopolis yield about as many as each other; Hermopolis twelve (Introduction, ch. 2).
- For a substantial proportion of the total (sixty texts, or 21%), the provenance is unknown; about twenty come from villages, eight others have assorted provenances (upper Egypt, "the east", Dakhleh Oasis); about twenty are Roman-law works annotated mostly in Greek or Latin-Greek transliteration mixture.
- Layout/density: "Only one in four annotated rolls - about thirty-five or forty - exceeds these dimensions" (margins above 5 cm top/bottom, intercolumn over 4 cm); "Notes consisting of more than eight words altogether survive in only about forty rolls (Comprehensive List, 'Long Notes')".
- Supralinear/sublinear datum for the interlinear-layout argument: "About half the seventy-five notes written above or above [sic: above or below] the main text are three or more lines in length, and another dozen are two lines long." (i.e., only c. 75 supralinear/sublinear notes survive in the whole corpus.)
- Annotators: "Approximately one hundred literary texts have annotations by the first scribe (Comprehensive List, column labeled 'Notes by Text Hand')"; "Multiple annotators are at work in about thirty-five ancient annotated books. About a dozen, in fact, have notes in at least three different scripts."
- Format: lemma-introduced marginalia in "about thirty papyri (both rolls and codices)"; paragraphi/sigla accompany notes "in about two dozen texts"; ecthesis/eisthesis "found in eight papyri".
- Content classes: "About one third of surviving annotated texts are practical manuals" (commentaries on classical authors, medical treatises, Roman law); at least twenty-seven texts carry attributions to scholarly authorities.

## Framing quotes usable for the scholia-linkage argument

- "There are tantalizingly frequent verbatim links between marginalia and scholia ... Because ancient notes antedate the scholia by several centuries, the linkages provide evidence for a strong, continuous tradition of classical scholarship." (Introduction)
- Terminology rule: "'Scholia' is a term reserved here for the often compendious commentaries found in the margins of manuscripts of the eighth or ninth century onward. A work of literary exegesis that circulated independently in antiquity I call either 'commentary' or 'hypomnema'."
- "The collection presented here should make textual analysis more convenient, for it brings together, for the first time, all the annotations in secular books from Graeco-Roman Egypt from earlier centuries."
