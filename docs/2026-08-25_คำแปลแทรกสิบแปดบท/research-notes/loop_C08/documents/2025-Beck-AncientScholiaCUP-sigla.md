## Class scheme from the open Introduction excerpt (verbatim fragments)

- "The most straightforward way of categorizing scholia is to differentiate them according to the manuscripts in which they are preserved. Thus, A-scholia are those transmitted by the manuscript known as Venetus A (A in Erbse's sigla), T-scholia are those transmitted by the Townleyanus manuscript (T in Erbse's sigla), b-scholia are those transmitted by the manuscripts of the b-family, and so forth."
- Section 2 "Classes and Sources": 2.1 "The VMK-scholia ... whose most important witness is the manuscript Venetus A, derive from a lost Late Antique compilation commentary known as the Viermaennerkommentar (= VMK), so called because it compiled the works of four scholars, Aristonicus, Didymus, Nicanor, and Herodian, all active between the first century BCE and the second century CE."
- 2.2 "The Exegetical Scholia ... whose best witnesses are the manuscripts of the bT-family, derive from the lost archetype c ... On the basis of the use of allos ('differently') in the scholia transmitted in the manuscript T, Hartmut Erbse argued that the compiler of c must have drawn on three exegetical commentaries" (footnotes cite Erbse 1953: 9-11; Erbse 1960: 170; Erbse 1969-88 i lii; van der Valk 1963-64 i 434; Schmidt 2002: 170-73; Schmidt 2011: 121-58; Schironi 2020: 157).
- Venetus A subscriptions identify the authorities per book (end of Book 2 subscription quoted: Aristonicus' Signs, Didymus' On the Aristarchean Recension, Herodian's Iliadic Prosody, Nicanor's On Punctuation; cf. Pagani 2014).

## Erbse's own edition scheme as summarized by contemporaneous review (Wathelet, L'Antiquite Classique 42 [1973] 201-203, Persée full text)

- Prolegomena of vol. 1 contains six chapters. Ch. 1 defines the classes: minor scholia ("dites de Didyme") vs major scholia (his edition's object); the latter divide into (a) the Aristarchean tradition condensed in the "resume des quatre" (VMK) carried chiefly by ms. A, and (b) the exegetical scholia of uncertain origin, not attributable to Crates/Pergamon but composed from 1st-c.-BCE commentaries. Papyrus scholion fragments attach directly to neither group.
- Chs. 2-3: minute description of the scholia manuscripts and fourteen papyri (thirteen in Pack2); Venetus A judged slightly later than Arethas; common source of A, the Etymologicum Magnum and Eustathius held to be the commentary of Apion and Herodorus (or Heliodorus) in minuscule exemplar (Erbse contra van der Valk); for the exegetical scholia: T (with its dependent Ge = Genavensis) on one side and archetype b of C, E3(?), B(?), E4 on the other descend from a common source c; c and b antedate the Apion-Herodorus recueil and influenced A; the h-scholia divide into two families and depend on the Apion-Herodorus recueil, useful where A is missing or lacunose; special value of the Genavensis at Iliad Phi 165-499 with identical explanations in P.Oxy. 221.
- Ch. 6 (edition principles): Erbse restores the text of two archetypes (Apion-Herodorus commentary; and c, ancestor of b and T); of the D-scholia in A he gives only first and last words, while those of c are edited in full; testimonia below the text chiefly from Eustathius, Etymologicum Genuinum, Suda and h-family.
- Review URL: https://www.persee.fr/doc/antiq_0770-2817_1973_num_42_1_1704_t1_0201_0000_1

## Corroboration from the new-edition project (Montana 2020, Lectio, abstract)

Fausto Montana, "Editing Anonymous Voices: The scholia uetera to the Iliad", DOI 10.1484/m.lectio-eb.5.118725: Erbse restricted his edition to the "scholia grammatica or maiora" represented by two classes (VMK-class with fundamental witness A; scholia exegetica with five witnesses collectively bT); he included D-material only selectively ("named c in Erbse's stemma" for the D-glosses present in the bT archetype) and excluded the h-family almost entirely; the new Genoa edition will host VMK, exegetica, D-family and h-only annotations. Specimen PDF (Muratore, unige.iris.cineca.it) exists but was JS-challenge-blocked this session; its indexed text confirms the same class description.

## Reception datum

David Sider in The Classical Journal (blurb on CUP page): "Beck's book can ... be considered an improvement over Erbse ... anyone interested in the Iliad scholia should consult Beck first; then proceed to the Greek in Erbse, van Thiel, or Beck's other sources, as necessary."
