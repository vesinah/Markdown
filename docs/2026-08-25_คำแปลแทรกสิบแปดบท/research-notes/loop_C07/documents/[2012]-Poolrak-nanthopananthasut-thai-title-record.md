English (verbatim): This thesis aims to analyse Nanthopananthasut Khamluang, a translated work of Prince Dharmadhipesra, in terms of the translation techniques and the factors influential in the translation strategies by applying Katharina Reiss's theory of translation as the frame of study. It is found that Nanthopananthasut Khamluang was translated from a Pali literature entitled Nandopanandavatthu, which was probably written by a senior monk named Bhuddhasiri during the Ayutthaya period. [...] Four factors responsible for these translation strategies include the Thai literary convention, the Thai Buddhist literary convention, the Pali literary convention and the convention of Pali literal translation (Plae Doi Phayanchana).

## Why this matters for the book

1. The fourth factor names the glossing register directly: ขนบการแปลโดยพยัญชนะ (convention of literal Pali rendering, plae doi phayanchana). The thesis thus treats kham luang composition as operating inside the same letter-by-letter Pali-rendering tradition whose manuscript-side practice (nissaya/vohara/namasadda) is the book's subject.
2. Source text fixed: the Pali Nandopanandavatthu attributed to the Ayutthaya-era elder Buddhasiri (พุทฺธสิริ) — dovetails with Sripum & Walker 2026 (Manuscript Studies 11.1), which studies this same Pali-Siamese kham luang among their three bitexts.
3. Manuscript witness named in the Jurairat/Yanyong article bibliography (see companion document): Samut thai khao, Thai and Khom script, Thai and Pali languages, khong lines, BE 2275, National Library no. 120 — the celebrated Ayutthaya original.
