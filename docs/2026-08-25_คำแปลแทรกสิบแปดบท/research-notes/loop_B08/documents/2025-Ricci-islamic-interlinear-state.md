## Key content

- Historiography of interlinear-text scholarship: earliest scholarly mentions traced to Van der Tuuk, BKI 13.1 (1866): 466-467 on Arabic-Malay/Javanese interlinear copies of al-Samarkandi's Bayan aqidah al-usul ("numerous" in west Java).
- Juynboll 1881 (BKI 29:215-227): "Een Moslimsche catechismus in het Arabisch met eene Javaansche interlineare vertaling in pegonschrift" - Dutch translation replaced the Javanese gloss, ignoring its pedagogical/cultural role.
- Van Ronkel identified as the colonial-era exception who studied translational practice itself: 1896 Cambridge manuscripts article (BKI 46:1-53); 1899 "Over de invloed der Arabische syntaxis op de Maleische" (Tijdschrift voor Indische Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde 41:498-528; Indonesian trans. Bhratara 1977). Snouck Hurgronje also attentive.
- Ricci's diagnosis: colonial bias favored Hindu-Buddhist antiquity and Sanskritic Old Javanese poetry; Islamic interlinear translation treated as a mere technical device, not as pedagogy/culture.
- Forthcoming anchor publication: Ricci, R., "Interlinear Texts from Indonesia: Preliminary Thoughts on their Study", Journal of Arabic and Islamic Studies (forthcoming 2025).
- Earliest extant regional interlinear witnesses cited: al-Attas 1988 (16th-c. Malay Nasafi); Drewes 1955 (Burda); Arps & Gallop 1991 Golden Letters - incl. Masail al-talim, Arabic with Javanese translation and notes, 1623, British Library Sloane 2645.
