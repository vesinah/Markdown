## Key passages (closely paraphrased/quoted from fetched text)

Definition of pepaosan:
- "In contemporary Bali, the tradition of pepaosan - a bilingual performance of sacred texts - has found a new home online. Traditionally performed in temple ceremonies, peposan [sic] now appears on YouTube, where performers chant Sanskrit verses then render them into Indonesian or Balinese, line by line."
- "This 'oral interlinear translation' combines melodic structure with textual interpretation, allowing performers to engage with scripture in ways that are both artistic and didactic."

Case study:
- Channel "Widhi Sastra" chanting Bhagavad Gita verses in a macapat meter with interlinear Indonesian explanation.
- Comment-thread negotiation of sectarian identity (ISKCON/Hare Krishna vs PHDI-recognized Hinduism); performer reframes singing as hobby and karma-generating act; equal merit of listening and chanting ("spreading bhakti through YouTube"); a provocative "Bacaan Al-Qur'an saja" comment defused by the performer.
- Notes the performer sings in macapat meters "which is unusual and puts him in a contrasting position to the state's authoritative pepaosan" - evidence of an official, institutionalized pepaosan sphere in Bali.
- Closing analogy: "the layering of voices in the comments mirrors the interlinear nature of pepaosan itself, where meaning emerges... from the dynamic interplay between recited text and translated response."
- Continuity claim: audiences of public manuscript readings gained merit by listening - old tradition, new medium.
