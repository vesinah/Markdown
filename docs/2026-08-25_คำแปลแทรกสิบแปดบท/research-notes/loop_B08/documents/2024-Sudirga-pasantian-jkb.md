## Significance for the pasantian terminology question

- Establishes "pasantian" as the term in current Indonesian-language scholarship for village singing/glossing assemblies (pasantian singing), alongside the older sekaa pasantian / sekaa mabebasan usage documented from manuscript-side sources by Widiantana 2024 (Manuskripta 14(2), harvested in full in Loop 15, which derives the term from santi, tranquility, and describes the peneges oral interpreter).
- A ResearchGate-indexed Indonesian study also attests institutional usage: "Pembinaan Pupuh Macepat Surki pada Sekaa Pasantian Swasti Marga Brata di Desa Selisihan Klungkung" (RG publication id 366332927; original venue not resolved this loop) - title-level evidence only.

## Notes for the book chapter

- Terminology synthesis supported by this loop's evidence:
  - maarti = written interlinear-equivalent format on lontar (Kamiishi 2025; Pusdok catalog cards).
  - mabasan = English-scholarship label for kakawin reading groups (Creese 1999, citing Rubinstein 1993).
  - pepaosan = Balinese/performance-studies label for the bilingual line-by-line oral rendering (Rubinstein 1993 title; Ganchrow 2025 definition).
  - pasantian = village literary school / sekaa context of such singing (Widiantana 2024; Sudirga-Hood-Yulianti 2024).
  - pamurtian remains unverified anywhere in accessible scholarship - do not use without field lexicography (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน).
