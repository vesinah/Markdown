## Fetched content (site description)

"The Latin grammarian Priscian wrote his magisterial Ars grammatica at Constantinople around AD 527. His work is the most exhaustive compendium of information on Latin (and Greek) language and literature to survive from the ancient world."

"The Stiftsbibliothek in St Gall in Switzerland contains a manuscript, numbered 904, that is a copy of Priscian written by Irish scribes in AD 850-1, probably in Ireland. It was brought to the Continent between 855 and 863, reaching St Gall sometime after 888."

"The manuscript also contains a commentary of over 9,400 interlinear and marginal glosses, as well as c. 3,000 symbol glosses. About one-third of the verbal glosses are written in Old Irish, providing one of our earliest sources for the Irish language."

"This digital edition provides access to the text of the glosses alongside that of Priscian, and to additional resources including links to manuscript images and linguistic information on the Old Irish glosses."

"The text of the glosses was originally transcribed by Rijcklof Hofman in the early 1990s. Pádraic Moran created the digital edition in 2010 and continues to maintain this site. Bernhard Bauer supplied translations and linguistic information for the Old Irish glosses in 2017."

Citation printed on site: Rijcklof Hofman, Pádraic Moran, Bernhard Bauer, *St Gall Priscian Glosses*, version 2.1 (2023) http://www.stgallpriscian.ie/

Features observed:
- Browse by Priscian book (Praefatio + books 1-17; manuscript breaks off in book 17, book 18 missing), by Hertz's edition (Grammatici Latini II-III) page, or by manuscript page.
- Full-text search across glosses/lemmata and Priscian text.
- Detailed typology of gloss types searchable by category: prosody marks; lexical glosses (Old Irish translations, synonyms, definitions); morphological/grammatical glosses (case, number, gender, pronouns, verb forms); syntactical glosses with symbols vs words; explanatory glosses (figures of speech, summaries, cross-references, etymological, encyclopedic, source glosses, variant readings, socio-historical notes).
