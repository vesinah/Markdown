## Fetched content (summary figures as displayed)

"Gloss Corpus is a platform for publishing and analysis of glosses on medieval manuscripts, based on Open Access and open standards for data. Contributors receive full credit for their work, with each edition of glosses assigned a unique DOI and persistent URL. Contributors also have the option to avail of peer-review."

Current holdings (v1.0, 2024): 66 collections of glosses on 4 primary texts, comprising 24,872 glosses total.

Collections:
- Isidore of Seville, Etymologiae book 1 — 54 gloss collections, 4,279 glosses (main editor E. Stein; manuscripts incl. BL Arundel 129; Paris BnF lat. 7585, 7588, 7670, 7671; Leiden Voss. Lat. O 41 = 682 glosses; Orleans BM 296 = 768 glosses; Reims BM 426 = 345 glosses; etc.)

## Network context (fetched from https://www.glossing.org/)

"The purpose of this network is to promote more and better collaboration between specialists in the field... Our central focus is on manuscripts of the medieval period (c. 5th to 15th centuries), and embraces not only glossing as narrowly defined but also closely related genres such as collected glosses (glossae collectae), glossaries, and commentaries."

"The Network for the Study of Glossing was founded in December 2015 by Alderik Blom (Marburg), Franck Cinato (Paris), Pádraic Moran (Galway), Andreas Nievergelt (Zürich), Mariken Teeuwen (Amsterdam), and Matthew Zisk (Tohoku)."

Register at time of fetch: 146 members from 23 countries.

## Notes for the book chapter

- Demonstrates the current infrastructure layer of gloss research: OA publication with per-collection DOIs, structured search/analytics planned, cross-linguistic scope (Latin, Old Irish, Old High German, Greek, Hebrew, Arabic, East Asian).
- Useful counterpoint to Cerquiglini-era polemic: variance is now systematically captured digitally rather than flattened into a single reconstructed text.
