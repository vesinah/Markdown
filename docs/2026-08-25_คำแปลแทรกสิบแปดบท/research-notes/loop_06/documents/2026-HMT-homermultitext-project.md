## Fetched content (structure of the site)

Downloadable texts:
- "HMT editions of texts of the Iliad and scholia of the Venetus A manuscript" (./texts.html)

Facsimile editions:
- Digital edition of the Venetus A manuscript browsable by Iliad line reference (https://www.homermultitext.org/facsimile/)

Archive exploration tools:
- alpha-search (alphabetic search of Greek texts in the HMT archive, filterable by manuscript/text)
- iliad-browser (browse photographed manuscripts by Iliad reference)
- codex-browser (visual browser of photographed and documented codices)
- lightbox (paged browsing of image collections)

Publication of archival data:
- Editing of Iliad manuscripts with scholia managed in public GitHub repository hmt-archive
- Current published release always available as plain-text file in Cite EXchange format (CEX 3.0.2): https://github.com/homermultitext/hmt-archive/raw/master/releases-cex/hmt-current.cex
- Authority lists (authlists)
- Digital image collections overview; Image Citation Tool for citing specific image regions

## Notes for the book chapter

- The Homer Multitext is the canonical large-scale answer to the question of what a post-Lachmannian, multitextual edition of an ancient scholiastic tradition looks like: facsimile + transcriptions of the Iliad AND its Alexandrian-derived scholia (esp. Venetus A = Marcianus graecus 454) as linked open data, rather than a single reconstructed text.
- Its data model (CEX plain-text serialization, citable image regions) exemplifies the "open data over monolithic PDF edition" turn in classical scholarship.
- Connects directly to Loop topic: modern rediscovery/use of Alexandrian scholarship (scholia) via Wolf's legacy, now digitally instantiated.
