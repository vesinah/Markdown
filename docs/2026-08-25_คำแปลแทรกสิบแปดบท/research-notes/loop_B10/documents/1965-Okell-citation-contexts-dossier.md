## Verified characterization 1 - Herngseng 2023 summarizing Okell

From Ven. Pannabhoga Herngseng, "Burmese Nissaya Literature: A Mainstream Monastic Education Learning Method and its Salient Features," Manusya 26.1 (2023) (full OA text harvested loop_17):

"According to John Okell (1965, 186-227) nissaya is a linguistic analysis with special emphasis on Pali grammar features in the Burmese language; since its early development in the late Pagan period Burmese has been heavily influenced by Pali grammatical syntax."

Same article groups Okell with Pruitt (1992), McDaniel (2008), Lammerts (2010), Blackburn (2010), Walker (2020) as having noted that nissaya literature has been important in monastic education since at least the 15th century.

## Verified characterization 2 - Semantic Scholar citation context (Mathias Jenny)

From M. Jenny, "Modality in Burmese: 'May' or 'Must' - Grammatical Uses of ya? 'Get'" (2009), citing context retrieved via Semantic Scholar citations API (fields=contexts):

"...causing the event described by V: Y CAUSE X V = X (DO/MAY/MUST) V or (in Burmese) Y X-obj V-se = X V-ya? The implicit causing event can be foregrounded and expressed in FB [Formal Burmese] by the postverbal auxiliary se 'let, make so. do sth.', a standard translation for Pali causatives (s. Okell 1965:203)."

Value: shows Okell's page-level analysis of grammatical particles that serve as standard renderings of Pali forms - consistent with his title's claim of systematic adaptation.

## Verified characterization 3 - Candier citing Okell 1967 (the JBRS reprint)

From Aurore Candier, "A Norm of Burmese Kingship? The Concept of Raza-dhamma through Five Konbaung Period Texts" (citation context via Semantic Scholar):

"a style of composition that is close to nissaya, Monywe hsara-taw translates Pali phrases into Burmese and then interprets them (Okell 1967; McDaniel 2003; Pruitt 1994)."

Value: confirms the 1967 JBRS reprint carries the nissaya-style analysis and is citable for Konbaung-period prose close to nissaya composition (Monywe Zetawun Sayadaw's chronicle work).

## Verified characterization 4 - Walker 2020 fn 2

"William Pruitt, building on earlier work by Tin Lwin and John Okell, meticulously demonstrates the extraordinary range of grammatical particles and abbreviation systems employed in a single type of Pali-Burmese bitext."

Value: positions Okell 1965 as foundational for the particle/abbreviation-system analysis later systematized by Pruitt 1994.
