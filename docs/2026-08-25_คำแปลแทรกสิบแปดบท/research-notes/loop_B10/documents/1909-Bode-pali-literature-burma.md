## Appendix to Chapter III: An Inscription of A.D. 1442 (the Taungdwin governor's library)

Verbatim core (OCR normalized):

"The inscription mentioned on p. 50 is among those collected by Forchhammer at Pagan. It is dated b.e. 804 (1442 a.d.), and commemorates the bestowal of various gifts on the Order by the Governor of Taungdwin and his wife. Together with a monastery, garden, paddy-lands, and slaves, the pious donors offered a collection of texts, of which a list is given."

"We notice here a number of titles of Sanskrit works, sometimes greatly disguised in the Burmese transcription, but still recognizable. These will aid us to form some notion of the point reached by the Sanskrit scholars in Burma in the fifteenth century."

Bode's source chain (her footnote):
1. "Inscriptions of Pagan, Pinya, and Ava. Deciphered from the ink impressions found among Forchhammer's papers. Printed at Rangoon, 1902. Translated with notes by Tun Nyein, Government Printing Press, Rangoon, 1899."
2. "The inscription containing the list is mentioned by M. Pelliot in article 'Deux itineraires', BEFEO, vol. v, p. 183."
3. References to Forchhammer's List indicate that MS copies of the works are in the Bernard Free Library at Rangoon.
4. Bode adds: "I do not see any reason to doubt that the gift recorded in the Pagan inscription was a collection really containing the works mentioned and not their titles only."
5. Forchhammer scepticism caveat: Forchhammer's discovery that the "Vedas" found in Burmese/Talaing/Siamese versions contain no trace of Vedic texts made him sceptical about palm-leaf MSS bearing famous titles (e.g. the Susruta); Bode thinks he spoke of later-date MSS.

## Other nissaya data points from Bode (chapter text)

1. Seventeenth century: "The Sagaing monasteries also produced a number of Burmese nissayas (interpretations or paraphrases) on Abhidhamma texts during the seventeenth century."
2. Abhidhammattha-sangaha popularity: "Probably no Pali work on the Abhidhamma has been more often translated and paraphrased than the Abhidhammattha-sangaha, of which the Pitakatthamain alone mentions twenty-three different Burmese nissayas."
3. Terminology gloss: footnote on Ariyavamsa's attributed work "Mahanissara": "I am not sure that this word should not be Mahanissaya (chief commentary or gloss in Burmese)". Ariyavamsa (15th c., Pagan-born, active Ava/Sagaing) also composed a Burmese-language atthayojana (interpretation) of the Anutika on the Abhidhamma - "the first name connected with a metaphysical work in the vernacular".
4. Colonial-era continuity: the Alankaranissaya of the Yaw-mya-sa Atwin-wun (written 1880), "an edition of Sangharakkhita's Subodhalamkara, with a commentary"; Chandovinanjari (1897) "followed by a nissaya in Burmese"; Ledi Hsayadaw's Niruttidipani "(a Pali grammar, and afterwards a Burmese nissaya on the same work)".
5. Printed nissaya editions listed in Bode's bibliography section: "Nvadi-Moggallana. Pali treatise on Grammar, by Sangharakkhita... with Burmese nissaya. Rangoon, 1900."; "Alahkara-nissaya. Being the Subodhalamkara of Sangharakkhita in Pali, with commentary. Rangoon, 1889."
6. Kaccayanasara currency: "it was known not only in Pegu but in Upper Burma by the middle of the fifteenth century, as we know from the fact that a copy was presented to a monastery at Pagan in 1442."

## Notes on spelling

Bode spells the place-name TAUNGDWIN. Herngseng 2023 uses "Thaungdwing". Same locality, different romanization; use "Taungdwin" when citing Bode 1909 directly.

## Caveats

- OCR quality moderate; diacritics garbled throughout; quotes above lightly normalized for spacing only.
- Bode gives the list as copied from the inscription via Tun Nyein's translation; she numbers items 1-295+ with notes. The list mixes Vinaya, Abhidhamma, Sutta, vamsa, grammar, lexicon, medicine, logic, polity - i.e. a whole monastery curriculum plus secular sciences.
