## The nissaya passage (verbatim from OCR, lightly normalized)

Editorial footnote on the text of the inscription:

"Sanskrit. What follows, as far as the middle of A 3, is in Pali (cf. VI 6-9). Then comes a nissaya of Pali words paraphrased in Mon."

and:

"'The blessed Sakya[muni],' or Sakya sage. These words begin the nissaya..."

Context lines around it include the invocation "Honour to Buddha! Honour to the Law! Honour to the Church!" and the royal titulature of "King Sri Tribhuwanadityadhammaraja" (= Kyanzittha's regnal title). The inscription is a four-faced pillar text (line references A/B/F/D/H follow its faces), with Pali passages followed by a running Mon paraphrase - i.e., precisely a bitextual arrangement.

## Cross-reference

- Trent Walker, JAOS 140.3 (2020): 678, fn 13 cites exactly this evidence for his claim that "the earliest surviving mainland Southeast Asian example is a Pali-Mon inscription from the late eleventh or early twelfth century": "C. O. Blagden, Epigraphia Birmanica: Being Lithic and Other Inscriptions of Burma, vol. 1, pt. 2, ed. Charles Duroiselle (Rangoon: Superintendent, Government Printing and Stationery, Union of Burma, 1960 [repr.]), 93." (Verified against the harvested PDF of Walker 2020.)

## Negative result recorded

The same EB full text (1919-1934 coverage) contains ZERO occurrences of Thaungdwin/Taungdwin(g) - as expected, because the 1442 inscription is Ava-period material published separately (see Tun Nyein, Inscriptions of Pagan, Pinya and Ava, Rangoon 1899; Bode 1909 Appendix III). The term "nissaya" occurs only twice in EB, both times in the Shwezigon passage quoted above.
