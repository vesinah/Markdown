## Verified content (from readable opening)

- Opens with George Coedès writing in 1912: "Les bonzes du Cambodge ont en général assez de soin de leurs manuscrits, surtout de leurs manuscrits pâlis: la disparition des textes intéressants n'est donc pas à craindre," followed by his recommendation that important texts be brought to libraries and made available to Pali philologists, describing the sattras as "un capital improductif" obtainable "par d'habiles et patientes négociations."
- Filliozat notes several Phnom Penh monks had already given Coedès Pali manuscripts deposited at the EFEO library (identities of those manuscripts unknown).
- By 1912 the EFEO library in Hanoi already held a small fund of Pali manuscripts in Burmese and Mon scripts assembled by Charles Duroiselle and Edouard Huber on missions, alongside a collection of Pali manuscripts from Cambodia; archival letters (Duroiselle to Finot/Maitre, Rangoon 1904–1905) document systematic copying for the École, including work at Pagan.
- Value for the chapter: establishes that early-20th-century Cambodian monastic Pali culture was intact and legible to European philologists, and that its manuscript corpus overlaps Burmese and Mon script traditions — context for arguing Khmer glossing practice (prè lot prayog, per Walker's terminology) participates in a shared mainland-SEA bitextual technology.
