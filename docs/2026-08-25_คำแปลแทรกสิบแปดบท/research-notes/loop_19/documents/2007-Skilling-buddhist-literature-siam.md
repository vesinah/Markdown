## Verified content (from readable opening)

- Guiding question: what is the oldest surviving Buddhist commentary from Southeast Asia? Answer proposed: the Durbodhāloka ("Illumination of [points] difficult to understand"), composed by Dharmakīrti of Suvarṇadvīpa at the beginning of the eleventh century. Written originally in Sanskrit (full title: Abhisamayālaṅkāra-nāma-prajñāpāramitopadeśaśāstra-vṛtti-durbodhāloka-nāma-ṭīkā), it survives only in a Tibetan translation in the Tanjur, rendered by Atiśa (c. 982–1054), who studied twelve years with Dharmakīrti in Suvarṇadvīpa (about 1012–1024).
- Implication used by the chapter: Siam's Buddhist textual culture was plugged into pan-Buddhist circuits (India-Suvarṇadvīpa-Tibet) centuries before the earliest surviving Siamese Pali-vernacular bitexts (1482 onward), so the Lanka-lineage/Sukhothai-Ayutthaya narrative of canon transmission sits atop an older intertextual geography.
- Author identity note: this Dharmakīrti of Suvarṇadvīpa is distinct from the north Indian logician and from the Pali Dhammakittis (Skilling fn 1).
