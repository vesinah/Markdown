## Content

### From stgallpriscian.ie/glosses
- The St Gall Priscian (Stiftsbibliothek, MS 904) contains the Ars grammatica (also known as Institutiones grammaticae) by Priscian, a Latin grammarian based at Constantinople in the sixth century AD; treatise completed c. 526/7; edited by Martin Hertz in Keil, Grammatici Latini (Leipzig 1855-80), vols 2-3.
- The manuscript was written in AD 850-1, probably in Ireland, and contains over 9,400 interlinear and marginal glosses, in addition to c. 4,000 construe marks (symbols to aid reading).
- More than one-third of the glosses are written in Old Irish and constitute one of the most important corpora for that phase of the Irish language.
- Old Irish glosses published in Stokes & Strachan, Thesaurus Palaeohibernicus (2 vols, London 1901-1910), vol. 2, pp. 49-224.
- Rijcklof Hofman published roughly the first half of all glosses (Irish, Latin and symbols) in The Sankt Gall Priscian Commentary. Part 1 (2 vols, Munster 1996).
- The digital edition presents the first complete transcription of all glosses, juxtaposed with Priscian's text, with links to manuscript images.
- Citation form: Hofman, Moran, Bauer, St Gall Priscian Glosses v2.1 (2023).

### From e-codices (Cod. Sang. 904 record)
- Shelfmark: St. Gallen, Stiftsbibliothek, Cod. Sang. 904; title "Prisciani grammatica".
- Parchment; II + 120 pp.; 39 x 28.5 cm; origin given as Ireland (Bangor? Nendrum?); dated 851.
- Languages: Old Irish and Latin.
- Summary: copy of Priscian's Institutiones Grammaticae with over 9000 glosses, among them 3478 in Old Irish; basis for the reconstruction of the Old Irish language; numerous elaborate pen initials; written in an Irish scriptorium (Bangor?, Nendrum?) around 845 (per summary note).
- Standard description: Hofman Rijcklof, The Sankt Gall Priscian Commentary. Part 1. Volume 1: Introduction; Book 1-5, Munster 1996, pp. 12-31.
- Additional description: Scherrer Gustav, Verzeichniss der Handschriften der Stiftsbibliothek von St. Gallen, Halle 1875, pp. 319-320.
