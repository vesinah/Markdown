## Content (full text, condensed notes preserving substance)

Abstract: Reviews a semi-diplomatic edition of the oldest gloss commentary on Martianus Capella's De nuptiis Philologiae et Mercurii, a popular fifth-century encyclopaedic allegory of the seven liberal arts. Based on and including facsimiles of the ninth-century manuscript Leiden, Universiteitsbibliotheek, Vossianus Latinus Folio 48; produced collaboratively by various specialists.

Einleitung:
- The website offers a digital semi-diplomatic edition of the ninth-century manuscript Leiden VLF 48, one of the four most important witnesses for the extensive commentaries on De nuptiis that began with the Carolingian educational reforms. Later commentators include John Scottus Eriugena and Remigius of Auxerre.
- The commentary is transmitted in sixteen manuscripts, twelve of them ninth-century; a partial print edition based on the incomplete Paris, BnF lat. 12960 was published by Cora Lutz in 1944.
- Division of editorial labour under Mariken Teeuwen: Books I-II S. O'Sullivan (Queen's University Belfast); Book III (grammar) M.J. Teeuwen with Iris Savelkouls and A.P. Orban; Book IV (dialectic) M. Garrison and M.J. Teeuwen, transcript; Thomas Brouwer correction/emendation; Book V (rhetoric) Garrison/Teeuwen, corrected by Aleksander Sroczynski; Book VI (geometry) N. Lozovsky; Book VII (astronomy) B.S. Eastwood; Book VIII (arithmetic) Teeuwen/Guillaumin; Book IX (music) Teeuwen.
- Published on the eLaborate platform developed by the Huygens Institute (The Hague).
- KEY RESULT: according to the editors, the earliest gloss commentaries cannot be attributed to a single author (as earlier scholarship attempted) but arose between 820 and 840 through lively exchange among discussing and commenting scholars at Carolingian monasteries and courts. Hence the edition follows the reading of a particular manuscript rather than a reconstructed authorial text.
- Project funded by NWO May 2003 - May 2007; hosted at Huygens Institute KNAW in cooperation with Utrecht University. Online without access restrictions since November 2008; presented as work in progress; last update noted August 2011.
- Teeuwen's detailed manuscript description attributes a "great personality" to VLF 48 because it carries many annotations including some by the hand of Bishop Ratherius of Verona (10th century).

Inhalte und Methoden:
- Black-and-white facsimiles of moderate quality; high-resolution colour photos exist at Leiden's portal but were not integrated.
- Because no two witnesses carry the same corpus of glosses (fluid, complex material), editors chose to present text and glosses as transmitted in VLF 48, fol. 2r-91v; the semi-diplomatic transcription was checked against three further manuscript witnesses and against the printed editions of the "Dunchad" commentary (= Lutz's edition), John Scottus, and Remigius of Auxerre. No continuous critical apparatus of variants across manuscripts; editor remarks appear inline in curly braces.
- Text organized by book and manuscript page; main text carries the standard paragraph numbering (par. 1-1000). Transcription line-faithful; abbreviations silently expanded, modern punctuation/capitalization.
- Four panels: navigation, facsimile (zoom), transcription of De nuptiis, transcription of annotations; cross-referencing by numbering with mouse-over pop-ups; fuzzy search tolerant of orthographic variation.

Fazit:
- Places the edition alongside other digital gloss editions: Die Glossen Ekkeharts IV. im Codex Sangallensis 621 (Eisenhut/Banziger, orosius.monumenta.ch); St Gall Priscian glosses (transcribed by Rijcklof Hofman, edited digitally by Padraic Moran); Die Glossen zu Martianus Capella im Codex 193 der Kolner Dombibliothek (Isepy/Posselt 2010, Munich LMU digital edition + Libelli Rhenani XV print).
- Notes irony: the follow-up critical treatment of the whole tradition appeared as two PRINT volumes of Corpus Christianorum: vol. 1 on books I-II already published (O'Sullivan 2010 = CCCM 237); vol. 2 with semi-diplomatic edition of books 3-9 announced as in planning (status 2014).
- Digital edition retains untapped potential for critical enrichment (further text layers, source references, integration of linked digitized manuscripts such as Besancon BM 594, Leiden BPL 88, Koln Dom cod. 193).
