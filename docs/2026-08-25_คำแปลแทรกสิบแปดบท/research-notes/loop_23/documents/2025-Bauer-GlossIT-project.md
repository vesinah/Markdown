## Content (condensed from official project pages)

Thesis statement (project site): "Glosses are fingerprints of the society in which texts were composed, copied, and read." Glosses play a much more significant role than previous research acknowledged and offer insights about the multilingual and multi-ethnic environment of medieval manuscript and text production that principal texts cannot: they are first-hand testimonies of close linguistic and cultural contacts between Insular Celtic (Old Breton, Old Irish, Old Welsh) speakers and Latin speakers.

Scope:
- Comparative study of vernacular Insular Celtic and Latin glosses showing that interlinear and marginal glosses (paratext) of 9th-10th century manuscripts have a marginal character only at first glance.
- Problem identified: striking lack of editions has been a strong obstacle to in-depth investigation.
- Gloss.IT produces digital editions to investigate interrelationships between the languages involved (Latin/vernacular contact and intra-vernacular contact) and knowledge transfer observable in early medieval glossing traditions.
- Methods combined: comparative philology and historical linguistics; digital humanities (handwritten text recognition, network analysis, natural language processing); cultural history; biological computation applying DNA-sequence alignment methods to glosses (first-of-its-kind approach).
- Core sources: early medieval copies of Bede's computistical works and Priscian's Latin grammar with multiple manuscript witnesses transmitting Insular Celtic and Latin glosses; large-scale investigation into language contact and knowledge exchange between the Celtic-speaking world and the Carolingian empire.
- Example core manuscript shown: glossed copy of Bede, De Temporum Ratione, late 8th/early 9th century, Vienna ONB Codex 15298 (Olim Suppl. 2698).

Team (2025 photo caption): Bernhard Bauer (PI; early medieval glossing traditions, digital humanities, language contact, comparative Celtic historical linguistics), plus researchers in Latin philology, digital humanities, history; student assistants.

Related output by Bauer (via Network for the Study of Glossing bibliography): dictionary of the Old Irish Priscian Glosses (2015); co-editor of St Gall Priscian glosses v2.0 (2017, with Hofman and Moran); studies on Vienna Bede glosses, technical signs, and language-contact typology in Celtic glosses.

Companion resource discovered in same search: Network for the Study of Glossing, glossing.org - maintains bibliographia and an inventory of digital editions of glosses (St Gall Priscian, Ekkehart IV's Orosius glosses Cod. Sang. 621, Persius Tradition-B glosses, LibGloss/Liber glossarum, GLOSS-E etc.).
