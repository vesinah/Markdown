## Abstract (verbatim จาก Brill)

The relation between Yaska's Nirukta and the Vedic literature has been discussed by several scholars, but other than focusing on etymologies, it has been insufficiently studied. This article casts further light on the Vedic background of the Nirukta by considering three kinds of evidence. First, it explores the occurrences of nir-vac in the Atharvaveda and in the Brahmanas. Next it studies a method for classifying mantras that is first advanced in the Brahmanas and then elaborated in the Nirukta. Third, it argues that Yaska's model of nirvacana analysis was informed by a particular kind of Vedic etymology involving the opposition between evident (pratyaksa "present before the eyes") and cryptic (paroksa "out of sight") names.
