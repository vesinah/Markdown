## Facts relevant to issaikyo copying and proofreading culture

- Nanatsu-dera (Shingon; founding attributed to Gyōgi, 735) stores an issaikyō 一切經 "copied between 1175 and 1180 at the behest of the governor of Owari province", kept since then in lacquered chests; manuscripts and chests declared National Treasures 1900; a printed catalog existed since 1968 (Owari shiryō: Nanatsudera issaikyō mokuroku), but scholars assumed it was a manuscript copy of the printed Northern Song canon (completed 983).
- Ochiai's breakthrough: the Nanatsudera Da Tang Xiyu qiufa gaoseng zhuan proved to descend from a NARA-PERIOD manuscript recension, not the Song print; many manuscripts stem from pre-Heian originals; the apocryphal 16-juan Foshuo Foming jing matched Dunhuang fragments exactly.
- Newly surfaced texts include Foshuo Piluo sanmei jing (oldest extant Chinese apocryphon, in Dao'an's 374 catalog), Foshuo Toutuo jing, Foshuo Qingjing Faxing jing, second juan of Jingdu sanmei jing, Dasheng pusa rudao sanzhong guan (only extant non-translation attributed to Kumarajiva), Sanjie fofa, manuscripts of the Zhenyuan xinding Shijiao mulu differing from the Taishō.
- Institutional questions Hubbard raises for late-Heian canon-copying (verbatim themes): "what kind of resources were required...How many people were involved, what was their training, and how much were they paid?"; patron identified as Yasunaga Ason of the Onakatomi 大中臣朝臣安長; 48 works clearly designated extra-canonical in the catalog underlying the set.
- Organizational detail: ongoing surveys (fourth survey Oct 1991 described first-hand); ten-volume publication series planned; study society of 50+ scholars jointly collating damaged passages ("reconstruction of damaged passages").

## What this document does NOT provide (honest gap note)

The review names the patron and describes surveys but does NOT transcribe individual copy/proofreading colophons or name collator role-titles (校経者 etc.) within the Nanatsudera set (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน at this source).
