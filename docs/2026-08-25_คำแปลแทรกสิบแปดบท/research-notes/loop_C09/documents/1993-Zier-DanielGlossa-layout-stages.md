## Why this article matters for the layout question

Zier's introduction contains the most compact published summary in an accessible venue of Christopher de Hamel's staged model of glossed-book page development (de Hamel, Glossed Books of the Bible and the Origins of the Paris Booktrade, Woodbridge 1984). The staged summary, with de Hamel's date windows:

1. Stage one - "used exclusively through the first third of the twelfth century": page ruled with a central column for the biblical text quite independent of preparations for the gloss; the biblical text of an entire book written out in full BEFORE any gloss is added (a two-stage process).

2. Between 1130 and 1150: the width of the central column begins to vary according to the amount of gloss (sometimes oddly shaped columns); writing has become a single-stage procedure where the relationship of text and gloss is determined page by page; per de Hamel, this shows "the gloss itself had begun to gel".

3. Between 1150 and 1170 - reversal under Gilbert de la Porrée and Peter Lombard: popularity of their continuous catena/expositio Psalms and Paul commentaries prompts scribes to rule the page first FOR THE GLOSS; the biblical text is then written in display script on alternate lines, marginalized into a narrow inner column or small blocks surrounded on three sides by commentary ("so to speak" marginalized). De Hamel claims this shift can be traced precisely to 1150-1170.

4. After 1170: the new procedure spreads to glosses on other biblical books; column width varies not only page-to-page but even within a single page; a ruled spare column may be reserved for personal notes; at this point, per de Hamel, the format of the gloss is fixed.

## Additional quantified / classificatory observations reported by Zier

- Placement rule by gloss length: "As a general rule, the shortest of the glosses are generally found between the lines of the biblical text, and the longest are found in the margins"; intermediate glosses (two or three phrases) float between marginal and interlinear positions from manuscript to manuscript.
- Hierarchy expressed in script sizes: in a heavily glossed witness (MS D), four distinct point sizes on one page - biblical text largest (display script), primary marginal gloss smaller, interlinear slightly smaller than marginal, secondary additions smallest (vs the usual two).
- Mature format mechanics: late manuscripts show regularity achieved by scoring or lining generally three columns for the writing frame (four in one witness), "thereby providing just enough room to put nearly all the glosses in the margins with the biblical text occupying sometimes one, sometimes two of the scored columns".
- Status of marginal vs interlinear as textual categories: no attempt made to distinguish them editorially since their assignment varies manuscript to manuscript.
- Textual-history note usable in the book: recensions pre-/post-c.1170 correlate with mise-en-page changes (cf. Song of Songs pre-/post-1170 versions).
