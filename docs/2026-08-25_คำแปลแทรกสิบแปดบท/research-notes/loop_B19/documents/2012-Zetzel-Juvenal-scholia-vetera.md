# Juvenal scholia vetera and the Bern scholia manuscripts - precision record

Harvested: loop B19, 2026-08-26. Sources: BMCR 2012.03.42 (full review fetched), e-codices descriptions of Bern Burgerbibliothek Cod. 370 (full itemized description fetched) and Cod. 354, Mayor's Thirteen Satires of Juvenal vol. 1 (IA full OCR text downloaded).

## 1. Bern Burgerbibliothek Cod. 370 = Lucan scholia, NOT Juvenal

From https://www.e-codices.unifr.ch/en/list/one/bbb/0370 :
- Shelfmark: Bern, Burgerbibliothek, Cod. 370.
- Title: Commenta Bernensia in Lucanum; Adnotationes super Lucanum.
- Parchment, 179 ff., 24-24.5 x 18-18.5 cm, end of the 9th century.
- "This manuscript, which was probably produced in Reims, consists of two parts that contain only the scholia on Lucan, but not the actual text."
- Part 1 (to f. 125v): Commenta Bernensia, preserved only in this codex; interspersed with 21 simple schemata in color, geographic representations, plans of cities and battles.
- Part 2 (from f. 125v): incomplete Adnotationes for books 1-4, 9, and beginning of 10.
- DOI of description: 10.5076/e-codices-bbb-0370.

The itemized standard description (Homburger 1962, ed. Mittenhuber 2018) confirms the complete contents inventory: Commenta (siglum C), a Frechulf/Orosius chronicon fragment (ff. 125v-128v), Adnotationes super Lucanum (ff. 128v-179v). NO Juvenal scholia anywhere in the codex. Bibliography cited there includes Shirley Werner, "On the History of the Commenta Bernensia and the Adnotationes super Lucanum", HSCP 96 (1994) 343-368, and Paolo Esposito, "Early and Medieval Scholia and Commentaria on Lucan", in Asso (ed.), Brill's Companion to Lucan (2011) 453-463.

Precision conclusion for the book draft: "Scholia Bernensia" properly names the Lucan commentaries in Bern 370 (Commenta Bernensia + Adnotationes super Lucanum). The Juvenal scholia vetera are transmitted by a different manuscript tradition entirely (below).

## 2. The Juvenal scholia vetera: Pithoeanus and the Carolingian recensions

From James E. G. Zetzel's review of Stefano Grazzini, Scholia in Iuvenalem recentiora: secundum recensiones phi et chi tomus I (satt. 1-6), Edizioni della Normale 2011 - BMCR 2012.03.42, https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2012/2012.03.42/ :

> "The great master of Latin scholia in the twentieth century was Paul Wessner; ... his edition of the Scholia Vetera on Juvenal, originally published in 1931, is, for students of such material, unsurpassed not only in its accuracy but in the care and intelligence he devoted to presenting a text of extraordinary complexity."

> "In the case of Juvenal (unlike Persius or Lucan, for instance) that is not impossible: one invaluable ninth-century manuscript, P, the Codex Pithoeanus, contains in its margins a set of notes unlike those found elsewhere in the tradition; their antiquity is guaranteed by a brief overlap with the marginalia in the fragmentary fourth-century palimpsest preserved in Vat. Lat. 5750. What is more, the P scholia have parallels in a few other Carolingian sources, and it is thus possible, with some confidence, to see these scholia as indeed of ancient origin."

> "...Wessner assembled in an apparatus of parallels drawn from four different sets of Carolingian marginalia some of the evidence..."

> "He has edited (for Satires 1-6) the two Carolingian commentaries labelled by Wessner phi and chi; the former is represented by four manuscripts, the latter by seven. He does not include, although he discusses in his introduction, two further commentaries (L and Z); and he also omits ... a recently identified and apparently very important manuscript, Cambridge, King's College 52, which may contain autograph comments of Heiric of Auxerre himself - generally considered to be, if not the sole author, at least a major contributor to the Carolingian commentaries on several authors."

On the origin/provenance question:

> "[Grazzini] deals (somewhat inconclusively, as is right) with questions of authorship and provenance".

> "Wessner and his contemporaries tended to discard what they thought of as Carolingian material as useless and uninformative; modern editors are more inclined to value the light these manuscripts cast on Carolingian intellectual life." (i.e., the old goal was recovering ancient scholarship behind medieval marginalia; the modern goal includes the Carolingian school context itself.)

Zetzel also characterizes the two strata comparatively: the P scholia are "very laconic, but more frequently intelligent", while the Carolingian recensions are "wordy, dull, and empty" school notes offering glosses, paraphrase, syntax and allusion explanation for students.

## 3. Pithoeanus shelfmark verification (from a fetched primary source)

John E. B. Mayor, Thirteen Satires of Juvenal, vol. 1 (London 1881), preface (Internet Archive identifier thirteensatireso00juve, djvu.txt downloaded this session):

> "I propose to prepare a critical text founded on a new collation of P (cod. Pithoeanus or Budensis saec. IX, once in the library of Matthias Corvinus, now in the bibliotheque de l'ecole de medecine at Montpellier H. 125) with the early mss. in this country."

So: P = Codex Pithoeanus (= Budensis), 9th century, ex-library of Matthias Corvinus, now Montpellier, Bibliotheque interuniversitaire, Section Medecine, MS H. 125. Corroborating study found via Crossref: S. G. Owen, "On the Montpellier Manuscripts of Persius and Juvenal", The Classical Review 19.4 (1905) 218-223, DOI 10.1017/S0009840X00000512 (metadata verified; paywalled).

Mayor also transmits Bucheler's observation on the ms physical history: "codices Pithoeanus et Sangallensis triginta versibus (vir 129-158) quae olim adscripta fuere scholia omittunt" - i.e., both mss omit thirty verses that once carried attached scholia, with Jahn's lost-leaf theory vs Bucheler's deliberately excised-folio theory recorded.
