## Key passages (quoted from the OCR text)

### Corpus scale

> "If we leave aside the signs normally used to mark new sections in an ancient text -- the paragraphus, the diple obelismene, the coronis, and the like --, and also the decorative space-fillers at the ends of lines, there are roughly three hundred Greek literary papyri from Egypt in which sigla appear in the margin or between the lines. Their function is readily apparent and utilitarian in about a hundred texts."

> "The great majority of the papyri in question are from a single city and were written in the space of two centuries."

### Method and organization

> "The material presented here was collected from all published literary papyri for which editors have reported either critical sigla or marks accompanying corrections or variant readings."

Footnote 1: evidence collected from all published literary papyri listed in R.A. Pack, The Greek and Latin Literary Texts from Graeco-Roman Egypt (2nd ed. Ann Arbor 1965) and F. Uebel, APF 21 (1971) 167ff, plus later publications. "Sigla are organized according to function in three tables: Table 1, Aristarchan signs in papyri of Homer; Table 2, symbols with utilitarian function; Table 3, sigla of uncertain meaning."

### Aristarchan signs in Homeric papyri

> "Most of the seven signs of Aristarchus had a precise meaning related to the text. They draw attention to spurious lines, questionable readings, lines out of place. The exception was the diple, a general-purpose symbol indicating that a line contained some noteworthy point -- linguistic, historical, or otherwise."

> "First, in cases where they fit the prescription of Aristarchus it is not the text-related signs but the all-purpose diple that appears most frequently. Secondly, only a little over half the texts containing Aristarchan sigla have all the marks in the right place at the right time."

> "the diple is the sign referred to most often in the scholia to Homer. In fact the scholia that treat the lines marked by diplai in Homeric papyri suggest a reason for their prominence. What they mainly offer are lectional help and pragmatic explanations of content."

> "Indeed, obeli are preserved in only a dozen Homeric texts."

> "the texts we are dealing with are likelier to have belonged to ordinary readers than to learned ones."

### Interlinear placement (relevant to interlinear-gloss project)

Examples of sigla written between lines rather than in margins, cited from her tables:
- P.Oxy. 10.1247: anchora and other mark "interlinear, beside each other, above words to transpose".
- P.Oxy. 7.1018: stroke "interlinear, used with b' and a' to mark words to be transposed".
- P.Oxy. 17.2102: "interlinear, with revision and at point of insertion".
- On dotted interlinear diacritics: "Many of the texts in question are very fragmentary, however, so it is unclear whether the dotted interlinear diacritics are errors or corrections."
- Chi: "one of the commonest in literary papyri ... appears now and then with variants or corrections, but in the majority of cases its meaning is no more apparent than that of many of the other signs."

### Second study: The Inflection of Marginal Notes (source of papyrus marginal glosses)

> "...correctly inflected glosses and comments. Secondly, general and rhetorical lexica are hardly the only sort of word lists that circulated in antiquity; and they are not necessarily the ones that the annotator of a literary work would consult. Glossaries that followed the order of the text and text-specific, alphabetized lexica also proliferated. Typically, these lists were short and their contents restricted to the literary text at hand. The Scholia Minora to Homer most copiously illustrate this type of glossary, but similar lists circulated for other authors. Surely if word-lists were the usual source of papyrus notes ..."

Cited examples of glossaries retaining order/inflection of original: P.Oxy. XLVII 3328 on Callimachus H. 3 (2nd c.), probably P.Oxy. XXIV 2393 on Alcman (2nd c.), Bodl. MS Gr. class. e. 44 (Apollonius Sophista), P.Cairo 50208 (Homeric lexicon); alphabetical word-list examples P.Oxy. XV 1802, P.Mon. II 22, P.Oxy. XLV 3239; Atticist/rhetorical lexica P.Oxy. XVII 2087, P.Ryl. III 532, P.Oxy. XV 1804.
