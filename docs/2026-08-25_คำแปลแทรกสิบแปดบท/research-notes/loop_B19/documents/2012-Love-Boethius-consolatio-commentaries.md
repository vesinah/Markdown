## What the chapter covers (verified via Melinda Nielsen's BMCR review)

Melinda Nielsen, review of the Companion, BMCR 2014.05.48, https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2014/2014.05.48/ :

> "Rosalind C. Love deals with the earliest reader responses in 'The Latin Commentaries on Boethius's De consolatione philosophiae from the 9th to the 11th Centuries.' Love's essay embodies the strengths of the volume as a whole ... Her essay begins by clarifying medieval reading and commenting practices, while yet giving a clear history of the vexed manuscript tradition, and also valuable light on the new findings of the 'Boethius in Early Medieval Europe Project' commenced in 2007 with Malcolm Godden and Rohini Jayatilaka."

Same review on the volume's framing and adjacent chapters:

> "the collection approaches Boethius primarily through his reception history ... Consequently the volume emphasizes the living reinterpretation of Boethius".

> Fabio Troncarelli's interpretative "Afterward[s]: Boethius in Late Antiquity and the Early Middle Ages" "makes widely accessible for the first time in English Troncarelli's on-going research into late-antique 'editions' of Boethius".

The "Boethius in Early Medieval Europe Project" (Oxford, dir. Malcolm Godden with Rohini Jayatilaka, begun 2007) is thus the major manuscript census of glosses/commentaries on the Consolatio in early medieval Europe referenced by Love's chapter - the natural next source to consult for chapter drafts on Consolatio glossae (its own catalogue pages were not fetched this session).

## Usable summary statement for the draft

Verified chain for the book: earliest surviving reader responses to the Consolatio take the form of Latin commentaries and glosses of the 9th-11th centuries; their manuscripts present a complex ("vexed") transmission that modern scholarship maps through projects like Boethius in Early Medieval Europe (Godden/Jayatilaka); the standard English-language survey is Love's Brill Companion chapter (pp. 75-133), complemented by Troncarelli on late antiquity and by Marenbon's monographs on Boethius' reception.
