> "The manuscript Marcianus Graecus Z. 453 [= 821], known to Homeric scholars as the Venetus B, was produced in the 11th century CE. Like the Venetus A, it was acquired by the Greek Cardinal Basileus Bessarion in the 15th century CE and donated together with his entire collection of Greek manuscripts to the Republic of Venice."

(The same file gives Venetus A = Marcianus Graecus Z. 454 = 822.)

## 2. Venetus B codicology and scholia layers (from venetusB.md)

- 338 folios; 40.5 x 31.5 cm.
- 11th-century text and scholia written by two contemporary scribes.
- Typical folio: 12 to 24 lines of Iliad text surrounded by a large body of scholia on three sides.
- Later layers: "On many folios the space around the scholia that surrounds the text of the Iliad is surrounded by additional layers of scholia in a later (13th-century) hand. According to Erbse (1969), these are generally mythological scholia and glosses taken from lexica or from the Epimerismi Homerici."
- Far margins contain material from Porphyry's Homeric Questions and from Pseudo-Heraclitus' Allegoriai Homerikai; still later scribes added Etymologicum Magnum, Suda, Epimerismi Homerici where space remained.
- Cross-reference apparatus: "The Venetus B has a different method of coordinating text with the commentary in the margins from that of the Venetus A ... Numbers are used, comparable to footnote numbers, to link the commentary to the text."

## 3. Sigla equivalences for the five main photographed manuscripts

From HMT blog post "What's in a Name (of a Manuscript)?" (Casey Due, September 4, 2012):

| HMT designation | Library designations | Allen siglum | West siglum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Venetus A / Marciana 822 | Marcianus Graecus Z. 454 | A | A |
| Venetus B / Marciana 821 | Marcianus Graecus Z. 453 | B | B |
| Marciana 841 | Marciana Graecus Z. 458 | U4 | (not included) |
| Escorial Y.1.1 | Escorialensis 294 (= older catalog 291) | E3 | E |
| Escorial O.1.12 | Escorialensis 513 (= older catalog 509) | E4 | F |

Origin of letter names: Villoison's 1788 editio princeps called the two Venetian manuscripts "A" and "B" ("Venetus" = Latinate "Venice"); Allen used U (Latinate consonantal U) for Venice mss beyond A/B since V was reserved for Vatican mss; West used far fewer mss and renamed Escorial E3/E4 as E/F.

Zanetti catalog context: "Marcianus Graecus Z." = Zanetti's 1740 catalogue; Z marks his catalogue; Greek poetry mss carry numbers 438-461.

## 4. The bT family and the D-tradition (family scheme per HMT)

From VenA-Introduction-2014.md:

> "Finally, there are scholia related to a group known to scholars as the 'bT' scholia. These scholia are transmitted in the 11th-century Venetus B manuscript (Marciana 821), the Townley Manuscript of the Iliad, an 11th-century codex now in the British Library (Burney manuscript 86, called 'T' for short), and in two 11th-century manuscripts in the Escorial library in Spain, Escorial Y.1.1 and Escorial O.1.12."

> "...and the two Escorial manuscripts (which scholars have considered closely enough related to posit a common prior source no longer extant, referred to as 'b')."

> "The bT scholia, which may also be derived from the work of Porphyry, offer explanations of thematic matters found in the Iliad, cultural practices, questions of cosmology or theology, discussion of the plot, and so forth."

On Erbse's editorial policy (same file):

> "Erbse excluded from his edition anything he judged to be from the 'D-scholia' tradition, the paraphrases that survive in some manuscripts, and anything written after the 10th century."

Family behavior example (blog post "Wake up! A return to Iliad 10...", June 13, 2013): at Il. 10.159 the bT-family witnesses read "egreo": "Some of our oldest manuscripts, including the Venetus B (and those in that family), the Townley, and Escorial O.1.12, as well as a fourth-century CE papyrus (481) read egreo, while the vast majority of other manuscripts read orseo." The same post cites Aristarchan didacha via Venetus A intermarginal scholia and Townley T.

Blog corroboration that B and Escorial Y.1.1 scholia closely match (post "Layout and Preparation of Venetus B Folios", July 12, 2013): "the fact that the Venetus B scholia so closely match the scholia in the Y.1.1 manuscript ..." with reference to Matthew Davis, "Are Venetus B and E3 'twins'?".
