## Structure visible in the scan

1. Dedication to Pope Leo X ("LEONI DECIMO, PONTIFICI MODIS OMNIBVS SVMMO, ERASMVS ROTERODAMVS THEOLOGORVM INFIMVS S. D.") — opening compliments to Leo's Medicean upbringing and his task of advancing "Christianam pietatem" after the wars of the times.
2. "PARACLESIS AD LECTOREM PIUM" — exhortation to the reader that scripture be available and read by all.
3. Greek text with facing Latin; later Methodus sections per the front matter headings.

## Paraclesis, opening argument (OCR, lightly normalized)

Erasmus invokes Lucian/Firmianus as model: Lucian "whose tongue Jerome alone admired", turning pagan eloquence to Christian defence; Erasmus wishes an eloquence "far different from Cicero's... certainly far more effective", if he is to summon mortals "to the most holy and most wholesome study of Christian philosophy". He complains that while every other discipline has been pursued with ardent minds, this one Christian philosophy is neglected even by Christians: "hanc unam Christi philosophiam a nonnullis etiam Christianis ridere, a plurimis negligi, paucis tractari, sed frigide."

The polemical point kept for the book chapter: Erasmus argues that those who know Aristotle's or Plato's dogmas by heart should much more "embrace the mind of their own author and prince, Christ" — the philological return to sources applied to theology.

## Context from Sandys vol. II (ch. X)

- "His edition of the Greek Testament, the first that was actually published, was accompanied with a Latin version and with notes suggested by those of Valla, which Erasmus had discovered in 1505." (Valla-to-Erasmus transmission; see [1440]-Valla file.)
- Adagia lineage: first edition 1500; second enlarged edition prepared at Venice as guest of Aldus (1508) — proverb-commentary = erudite annotation culture in print.
- Sandys quotes the NT preface: "aliorum litterae sunt eiusmodi ut non parum multos paenituerit insumptae in illis operae... at felix ille quem in hisce litteris meditantem mors occupat."
- Erasmus' own program statement: "I wrote, not for Italy, but for Germany and the Netherlands."
