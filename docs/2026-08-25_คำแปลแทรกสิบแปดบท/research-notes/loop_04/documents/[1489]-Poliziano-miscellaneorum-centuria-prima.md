## What the work is (from Sandys vol. II, ch. VII — see companion file)

"The most learned of his extant productions is his Miscellanea (1489). Among the many topics discussed in its pages are the use of the aspirate in Latin and Greek, the chronology of Cicero's 'Familiar Letters', the evidence in favour of the spelling Vergilius in preference to Virgilius, the details of the discovery of purple dye, and the differences between the aorist and the imperfect in the signatures of Greek sculptors."

Example of method recorded by Sandys: Gellius quotes a Latin riddle referring readers to a lost book of Varro; "Politian solves the riddle with the word Ter-minus, adding a reference to Ovid's Fasti" (Misc. c. xli).

Publication context: its appearance provoked a feud with Giorgio Merula, who claimed part of Poliziano's learning was stolen from him.

## Sandys on his manuscript investigation

"Politian was a keen investigator of all the ancient MSS that came within his reach in Florence or elsewhere." He was also commissioned to annotate the Horace (1482) and Virgil (1487) editions — annotation work migrating from margins into printed commentary apparatus.

## RQ8 significance

The Miscellanea genre itself — published reading notes ("Miscellanea or Adversaria... set by Politian and Victorius, by Turnebus and Muretus", per Sandys ch. XIII) — is the bridge between private marginal scholia and public critical method. Scaliger later rejected precisely this genre (see [1908]-Sandys file, extract 12).
