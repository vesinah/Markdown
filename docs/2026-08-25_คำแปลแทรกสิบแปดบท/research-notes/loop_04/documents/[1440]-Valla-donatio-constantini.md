## Historiographic framing (Coleman introduction)

Coleman situates the work among "achievements of modern historical criticism": Valla's treatise "was a conspicuous pioneer". Key judgements:

1. Priority nuance: "Nicholas Cusanus some seven years earlier in his De concordantia catholica covered part of the same ground even better than Valla did, and anticipated some of his arguments."
2. But: "Valla's treatise is more exhaustive, is in more finished and effective literary form, and in effect established for the world generally the proof of the falsity of the Donation."
3. Method claim (core for RQ3): "for the first time, he used effectively the method of studying the usage of words in the variations of their meaning and application, and other devices of internal criticism which are the tools of historical criticism to-day."
4. Reception: the Donation was "almost universally accepted as genuine from the ninth to the fifteenth century"; cited by at least ten popes; when spuriousness became known it "contributed powerfully to the revolt from Rome" (Luther named).
5. Erasmus' praise of Valla quoted by Coleman: "Valla, a man who with so much energy, zeal and labor, refuted the stupidities of the barbarians, saved half-buried letters from extinction, restored Italy to her ancient splendor of eloquence, and forced even the learned to express themselves henceforth with more circumspection."

## Sample of Valla's linguistic argument (from the translation)

On the imperial insignia passage of the forged decree — the word phrygium (tiara):

"Quis umquam phrygium... [OCR garbled Latin]" — English side: "...ever heard 'tiara' [phrygium] used in Latin? You talk like a barbarian... but what does 'phrygium' mean? You do not explain this..."

Context lines preserved by OCR around L4537-4615 show Valla dissecting the document's lexicon item by item ("Simulque phrygium, nec non superhumerale... quod imperiale circumdare solet collum") — i.e., anachronistic vocabulary betraying a later composer. This is the word-usage method Coleman highlights.

## Sample of Valla's chronological argument

At the consulship dating clause: "...In any case, however, the date is impossible; no such consulship as [named]..." (translation, near L5949 of OCR). Valla checks the regnal/consular formulae against independent chronologies.

Overall structure visible in OCR: Valla first quotes Gratian's decree text (Chapters XIII-XIV of Gratian's Decretum are reproduced in the edition), then refutes section by section; his summary charge that the decree contains things "quaedam contraria, impossibilia, stulta, barbara, ridicula" (L1081) with facing English "comprised of contradictions, impossibilities, stupidities, barbarisms, ridiculous [claims]" (L1128).

## Cross-check with Sandys vol. II (see companion document file [1908]-Sandys-classical-scholarship-v2.md)

- Political motive: written while Valla served Alfonso of Aragon, discrediting papal claims to Naples.
- Grounds: legal, linguistic, political, historical; silence of the ancient Sylvester-legend MSS about any donation.
- Afterlife: printed by Ulrich von Hutten 1517; removed from the Roman Breviary thereafter.
