## Verbatim fragment 1 (pp. 89-90), quoted in a fetched Maynooth source

The ChronHib project slide deck "Statistical methods in the Old Irish language" (Maynooth University, ERC #647351; PDF at maynoothuniversity.ie/sites/default/files/assets/document//ChronHib.pdf) quotes McCone verbatim with page numbers:

"Kim McCone, 'The Würzburg & Milan Glosses: our Earliest Sources of 'Middle Irish'', Ériu 36 (1985), 89-90: 'There are a few instances of na for inna even in Wb. (12c11, 16a12, 18d14, Ml. 18d24, etc.; cf. GOI 293) to show that this reduction had taken place by then, restricting inna to a learned register variant that was coming under increasing pressure from na and the preponderance of which in the Glosses is probably due at least in part to orthographic conservatism.'"

Methodological significance: McCone treats sporadic "deviant" (non-classical) forms as CHRONOLOGICAL EVIDENCE, not scribal corruption: the article's core move is to read the distribution of such forms across Wb./Ml./Sg. as dating data.

## Secondary characterisations (fetched sources only)

1. Doyle, McCrae & Downey 2019 (aclanthology.org/W19-6910.pdf): "McCone has identified features more suggestive of Middle Irish than Old in manuscripts as early as that of the Würzburg glosses (1985)". Same paper: linguistic differences exist between the three Wuerzburg scribal hands, the prima manus suggesting "a more archaic form of Irish than that of the second and third hands".
2. Roczniki Humanistyczne 2021 (ojs.tnkul.pl/index.php/rh/article/view/16367, open access) cites the article under the title "...Our Earliest Sources for MI." as the standard reference point for placing Middle Irish beginnings early, in its glottochronological survey of the Old/Middle Irish boundary.
3. Standard textbook datings preserved in the same secondary literature (Samperi thesis, unitesi.unipv.it): Wb. c. 750, Ml. c. 800, Sg. c. 850 - i.e., the manuscript datings that McCone's title polemicises against treating as pure "Old Irish".

## Counterpoint recorded in the same Maynooth deck (for balanced citation)

- James Carney, "The dating of Early Irish verse texts, 500-1000", Eigse 19 (1982-3), 199: "We have plainly here a dating criterion of some importance. The graph of inna falls slowly and regularly from 100% in the seventh century to zero from about 900 onwards..."
- Stifter's corpus data (deck, per David Stifter): inna/na ratios - Wuerzburg 146/1084 (13.5%), Milan 859/4405 (19.5%), St Gall 179/1274 (14.1%) total tokens; detailed case tables given. These show na already present in all three corpora but not linearly increasing - the empirical backdrop against which McCone's pp. 89-90 claim must be read.
