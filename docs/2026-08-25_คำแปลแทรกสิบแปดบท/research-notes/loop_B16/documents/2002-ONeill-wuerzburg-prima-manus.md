## Substance accessible without the paywalled chapters

From O'Brien 2022 (open-access Peritia article, fetched and text-extracted):
- O'Brien relies on O Neill 2001 at pp. 44-45 precisely for reading Wuerzburg gloss Wb. 6a (TPH i 534: "assindet sunt traictne cumacte diandid coir infognam .i. indai osechat hulcu et morate mathi") on Romans 13: the Latin+Irish glossing radically moralises secular power ("power ... counteth not for a power"), turning "all power is from God" into the suggestion that the wicked cannot legitimately wield power. This shows O Neill's approach: treat Latin lemmata and Irish interpretamenta as one exegetical unit ("unity in diversity").
- O'Brien also uses O Neill's Quiggin pamphlet "Biblical study and mediaeval Gaelic history" (Cambridge 2003) for the Anglo-Saxon connections of the southern-Irish scholarly milieu behind such exegesis.
- The wurzburg.ie site references O Neill 2002 "throughout this website's commentary on the glosses", i.e., Doyle's digital edition adopts O Neill's hand/context distinctions for the prima manus glosses.
