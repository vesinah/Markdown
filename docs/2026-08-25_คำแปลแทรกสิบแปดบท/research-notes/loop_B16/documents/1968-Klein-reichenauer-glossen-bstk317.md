# Document: Die Reichenauer Glossen / Gloses de Reichenau - manuscript identity, editions, and the Bavarian/Alemannic confusion

Harvested: 2026-08-26 (Loop B16). Purpose: close loop_24 remaining gap 1 ("Reichenau glosses - Bavarian or Alemannic? BStK/Clm number?").

## 1. What the name refers to

The established scholarly referent of "Reichenauer Glossen" (French: "Gloses de Reichenau"; English: "Reichenau Glossary/Glosses") is a Gallo-Romance corpus: late-8th-century glossary-glosses that explain or translate difficult Latin words of the Vulgate into contemporary vernacular Latin / early Romance (proto-Old French). Standard examples cited in the literature: forum -> mercatum 'market'; liberos -> infantes 'children'; pulchra -> bella 'beautiful'. The corpus also contains a small amount of Germanic material (Old English glosses; some scholars count early Dutch/Frankish elements). It is NOT an Old High German collection and NOT Bavarian.

- Discovery/publication history: brought to scholarly notice by Adolf Holtzmann, "Die alten Glossare", Germania 8 (1863), S. 401f., 403-414 (discovery year 1863; confirmed also by the ProQuest book-review snippet on Klein's edition: "die Geschichte der Reichenauer Glossen seit ihrer Entdeckung durch Adolf Holtzmann im Jahre [1863]").
- Earlier treatments: Kurt Hetzer, Die Reichenauer Glossen. Textkritische und sprachliche Untersuchungen zur Kenntnis des vorliterarischen Franzoesisch, Halle (Karras) 1906 (title confirms: study of pre-literary FRENCH); Andreas Labhardt, Contributions a la critique et a l'explication des "Gloses de Reichenau", Borna-Leipzig (Noske) 1936; Andreas Labhardt (ed.), Glossarium biblicum codicis Augiensis CCXLVIII, Neuchatel 1948.
- Standard modern edition: Hans-Wilhelm Klein (ed.), unter Mitarbeit von Andre Labhardt, Die Reichenauer Glossen, Teil I: Einleitung, Text, vollstaendiger Index und Konkordanzen, Beitraege zur romanischen Philologie des Mittelalters 1,I, Muenchen (Hueber) 1968 (facsimiles of f. 1r and 20r at pp. 406f.; page counts in trade sources vary 403/407 pp.). Teil II: Manfred Raupach, Die Reichenauer Glossen, Teil II: Entstehung und Aufbau, same series 1,II, Muenchen (Hueber) 1972.
- Dating/localisation discussion: J. Engels, "Les 'Gloses de Reichenau' reedites. Leur datation et localisation", Neophilologus 52 (1968), p. 378-386, DOI 10.1007/bf01515488. Bernhard Bischoff, "A propos des Gloses de Reichenau: entre latin et francais", in: Mittelalterliche Studien III, Stuttgart 1981, S. 234-235. Germanic share: Willy Sanders, "Der germanische Anteil an den Reichenauer Glossen", Rheinische Vierteljahrsblaetter 33 (1969), S. 425-461.

## 2. The manuscript - authoritative description (BStK Online, fetched 25/26 Aug 2026)

Source: BStK Online: https://glossen.germ-ling.uni-bamberg.de/bstk/317 [Stand 25.08.2026] (Handschrift tab https://glossen.germ-ling.uni-bamberg.de/manuscripts/12418 ; Glossen tab https://glossen.germ-ling.uni-bamberg.de/glossed_contents/12418 ; last change Michaela Poelzl 31.07.2020 / 30.03.2020).

- Shelfmark: Karlsruhe, Badische Landesbibliothek, Aug. perg. 248 (= Codex Augiensis CCXLVIII). There is NO Clm number: the codex is not in Munich; the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek is not involved. Any "Clm" attribution for this corpus is wrong.
- Census identifiers: StSG. 70 (Steinmeyer/Sievers, Die althochdeutschen Glossen IV, S. 408f., Nr. 70); BStK.-Nr. 317 (Bergmann/Stricker catalogue numbering).
- Codicology: codex in 4 parts (f. 1-39, 40-62, 63-101, 102-168); parchment; 168 leaves; water damage and mould; 21.0 x 16.0 cm; Carolingian minuscule by SEVERAL HANDS OF THE 10TH CENTURY.
- Origin/provenance per BStK: individual parts written in the 10th century PERHAPS AT SOISSONS; later in the Benedictine abbey of REICHENAU; to the Badische Hof- und Landesbibliothek Karlsruhe in 1805 through secularisation. Digitisation: URN nbn:de:bsz:31-14276 (digital.blb-karlsruhe.de; bot-protected, not fetchable this loop).
- Contents: f. 1ra-39vb "Reichenauer Glossen" (f. 1ra-20ra biblical glossary; f. 20rb-39vb alphabetical glossary on the Regula Benedicti); f. 40ra-59ra glossary; f. 59ra-rb explanation of the Hebrew letter names; f. 59va-62vb Genesis commentary (incomplete); f. 63va-101vb Jerome, Expositio IV euangeliorum; f. 102va-154vb second biblical glossary; f. 155r-168v Genesis commentary (incomplete).
- The OHG-relevant residue (Glossen tab, quoted): within the 3,000-line Bible glossary of f. 104va-118vb stand exactly 10 glosses relevant to Germanic studies: 5 to Exodus (f. 104va, 105rb (2), 105vb, 106rb), 3 to Leviticus (f. 106vb (2), 107ra), 1 to Deuteronomy (f. 110va), 1 to 2 Samuel (f. 118vb); "zudem sind altenglische und altfranzoesische Glossen eingetragen" (Old English AND Old French glosses are entered as well).
- Language classification: "Sprachgeographische Einordnung: Alem. (nach K. Weinhold, S. XVI)" - ALEMANNIC per Karl Weinhold, Alemannische Grammatik I, Berlin 1863, S. XVI. Not Bavarian.
- Editions of the OHG residue: Nachtrag StSG V, S. 90, Z. 1, 14-17 (Exodus zu Nr. XXXII); StSG I, S. 345 Nr. XLII (Leviticus) mit Nachtrag StSG V S. 90 Z. 23; StSG V S. 367 (Nr. LXVII Deuteronomium); StSG V S. 414 (Nr. CXVI 2. Samuel).

## 3. Resolution for the book draft

1. The label "Reichenauer Glossen" must never be rendered as "Bavarian OHG biblical glosses". Correct: Gallo-Romance (early Old French) Vulgate glossary-glosses, compiled late 8th century, localised in northwest France (Corbie is the common candidate; Engels 1968 discusses dating/localisation), transmitted in a Reichenau codex now Karlsruhe Aug. perg. 248.
2. Neither Bavarian nor Alemannic applies to the main corpus; the small OHG residue (10 glosses) is classified ALEMANNIC by the BStK.
3. No Clm number exists for this corpus; cite Karlsruhe, BLB, Aug. perg. 248 = StSG 70 = BStK 317.
4. For genuinely Bavarian OHG biblical vernacular, cite Mondsee/Monsee-Wiener Fragmente and Freising material instead (loop_24 finding stands).
5. Caution flag retained: the popular claim that the glosses were "compiled at Corbie" specifically rests on the Engels-era discussion and general literature; the BStK itself only says "vielleicht in Soissons entstanden" for the 10th-century copy. Exact place of composition of the archetype remains debated (Corbie vs other NW-French centres) - (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน สำหรับจุดเรียบเรียงที่แน่นอน).
