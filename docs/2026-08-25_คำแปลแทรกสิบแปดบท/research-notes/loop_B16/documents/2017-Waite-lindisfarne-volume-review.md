## Volume facts

Julia Fernández Cuesta and Sara M. Pons-Sanz (eds), The Old English Gloss to the Lindisfarne Gospels: Language, Author and Context, Anglia Book Series 51, Berlin: De Gruyter Mouton, 2016; cloth; pp. xii, 432; R.R.P. £99.95, US$140.00, EUR74.99; ISBN 9783110449105. Papers from a workshop on the Lindisfarne gloss held at the University of Westminster, London, April 2012, plus additional papers. Aim (p. 1, quoted by Waite): gather palaeographical, glossographical, historical, linguistic and philological perspectives to understand better "the socio-historical context and the cultural and intellectual milieu in which [the gloss] was produced".

Review source: Greg Waite (University of Otago), Parergon 34.2 (2017), pp. 196-198, DOI 10.1353/pgn.2017.0050; open PDF mirrored by the Seville research group (grupo.us.es/northernenglish/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/review-de-LG.pdf), downloaded and text-extracted this loop.

## Part I - The Gloss in Context

1. Michelle P. Brown, "'A Good Woman's Son': Aspects of Aldred's Agenda in Glossing the Lindisfarne Gospels": develops her 2003 monograph (The Lindisfarne Gospels: Society, Spirituality and the Scribe); Aldred's gloss must be understood against Anglo-Scandinavian political relations, asserting "Englishness" and Christian character in the North.
2. Jane Roberts, "Aldred: Glossator and Book Historian": investigates Aldred's colophon and its evidence for the Gospels' origin at Lindisfarne; revisits her hypothesis that a vernacular poem served as source for Aldred's claims; compares Aldred's Gospels glosses with his Durham Collectar glosses.
3. Philip G. Rusche, "The Glosses to the Lindisfarne Gospels and the Benedictine Reform: Was Aldred Trained in the Southumbrian Glossing Tradition?": KEY RESULT - Aldred was influenced mainly by OLDER traditions of ninth-century Psalter glossing rather than new developments of the monastic reform period.
4. Paul Cavill, "Maxims in Aldred's Marginalia": contra Rusche's direction, finds the marginalia embody a reforming spirit (material poverty, purity of heart), using wisdom-literature style to urge monastic ideals regardless of where he trained.
5. Stewart Brookes, letter-form survey: Aldred's script mixes Square minuscule and Carolingian forms with a principal old-fashioned Insular minuscule; variation is a creative response to script variety in the Latin exemplar.

## Part II - The Language of the Gloss

- Robert McColl Millar: noun-phrase morphology simplification; early-Middle-English-type developments already underway in Northumbria.
- Marcelle Cole (present-tense verb morphology): variant distribution shows Aldred's work does NOT reflect a uniform idiolect throughout; it must have been based on earlier source materials (building on Brunner, Blakeley et al.; the introduction notes the demarcation at the start of Mark's Gospel - Brunner 1947-48, supported by van Bergen 2008 and Cole 2014).
- Luisa García García: causative (-jan) verbs - inflectional change outpaces derivational morphology.
- María Nieves Rodríguez Ledesma: genitive phrase morphology and word order.
- George Walkden: null subjects as syntactic evidence; subject-pronoun omission not simply Latin interference (frequent only in third person) - gloss usable for phrasal-level syntax study with caveats.
- Julia Fernández Cuesta, "Revisiting the Manuscript of the Lindisfarne Gospels": Skeat's edition compared against MS Cotton Nero D.iv itself; transcription errors, emendations and inconsistencies documented with photographs (per DeepDyve summary of same chapter).

## Part III - Glossing Practice

- Christine Bolze: multiple glosses to present-tense forms of beon.
- Sara Pons-Sanz, "A Study of Aldred's Multiple Glosses": PRINCIPLES OF ORDERING of double/multiple semantic glosses (pp. 301-328).
- Patrizia Lendinara, "The Unglossed Words of the Lindisfarne Glosses" (pp. 329-360): informed principles operate behind unglossed items - not gaps in Aldred's knowledge.
- Karen Jolly, "The Process of Glossing and Glossing as Process" (pp. 361-375, on Durham Collectar A.iv.19): glosses arose from oral AND written pedagogy at Chester-le-Street plus spiritual motivation.
- Tadashi Kotake: relationship between Lindisfarne gloss and Owun's Ru2 gloss in Rushworth/MacRegol - AGAINST received wisdom (Ru2 as direct copy of Li with occasional other sources), proposes both may stem from a LOST COMMON SOURCE.

## Supplementary scholarly depth harvested (Ramirez Perez's own research trajectory)

- Elisa Ramirez Perez, "A conditioned scribe: The use of multiple interlinear glosses to the Lindisfarne Gospels", Leiden Medievalists Blog, 3 March 2022: her morphological programme - loss of the medial -i-formant in weak class 2 verbs FIRST RECORDED in these glosses (example: respicite glossed behaldas ł locas, fol. 38r); double-gloss conditioning hypothesis: when BOTH elements are weak class 2 the formant is retained (eradicetis = unwyntrumias ł unclænsias), when the first element is another class it is lost.
- Same author, "Multilingualism in Anglo-Saxon England: The art of glossing and translating", Leiden Medievalists Blog, 9 Feb 2024: Norse-derived loanwords occur as single glosses or as first elements of double glosses - evidence they were accepted in Aldred's dialect/idiolect, not foreign; example of semantic double gloss: sero = efern ł ic sædi 'evening ł I sow' (MtGl (Li) 20.8).
