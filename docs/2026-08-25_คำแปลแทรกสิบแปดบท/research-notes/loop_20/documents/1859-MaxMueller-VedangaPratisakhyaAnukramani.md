> The Six Vedangas, or Branches of Vedic Exegesis; The Name of the Vedangas; The Number of the Vedangas; The First Vedanga, Siksha or Pronunciation; It formed part of the Aranyakas; It became the principal Subject of the Pratisakhyas; Origin of the Pratisakhyas; Numerous Authors quoted in the Pratisakhyas; Pratisakhyas attached to the different Sakhas of each Veda; The proper meaning of Sakha and Pratisakhya; Difference between Sakha and Charana...

### Śikṣā embedded in the Taittirīya-Āraṇyaka, with Sāyaṇa commenting

> ...why the whole chapter should be called the śikṣādhyāya [śikshadhyayah], and why it should begin with the words "Let us now explain the Śiksha." Sayana, who was certainly acquainted with the Vedic tradition, takes the same view in his Commentary... The other headings are, "On Accents," "On Quantity," "On the Organs of Pronunciation," "On Delivery," "On Euphonic Laws."

### Prātiśākhya authors quoting named masters (RV-Prātiśākhya / Śaunaka)

> ...who is most likely the same who is called the Master, Vedamitra (friend of the Veda), and who is called Śākalya-pita, the father of Śākalya. His opinions... differed from those of the younger Śākalya. In i. 185 we meet with him again under the name of Śākalya Sthavira, Śākalya the elder, and he is there represented as advocating a pronunciation from which Śaunaka, the author of the Prātiśākhya, dissents. In i. 199 Śaunaka adopts the opinion of Śākalya...

### Pada-texts outside the Rigveda (Black Yajurveda school tradition)

> ...the Anukramani says that Vaiśampāyana handed it down to Yāska Paiñgi, Yāska to Tittiri, Tittiri to Ukha, and Ukha to Ātreya, who was the author of a Pada-text, while Kuṇḍina composed a commentary (vṛtti) on the same Śākhā.

### The Anukramaṇī corpus (table of contents)

> Kātyāyana's Sarvānukrama to the Rig-veda; Five previous Anukramanis, ascribed to Śaunaka; Their style; The Bṛhaddevatā and its Authors; Number of Hymns, Verses, and Words, according to different Anukramanis; The three Anukramanis of the Yajur-veda; The Anukramanis of the Sama-veda, two classes; The Bṛhatsarvānukramaṇī to the Atharvana; How to fix the age of Śaunaka and Kātyāyana as Authors of the Anukramanis.

### Metrical index function (Nidāna-sūtra)

> For the metres of the Sama-veda we have the Nidāna-sūtra in ten prapāṭhakas, which after explaining the nature and different names of all the Vedic metres, gives a kind of index (anukramaṇī) to the metres as they occur in the hymns employed at the Ekāha, Ahīna, and Sattra sacrifices.

(Also:) Pingalanāga's Chhandas work "most frequently quoted under the title of Vedanga" does not claim greater antiquity than the Mahābhāṣya.

### Memorization culture sustaining the whole apparatus

> To introduce and to maintain such a species of literature was only possible with the Indian system of education, which consisted in little else except implanting these Sūtras and other works into the tender memory of children, and afterwards explaining them by commentaries and glosses. An Indian pupil learns these Sūtras of grammar, philosophy, or theology by the same mechanical method which fixes in our minds the alphabet and the multiplication-table...
