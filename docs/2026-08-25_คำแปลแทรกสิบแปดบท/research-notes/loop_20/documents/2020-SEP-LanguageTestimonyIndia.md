## Extracted evidence

### Vedic corpus layering (Samhita → Brahmana → Aranyaka → Upanishad)

> The Vedic scriptural texts (1500-500 bce) consist of the four ancient collections, i.e., the Rigveda, the Samaveda, the Yajurveda, and the Atharvaveda. The next layer of Vedic texts, the Brahmanas, consists of prose ritual commentaries that offer procedures, justifications, and explanations. The last two categories of Vedic literature are the Aranyakas, "Forest Texts", and the Upanisads, "Secret Mystical Doctrines".

### Grammar tradition as systematic science (Pāṇini, Kātyāyana, Patañjali)

> Pāṇini (400 bce) composed his grammar of Sanskrit with a certain notion of Sanskrit as an atemporal language... The early commentators on Pāṇini's grammar from the late Mauryan and post-Mauryan periods, Kātyāyana and Patañjali (200-100 bce), display a significant reorganization of Brahmanical views...

> [Kātyāyana:] "While the relationship between words and meanings is established on the basis of the usage of specific words to denote specific meanings in the community of speakers, the science of grammar only makes a regulation concerning the religious merit produced by the linguistic usage..." (first Vārttika on the Aṣṭādhyāyī)

### Bhartṛhari and abstraction (apoddhāra) — analysis of text into units

> Just as stems, affixes etc. are abstracted from a given word, so the abstraction of words from a sentence is justified. (Vākyapadīya II.10; Bhartṛhari c. 400 ce)

### Mīmāṃsā hermeneutics of the authorless Veda

> ...the Vedas were claimed to be eternal and intrinsically meaningful words, uncreated by any human being (apauruseya)... The Mimamsa conceives of an unbroken and beginningless Vedic tradition.
