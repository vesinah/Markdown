## Abstract points
- Investigates for the first time manuscript Ba 7 held in the Fulda Public Library (Germany), a bark-paper (dluwang/daluang) manuscript from sixteenth-century Java.
- Authors assign it the title "A Guide to Islamic Prayer and Belief"; an introduction to basic ideas and expressions of Islam for a Javanese audience.
- It is one of only seven Islamic texts known to have survived from sixteenth-century Java ("this obscure period").
- Themes illuminated: transmission of canonical Islamic texts to the archipelago; assimilation of Arabic vocabulary into local languages; role of Islamic religious education.

## Script question (crucial for pegon chronology)
- The Guide is written in MODERN JAVANESE script (Indic-derived), NOT in pegon (Arabic script). Quote: "The Guide is unique within the corpus in that it renders long portions of Arabic-language text in Javanese script, as opposed to the locally adapted Arabic script called pegon."
- Authors state: "Research on the oldest manuscripts in pegon script is still in its infancy, so here we focus only on manuscripts in Javanese script." (p. 183)
- Codicology: forty unnumbered daluang pages bound as a single codex; first three blank; 36 written pages; twelve lines per page; black ink; European pulp-paper flyleaf and covers added later; Latin inscriptions and Fulda library seal on p. 36. Original owners/librarians thought the book was right-to-left ("Est liber Malabaricus Linguae et Charactere"), so the digitized page order on Fulda Digital Collections runs backwards.

Notes captured from the article body:
- Fiqh (Gg.5.22): "probably in the possession of Joseph Justus Scaliger (1540-1609), then Thomas van Erpe/Erpenius (1584-1624). After Erpenius' death it became the property of the British royal family in 1625 ... donated to Cambridge University Library by ... Katherine Villiers in 1632."
- Amir: donated 1629 by William Herbert, third Earl of Pembroke; possibly originates from the English trading post in Banten.
- Ferra: undated; internal evidence suggests possibly a seventeenth-century copy of a sixteenth-century original.
- Four of the seven (Guide, Admo, Cat, Fiqh) are among the oldest known Javanese manuscripts on daluang (attested medium in highland Sumatra from early 15th c.).

## Pegon-relevant passage (verbatim extraction)
"One of these entered the collection of the Leiden Orientalist Jan Theunisz. before 1610: a Javanese interlinear translation of the Arabic text of the Idah fi al-Fiqh, one of the sources for the Javanese fiqh (Voorhoeve 1980, 121-22). More than 150 Arabic manuscripts with interlinear translation in pegon are known to exist in Dutch collections. Some of these texts came from Banten, West Java, and date to the seventeenth century. Islamic texts written in pegon, possibly of the seventeenth century, have also been found in Iran (Daneshgar 2021). Similar contemporary texts are known from Sumatra, such as a Quranic exegesis in which the Arabic is accompanied by translation and commentary in Malay (Riddell 1993)."

Footnote 22 (same article): "Interlinear translation has a long pedigree in Java, going back to the oldest pre-Islamic Javanese texts that glosses Sanskrit terms and passages in Hindu and Buddhist contexts (Acri and Hunter 2020)." (Reference given: Acri and Hunter, "Added in Translation: Keywords for the Study of Javanese Islamic Texts", Philological Encounters 9, 2024, pp. 36-59.)

## Other observations useful for the book chapter
- Content of the Guide shows Shafi'i/Ash'ari orientation consistent with al-Ghazali and al-Nawawi and the Nizamiyya schooling tradition; no Hadrami, Persian, Kubrawi or Shattari traces detected.
- Earliest phase of modern Javanese script had predominantly rounded shapes, closer to Balinese script.
- Language: Old Javanese with heavy Arabic assimilation; some Arabic terms already nativized, others glossed vernacularly.
- References cited include H. [Gunning?] 1881, "Een Javaansch Geschrift uit de 16de Eeuw Handelende over Den Mohammedaanschen Godsdienst" (Leiden: Brill) - the old edition of Leiden Or. 266; Ricklefs 1976 "Banten and the Dutch in 1619: Six Early 'Pasar Malay' Letters" BSOLA 39(1); Wain 2021 on a 16th-century Kubrawi silsila in the Sejarah Banten; Daneshgar 2022 carbon-dating of a Persian-Malay anthology (IMW 50(147)).

## Relevance for Loop B09
1. Provides a documented pegon interlinear attestation acquired BEFORE 1610 (Jan Theunisz collection, Idah fi l-fiqh with Javanese interlinear translation, per Voorhoeve 1980:121-22) - i.e., earlier than BL Sloane 2645 (copied 1623/4), although undated internally; must be flagged as "acquired before 1610", not "copied before 1610".
2. Quantifies the Dutch-collection corpus: more than 150 Arabic manuscripts with pegon interlinear translation; some 17th-century Banten provenance.
3. Establishes the seven-text canon of earliest Javanese Islam and clarifies that most of those are in Javanese script, so they must NOT be cited as pegon examples (common error).
4. Confirms scholarly consensus that the palaeography of early pegon is under-studied ("still in its infancy").
