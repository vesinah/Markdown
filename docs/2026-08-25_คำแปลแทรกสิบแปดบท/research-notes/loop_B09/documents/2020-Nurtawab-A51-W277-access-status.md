## Abstract (verbatim via Semantic Scholar/T&F)
"This article examines two copies of the Qur'an from 18th-century Banten held in the National Library of the Republic of Indonesia, A.51 and W.277, that contain interlinear Malay translations, focusing on two aspects, i.e. Qur'anic readings and Malay translations, to reveal Qur'anic pedagogical practices in the region."

Additional abstract sentences surfaced in search snippets: differences in how a Qur'an is copied (readings systems and verse numbering) reflect distinct intended readerships; identical Malay translations across Banten Qur'ans suggest a coherent Quranic pedagogy in Banten.

## Content recovered through the author's own later publications (open access)
From Nurtawab 2025 (Archipel 110, https://journals.openedition.org/archipel/9536):
- A.51 uses a non-Hafs reading system (Warsh/Qalun transmission line), with qira'at options and differing verse counts presented as marginalia.
- W.277 shares an IDENTICAL Malay translation text and layout with A.51 but its copyist converted the Quranic text toward the Hafs reading and simplified the margins.
- The Jalalayn served as the main reference for these interlinear translations.
From Nurtawab 2023 (IMW 51(150):119-142, DOI https://doi.org/10.1080/13639811.2023.2213588, also CLOSED per Unpaywall/Semantic Scholar; abstract verified): adds RAS Arabic 4 as third member of the textual family; concludes RAS Arabic 4 was probably copied LAST, referring to both A.51 and W.277 (per Archipel 2025 summary citing Nurtawab 2023: 11-19).
From Phase A (loop_16) records: A.51 appears designed for advanced reciters while W.277 targets beginners; their Malay renderings diverge from both the Cambridge Aceh commentary (CUL Or. Ii.6.45), Tarjuman al-Mustafid, and Tafsir al-Jalalayn - evidence of continued Malay exegetical activity into the 18th century.

## Physical description of PNRI A.51a-e (secondary, specialist blog)
Ali Akbar (BRIN researcher; quran-nusantara.blogspot.com, post "Mushaf Banten Koleksi Perpustakaan Nasional RI (1)", April 2012): https://quran-nusantara.blogspot.com/2012/04/banten-mushaf-asal-banten-koleksi.html
- PNRI holds Banten Qur'ans under shelfmarks A.50, A.51a-e, A.52a-k, A.53a-k, A.54a-e and W.278 (blog numbering; Nurtawab uses "W.277" - one of the two digits may be a cataloguing variant; flag for library catalogue check).
- A.51a-e = five bound volumes covering six juz each; covers 31 x 20 cm; page counts per volume [a] 496, [b] 643, [c] 608, [d] 601, [e] 691; paper heavily damaged by ink corrosion ("dimakan tinta"), many parts illegible.
- Only A.50 carries a colophon: Javanese wording naming Sultan Abu al-Mafakhir Muhammad Aliyuddin ibn Sultan Muhammad Arif (reign dated by the blog 1777-1802; Nurtawab 2025 gives r. 1773-1799).
- Physical traits of this group: unusually large size, very narrow margins, disciplined calligraphy, word Allah often in red ink.
- Provenance narrative: these manuscripts, together with regalia (crown now in Museum Nasional), weapons and gamelan, were taken by the Dutch from the Banten kraton after the exile of the last sultan in 1832.
CAUTION: blog source used only for physical measurements and shelfmark context; treat details as unverified until checked against Gallop & Akbar 2006 (Archipel 72) or the PNRI catalogue.
