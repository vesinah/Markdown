# [2025] Nurtawab - The Abolished Sultanate and its Royal Library: Arabic Qur'an Commentaries from Eighteenth-Century Banten (Archipel 110)

Source: Ervan Nurtawab, "The Abolished Sultanate and its Royal Library: Arabic Qur'an Commentaries from Eighteenth-Century Banten", Archipel 110 (2025), pp. 119-144, DOI: https://doi.org/10.4000/15d9o

Open access full text at OpenEdition: https://journals.openedition.org/archipel/9536 (harvested via DOI resolution and HTML text extraction, 2026-08-26)

## Context of the article
- Studies the Banten royal library manuscripts now in the National Library of Indonesia (PNRI) after the abolition of the Banten Sultanate; transfer to Dutch hands is "well-recorded in colonial documents", but some volumes also reached other collections (par. 13).
- Author's earlier work summarized (par. 4): Gallop and Akbar (2006) surveyed 13 Banten Qur'ans from the eighteenth century ("The Art of the Qur'an in Banten: Calligraphy and Illumination", Archipel 72: 95-156); Nurtawab's own prior studies covered Qur'ans with interlinear Malay translation (Nurtawab 2020) and Javanese translation (Nurtawab & Syukroni 2023), and reading/verse-numbering systems of three Qur'ans with identical Malay translations - A.51, W.277 and RAS Arabic 4 (Nurtawab 2023).
- Peacock (2024) treated the whole library within "Arabic Literary Culture in Southeast Asia in the Seventeenth and Eighteenth Centuries" (Brill).

## Key paragraphs on interlinear-translation Qur'ans (verbatim extracts)
Par. 25: "We now turn our attention to Qur'an manuscripts that contain interlinear translations. There are at least three Qur'ans that contain identical translations in Malay (mss. A.51, W.277 and RAS Arabic 4) and one containing Javanese translation (ms. A.54). ... A.51 was written using a non-Hafs reading system, that is reading by the primary transmitters of the Quranic recitation methods (qira'at) other than Hafs (d. 796). ... provides more complex information regarding qira'at options and the adoption of different numbers of verses from the recognised traditions. Both types of information are presented as marginalia."

Par. 26: "Qur'ans W.277 and A.51 are identical in terms of their translation texts and text layout. However, this is not the case with the writing of the actual Quranic text: the copyist of Qur'an W.277 attempted to change the Warsh/Qalun qiraa used for copying Qur'an A.51 to that transmitted by Hafs. Physically, Qur'an W.277 also looks simpler, due to the elimination of extra information in the margins that is included in Qur'an A.51. Meanwhile, it is possible that Qur'an RAS Arabic 4, which is a single-volume Qur'an, in which the copyist appears to have consistently applied the Hafs reading, might give us some insight into the ways these three manuscripts might be related. ... I contend that Qur'an RAS Arabic 4 might be the most recently created Qur'an copy in the royal collection, as its copyist seems to have referred to both Qur'ans A.51 and W.277 (Nurtawab 2023: 11-19)."

## Jalalayn copies with Javanese interlinear translations in the same library
- Par. 17: The Jalalayn served as the main reference for producing these interlinear Qur'an translations, consistent with the large number of Jalalayn manuscripts in the royal collection; some copies are multi-volume, large format.
- Par. 19-20 (ms A.55, five volumes a-e): very large folio format 48.5 x 34.5 cm, European paper; one juz per volume (juz 1, 3, 4, 13, 22 extant; the other 25 volumes not found). "A.55a contains the texts of the Qur'an and the Jalalayn, with Javanese interlinear translations of both Arabic works placed underneath the Arabic source text. The copyist has also included extra information on the grammatical points of each word written diagonally or vertically above the Arabic text of the Qur'an... In later developments, we know this practice - locally known as loghatan (Javanese) or ngalogat (Sundanese) - became very popular among students at traditional Islamic learning centres. When listening to the kyai's reading of the kitab kuning ..., students took notes by writing specific symbols that designate grammatical points relating to each word read by the kyai." Tajwid symbols are also added, making the copy a manual for learning recitation aloud.
- Par. 22 (ms A.59a-d): red ink for Jalalayn text, black ink for Qur'anic text; Javanese interlinear translations present; A.59a covers juz 5-9 (from Surat al-Nisa 24 to Surat al-Anfal 40); originally probably five or six volumes.

## Other translated tafsir in the library
- Par. 28: Tafsir al-Baghawi manuscript: "neatly written, most of the Arabic text is accompanied by interlinear translations in Javanese and is vocalised" (per Friederich and van den Berg 1873: 69). Translation thus served not only the Qur'an text but also Arabic tafsir works.
- Par. 29: ms A.60 = Jam' al-Jalalayn (gloss on the Jalalayn) by Nur al-Din ibn Sultan Muhammad al-Qari' (d. 1605); colophon copied 1178 H / 1764 AD; owner Pangeran Ratu Abu al-Mafakhir Muhammad Aliyuddin, likely the future sultan (r. 1773-1799), who ordered the copy ten years before enthronement under Sultan Arif Zayn al-Asyiqin (r. 1753-1773).
- Par. 42: A.63 copied 1131 H / 1718-1719 AD.
- Par. 30: majority of translated tafsir works in the royal library were rendered into JAVANESE, the language of education and court administration in Banten (citing Nurtawab 2020 and Yakin 2016).
- Par. 34, 58-59: two mss titled Tafsir al-Asrar (A.62, A.63) actually contain Ruzbihan al-Baqli's (d. 1209) Sufi commentary 'Ara'is al-Bayan under a local epithet - evidence of Sufi tafsir continuing in use until the end of the kingdom.
