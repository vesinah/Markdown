# [2023] Daneshgar & Wieringa - The Oldest Javanese Islamic Text at Cambridge University Library (CUL MS Gg.5.22)

Source: Majid Daneshgar (Munby Fellow, CUL) and Edwin Wieringa (University of Cologne), "The Oldest Javanese Islamic Text at Cambridge University Library", Cambridge University Library Special Collections blog, posted 22 May 2023. URL: https://specialcollections-blog.lib.cam.ac.uk/?p=25175 (full text fetched 2026-08-26)

Related print version: Yayasan Sastra Lestari, Majid Daneshgar and Edwin Wieringa, "An Early Javanese Manuscript on Islamic Law (Fiqh): MS Gg.5.55 at Cambridge University Library" (2023), https://www.sastra.org/agama-dan-kepercayaan/wulang/3265-pranatan-islam-cambridge-university-library-gg-5-22-sebelum-1609-922-pendahuluan-inggris ; and Majid Daneshgar, "Reconstructing Erpenius' Library: The First Collection of Oriental Manuscripts at Cambridge University Library" (Leiden: Brill, 2024).

## Provenance and dating
- CUL MS Gg.5.22 comes from the collection of Thomas Erpenius (1584-1624), first professor of Arabic at Leiden; first catalogued in Vossius' 1625 handlist of the Erpeniana as: "Liber Indicus alijs characteribus ignotis ... omega Graecorum, cum longis caudis" ("unknown characters ... like Greek omega with long tails").
- Described as a "Japanese Book" by early cataloguers (Pinder, with Wheelock); correct identification as Javanese was made only by Ricklefs and Voorhoeve in 1977 (Ricklefs, Voorhoeve & Gallop, Indonesian Manuscripts in Great Britain, 2014 ed., p. 55).
- Copied on dluwang (Javanese bark paper from Broussonetia papyrifera). Loose and fragile; divided into four parts with Arabic numerals by a European hand.
- First folio shows: microscopic traces of names/titles in ARABIC script; traces of an illuminated frame; capital letter "S" over floral painting - possibly Erpenius' mark for Scaliger items. If it belonged to Joseph Justus Scaliger (d. 1609), terminus ante quem = 1609.
- Path into England: Scaliger (?) -> Erpenius -> bought 1625 by George Villiers, Duke of Buckingham for the royal family -> donated to CUL in 1632 by Katherine Villiers.

## Contents
1. Fiqh treatise on the principal religious duties (ff. 2-36), citing three Arabic kitab sources on f. 2r: (a) kitab Muharar = al-Muharrar; (b) kitab Ilah = Idah fi l-Fiqh; (c) kitab Sujjai = Abu Shuja's Shafi'i compendium. The same three works are named in the Serat Centhini as the most authoritative fiqh textbooks.
2. Short divination text on solar/lunar eclipses (ff. 36-37): eclipses read as tandha saking Pangeran ("signs of the Lord"); compared with Centhini material.
3. Fragments of a catechism (ff. 37-41), usul agama (usul al-din) in question-and-answer format: lamun sira tinakonan... ("if you are asked...") / sauranira... ("your answer is...").
- Opens with basmala rendered in Javanese: sakehing puji ing Allah, Pangeraning alam kabeh; God called Pangeran still in its Old Javanese sense (Ricklefs, Mystic Synthesis in Java, 2006, p. 22).
- Marginal notes and corrections throughout; ends part one with Arabic phrase wallahu a'lam bi-l-sawab between Javanese punctuation marks.

## Assessment
- "Gg.5.22 is one of the oldest extant Javanese Islamic manuscripts, originating from the period of transition from Hindu-Buddhism to Islam." Provenance unknown, most likely Java's north coast (pasisir).
- IMPORTANT CAVEAT for pegon chronology: Gg.5.22's main text is written in JAVANESE script (aksara Jawa), not Arabic/pegon script; the Arabic-script traces are limited to the first folio ownership notes and formulae such as the closing wallahu a'lam phrase. It therefore documents pre-1609 Islamic book production on dluwang in Java, but NOT an early pegon interlinear translation.
- Comment thread: Mark Woodward asked about a romanized edition; authors responded positively (May 2023).

## Relevance for Loop B09
Supports Gap 2 by establishing that fully developed Islamic vernacular book production on dluwang existed in Java before 1610, and by identifying the Idah fi l-Fiqh as a core text of the earliest Javanese Muslim curriculum - the very work whose Jan Theunisz copy carries a pegon interlinear translation acquired before 1610 (Voorhoeve 1980:121-22, via Sastrawan et al. 2026).
