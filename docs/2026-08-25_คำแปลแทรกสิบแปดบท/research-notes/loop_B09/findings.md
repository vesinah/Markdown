## Key findings (with URLs)

1. "Sultan Alauddin Qur'an" (Aceh, early 17th c., interlinear Malay): (ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน). No museum record, auction/catalogue entry, or scholarly publication retrieved describes any Acehnese Qur'an associated with a sultan named Alauddin or bearing interlinear Malay translation. The verifiable "Sultan Alauddin" artefact set consists of (a) royal LETTERS - Alauddin Riayat Syah's ca. 1602 letter to Sir Henry Middleton and Iskandar Muda's illuminated 1615 Malay letter to James I now in the Bodleian (oldest Malay royal letter in Britain) - see http://www.sabrizain.org/malaya/library/acehletters.pdf , https://bl.iro.bl.uk/downloads/a0661c0a-e5bb-41b8-8537-619f1ae624b3 ; (b) his Ottoman-style SEAL (Gallop 2004, IMW 32(93)); (c) coincidentally, Museum Negeri Aceh stands on Jl. Sultan Alauddin Syah, Banda Aceh (https://quran-nusantara.blogspot.com/2012/04/aceh-mushaf-aceh-koleksi-museum-negeri.html) - a plausible conflation vector. For early Acehnese Qur'ans, the scholarly baselines remain Riddell's Rotterdam MS 96 D 16 (oldest surviving Malay-world Qur'an, no interlinear translation reported; https://doi.org/10.1080/13639810220134638) and the two 17th-c. Aceh commentaries (CUL Or. Ii.6.45; Tarjuman al-Mustafid). Recommendation: treat the "Sultan Alauddin Qur'an" as unverified lore; do not cite.

2. Pegon chronology moved BACKWARD beyond Sloane 2645 (1623/4): Sastrawan, Aldahesh & Vickers (Studia Islamika 33(1), 2026, https://doi.org/10.36712/sdi.v33i1.46614) report that Leiden Orientalist Jan Theunisz. acquired BEFORE 1610 a Javanese interlinear translation (pegon) of the Arabic Idah fi l-Fiqh, citing Voorhoeve 1980:121-22 (Handlist of Arabic Manuscripts in the Library of the University of Leiden). This is an attestation of pegon interlinear practice predating Sloane 2645 by over a decade - though internally undated (acquired-by date, not copy date). The same article states that MORE THAN 150 Arabic manuscripts with pegon interlinear translations survive in Dutch collections, some from 17th-century Banten, and that pegon palaeography research "is still in its infancy". No dated Sultan Agung-era original letters in pegon surfaced this session; VOC-era Mataram letters remain a lead (Blundeanu worked from received correspondence; originals not verified online).

3. Script caution for Gap 2: the famous early Javanese Islamic manuscripts (Fulda Ba 7 Guide, CUL Gg.5.22, Leiden Or. 266 primbon, Or. 1928 Admo, Or. 11.092 Cat, Ferrara kropak, Bodleian Jav. b. 2 (R)) are mostly written in JAVANESE script, not pegon (Sastrawan et al. 2026 Table 1; Daneshgar & Wieringa 2023). They evidence pre-1610 dluwang book production and the Idah fi l-Fiqh curriculum but must not be cited as pegon witnesses. CUL Gg.5.22 carries only microscopic Arabic-script ownership traces plus closing formulae.

5. A.51/W.277 detail (Gap 4): both are PNRI Qur'ans from 18th-century Banten sharing IDENTICAL interlinear Malay translation texts and layout; A.51 uses a non-Hafs reading system (Warsh/Qalun) with qira'at options and variant verse counts in the margins, while W.277 converts the Quranic text toward Hafs and strips the extra marginal apparatus (simpler look, beginner-friendly); the single-volume RAS Arabic 4 applies consistent Hafs and was probably copied LAST, referring to both predecessors (Nurtawab 2025 par. 25-26; Nurtawab 2023:11-19). The same palace library holds the supporting apparatus: Jalalayn copies with Javanese interlinear translation, diagonal/vertical loghatan grammar symbols and tajwid signs (A.55a, 48.5x34.5 cm, one juz per volume; A.59a-d covering juz 5-9), a vocalized Tafsir al-Baghawi with interlinear Javanese (per Friederich & van den Berg 1873:69), and the Jam' al-Jalalayn gloss A.60 copied 1178H/1764AD for Pangeran Ratu Abu al-Mafakhir Muhammad Aliyuddin (r. 1773-1799 per Nurtawab 2025; blog gives 1777-1802 - discrepancy flagged). Physical profile of PNRI A.51a-e (five volumes; pages 496/643/608/601/691; covers 31x20 cm; heavy ink corrosion; Dutch seizure after the 1832 exile) comes from Ali Akbar's specialist blog (https://quran-nusantara.blogspot.com/2012/04/banten-mushaf-asal-banten-koleksi.html) and awaits catalogue confirmation.

6. Jenggotan/makna gandul documentation (Gap 5): added four Indonesian academic sources to van Bruinessen 1990. Anchor source: Irhamni, Ulumuna 15(1):95-118, 2011, DOI https://doi.org/10.20414/ujis.v15i1.211 - grounded-theory fieldwork on living jenggotan practice at Pesantren Gondanglegi, Malang, concluding the technique is sustained by two values (kepesantrenan and intellectual-academic). Corroboration: Syaiful 2009 skripsi on makna gandul at PP al-Luqmaniyyah Yogyakarta (all grade levels; still relevant; https://digilib.uin-suka.ac.id/2226/); Wahyuni & Ibrahim 2017 (DOI https://doi.org/10.32699/mq.v17i1.920); Jamaluddin 2019 (ResearchGate 369947665). Historic depth added by Nurtawab 2025 par. 20, which projects the loghatan/ngalogat symbol-glossing habit of kitab kuning study back onto the 18th-century Banten manuscript A.55a.
