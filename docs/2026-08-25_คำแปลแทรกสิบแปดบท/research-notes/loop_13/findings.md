## Key findings

### 1. Components precisely defined (RQ1)
- Kanbun kundoku 漢文訓読 is not translation in the strict sense: it produces no parallel target-language text; the Chinese original stays present as a "visual outline" guiding comprehension (Alberizzi 2014, citing Ishizuka Harumichi). https://shesl.org/dossier7-lecture-vernaculaire/intro-kunten-gloss/
- The kunten apparatus comprises (Alberizzi's enumeration, all displayed in a single line of the 1168 Hizōhōyaku manuscript): compound marks on/kun gōfu 音訓合符; phonogram glosses kanaten 仮名点 (= small-kana readings supplying inflections/postpositions, i.e., okurigana-type suffixes and furigana-type readings); tone glosses shōten 声点 (Middle Chinese tone dots); inversion glosses kaeriten 返点 (re/一二三四... ordering marks converting SVO to SOV); punctuation kutōten 句読点; plus the morphosyntactic dot system wokototen ヲコト点 whose shape+position inside/around the character square (tsubo 壺) encodes particles/auxiliaries (te, ni, wo, wa, koto, nari, su...). https://shesl.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/alberizzi2014.pdf
- Quantitative profile of real wokototen practice (Tsutsumi et al. 2023): dot ・ dominates (2,199/6,411 marks), then single-stroke shapes ｜ ― ＼ └; readings te(203)/wo(199)/ni(199)/to/no/ha most frequent; marks cluster at four corners+center, lower-right corner commonest, and outside-character marks sit overwhelmingly on the right side like furigana. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10521107/

### 2. Historical development (RQ2)
- Oldest extant glossed Japanese manuscript: Zokukegonkyō ryakuso kanjōki 続華厳経略疏刊定記, transcribed by layman Chikyō 智鏡, collated at Tōdaiji in Enryaku 2 and 7 (783, 788); white pigment = punctuation only, vermillion = syntactic reorder marks — an archaic two-stage division of labor between mark types (Alberizzi 2014).
- Another Nara-era witness: Sato-bon Kegon mongiyoketsu (text brought to Japan before 751) carries brown-ink kunten c.800 — simple wokototen, punctuation, and kaeriten — with genuine uncertainty whether they served Korean or Japanese vernacular reading (Cornell/Whitman page; Kobayashi Yoshinori 2004). https://conf.ling.cornell.edu/japanese_historical_linguistics/
- Continental roots: Chinese punct/gloss conventions (incl. poyin 破音 red-dot alternate readings) attested from 6th-7th c.; Beishi records Turfan reading Chinese classics "in their own words"; Seol Chong credited in Samguk sagi with Silla-language reading of the Nine Classics (Alberizzi 2014).
- Evolution into print: the kunten habit carried into early-modern movable-type books — witness NINJAL's database for the old movable-type third-edition Shangshu 尚書（古活字版第三種本）with searchable wokototen, gōfu 合符, shōten, and gojunten 語順点 (https://cid.ninjal.ac.jp/kunten-syousyo3/). Note: the term "sōrōbun" belongs to Edo epistolary hentai-kanbun, not to print kunten per se (Airiti abstract, separate genre) — do not conflate.
- Decline curve (Kotobank/Nipponica, Tsukimoto Masayuki): devised c.800 by Nara scholar-monks; flourished through mid-Heian; ossified in Kamakura; replaced largely by kana glosses; almost extinct after Muromachi/Edo except vestiges. https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B9%8E%E5%8F%A4%E6%AD%A2%E7%82%B9-40163

### 3. Institutional context: wokototen school mapping (RQ3)
- Over one hundred distinct wokototen systems existed; Nakata Norio 中田祝夫's canonical eight-group classification (by corner positions of te-ni-wo-ha), with principal named systems (Kotobank, Nipponica + Sekai Daihyakka):
  - Group 1: Nishihaka-ten 西墓点 (Tendai Jimon line per Historist; also Nitohaka-ten)
  - Group 2: Kitain-ten 喜多院点 (Hossō line per Historist)
  - Group 3: Tōdaiji Sanron-shū-ten 東大寺三論宗点 (Sanron lineage confirmed!), Chūin-sōjō-ten
  - Group 4: Teniharu-ten 天仁波流点
  - Group 5: Endō-ten 円堂点 (Shingon Ninnaji line), kiden/myōgyō (Hakase-house Confucian) points
  - Group 6: Eizan-ten 叡山点 (Zenrinji-ten)
  - Group 7: Hōdōin-ten 宝幢院点
  - Group 8: Jungyō-ōshō-ten 順暁和尚点
- Origins hypothesis (Alberizzi 2014 + Sekai Daihyakka): earliest sophisticated notation tied to Kegon 華厳宗 and the Six Nara Schools at Tōdaiji; diffusion to Tendai/Shingon via Ishiyamadera on the Enryakuji-Ninnaji axis; transmission to laity incl. Emperor Uda. Complexity example: Nishihaka-ten uses 31 boxes/230 glosses; 200+ schemes in 9th-10th c. reduced to ~a dozen by the 11th c.
- NINJAL quantitative infrastructure: WokotoDB digitizes the 26 major school charts (Tsukishima, Kuntengo gosei shūsei vol. 1, 2007) with 7x7-grid coordinates; machine classification reproduces Tsukishima's group assignment for 153/159 charts (>95%) from star-dot positions alone (Tsutsumi et al. 2021, 2022, 2023). https://cid.ninjal.ac.jp/wokototendb ; https://ipsj.ixsq.nii.ac.jp/record/215787/files/IPSJ-CH2021026.pdf
- Actual manuscripts vs idealized school charts differ measurably: inductively compiled charts foreground particles te/wo/ni while comprehensive school charts foreground auxiliary verbs su/nari/tari; real marks also stray rightward/outside characters more than schemata show (Tsutsumi et al. 2023).

### 4. Kunten as linguistic data (RQ4)
- Scale of the corpus: Tsukishima's nationwide census counts 3,355 kunten manuscripts dated 8th-13th c. (783-900: 180; 901-1000: 189; 1001-1100: 582; 1101-1200: 2404); only ~100 thoroughly studied (Alberizzi 2014, citing Tsukishima 1996, p. 26).
- Why crucial: kunten materials use vocabulary/grammar differing from kana literature — evidence of linguistic diversity and of the kundoku register; the early-Heian Saidaiji Konkōmyōsaishōōkyō vol. 1 preserves first-half-9th-c. Japanese, capturing Old Japanese decline and subsequent developments (NINJAL CHJ Heian II overview). https://clrd.ninjal.ac.jp/chj/heian-en.html
- Corpus infrastructure: CHJ Heian Period Series II delivers a morphologically annotated kundokubun of that sutra (Ver. 0.4, 2022, Yanagihara et al.), searchable via Chūnagon; related XML/TXT downloads exist. Series III covers Heian kanbun-based mixed-style texts (Hokke Hyakuza Kikigaki-shō etc.).
- Phonology angle: kana phonogram glosses show 9th-c. orthographic instability (multiple variants per syllable), 10th-c. school-stabilized 48-sound sets, 11th-c. single-variant standardization (Alberizzi 2014) — a datable record of Late Old Japanese segmental inventory; shōten tone dots carry Middle Chinese tonal categories underlying Sino-Japanese pitch (component verified; detailed exploitation not covered in fetched texts).
- Caveat for linguistic use: glosses are provisional jottings tied to writer competence, with frequent errors; spoken-vs-written mixture hard to disentangle (Alberizzi 2014).

### 5. Modern research infrastructure and AI (RQ5)
- Databases: WokotoDB (school chart DB), Shangshu print-kunten DB, NINJAL structured-description project 訓点資料の構造化記述 (with Korean gugyeol collaboration; prototype digital transcriptions paired with original images; sample text Kongōchō... kyō vol. 1 annotated in Insei period). https://www.ninjal.ac.jp/research/cr-project/project/c/kunten/kunten-detail
- English entry points: Alberizzi 2014 (SHESL dossier ed. Whitman/Cinato) and Whitman et al. 2010 "Toward an International Vocabulary for Research on Vernacular Readings of Chinese Texts", Scripta 2 (PDF: http://conf.ling.cornell.edu/whitman/WhitmanAlberizziTsukimotoKosukegawa2010Toward.pdf) proposing stable English terms ("vernacular reading" for kundoku, "gloss" for kunten).
- AI: Kanbun-LM (Waseda, ACL Findings 2023) built the first Classical-Chinese-to-Kanbun dataset and tasks; follow-up work (arXiv:2511.05239) formalizes kaeriten decoding with an automaton/transducer, generates kundoku marks with LLMs validated by the automaton, and fine-tunes classical-Chinese LMs — demonstrating the kunten protocol is computationally complete. https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.545/ ; code https://github.com/nlp-waseda/Kanbun-LM

### 6. Comparison hooks (RQ6)
- Korea gugyeol 口訣: sign-based puho kugyŏl (incl. stylus kak'pil depressions — same instrument class as Japanese kakuhitsu 角筆) vs character-based muncha kugyŏl/chat'o 字吐; two methods — sŏktok/hundok kugyŏl with inversions (t'o 吐 marks + yŏktokchŏm 逆讀點) vs udok/sundok kugyŏl without; earliest extant 10th-13th c. but likely 7th-8th-c. origin; declined late 13th c.; Chosŏn state ŏnhae editions under Kan'gyŏng togam. (Sungkyun JEAS 2025.) Japan-Korea cross-influence: 2000 discovery of wokototen-like organized glosses in 11th-c. Korean documents (Alberizzi 2014); NINJAL project explicitly pursued Japan-Korea comparison; gugyeol studies use an analogous 5x5 coordinate grid (data-incompatible with NINJAL's 7x7). https://read.dukeupress.edu/sungkyun-journal-of-east-asian-studies/article/25/1/51/400317/The-Power-of-Sound-Rethinking-the-Invention-of-the
- Vietnam: only a passing scholarly claim located — traces of kundoku-like practice in Vietnamese documents (Alberizzi 2014 citing Wezler/Hammerschmidt 1992, pp. 649-650). No dedicated source harvested this loop.
