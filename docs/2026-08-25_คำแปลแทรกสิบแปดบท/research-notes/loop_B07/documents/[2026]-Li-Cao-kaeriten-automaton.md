## Content notes

- Scope: focuses exclusively on Kaeriten (reading order) and Okurigana (grammatical/inflectional suffixes); Kutoten punctuation assumed already present in most books. Kundoku system = three mark types guiding conversion of Classical Chinese into Japanese.
- Kaeriten reduced to three functional classes:
  - Re-mark: character must appear immediately after its successor; implemented as stack Push/Pop.
  - Ordinal-hierarchy marks (一二, 上下, 甲乙): explicit ordering with enforced nesting hierarchy preserving LIFO.
  - Hyphen mark (-): binds characters into one movable unit.
- Expressiveness theorem: sequences sortable by these marks = Knuth's stack-sortable permutations, count a_n = C(2n,n) - C(2n,n-1) = Catalan number C_n/(n+1)... stated as 1/(n+1) * binom(2n,n). With the hyphen mark the structure becomes a "stack of queues" (Atkinson & Stitt 2002); generating function given; closed form via Kruchinin & Kruchinin 2013; OEIS A078482 cited as theoretical upper bound of kaeriten expressiveness.
- Percentage of n! permutations expressible by kaeriten (Table 1): n=1-3: 100%; n=4: 20/24 (83.33%); n=5: 70/120 (58.33%); n=6: 254/720 (35.28%); n=7: 948/5040 (18.81%); n=8: 3618/40320 (9.97%). Interpretation quoted: natural-language syntax imposes strong structural constraints; kaeriten reduces the reasoning space increasingly with sentence length.
- Formal artifact: pushdown automaton PDA over character-mark pairs (states q0-q4; mark alphabet {E, re, O_ij, hyphen} where O_ij = ordinal mark with hierarchy number i and ordinal j) that accepts exactly valid annotations; companion transducer outputs reordered characters; appending okurigana yields the Japanese rendering.
- Combinatorics lineage credited: Shimano 2009 (tree-structure and matrix models of kaeriten reading; recursive formula counting describable permutations), Shimano 2012 (solved recursion, generating function), Shimano 2018 (kundoku process equivalent to a context-free language in the Chomsky hierarchy). Rule-based generation lineage: Yasuoka 2018 (UD-parse-driven kaeriten generation), Yasuoka 2020 (okurigana attachment dictionary). Prior neural pipeline: Wang et al. 2023b (Kanbun-LM, ~3,400 Tang-poem sentence pairs, no mark annotations).
- New dataset: 9,292 sentences / 95,066 characters built from kanbun.info (Web Kanbun-Taikei acknowledged as biggest open resource); genres history, philosophy, military strategy, poetry across periods; length distribution 6,099 <10 chars; 2,667 in 10-20; 388 in 20-30; 138 >30. Marks generated by rule-based alignment; PDA used as validator; almost all generated kaeriten accepted by the PDA (failures traced to errors in the source Japanese translations, manually corrected). Okurigana/particle generation: GPT-4o-refined GiNZA POS tagging; of 42,473 marked characters, 11,834 corrected manually; LLM annotation acceptance rate 72.14%. Fine-grained scheme distinguishes okurigana from particle kana (label spaces: okurigana 434, particle 476, kaeriten 20).
- Modeling: sequence-tagging formulation; multi-task fine-tuning of classical-Chinese pretrained LMs (sikubert, bert-ancient-chinese, roberta-classical-chinese-base-char) plus bert-base-japanese-char baseline; auxiliary classical-Chinese tasks (word segmentation, POS tagging, dependency arc labeling 115 labels, dependency type labeling 44 labels via HanLP parses); best results generally with two auxiliary tasks; classical-Chinese pretraining decisively helps.
- Headline numbers (Table 3, on Wang et al. dataset, roberta-classical-chinese-base-char base): their approach BLEU 55.51 / RIBES 63.67 / TER 27.13 / Kendall tau 92.23 / PMR 90.00 / PDA pass rate 95.41 vs Wang et al. best: BLEU 51.40 / RIBES 58.30 / tau 94.40 / PMR 78.30 - i.e., better translation and perfect-match ordering metrics with fewer parameters and character-level annotation output.
- LLM evaluation (Table 4, 50 random test sentences): few-shot Gemini-3-pro-preview highest BLEU 73.23; but on kaeriten annotation/ordering metrics all evaluated LLMs (DeepSeek-V3.2, Gemini-3-pro/flash-preview, GPT-5.2) fall below the fine-tuned tagger. Applying the authors' annotation pipeline to few-shot Gemini translations lifted Kendall tau to 96.18 and PMR to 93.72: conclusion that LLMs possess implicit CC-to-Japanese ordering knowledge but often fail to express it explicitly in marks; the tagger can supplement LLM output.
- Limitations admitted: still low-resource; semi-automatic dataset construction needs expert correction; coarse mark classification based on linguistic intuition.

## Quotable formulations
- "We theoretically analyze the expressiveness of Kaeriten marks and design an automaton with transducer that decodes characters with marks into Japanese sentences. This validates our hypothesis that reducing classical Chinese-Japanese translation to Kundoku marks tagging tasks is both theoretically sound and practically feasible."
- On the historical system: "The high acceptance rate demonstrates the validity of our Kaeriten marks generation method. This result also underscores the nature of the historical annotation system and the syntactic alignment between classical Chinese and Japanese."
