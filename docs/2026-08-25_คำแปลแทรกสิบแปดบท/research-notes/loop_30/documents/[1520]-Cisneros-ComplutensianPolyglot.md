# Complutensian Polyglot Bible (1514-1517, pub. 1520) and the English Hexapla (Bagster, 1841) — interlinear/parallel layouts for ch. 18

## Metadata
- Complutensian Polyglot: Biblia polyglotta, Alcalá de Henares: Arnao Guillén de Brocar for Cardinal Francisco Jiménez de Cisneros (1436-1517), printed 1514-1517, formal publication delayed to 1520/1522.
- Authoritative descriptions harvested:
  - Library of Congress, World Digital Library record (vol. 1): https://www.loc.gov/resource/gdcwdl.wdl_10636_001/?sp=2&st=list
  - Harvard Divinity School Library exhibit "The First Polyglot Bible": https://library.hds.harvard.edu/exhibits/incomparable-treasure/complutensian-polyglot
  - Bridwell Library (SMU) exhibition note: https://bridwell.omeka.net/exhibits/show/earlygreek/newtestament/polyglot
  - Full scans: https://archive.org/details/polyglotcomplutensis and https://archive.org/details/complutensian_polyglot_bible

## Verified layout facts (LoC + HDS + Bridwell)
- Six volumes; vols 1-4 Old Testament; vol 5 New Testament; vol 6 Hebrew/Aramaic/Greek dictionaries and study aids.
- OT page: upper three-quarters in three columns — Greek Septuagint (inside/left), Latin Vulgate (center), Hebrew (outside/right); the Septuagint column carries an original Latin interlinear translation composed by the Complutensian editors (word-for-word under the Greek).
- Pentateuch (vol 1): lower section in two columns — Targum Onkelos (Aramaic, inside) with its own Latin translation (outer); first printing of Targum Onkelos.
- NT (vol 5): two parallel columns — Greek left, Latin Vulgate right; first printed Greek New Testament (colophon dated 10 January 1514); includes Greek-Latin lexicon ("beginning of New Testament lexicography" per textandcanon analysis).
- Editio princeps of the complete Septuagint; ~600 sets printed, ~123 surviving (figure repeated by LoC-linked description and archive.org record).
- Editorial context verified via textandcanon.org essay (https://textandcanon.org/the-incredible-complutensian-polyglot-bible/, 2026): Cisneros invited Erasmus to collaborate (declined); Erasmus's 1516 privilege delayed Complutensian publication; López de Zúñiga/Stunica polemic against Erasmus; HDS adds that Ximénez's death and papal approval delayed release to 1521/22; Vulgate placed between Greek and Hebrew with symbolic rationale reported in prefatory materials.

## English Hexapla (Samuel Bagster and Sons, London, 1841)
- Full title substance: "The English Hexapla, Exhibiting the Six Important English Translations of the New Testament Scriptures... the original Greek text after Scholz... Preceded by An Historical Account of the English Translations" (by S. P. Tregelles, c. 160+ pages).
- The six versions in parallel columns beneath the Greek: Wiclif 1380; Tyndale 1534; Cranmer (= Great Bible) 1539; Genevan 1557; Anglo-Rhemish (Rheims) 1582; Authorised/King James 1611. Greek base text after M. A. Scholz (compiled 1830-1836, largely following Griesbach).
- Scans: https://archive.org/details/1841-english-hexapla ; https://archive.org/details/ENGHEX_DBS_HS ; https://archive.org/details/englishhexaplaex00schouoft
- Descriptions cross-checked across bookseller bibliographic records (Rooke Books full-title transcription; davidclachman.com note "Greek text at the top and the six translations in parallel columns below") — Wikipedia-derived mirrors used for keywords only, not cited.
- Bagster's firm self-described on the title page as a "warehouse for Bibles, New Testaments, Prayer-books, Lexicons, Grammars, Concordances, and Psalters, in ancient and modern languages."
- Term note: "hexapla" = sixfold/six-columned; the volume's own apparatus invokes the precedent of Origen's third-century six-column Old Testament (Hebrew, Greek transliteration, Aquila, Symmachus, Septuagint, Theodotion) — attested in the archive.org item descriptions and polyglot-history aggregators; detailed Hexapla claims not independently verified this loop beyond these secondary notices.

## Relevance to ch. 18
- Milestone chain for interlinear/parallel Christian reference layout: Origen's Hexapla (conceptual ancestor) -> Complutensian Polyglot (first printed polyglot Bible; Latin interlinear under Greek LXX; Targum+Latin section) -> Erasmus's annotated parallel Greek-Latin NT (1516) -> later polyglots (Antwerp/Regia 1568-73, Walton's London Polyglot 1657, per aggregator pages, verify separately if used) -> 19th-century vernacular hexaplas (English Hexapla 1841) -> digital interlinears (BibleHub etc., present day, informal infrastructure).
