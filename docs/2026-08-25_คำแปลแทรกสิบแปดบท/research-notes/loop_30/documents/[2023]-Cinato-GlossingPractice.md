## Verified content notes
- HTL-CNRS notice (translated substance): "the first book to focus specifically on the topic of comparative glossing"; brings together new research on glossing practices from traditions in both the West and East Asia, with a focus on Japan; touches on the relation between glossing in the medieval manuscript tradition and the modern linguistic use of the gloss; glosses are "a cross-cultural phenomenon anchored in language... manifestation of hermeneutic processes involved in the transfer of knowledge from one linguistic area to another."
- Zisk chapter verified via academia.edu record: Matthew Zisk, 2023, "Glossing Glosses: Methods for Transcribing and Glossing Japanese Kundoku Texts" (Crossref DOI 10.5040/9781666995886.ch-3). Abstract fragment: kundoku = "reading LS [Literary Sinitic] through interpretation", word-by-word rendering into Japanese; Kasuga's transcription methods influenced over 100 subsequent kundoku text publications.
- Related comparative article by two of the same network: Pádraic Moran and John Whitman, "Glossing and Reading in Western Europe and East Asia: A Comparative Case Study", Speculum 97/1 (2022), DOI 10.1086/717331 (Crossref-verified authorship and date). Compares Irish glossing (St Gall Priscian) and Japanese kunten annotation (Saidaiji Konkomyo saishoo kyo manuscript, Kasuga's 1943 edition); findings include similar chronology of book literacy then glossed manuscripts (Ireland 5th c./Japan mid-6th c.), glossing as stepping-stone to vernacular literary traditions, and the role of abstract symbols alongside verbal glosses.
