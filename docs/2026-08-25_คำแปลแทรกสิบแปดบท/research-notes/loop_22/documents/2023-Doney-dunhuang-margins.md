## Content notes (from retrieved material)

Abstract: the article explores the disparity between the Central Tibetan Buddhist doctrines espoused and spread by the Tibetan Empire (Bod chen po, ca. 7th c. to 842) and those of the multi-ethnic inhabitants of Dunhuang during the same period. It unpacks Tri Songdetsen's (742-ca. 800) royal discourse (bka' mchid) of doctrine spread through the empire by state administrative machinery; the second half focuses on Tibetan-ruled Dunhuang (late 750s/early 760s or 787 to 848) and evidence of beliefs not contained in the royal discourse, examining the Aparimitayurnamasutra from content and from scribal-practice evidence across its many copies in Mogao Cave 17.

Companion paper verified: Doney, "Tibetan Ritual Texts and the Uṣṇīṣavijayādhāraṇī on the First Panel of IOL Tib J 466", BuddhistRoad Paper 2.7 (2023), https://omp.ub.rub.de/index.php/BuddhistRoad/catalog/download/266/243/1351?inline=1 :
- IOL Tib J 466: first panel (466/1 ritual texts + 466/2 Usnisavijaya dharani) added as a patch of paper backed with a darker piece before writing — physical broadening of a ritual collection; panel 466/3 contains prayers including praise of Tri Songdetsen.
- Ends of phrases of the Buddha marked with vertical dotted lines between double shad signs (per Zorin's related description of Дх. Тиб. 217).
- Colophon example: IOL Tib J 374/1 ends "mchod pa bsdus pa'i le'u rdzogs + ho" attributed to dge slong dpal brtsegs.
- Doney/Dalton-van Schaik catalogue context: Dalton & van Schaik 2006, Tibetan Tantric Manuscripts from Dunhuang (Brill), remains the standard descriptive catalogue of the Stein collection tantric materials.

Related hybrid Chan-Tantra manuscript evidence (from BuddhistRoad Paper 2.5, Goodman 2022, same repository):
- van Schaik & Dalton 2004 "Where Chan and Tantra Meet" linked P.T. 626, P.T. 634 and P.T. 699 (five 'hybrid' manuscripts P.T. 322, 626, 634, 699, 808 likely one scribe/hand); Dalton identified the same dbu-can/cursive hand in IOL Tib J 320 sharing a decorative marker.
- Three copies of the Lung chung ("Small Treatise"): IOL Tib J 689, IOL Tib J 1774, P.T. 121. Most extensive three-samadhi treatment at Dunhuang: IOL Tib J 437/2.
- van Schaik 2014, The Tibetan Chan Manuscripts: A Complete Descriptive Catalogue (Indiana University) is the companion catalogue for Chan texts.
