## Content notes (from fetched material)

The new fragments of the Two Volume Lexicon (sgra sbyor bam po gnyis pa), also known as Madhyavyutpatti ("Middle Vyutpatti", bye brag tu rtogs byed 'bring po), discovered at Tabo monastery in Spiti, Himachal Pradesh, suggest that the Lexicon was very likely composed in 783 CE during the reign of King Trisong Detsen (khri srong lde btsan, r. 755-797). The article illustrates each dharma-translation method (chos bsgyur ba'i thabs) witnessed both in the first part of the Lexicon (imperial decree, 783) in the Tabo version and in the version (imperial decree, 814) stored in the Tibet Museum (bod ljongs rten rdzas bshams mdzod khang).

Key translation-method examples documented in the article:
- skad gcig las ming du mar 'dren pa: deriving several terms from one Indian word according to contextual meaning.
- anuśaya rendered either phra rgyas or bag la nyal ("dormancies") depending on context.
- Names of places, sentient beings, flowers and plants: if translating makes the rendering confusing or doubtful, keep the Indian word phonetically preceded by a generic term: yul vārāṇasī, chu srin makara. Earlier 'khor mo 'jig for Vārāṇasī was "hard to understand".
- kauśika left untranslated per the rule on unnecessary translation (e.g., vyākaraṇa-type names such as rtswa kuśa thogs pa, mkhas pa, pad ma la dga' ba, 'ug pa, mdzod ldan should be left in Sanskrit unless necessary).
- Historical passage quoted: under Trisong Detsen a number of chos kyi skad terms not known in Tibetan were coined; some did not accord with chos kyi gzhung and vyākaraṇa'i sgra'i lugs and were corrected; under Khri lde srong btsan experts gathered, coined and finalized terms, and the emperor decreed them mi bcos bar bzhag pa ("not to be falsified/altered").
- The article draws a modern parallel with twentieth-century Chinese-to-Tibetan terminology committees (e.g., rin mīn mnga' 'bangs / mi dmangs for renmin "people"; rgyal spyi for guoji "international"), showing the same two principles: do not violate the meaning, and render fluently in Tibetan.
