## Content notes (from fetched material)

- Modern Tibetan books head their contents pages with the word dkar chag; but the traditional genre answers "Contents of what?" — these are "among the most challenging, intriguing and fascinating documents for the historian of Tibetan culture, society, religion, politics" and remain underused by researchers ("just boring lists").
- Types: majority are tables of contents to collected works (gsung 'bum) or multivolume sets; others are bibliographies of bodies of Buddhist teaching or hand-lists of library holdings; many are inventories of holy places, temples, monasteries, mchod rten.
- The most significant dkar chag for Buddhist studies and perhaps the oldest surviving text of the genre is one of three imperial "hand-lists" of scriptural manuscripts: the lDan dkar ma catalogue. The other two, the 'Phang thang ma (the earliest) and the mChims phu ma, were known to Tibetan writers of past centuries but have yet to come to light. The lDan dkar ma helps ascertain which texts were actually available in late imperial times (early 9th century). These three are forerunners of later canon catalogues such as Bu ston's 1322 Chos 'byung catalogue and the catalogues accompanying woodblock-printed Kangyur editions (Lhasa, sDe dge, Co ne, etc.).
- Celebrated examples of temple dkar chags: the Fifth Dalai Lama's Shel dkar me long (1645) on the Jo khang; his regent Sangs rgyas rgya mtsho's exhaustive two-volume 1697 work on the Potala stupa naming about 1500 artisans from Tibet, India, Kashmir, China, Nepal.
- Conceptual frame: dkar chags may be written for any of the Three Receptacles (rten gsum): Body (icons), Speech (bka', especially the bKa' 'gyur), Mind (stupa) — the genre's key.
- Related scholarship noted by Martin: Lalou 1953 on texts under Khri srong lde btsan; Vostrikov 1970: 205-230 on the genre; Eimer 1983 on canon catalogues; Ferrari 1958 geography from a dkar chag. An Old Tibetan legal document uses dkar chags suggesting "to be proven blameless".
