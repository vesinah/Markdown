## Key findings (with URLs)

1. Scribal audit culture at imperial Dunhuang is documented by an actual paper-debt register: IOL Tib J 1359 records blank-paper assignments returned as copied sutras, with debts enforced by confiscation up to twice the value and whipping (rgyab chad, ten lashes per ten sheets); the office operated as a "distributed scriptorium" using Chinese-style village heads (li ceng). Source (page fetch blocked 403; content from search excerpt): https://idp.bl.uk/blog/scribes-at-dunhuang-pens-paper-and-punishments/
2. Colophons functioned as identity anchors: Perfection of Wisdom pothi copies found recently in Central Tibetan monastic libraries were identified as Dunhuang products precisely because their colophons carry the same Chinese scribes' names as Dunhuang manuscripts; scrap paper (legtse) even preserves a rebellious poem signed by scribe Lenho Wenman. https://earlytibet.com/2011/05/10/tibetan-history-i/
3. sa bcad definition and origin debate: Wangchuk reads sa bcad literally as "cutting the ground" — signposts/milestones of a treatise; Steinkellner suggested Chinese origin (via Byang chub rdzu 'phrul evidence) against Indian derivation, and the Tantric "five ligatures" (chings lnga) offer another candidate origin; sa bcad may have originated physically as mchan (marginal glosses) integrated into main text or as teachers' lecture notes. Full text: https://genus-literatura-tibetica.blogspot.com/2016/02/sa-bcad.html
4. Nishizawa (IBK 2021) fixes a terminus for the mature six-topic scholastic sa-bcad format (dbye gzhi, dbye ba'i don, ming gi don, grangs nges pa, mtshan nyid, mtshan nyid nges byed kyi tshad ma): created by early gSang phu ne'u thog scholars in the 11th-12th centuries, NOT adopted by Pa tshab lo tsA ba's line — i.e., the format is a Tibetan scholastic invention, post-dating translation-period texts. https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ibk/70/1/70_376/_article/-char/en ; companion structural studies by Fukuda (IBK 64.1, 2015, free PDF) and Fukuda-Ishihama 1989 comparative tables show commentator-specific outlines.
5. Script question answered at the level of scribal training: van Schaik argues early dbu med was a cursive developed out of dbu can, used throughout the empire and mainly taught to official scribes — so annotations, registers and administrative writing are expected in dbu-med-type cursives while formal scripture uses dbu can. https://brill.com/previewpdf/display/book/edcoll/9789004233454/B9789004233454-s016.xml (full text binary-only; thesis verbatim in excerpt)
6. Tsheg/shad conventions have dating value: the double tsheg was discouraged in Sad na legs's 812 reforms and disappears from dated inscriptions after 812, making it a palaeographical criterion for pre-mid-9th-century Dunhuang manuscripts (Dotson's IOL Tib J 740 statistics: ~75% single tsheg after nga/na/da/ra finals); mid-line and long tshegs likewise vanish from 10th-century hands. Blockprint-era rules add rin chen spungs shad for line-break shad, yig chung small type (~25-30%) reserved for commentaries/annotations/headlines, and sbrul shad for insertions/chapter boundaries. https://readingtibetan.wordpress.com/resources/punctuation/ ; https://digitaltibetan.github.io/DigitalTibetan/docs/tibetan_formatting.html
7. The sgra sbyor bam po gnyis pa / Madhyavyutpatti is a state-level terminology project with two attested decree layers: Tabo fragments indicate composition in 783 under Trisong Detsen, revised under an 814 decree of Khri lde srong btsan preserved at the Tibet Museum, Lhasa; the decree ordered finalized terms to remain mi bcos bar bzhag pa ("not to be altered"), with named committees (e.g., lce khyi 'brug, Jnanadevakosa, Brahmin Ananda under the father's reign). https://journaloftibetanliterature.org/index.php/jtl/article/view/42/210 ; diplomatic analysis: https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/jiabs/article/view/8927
8. The Mahavyutpatti itself is fully digitized multilingually in Bibliotheca Polyglotta (TLB vol. vid=263): sentence-by-sentence Sanskrit-Tibetan with ~283 thematic sections (tathagata names through hell realms), usable directly as a state-glossary case study. https://www2.hf.uio.no/polyglotta/index.php?page=volume&vid=263
9. Kangyur/Tengyur transmission apparatus is well mapped: dkar chag genre spans imperial hand-lists (lDan dkar ma extant; 'Phang thang ma and mChims phu ma lost) to blockprint-edition catalogues (Lhasa, Derge, Co ne); Bu ston's 1322 catalogue and the Tshal pa (1347-51) vs Them spangs ma recension families structure the tradition; Skilling's "Kanjur Titles and Colophons" (1994) covers title/colophon conventions across editions. https://texts.mandala.library.virginia.edu/book_pubreader/15943 ; https://texts.mandala.library.virginia.edu/book_pubreader/15711
10. Hybrid Chan-Tantra annotation culture verified at manuscript level: five Pelliot tibetain manuscripts (P.T. 322, 626, 634, 699, 808) plus IOL Tib J 320 and 689 share one scribal hand and decorative markers, materializing the Chan-Mahayoga syncretism argued by van Schaik & Dalton 2004 ("Where Chan and Tantra Meet"); three copies of the Lung chung compendium (IOL Tib J 689, 1774, P.T. 121) circulated alongside. https://omp.ub.rub.de/index.php/BuddhistRoad/catalog/download/245/222/1273?inline=1
