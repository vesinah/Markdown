## B. The six-column arrangement as described by standard references

1. Britannica, "Hexapla": "compiled by Origen of Alexandria in Caesarea, Palestine, before ad 245. The Hexapla presented for comparison the Hebrew text of the Old Testament, the Hebrew text in Greek characters, and the Greek versions of Aquila, Symmachus, the Septuagint, and Theodotian in six parallel columns. For some books, including the Psalms, Origen presented as many as three additional Greek texts..." https://www.britannica.com/topic/Hexapla ; companion essay "Origen's Hexapla" adds: working at Caesarea between 230 and 240 ce; "The main interest of Origen lay in the fifth column" (the recensed LXX with critical signs). https://www.britannica.com/topic/biblical-literature/Origens-Hexapla

2. Swete, An Introduction to the Old Testament in Greek, ch. III "The Hexapla" (full text accessible): order = Hebrew; Hebrew transliterated; Aquila; Symmachus; LXX; Theodotion, with the classic explanation that "Aquila is placed next to the Hebrew text because his translation is the most verbally exact", and a printed specimen of Ps 45(46):1-3 showing word-by-word alignment across columns (Milan palimpsest fragment evidence). https://mail.biblehub.com/library/swete/an_introduction_to_the_old_testament_in_greek_additional_notes/chapter_iii_the_hexapla_and.htm

3. Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), "Hexapla": Jerome and others agree on the six-column order; fragments show "one or at most two Hebrew words were placed on each line, with the transliteration in the adjoining column and the various renditions in the succeeding columns, all on the same level"; fifth-column obelus/asterisk/metobelus system described (obelus = LXX without Hebrew counterpart; asterisk = Hebrew matter lacking in LXX). https://www.ecatholic2000.com/cathopedia/vol7/volseven353.shtml

## C. Why this matters for the book's genealogy (ch18)

- The Hexapla is the ancient prototype of synchronised multi-version display: source language (Hebrew), phonetic bridge column (Secunda transliteration - itself an interlinear-style pronunciation aid), and four Greek versions aligned word-by-word, plus marginal critical signs.
- Direct scholarly bridge to loop_30's Complutensian harvest: Salvesen's rationale chapter for the new Hexapla edition was published precisely in the volume celebrating the Fifth Centennial of the Complutensian Polyglot (Brill 2017) - modern scholarship itself treats the two as one lineage.
- Honesty rule for drafting: per hexapla.org, the exact original page-by-page layout is not recoverable; describe the arrangement as attested by patristic testimony and the surviving fragmentary witnesses (Milan palimpsest Rahlfs 1098, Cairo Genizah psalter fragment Rahlfs 2005, Syro-Hexapla tradition), not as a preserved artifact.
