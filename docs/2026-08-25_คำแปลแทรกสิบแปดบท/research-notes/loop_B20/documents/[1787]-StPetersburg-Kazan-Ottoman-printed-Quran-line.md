# Printed Qur'an milestones outside/around the Ottoman ban: St Petersburg 1787, Kazan 1803, Ottoman official print 1874 (Sekerzade), first Ottoman lithographed mushaf 1887/88

Harvested: Loop B20, 2026-08-26. Purpose: add one more verifiable print-history milestone beyond loop_29's Zengin 2023 (1874 state mushaf) and Wilson 2009, and connect the Kazan line to the Turkic-interlinear geography.

## 1. St Petersburg 1787 - first printed complete Qur'an in Russia / first Muslim-audience metal-type mushaf

Source A (institutional portal page fetched in full): "St. Petersburg Editions of the Quran", published 18 Mar 2025, https://igelek.tatar/en/publications/st-petersburg-editions-of-the-quran/

Verbatim core claims:
- "In 1783, Russian Empress Catherine II signed a decree on free printing houses."
- "The complete Arabic text of the Quran was printed here [the Asiatic Printing House of Johann Karl Schnoor] for the first time in Russia in 1787. By the order of Catherine II, it was published in an edition of 1200 copies."
- "The text was prepared for printing by the Tatar calligrapher Usman Ismail... He also prepared explanations on how to read the text, which were placed in the margins of the main text of Quran. These features allow researchers to speak of the Islamic character of the St. Petersburg Quran, setting it apart from its European predecessors."
- "Over the next 10 years, this edition of the Quran was reprinted six times." In 1798 Paul I ordered another Senate-printing-house edition.
- Equipment/fonts later moved to Kazan where mass printing began.

Source B (encyclopedic): Encyclopaedia of the Qur'an entry "Russian Arabic Edition of the Qur'an, St. Petersburg, 1787" (EQCOM_056211) exists at https://referenceworks.brillonline.com/entries/encyclopaedia-of-the-quran/russian-arabic-edition-of-the-quran-st-petersburg-1787-EQCOM_056211 - snippet visible only (paywalled body).

Source C (digitised copy): Basel University Library scan mirrored at https://archive.org/details/quran-st-petersburg-1787 ("St. Petersburg, 1787, during the reign of Catherine II... Early Printed complete Qur'an in Arabic using metal type", source e-rara.ch/bau_1/doi/10.3931/e-rara-84359).

## 2. Kazan 1803 - the famous "Kazan Basmasy"

Source A (institutional portal news, fetched in full): https://igelek.tatar/en/news/220-years-later-the-most-famous-kazan-edition-in-the-world-quran-kazan-basmasy-was-printed-for-the-first-time-in-modern-mushaf-format/
- "It was printed in 1803 in the Asian Printing House in Kazan"; pre-revolutionary Kazan edition "went down in history as the first printed Qur'an that received theological recognition in the Muslim world and corresponded to the Ar-Rasm Al-Usmani standard"; unique font; companion piece "Two-volume Kazan edition of the Quran published in 1803" linked there.
- Discrepancy note: a translated Arabic essay (Tulayhah blog, secondary, use with caution) dates the first Kazan edition to 1801 under Muhammad Shaakir Murtezaoğlu and states it "did not adhere to the written form of the 'Uthmani Mushaf nor did it include verse numbers": https://tulayhah.wordpress.com/2021/02/20/a-history-of-the-printing-of-the-quran/ . Same essay: Iran lithographs Tehran 1828 and Tabriz 1833; Bulaq (Cairo) first print most likely 1833 with errors prompting confiscation pressure; India Lucknow 1850. These regional dates corroborate Zengin's point that printed mushafs circulated while Ottoman law still banned them.

## 3. Ottoman official print named witness: Sekerzade Mushaf-i Serifi, Istanbul 1291/1874

Source (IRCICA library official page, OIC research centre, fetched via search capture): https://library.ircica.org/rare-books-and-manuscripts-collection/
- Verbatim list item: "The first Mushaf officially printed in the Ottomans: Sekerzade Mushaf-i Serif, Istanbul, 1291/1874."
- Same page also lists "The first book printed in Ottoman Turkish: Mehmed b. Mustafa Vankulu, Lugat-i Vankulu, Istanbul, 1141/1729" (Muteferrika era context) and notes IRCICA's manuscript collection includes works in Ottoman Turkish, Arabic, Persian AND Chagatai.
- This names the specific edition behind Zengin 2023's "first legal state mushaf printed 1874 under Ministry of Education supervision".

## 4. Lithography branch (dealer description - flag: commercial provenance)

Melikian Collection item page, "Ottoman Lithographic Qur'an": https://melikiancollection.com/artwork/ottoman-lithographic-quran-5727/
- "The first lithographic press in Istanbul was founded by a Frenchmen Henri Cayol, in 1831".
- "The first Qur'an in the Ottoman Empire was not lithographed until 1304 Rumi (1887 or 1888), when the stone printed version of the holy text based on the manuscript of Hafiz Osman was produced under a strict supervision of the sultan on the premises of the imperial palace."
- Also: Matbaa-i Osmaniye founded Istanbul 1866 by calligrapher Osman Zeki Bey, who allegedly received a fifty-year monopoly for Qur'an printing in 1880.
- Treat as dealer narrative pending scholarly corroboration; the Cayol-1831 press date is consistent with Kuran-Burcoglu's Matbaa-i Osmaniye study cited by Wilson's bibliography.

## Use for the book

Print-era chain now runs: Muteferrika condition 1727 (no religious books) -> Russian/Muslim-world line St Petersburg 1787 -> Kazan 1803 -> Bulaq 1833 / Tehran 1828 / Tabriz 1833 lithographs -> Cayol litho press Istanbul 1831 -> Sublime Porte decision 1873 -> Sekerzade official mushaf 1291/1874 (IRCICA + Zengin) -> first Ottoman lithographed mushaf 1304/1887-88 -> Matbaa-i Osmaniye monopoly era. Interlinear/mealli Turkish print Qur'ans then develop inside this regulatory history (Wilson 2009; loop_29 records).
