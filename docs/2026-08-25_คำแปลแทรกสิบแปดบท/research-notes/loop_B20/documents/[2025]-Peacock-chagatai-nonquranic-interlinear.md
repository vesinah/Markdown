Source: A.C.S. Peacock, "Medieval Eastern Turkish Interlinear Translation: Their Manuscripts, Audience and Persian Background", Journal of Abbasid Studies 25.3 (2025), pp. 19-50, DOI 10.5617/jais.12693, open access.

## Verbatim key passages (from section "Other Interlinear Translations", pp. 43 ff.)

1. On non-Qur'anic scope: "Compared to Persian the practice of translating the Qur'an into Turkish seems to have remained relatively limited at least until the fifteenth century... However, interest in interlinear translations in Turkophone elite circles was not restricted to Qur'ans, although these remain by far the best known type of such translations, and probably the most common too."

2. Khwarazmian interlinear Tusi: "One such text which has recently come to light is a Khwarazmian Turkish interlinear translation on the Persian text on minerals by the Ilkhanid scholar Nasir al-Din Tusi (d. 1274). Both on palaeographic and philological grounds the translation and manuscript must date to the late fourteenth century. ... the unique manuscript of the interlinear Turkish version, held in al-Azhar Library in Cairo as MS 1252, indicate it is carefully written, using red ink for the translation in contrast to black for the main text, again suggesting it was composed for a patron who wanted a high-quality product."
   - HOLDING VERIFIED: al-Azhar Library, Cairo, MS 1252 (unique manuscript).

3. Chaghatay Qur'an statement: "Over the fifteenth century, Khwarazmian evolved into the language we know as Chaghatay, which was especially patronised in late Timurid Herat. Interlinear Chaghatay Qur'ans are known, but have scarcely been studied" - i.e., the existence of the category is asserted by a peer-reviewed survey, while detailed catalogues remain a desideratum.

4. Navai's Chihil Hadith as the prime interlinear text of the period: "...the prime example of an interlinear text from the period is the Chihil Hadith (Forty Hadith) composed in 1481-1482 by the famous Eastern Turkish litterateur Mir 'Ali Shir Nava'i (1441-1501), who was attached to the prestigious court of Herat. Each of the forty hadith are given in Arabic and then on the line below are followed by Turkish translations in verse, which are themselves based in 'Abd al-Rahman Jami's Persian verse version. While largely ignored today in the pantheon of Nava'i's works, in the fifteenth century and sixteenth centuries this text was copied in several luxury manuscripts (in fact no non-luxury copies of it seem to be known)... including the Suleymaniye Library MSS Nuruosmaniye 4968, Ayasofya 3981/1, Fatih 4056/2, Topkapi Saray Muzesi Revan 328."
   - HOLDINGS VERIFIED (luxury copies): Suleymaniye Nuruosmaniye 4968; Ayasofya 3981/1; Fatih 4056/2; Topkapi Palace Museum Revan 328.

## Census details relevant to catalog evidence (from earlier sections of same PDF)

- Copyist profile of TIEM 73 (Turk ve Islam Eserleri Muzesi, Istanbul): "Muhammad ibn al-Hajj Dawlatshah, the copyist of TIEM 73, was a Sufi or closely associated with Sufi communities, and was active in the court of the Golden Horde rulers of the Crimea".
- Trilingual witnesses: "the Rylands and Tashkent manuscripts are trilingual, containing the complete Persian text alongside the Turkish and Arabic, while all of the Mashhad manuscripts also contain Persian text for some sections (regrettably as Astan-i Quds 1007 has not been published it is not possible to comment more fully on this)".
- Dated Anatolian branch witness: "Istanbul Hekimoglu Cami 2, dated by colophon to the middle of Rabi' II 764/early February 1363".
- Sample of trilingual interrelation (Rylands, transcribed after Ata, Turkce Ilk Kur'an Tercumesi): "Ey anlar kim bittiler sabr kilin menin birle..." - used to argue the Turkish layer sometimes follows the Arabic against the Persian.

## Adjacent verified witness outside this PDF (Leiden University Libraries catalogue)

- Leiden Or. 504: "Koran with interlinear Turkish translation"; copy completed 5 Rajab 926 (= 1 July 1520) by Mehmed b. Saruhan at Gallipoli; "Contains a word by word interlinear Turkish translation, with occasional commentary, in Old Anatolian Turkish of the entire Arabic text. The script is bold for the Arabic text, smaller for the translation." Catalogue reference printed there: Jan Schmidt, Catalogue of Turkish manuscripts in the library of Leiden University and other collections in the Netherlands, vol. 1 (Leiden 2000), Or. 504, pp. 131-134; also Abdukadir Inan (Ankara 1961) and Ahmet Topaloglu ed., Muhammed bin Hamza XV. yuzyil baslarinda yapilmis "satir-arasi" Kur'an tercumesi I (Istanbul 1976).
