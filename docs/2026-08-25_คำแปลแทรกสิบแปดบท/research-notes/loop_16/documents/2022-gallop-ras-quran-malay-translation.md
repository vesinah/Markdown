## Fetched content (key verbatim passages)

"The Royal Asiatic Society holds a Quran manuscript (Arabic 4), which is of exceptional importance as being the only known single-volume Qur'an with a full interlinear translation in Malay."

Provenance: "The manuscript is inscribed 'Presented by Admiral C. M. Pole, Bart., June 19, 1830'. Sir Charles Moris Pole (1757-1830) served in the East Indies Station from 1773 to 1778..." Each page contains nine lines of Arabic in a wide hand "with the Malay text sloping beneath in a much smaller, very fine, hand"; verses separated by small gold roundels; laid paper probably of Asian manufacture.

On dating and Banten attribution: "One example is shown below, of Surat al-Kahf verse 11 ... It is thus highly likely that Arabic 4 originates from Banten, for not only are two of the only four other known Quran copies with interlinear Malay translations (all in multiple volumes) from Banten, so too are three of the eleven known Qur'an manuscripts with Javanese translations, highlighting the association of Banten with this pedagogical tradition." Gold verse markers "very rare in Southeast Asia" also recorded in two other Banten Qur'ans; Asian paper and Pole's service period support an early 18th-century date "at a time when the sultanate of Banten was flourishing as a centre for Islamic scholarship."

Sample comparison (Q 18:11), PNRI A.51 vs RAS Arabic 4 — the two texts share almost identical Malay wording; RAS Arabic 4 reads: "maka kami tutup telinga mereka itu supaya tiada didengar mereka itu suatu suara dan kami keraskan atas mereka itu // tidur dalam guha itu berapa tahun lamanya kata setengah adalah tidur mereka itu dalamnya tiga ratus sembilan tahun * maka tiap2 setahun dibalikkan Allah mereka itu supaya jangan dimakan tanah tetapi pada mereka itu seperti siang hari juga."

Exegetical framing: cites Riddell's chronology (mid-17th-c. Aceh exegetical activity; Tarjuman al-Mustafid by Abd al-Rauf second half of 17th c.; next full Malay tafsir only in 1925-1927, Tafsir Nur al-Ihsan by Muhammad Said bin Umar of Kedah) and Nurtawab's proposal that "Qur'an manuscripts with interlinear translations should certainly be regarded as tafsir, as they were evidently created with the explicit intentions of explicating the meaning of the Qur'an"; Nurtawab identified interpretation and variant views beyond pure translation in PNRI A.51 and W.277, "composed independently of 'Abd al-Ra'uf's work."

Further reading cited in post: Nurtawab, "Qur'anic readings and Malay translations in 18th-century Banten Qur'ans A.51 and W.277", Indonesia and the Malay World 48(141), 2020: 169-189; Riddell, Islam and the Malay-Indonesian world (2001); Riddell, Malay court religion, culture and language (Brill 2017).

## Relevance to Loop 16
Core evidence for research question 1: identifies and dates the only known single-volume Qur'an with complete interlinear Malay translation; situates it within a small corpus of Malay-interlinear Qur'ans concentrated in 18th-century Banten; links interlinear translation to tafsir-level exegesis and pesantren-style pedagogy.
