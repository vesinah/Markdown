## Abstract facts (CEEOL/Akjournals, verbatim fragments)

"This paper first examines a colophon to the Chinese premier Qianziwen kept in the Berlin Turfan Collection with the shelf number Ch 3716 (T II Y 62) which clearly followed the syntax of Old Uyghur, and then reconstructs the text with the assumption that the text was read in Old Uyghur. After briefly discussing some aspects of Old Uyghur's use of the Qianziwen, this paper examines another Chinese colophon in the same manuscript."
Keywords: Old Uyghur's use of Chinese; colophon to the Qianziwen; Old Uyghur-based Chinese; language contact; languages and scripts of the Northern Silk Road.

## The dated colophon - author's own PIAC abstract (verbatim)

From https://www.altaist.org/home/confessions/yakup-abdurishid/crossroads-of-languages-old-uyghur-interpretation-of-a-chinese-colophon-in-the-berlin-turfan-collection/ (64th Meeting Budapest, 2022):

"a colophon of the Qianziwen 'Thousand Characters Essay' written in Chinese by a Uyghur called 勝泉都统 Šingsön Tutong of Yarkhoto in the 13th year of Tianxi 天禧, year Xinwei 辛未, which actually was read in Old Uyghur, or reflect clear traces of the Old Uyghur language."

So the three sought elements are present: SCRIBE = Šingsön Tutong (勝泉都統), an Uyghur of Yarkhoto (Jiaohe, Turfan region); TEXT = Qianziwen booklet (Chinese primer); DATE = Tianxi era year 13, cyclic year xinwe 辛未.

## Date conversion caveat (zero fabrication)

The CE equivalent was NOT independently confirmed this session. Standard tables place the Qara-Khitai (Western Liao) era name Tianxi under Yelü Zhilugu at 1178-1211, which would make "13th year" ca. 1190 = gengxu 庚戌 - in tension with xinwe 辛未 (adjacent xinwe years: 1151, 1211). The discrepancy may involve era-reckoning practice in Turfan documents or an error in one element of the date; resolution presumably in Yakup 2023:529-542, which remains paywalled here. ## Relevance for the project

- Supplies the required DATED colophon (scribe + date) attached to a Qianziwen witness, complementing the Uighur-language Qianziwen translations (which are undated; see Umemura-Zieme 2015 witness list).
- Shows Chinese written by a Uyghur in Old Uyghur syntactic order - i.e., reading/writing Chinese through the Uighur language, the same competence Shogaito called kundoku-type reading; usable in the comparative chapter on East Asian glossing/reading traditions.
- Distinction to keep in prose: Ch 3716 is a CHINESE-language Qianziwen manuscript with Uighur-style colophon(s); it is not one of the five Old UIGHUR-translation Qianziwen manuscripts (SI 4000/4006/4084; SI 4083; SI 5778+5836+1787+1850) catalogued by Umemura & Zieme 2015 (https://journals.eco-vector.com/2410-0145/article/view/25776).
