## Key passage (verbatim, from the capture)

"Although the process of transferring the Banten royal library collection is well-recorded in Dutch colonial documents, several manuscripts that probably came from this collection also found their way into private collections or the holdings of other institutions. For example, ms. W.277 and ms. W.278, which are Qur'ans that probably originate from eighteenth-century Banten, were at some stage part of the collection of Hermann von de Wall (1807-1873). His collection was handed over to the Bataviaasch Genootschap in 1873, at which point these two Qur'an manuscripts were reunited with their other royal library holdings."

The sentence cites Gallop & Akbar 2006: 96-97 (Annabel Teh Gallop & Ali Akbar, "The art of the Qur'an in Banten: Calligraphy and Illumination", Archipel 72, 2006, pp. 95-156).

## What this resolves

- W.277 and W.278 are treated by Nurtawab himself as TWO SEPARATE Qur'an manuscripts sharing a provenance path (Banten royal library -> von de Wall private collection -> Bataviaasch Genootschap 1873 -> PNRI). The "one digit apart" shelfmark pair is not a cataloguing variant of a single item.
- W.277 is the interlinear-Malay Qur'an studied in Nurtawab 2020 (IMW 48) and 2023 (IMW 51): identical translation text and layout with A.51, but its copyist converted the Quranic text toward the Hafs reading and simplified the margins ("Qur'ans W.277 and A.51 are identical in terms of their translation texts and text layout ... the copyist of Qur'an W.277 attempted to change the Warsh/Qalun qira'a used for copying Qur'an A.51 to that transmitted by Hafs").
- W.278 is NOT described as containing an interlinear translation anywhere in the article.

## Corroborating physical profile of W.278 (Ali Akbar blog, item-by-item survey)

From http://quran-nusantara.blogspot.com/2012/08/banten-2-koleksi-perpustakaan-nasional.html (post dated 26 Aug 2012), item "(6) Naskah W.278":
- Complete 30 juz in one volume, good condition; black ink except Allah and markers (rubu', nisf, sajdah, maqra') in red.
- Covers/pages 35 x 23.5 cm; text block 23 x 13 cm.
- 379 pages, normally 13 lines per page (opening Fatihah/Baqarah page 9 lines, smaller block 16 x 7 cm).
- Verse markers: yellow roundels with black outline; European paper.
- Calligraphy constant throughout = single scribe; Naskhi with strong Persian/Indian influence; no colophon; origin "dapat diduga kuat berasal dari Banten" (strongly suspected Banten on letterform grounds).
The same survey's part 1 (https://quran-nusantara.blogspot.com/2012/04/banten-mushaf-asal-banten-koleksi.html) explicitly notes for sibling manuscript A.51a-e: "Di sela-sela teks Al-Qur'an terdapat terjemahan dalam bahasa Melayu" - i.e., when Ali Akbar sees an interlinear Malay translation he says so; his silence on W.278 is informative.
