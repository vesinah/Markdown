## Core content (verified from extracted text)

1. Khara-Khoto corpus profile: printed books AND manuscripts circulated concurrently ("prints and manuscripts circulated concurrently and scribal culture did not disappear with the spread of printing"); movable-type printing amply demonstrated among Tanguts (criteria for movable type quoted from Zhang Xiumin 2006: non-connecting frame corners, uneven ink, occasional upside-down characters, traces of column rules).
2. The case study: the Tangut translation of Sunzi bingfa survives BOTH as a printed butterfly-format edition and as a manuscript scroll (90 lines, 17-20 chars/line, cursive except last eight lines); the two are almost identical in wording, i.e., two copies of one translation.
3. COMMENTARY EMBEDDED IN THE PRINT: "In addition to the main text, the Tangut Sunzi comes with three commentaries, in an unattested combination that does not match any known Chinese edition" - the three being Cao Cao (155-220), Li Quan (8th c.), Du Mu (803-852). The printed edition "includes the three commentaries not only in its title but also in the text"; the manuscript scroll carries main text only despite its title announcing the san zhu (three-commentary) version.
4. Printed end-titles (weiti): two end-titles survive (end of juan 2 per Kepping 1979:526; end of juan 3 per ibid.:566); the former followed by a note giving total character count of main text plus commentary.
5. Appended Sunzi zhuan (biography translated from the Shiji) attached to both versions - a Tangut-era editorial combination unknown in Chinese transmission.
6. Argument: the received view that the manuscript was a draft for the print is reversed - the manuscript was probably copied FROM a printed edition; manuscript culture persisted long after printing became common.
7. Other Chinese military texts in Tangut translation noted: Liutao and Huangshi gong sanlue are woodblock prints; Jiangyuan also attested.
