## Verified facts (from the harvested full text)

1. The manuscript: an ALMOST COMPLETE Old Uyghur manuscript of the Altun Yaruk Sudur (Suvarnabhasottama-sutra, translated from Yijing's Chinese version), discovered at the Mount Wenshu (文殊山) mountain grottoes near Suzhou 肅州 (Gansu), today kept mainly in St Petersburg.
   - Editio princeps: Radlov/Malov, Suvarnaprabhasa. Text ujgurskoj redakcii, St Petersburg 1913-1917 (cast Uighur type, not facsimile).
   - Two stray folios in Stockholm: Kudara & Rohrborn 1982, ZDMG 132.2: 336-347.
2. DATE: "According to colophons attached to this manuscript, it was copied in 1687", post-dating most extant Old Uyghur Buddhist texts by about three centuries. Colophon studies: Hamada Masami, "Une note sur le colophon du Suvarnaprabhasa en version ouigoure", Memoirs of the Research Department of the Toyo Bunko 48 (1990): 49-55; Mori Masao 1997 (Kodai toruko minzokushi kenkyu III: 545-576).
3. Donors named in these colophons also appear among Uyghur pilgrim wall inscriptions at Mt Wenshu caves datable 16th-18th c. (Yusup & Zhang 2012; Kitsudo 2024) - the ms belongs to a continuous Gansu Uyghur Buddhist community after relocations from Turfan (1493 Ming relocation to Suzhou under Baiyan bula / Jingxiu Guoshi; earlier Yongchang move by late 13th c.).
4. Berlin Turfan Collection fragments of the Altun Yaruk catalogued completely in Raschmann VOHD volumes Teil 5/6/7 (2000/2002/2005); Teil 7 covers Kolophone, Kommentare und Versifizierungen - the place to look for further dated colophons.
5. Bonus datum for dating horizons: "the latest datable Buddhist text in Old Uyghur discovered in Dunhuang was printed in 1361" (Zieme & Kudara 1985: 31-32, on the Guanwuliangshoujing volume).
6. Related fragment study: Turanskaya 2022 (WMO 8(1), https://journals.eco-vector.com/2410-0145/article/view/77048): the Caityastotra belongs to the preface section of this LATE (=1687) edition.

## What is still missing

- Full transcription of any 1687 colophon sentence: requires Radlov/Malov or Hamada/Mori.

## Use in the book chapter

Anchors the ENDPOINT of the Old Uighur manuscript tradition: a fully dated (1687), placeable (Mt Wenshu/Suzhou), donor-attributed witness of interlinear-era reading culture surviving three centuries past the Mongol period - useful as chronological bookend against the Dunhuang 1361 print and the medieval Turfan dated colophons (cf. Ch 3716 Qianziwen colophon file).
