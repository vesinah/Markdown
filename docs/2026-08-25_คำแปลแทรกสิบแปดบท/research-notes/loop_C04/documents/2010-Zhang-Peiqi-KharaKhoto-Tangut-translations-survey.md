## Why it matters for Loop C04

The article is a systematic survey of the Khara-Khoto corpus of Tangut translations FROM Chinese (secular + Buddhist), built on Gorbacheva & Kychanov 1963 (Tangutskie rukopisi i ksilografy) catalogue data plus Kepping/Solonin/Nie Hongyin scholarship. It describes EVERY bilingual presentation strategy found in the corpus - and none of them is an interlinear translation arrangement. This makes it a citable backbone for the documented negative recorded in findings.md.

## Recovered content points

1. Corpus classification: Khara-Khoto finds divide into five classes (Tangut secular works; Chinese books; Tangut translations OF Chinese books [largest class]; Tangut translations of Tibetan books; other minority-language texts).
2. Chinese books printed IN Xia state vs imported: prints made in Xia carry colophons with Xia officials' names and Xia donors (per Terent'ev-Katanskii); mostly Buddhist works plus documents.
3. Secular translations include military books (Sunzi bingfa san zhu 孫子兵法三注, Liutao, Huangshi gong sanlue, Jiangyuan) and Confucian classics (Lunyu 論語, Xiaojing zhuan 孝經傳, Mengzi 孟子, Mengzi wen), plus Leilin 類林, Zhenguan yaowen, Shier guo, Dexing ji.
4. Codicological parameters (from Gorbacheva & Kychanov):
   - Lunyu: blockprint, butterfly binding, page 25.3 x 17.2 cm, text block 18 x 12.7 cm, 6 lines/page, main text 17-18 chars per line, COMMENTARY lines 16 chars per line; only juan 5, 11, 15, 19, 20 survive; commentator/translator possibly Xia chancellor Wodaochong 斡道沖.
   - Mengzi: blockprint, butterfly binding, page 27 x 16.5 cm, 7 lines/page, 20-21 chars/line; 57 leaves survive (juan 4-6). Mengzi wen also blockprinted WITH an anonymous commentary.
   - Xiaojing zhuan (Lü Huiqing's lost-in-China commentary version): manuscript draft, butterfly binding, inv. 2627, page 24 x 14.7 cm, 7 lines/page, main text 20 chars/line, commentary 19 chars/line, 77 leaves complete except juan 18; two further cursive Xiaojing zhuan mss in the British Library (Or.12380/3858, Or.12380/3855 per Hu Ruofei 2005:97).
   - Shier guo (inv. 132, 133, 4173): blockprint, butterfly binding, 7 lines/page, 15 chars/line.
   - Leilin (inv. 125-131, 2625, 6686): blockprint, butterfly binding, page 18.4 x 12.7 cm, 7 lines/page, 15 chars/line.
5. Vimalakirtinirdesha (Tang. 171 = T.475 Kumarajiva base) survives in FIVE distinct versions/layouts (inv. 6046 concertina ms; inv. 2529+709+119+2560+2881 butterfly print; inv. 2331 butterfly print; inv. 5727 concertina print; inv. 233+4236+232+361+362+2310+737 later concertina print) - separate-edition plurilingual/multiversional culture, not glossed editions.
6. Translation practice notes: Tangut translators preserved SOV syntax markers; in Buddhist verses they matched character counts to the Chinese base while keeping Tangut grammatical markers; calques mirror Chinese compounding (金剛 rendered stone+king in the TIBETAN-based strand vs phonetic/semantic strategies elsewhere).
7. KEY annotation datum: "西夏譯經者對於西夏人不易理解的詞語，有時會給予特別的注釋。如在[椰子]二字下注「漢語」二字，表示是借自漢語的詞彙" - where a word was hard to understand, translators added a small note labeling it as a CHINESE LOAN (annotation under the two characters for 'coconut'). This small-character loan-labeling inside the translated text is the closest thing to a glossing habit found - it is semantic sourcing info, not an interlinear translation.

## Cross-checks harvested alongside (same index pass)

- Lin Ying-chin's Xixia xue collation essays (Zunshengjing): St Petersburg copies No. 3707 (22-leaf thread-bound print), No. 4078 + 7592a/b (concertina prints, mixed contents incl. dharani transcriptions), No. 6796(6821) = Tang.109; her transcription apparatus marks spaces within Tangut lines with a special sign ("∥") - i.e., even the editorial apparatus treats the Tangut text as single-language running text; no interlinear Chinese anywhere. Also quotes Renzong-era print-run figures from Menyshikov 1984: 1167 Heart Sutra "番漢共兩萬卷"; 1184 Three Refuges sutra "番漢五萬一千餘卷".
- Hamanaka & Sizova 2020 (WMO): the three-language 1149 edition set (Chinese TK-164/165; Tangut Tang.109; Tibetan Kh.Tib. 63/64/67/68), linkage revealed by Nishida in the introduction to Kychanov 1999 (pp. XXV-XXVI).

## Use in the book chapter

Quote Zhang Peiqi's line-density data (main text vs commentary char counts) as evidence that Tangut bilingual/commentarial book design used SEPARATE LINES or SEPARATE EDITIONS, never between-the-lines translation; pair with the Pearl-in-the-Palm columnar glossary as the Tangut answer to bilingual pedagogy.
