## Key findings (with URLs)

1. TARGET RESOLVED - W.277 vs W.278 are TWO DISTINCT MANUSCRIPTS, not a digit variant of one shelfmark. The premise of the discrepancy (Ali Akbar's blog using W.278 while Nurtawab writes W.277) dissolved when the Archipel 2025 article was finally text-searched: Nurtawab himself names BOTH items in one sentence - "ms. W.277 and ms. W.278, which are Qur'ans that probably originate from eighteenth-century Banten, were at some stage part of the collection of Hermann von de Wall (1807-1873)", whose library went to the Bataviaasch Genootschap in 1873 where they rejoined the ex-Banten royal holdings (citing Gallop & Akbar 2006: 96-97; https://journals.openedition.org/archipel/9536 via Wayback capture). Profile match confirms distinctness: W.277 is the interlinear-Malay Qur'an of the A.51 family (identical translation and layout, Hafs-ized qira'a, simplified margins - Nurtawab 2025 pars. on the three-Malay-Qur'an family), whereas Ali Akbar's W.278 is a COMPLETE single-volume 30-juz codex of 379 leaves, 13 lines/page, single scribe, no colophon, Indo-Persianizing naskhi, Von der Wall provenance, with NO interlinear translation mentioned in his item-by-item physical survey (http://quran-nusantara.blogspot.com/2012/08/banten-2-koleksi-perpustakaan-nasional.html). Book-chapter citation practice: use W.277 (Nurtawab 2020/2023/2025) for the interlinear-Malay witness; W.278 exists separately as a non-interlinear Banten Qur'an. Direct PNRI catalogue confirmation remains impossible online from this session (katalog.perpusnas.go.id DNS failure; opac.perpusnas.go.id 403).

2. TARGET CLOSED AS DOCUMENTED NEGATIVE - no concrete description of a Chinese-Tangut INTERLINEAR arrangement inside any Khara-Khoto printed sutra was found after checking the requested channels (Kychanov catalogue-derived descriptions via Hamanaka-Sizova 2020; IOM RAS texts; Nishida-linked scholarship; Shi Jinbo channel via Brill/WMO citations; plus two independent surveys: Galambos's Sunzi print chapter and Zhang Peiqi 2010). What IS attested at Khara-Khoto/Kara-Khoto for Chinese-Tangut bilingualism: (a) PARALLEL SEPARATE-LANGUAGE EDITIONS - the 1149 Renzong dharani-sutra set exists as Chinese prints TK-164/TK-165, a Tangut print Tang. 109 with identical text, and Tibetan butterfly prints (Nishida's linkage in the introduction to Kychanov 1999 catalogue, pp. XXV-XXVI; https://journals.eco-vector.com/2410-0145/article/view/48894); (b) COMMENTARY EMBEDDED IN THE SAME PRINT - the Tangut Sunzi carries the three commentaries within the text flow (Galambos, frogbear PDF), and Tangut Confucian prints reserve separate line-densities for commentary (Lunyu: main text 17-18 chars/line vs commentary 16 chars/line; Xiaojing zhuan likewise - Zhang Peiqi 2010, data from Gorbacheva & Kychanov 1963); (c) SMALL IN-TEXT ANNOTATIONS inside Tangut translations marking Chinese loanwords with the label "Chinese" (Zhang Peiqi 2010); (d) the COLUMNAR GLOSSARY format of the 1190 Pearl in the Palm; (e) TIBETAN-script phonetic glosses added over Tangut Buddhist manuscripts (Or.12380 group, already recorded in B04). Conclusion for the monograph: Tangut bilingual practice used parallel editions, glossaries, embedded commentary and loan-labels - NOT interlinear glossing; the negative can now be stated with named checked sources. Pitfall flagged for the book: Lin Ying-chin 2011's "interlinear translation into Chinese" describes HER modern collation edition, not a medieval artifact - do not quote it as historical evidence.

Composition of the four elements (Tangut word; its sound in Chinese characters; Chinese word; its sound in Tangut characters) is thus solidly attested; the strict right-to-left column ORDER is stated in tertiary sources but was not captured from a primary/scholarly description this session "(ลำดับคอลัมน์จากขวาไปซ้ายยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยันจากแหล่งปฐมภูมิที่เข้าถึงได้)". TARGET CLOSED - DATED UIGHUR COLOPHON DELIVERED (Qianziwen witness, with a date-conversion caveat): Berlin Turfan Collection Ch 3716 (T II Y 62) carries a colophon to the Qianziwen written in CHINESE but following Old Uyghur syntax, by the Uyghur 勝泉都統 Šingsön Tutong ("Šingsön Tutor-of-the-army") OF YARKHOTO, dated "the 13th year of Tianxi 天禧, year Xinwei 辛未" (verbatim from Yakup's own PIAC 2022 abstract, https://www.altaist.org/home/confessions/yakup-abdurishid/crossroads-of-languages-old-uyghur-interpretation-of-a-chinese-colophon-in-the-berlin-turfan-collection/; expanded in Yakup 2023, Acta Orientalia Hung. 76(4):529-542, abstract at https://www.ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=1205229; MTAK REAL record https://real.mtak.hu/192005/). This is exactly the scribe+date combination sought, and it doubles as evidence of Uighur oral reading of Chinese texts. CAVEAT (zero fabrication): the CE year could not be independently fixed this session - the Qara-Khitai Tianxi era is normally tabled 1178-1211, which makes "13th year" ca. 1190 (gengxu), while xinwei years fall at 1151 or 1211; Yakup 2023 presumably resolves the reckoning, but the full text is paywalled, so the CE conversion stays "(ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน)". SECOND DATED WITNESS (Altun Yaruk): the almost-complete St Petersburg Altun Yaruk Sudur from Mt Wenshu near Suzhou was copied in 1687 according to its appended colophons (Kasai, CrossAsia Transactions PDF, https://repository.crossasia.org/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/crossasia_derivate_00000640/Crossasia_Transactions%20Xinjiang_Yukio%20Kasai.pdf); its colophons were studied by Hamada 1990 and Mori 1997, and its donors' names recur in the datable Mt Wenshu cave inscriptions (16th-18th c.). Named individual SCRIBE for the 1687 ms: "(ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน)" behind Hamada/Mori paywalls. Bonus datum: the latest datable Old Uighur Buddhist text from Dunhuang is a blockprint of 1361 (Zieme/Kudara 1985: 31-32, cited in Kasai fn 6).
