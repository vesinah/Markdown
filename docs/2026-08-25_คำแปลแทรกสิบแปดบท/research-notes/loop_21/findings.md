## Key findings (with URLs)

1. Corpus scale (NGMPP): The official NGMPP page states the project was founded in 1970 under an agreement between His Majesty's Government of Nepal and the German Oriental Society, financed by the German Research Council (DFG), and filmed approximately 180,000 manuscripts before ending in March 2002; Vergiani 2017 gives the figure "around 190,000" and end date 2001 (minor discrepancy between sources, both retained here). Among the filmed material: the oldest preserved Vedic manuscript (11th c.), the oldest preserved Pali manuscript, and a Skanda Purana manuscript that is possibly the oldest dated Nepalese manuscript (810 AD). Originals stayed with owners/institutions; master films centralized at the National Archives (NAK), Kathmandu — described as the world's largest microfilm collection of South Asian manuscripts — with a further copy on permanent loan to the State Library in Berlin via the German Oriental Society. https://www.aai.uni-hamburg.de/en/forschung/ngmcp/history/about-ngmpp.html ; https://www.aai.uni-hamburg.de/en/forschung/ngmcp

2. Oldest dated stratum of Nepalese manuscripts: Cambridge UL Add.1049 transmits the Parameshvaratantra (Shaiva Siddhanta; otherwise unknown) and is "one of the oldest known dated Sanskrit manuscripts from South Asia", copied in year 252 judged to be the era of Amshuvarman (Manadeva) = 828 CE; Formigatti 2017 counts Add.1049 (828 CE) and Add.1702 (8th c.) among the two oldest Nepalese palm-leaf manuscripts in the UL, and Add.1412 (1278 CE) as one of the oldest dated Nepalese paper manuscripts. Note: the famous early-dated Bower Manuscript is Central Asian, not Nepalese; the often-repeated "CE 828" belongs to Add.1049's Manadeva-era date, not to the Nepal Era (which begins 879/880 CE). https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/view/MS-ADD-01049-00001/1 ; https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110543100-002

3. Newari scribal/colophon conventions (dating): Vergiani quotes a canonical dated colophon — NAK 3/379 (A 53/2; A 1279/8), Chandravyakarana, samvat 276 (= NS; 14 December 1155 CE per Petech), during the vijayarajye of Anandadeva, with owner statement (yamtakudumbajakulaputra-shri-manaharshavarmasya pustako 'yam); standard elements are Nepala-Era year + lunar calendar placement + reigning king's victorious rule + scribe/donor/owner. Scribal number convention: years like "2005" can mean "100+5" style 205 (NGMCP catalogue note). Colophons frequently state place/date of copying in the Nepala Era; when absent, distinctive varieties of north-Indian script (e.g. hook-topped Bhujimol/Nepalakshara) indicate Nepalese provenance. https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110543100-004

4. Colophon structure and corrections in 14th-c. Nepalese mss (Formigatti 2022): corpus = dated mss 1320-1395 CE across Jayarimalla/Jayaraajadeva/Jayarjunadeva/Jayasthitimalla; the deyadharmo('nam) donation formula occurs only in Buddhist manuscripts; donors include bhāroka officials and craftsmen (Hitopadesha ms commissioned by goldsmith Ratnabhara); recurring scribe humility stanzas (e.g. yadrk samsthitam adarse...) invoke the antigraph (adarsha/adrisa) to excuse copying errors; preservation stanza tailad rakshed jalaad rakshet... The chapter's transcription conventions explicitly encode "Insertion by the scribe (interlinear or marginal)" and expunctions/erasures — i.e. correction and insertion in interlinear space and margins was normal Newari scribal behavior, and modern cataloguing rubrics (also in NGMCP-style descriptions quoted by Hidas: "No marginal or interlinear corrections") treat presence/absence of such annotations as a standard descriptive feature. https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110795271-003 ; https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110543100-015

5. Annotated/illuminated Prajñāpāramitā exemplar: Cambridge UL Add.1643 (digitized, IIIF) — Nepalese palm-leaf Ashtasahasrika copied 1015 CE by Sujatabhadra at Hlam vihara during the joint reign of three kings (Petech 1984 discussed); 85 miniatures at chapter ends whose subjects differ from Indian Pala counterparts; crucially it "contains notes for the illuminator added by the first scribe, each of which describes the Buddha or the Bodhisattva and the location to be depicted in the miniature" (marginal production annotations); plus a 1139 CE verse colophon by Karunavajra recording his rescue of the manuscript "from falling in the hands of unbelievers"; final folio is the only surviving Sanskrit witness of the Vajradhvajaparinamana. https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/view/MS-ADD-01643/1

6. Pancaraksha protective-text copies as an annotated Nepalese genre: more than 300 Pancaraksha manuscripts survive, a few from Eastern India (11th-13th c.) but the majority from Nepal (11th-20th c.); Hidas's edition of the Mahadandadharani-Sitavati within the collection uses Nepalese palm-leaf witnesses photographed by NGMPP (reels E 1714/22, 1715/1 etc.), described folio-by-folio (41-142 leaves, string holes, multiple foliation sets on versos, donor name Shribala inserted in the text, colophons); the claimed "Pañcarakṣā ms. dated NS 19 (899 CE)" was shown to misread a Pala regnal year (actual ms = Bendall Add.1688). Interleaving commentary: in the grammatical domain Vergiani shows root-text-plus-commentary transmission where sutrapatha circulates with or without Dharmadasa's Vritti in the same bundle/pothi. https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110543100-015 ; https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110543100-004

7. Medical (Ayurveda) manuscripts in Nepal: a 2025 survey studied 2,324 Ayurveda-related manuscripts across the National Archives, Kaiser Library, Asa (Asha) Archive and private collections — written in seven languages, eleven scripts, eight materials, covering eighteen branches of Ayurveda; the authors note the NAK collections were digitized with help of the German government (i.e. the NGMPP program) and that Ayurvedic manuscripts remain largely unedited. https://doi.org/10.48165/irjay.2025.81002

Comparative interlinear-gloss evidence from the same volume (useful for the book project): Jain pothis at Cambridge carry true interlinear vernacular commentaries/quasi-translations (Gujarati) between lines of Prakrit root text — Add.1779 Antagadadasao (VS 1801 = 1744 CE), Add.1787 Laghunishithashastra (VS 1794 = 1737 CE), Add.1811 Dashashrutaskandha (VS 1830 = 1773 CE), Add.2209 Vyavaharasutra with Thabo in compartments (VS 1765 = 1708 CE), Add.2225 Navatattva (VS 1753 = 1696 CE, copied by monk Jinavijayagani for laywomen readers).
