# Harvest: CUDL Add.1049 — Pārameśvaratantra (828 CE)

## Facts as stated on the CUDL item page

1. Status: "One of the oldest known dated Sanskrit manuscripts from South Asia, this specimen transmits a substantial portion of the Pārameśvaratantra, a scripture of the Śaiva Siddhānta, one of the Tantric theological schools that taught the worship of Śiva as 'Supreme Lord'".

2. Uniqueness: "No other manuscript of this work is known, but nine chapters are transmitted in the Prāyaścittasamuccaya of Hṛdayaśiva (see Add. 2833), where the work is referred to as the Puskaratantra or Pushkara-Pārameśvaratantra (see Goodall 1998, particularly p. xliii). The Pārameśvaratantra is so far unpublished."

3. Dating: "According to the colophon, it was copied in the year 252, which some scholars judge to be of the era established by the Nepalese king Aṃśuvarman (also known as Mānadeva), therefore corresponding to 828 CE." (Note for our book: this is NOT a Nepal-Era date — the Nepal Era starts only in 879/880 CE.)

4. Codicological caution: "Since only few of the folio numbers have been preserved and are clearly identifiable, the sequence of folios must be regarded as provisional until the whole text is transcribed and properly studied... here the folios are numbered sequentially, from 1 to 62"; final folio bears colophon and old folio number 113.

5. Companion fragment: "The bundle also includes a one-leaf fragment of a Jñānārṇavamahātantra (Add.1049.2)" with its own digitized set: http://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/view/MS-ADD-01049-00002/1

6. Cross-reference: Formigatti 2017 ("Sanskrit Manuscripts in the Cambridge University Library", DOI 10.1515/9783110543100-002) counts Add.1049 (dated 828 CE) together with Add.1702 (dated to the 8th century) as "two of the oldest Nepalese palm-leaf manuscripts" in the UL, and Add.1412 (dated 1278 CE) as one of the oldest dated Nepalese paper manuscripts.

## Relevance
Anchor item for the claim that Nepal preserves the earliest dated layer of Sanskrit palm-leaf manuscript culture; also shows a Shaiva (not Buddhist) example, balancing the Buddhist Prajñaparamita/Pañcaraksha evidence.
