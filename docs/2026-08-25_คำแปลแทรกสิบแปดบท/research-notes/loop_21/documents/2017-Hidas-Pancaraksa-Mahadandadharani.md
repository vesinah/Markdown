# Harvest: Hidas 2017 — Pancaraksha / Mahadandadharani-Sitavati

## Why relevant
Concrete codicological descriptions of Nepalese palm-leaf Pancaraksha copies (annotation-relevant features: margins, foliation, inserted donor names, corrections), plus corpus statistics.

## Key extracted facts

1. Corpus statistics:
"The other surviving textual traditions have been transmitted almost exclusively in Pañcarakṣā manuscripts of which more than three hundred survive. A few of these originate from Eastern India from the eleventh to the thirteenth centuries and the majority from Nepal from the eleventh to the twentieth centuries."
(Nepalese listings referenced: Tsukamoto et al. 1989, 62-64; NGMPP database; Mevissen 1989.)

2. Dating caution example:
"a Pañcarakṣā ms. dated NS 19 (899 CE) is listed in Wright 1877, 324. Thanks to Dr Camillo Formigatti it has been clarified that this manuscript is actually the one catalogued as Add.1688 in Bendall 1883... Wright must have read 19 for 14 in the colophon and taken this as Nepal Samvat instead of a Pāla regnal year."

3. Sample codicological description (one witness, photographed by NGMPP):
"...photographed on 1 November, 1984 and reproduced on microfilm reels E 1714/22 and 1715/1 by the Nepal-German Manuscript Preservation Project (NGMPP). 142 palm leaves measuring 38.4 x 5.5 centimetres, with five or three lines on a folio. Hook-topped Nepālākṣara script (Bhujimol). Clear, balanced, bold handwriting. Complete, with folio 132 being a paper supplement. No marginal or interlinear corrections. Foliation: two different sets of numbering (one with numerals referring to the whole manuscript on the left margin, and one with numerals referring to the individual section on the right margin) on the verso... Donor's name inserted in the text: Śrībala. Colophon at the end of the manuscript."
(Another witness: "41 palm leaves measuring 54 x 5 centimetres, with two stringholes and three, four or five lines on a folio... The margins of most leaves are damaged and broken off in various degrees. No marginal or interlinear corrections. Foliation: three different sets of numbering..." Another: "76 palm leaves with six lines on a folio... The manuscript appears to have once been illuminated... occasional notes in Tibetan script.")

4. Interpretation for our project:
- The rubric "No marginal or interlinear corrections" shows that presence of marginal/interlinear scribal corrections is an expected, systematically recorded feature of Newari-copied pothis.
- Multiple foliation sets in different hands on verso margins = layered later use of margins.
- Donor names inserted inside the running text; occasional notes in Tibetan script = cross-script annotations.
- Illumination once present; damage concentrated at leaf margins.

5. Tibetan translation context: translated around 800 CE by Jinamitra, Danashila and Ye shes sde; listed in Lhan kar ma (no. 373) and 'Phang thang ma (no. 355); Chinese translation 984 CE by Fatian (T. 1392).
