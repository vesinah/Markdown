# Harvest: Formigatti 2022 — Colophons in fourteenth-century Nepalese manuscripts

## Scope stated by the author
"The present study examines colophons in fourteenth-century Nepalese manuscripts. More precisely, it focuses on manuscripts written between 1320 and 1395 CE as part of ongoing research on the cultural history of Nepal in this pivotal century – particularly its second half, during which the country experienced a devastating invasion at the hand of Sultān Shams ud-dīn in 1349 and a dynastic change marking also major changes in the cultural landscape." Nepal = historical Nepalamandala (Kathmandu Valley); kings Jayarimalla, Jayaraajadeva, Jayarjunadeva, Jayasthitimalla.

## Colophon theory (South Asian terminology)
- Jinavijayamuni's distinction: granthaprasasti ("colophon of the text", authorial explicit) vs pustakaprasasti ("colophon of the book", scribe-composed, varies per manuscript).
- Indologists' habit of calling end-titles "colophon" via the term pushpikā is shown to be a lexicographic accident (Wilson 1832 gave only dental/mucus meanings; the 'conclusion of a work' sense appears in Vacaspatya 1873 and Shabdakalpadruma 1886 and then entered MW2/Apte).
- Related term granthālankāra for concluding eulogy section attested from sixteenth-century mss (S.R. Sarma 2006).

## Corpus observations relevant to annotation/gloss research
1. Dated examples listed include: A 54-26 Pretayajneshti (NS 451, c. 1331; uncatalogued); A 56-21 Devimahatmya (NS 462, c. 1342); A 48-3 (NAK 3/402) Vajravali with "Samvat 202" whose era is ambiguous — Lakshmanasena Samvat according to NGMCP, Nepala Samvat according to Shastri 1915 (era ambiguity is a real research problem).
2. deyadharmo('nam) donation formula occurs ONLY in Buddhist manuscripts of the corpus (Amoghapashahridaya, Pancaraksha, Karandavyuha x2, Vasudharadharni etc.); donors named include Vyadhojasaramaka and the bharoka Abhayamala.
3. Non-Buddhist colophons are structurally simpler: Tripurapaddhati donor = thakkura Shurapatipadmarama (for nityapujanartham), Jayottaratantra donor = brahmin Jasadeva (svarthapararthahetukamartham), Hitopadesha commissioned by goldsmith Ratnabhara (no reason given). Two mss (Pancaraksha; Karandavyuha) lack a named scribe.
4. Recurring scribe humility stanzas (recombined half-stanza modules), e.g.:
   - "yadṛk saṃsthitam ādarśe tādṛśaṃ likhitam mayā | yadi śuddham aśuddhaṃ vā mama doṣo na dīyate ||"
   - "yathādṛśadarśaṇena likhitaṃ | lekhakasya doṣo na dhāryate ||"
   Gist: the scribe copied exactly what stood in the antigraph (Skt. adarśa/adṛśa), so errors are not his fault.
5. Book-preservation stanza: "tailād rakṣed jalād rakṣet śithilabandhanāt..." (protect from oil, water, loose binding...).
6. Transcription conventions table explicitly encodes scribal interventions on the page:
   - "Insertion by the scribe (interlinear or marginal; if used to add a vowel replacing the inherent short a, the latter is retained in the transcription)" — notation \ta/thā.
   - "Characters or words deleted (expuncted or erased) by the scribe (including later deletions)" — double-bracket notation.
   - Treatise-initial symbol (siddhi), string hole, pushpika symbol, line-filler, word/sandhi divider, damaged-character notation.
7. Bibliographic anchor points: Petech 1984 (Mediaeval History of Nepal) pp. 124-125; Formigatti 2016 "Towards a Cultural History of Nepal, 14th-17th Century. A Nepalese Renaissance?" (Rivista degli Studi Orientali 89 Suppl., 51-66, in Studies in Honour of Luciano Petech); von Hinüber 2017 on colophon awareness in India being essentially Jain until recent times.

## Interpretation for the book project
- Demonstrates a full descriptive vocabulary for Newari scribal page-behavior: interlinear and marginal insertions were common enough to need systematic encoding; erasures/expunctions likewise.
- Shows how samvat dating formulas work in practice (NS vs LS ambiguity; reign clauses; lunar calendar detail), which the book can use to explain Nepalese colophon conventions.
- Provides social-history frame ("Nepalese Renaissance") linking annotation/donation practice to post-1349 reconstruction.
