# Harvest: CUDL Add.1643 — illuminated Nepalese Prajñāpāramitā with illuminator's notes

## Facts as stated on the CUDL item page

1. Identity/material: "An 11th-century composite and multi-text manuscript containing three texts: Rāhulabhadra's Prajñāpāramitāstotra, the Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā and the Vajradhvajapariṇāmanā"; "This Nepalese palm-leaf manuscript..."

2. Date/provenance from colophon: "...it was written by a scribe named Sujātabhadra in 1015 CE during the joint reign of three kings, Bhojadeva, Rudradeva and Lakṣmīkāmadeva in the Hlāṃ vihāra (on the joint reign of these three kings cf. Petech 1984: 36-9)." Identification of the monastery debated: Hlom/Lhom Hit in Tangal Tol, Patan (Petech) vs Lam Baha in Kathmandu (Locke 1985: 421).

3. Illuminations: "decorated with 85 miniatures occurring at chapter ends and at the beginning of the entire manuscript (probably the miniatures were originally 88, since the first palm-leaf folio is missing and has been replaced with a paper supplement)". Subjects differ from Indian Pala counterparts: "specific iconographic representations of the Buddha or Bodhisattvas or other divinities, or of important stūpas or caityas", places mostly eastern India but also China, Java, Ceylon, Gujarat, southern India (Losty 1992: 31).

4. ANNOTATION FEATURE (key for Loop 21): "the manuscript contains notes for the illuminator added by the first scribe, each of which describes the Buddha or the Bodhisattva and the location to be depicted in the miniature."

5. Later annotated colophon: "an additional colophon in verses, added in 1139 by a certain Karuṇavajra, who states that he rescued the Prajñāpāramitā from falling in the hands of unbelievers (most probably, people of Brahmanical affiliation)."

6. Textual uniqueness: "the last folio of this manuscript is the only surviving witness of the Sanskrit original of the Vajradhvajapariṇāmanā, a short hymn hitherto considered to have survived only in its Tibetan translation" (fully transcribed in the CUDL excerpts).

7. Reception: featured in The Kathmandu Post (26 Feb 2019, "Perfection of art") and in a Nepali school textbook Chitrakala; included in the UL 600th anniversary exhibition "Lines of Thought".

## Relevance
A dated, digitized, IIIF-accessible Nepalese pothi showing three annotation layers on the same object: production annotations (illuminator's notes), ritual-historical colophon added ~124 years later, and painted program tied to chapter boundaries.
