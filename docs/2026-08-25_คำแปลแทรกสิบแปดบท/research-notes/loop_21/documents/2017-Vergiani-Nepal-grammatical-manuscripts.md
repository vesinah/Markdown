# Harvest: Vergiani 2017 — Sanskrit grammatical traditions in Nepal

## Why relevant to interlinear gloss project
Demonstrates how NGMPP microfilm corpus + colophons enable statistical/textual history of a Nepalese discipline; documents colophon conventions (Nepala Samvat dating, reign formula, owner statements) and root-text-plus-commentary transmission in the same bundles.

## Key extracted passages and data points

1. NGMPP scale and database:
"the Nepalese-German Manuscript Cataloguing Project (henceforth, NGMCP) has produced a large and easily accessible database of the manuscripts microfilmed by its predecessor, the Nepalese-German Manuscript Preservation Project (NGMPP), which between 1970 and 2001 reproduced virtually all the manuscripts (around 190,000) held in Nepalese collections."

2. Preservation rationale:
"It has often been remarked that [Nepal's] temperate climate has allowed the preservation of manuscripts for much longer than in any other region of South Asia, with the earliest exemplars going back to the second half of the first millennium CE."

3. Colophons as access to annotation culture:
"colophons (and other similar paratexts: introductory verses, marginal annotations, etc.) give us access – especially rare for pre-modern South Asia – to a first-hand account of the social dynamics surrounding the production and transmission of knowledge."

4. Provenance signals:
"the place of production of the copy is sometimes explicitly stated in the colophon and/or, more frequently, the year is given in the Nepāla Era. But even when the colophon is not available, the particular variety of north-Indian script used in the country has distinctive features that are a reliable indicator of the provenance."

5. Quoted dated colophon (NAK 3/379 = A 53/2; A 1279/8, Chandravyakarana):
"samvat 276 prathamapaushakrishnadiva caturthyam shryanandadevasya vijayarajye likhitam idam pustakam || yamtakudumbajakulaputrashrimanaharshavarmasya pustako 'yam ||"
Translation given: "This book has been copied in the year 276, on the fourth [lunar day] of the dark fortnight of the first [intercalary] month of Pausha, during the victorious reign of Anandadeva. This book belongs to Manaharshavarman, the scion of the northern family." Date verified for 14 December 1155 CE (Petech 1984, 62). Note: "yamta" is Newari for 'northern'.

6. Scribal numeral convention (NGMCP catalogue note quoted): scribes may write "2005" meaning NS 205 ("100+1"/"200+5" style); catalogue remarks this is not uncommon.

7. Earliest dated grammatical mss are 12th century (NS 254 = 1134 CE Chandravyakaranavritti from reign of Indradeva, recorded by Sankrityayana in Tibet; NS 276 = 1155 CE above). Sutrapatha circulates with or without Dharmadasa's Vritti — root text and commentary not treated as inseparable.

8. Citation practice: Nepalese mss cited as NAK classmark followed by NGMPP reel number in brackets (e.g. NAK 1/1697 (A 51/15)); NGMCP catalogue lists mss under reel numbers; transcripts in NGMCP entries used where images were unavailable.

9. Historiography built on colophons: Regmi (1960, 1965), Petech (1984); centres of learning mentioned: monasteries, temple schools, pathashalas, courtly circles.
