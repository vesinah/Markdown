# Harvest: Tamrakar et al. 2025 — Ayurveda manuscripts available in Nepal

## Abstract facts (from Crossref-deposited abstract, verbatim data points)

- "Nepal has a rich collection of manuscripts in both public and private collections. They are mainly stored in the National Archive, Kaiser Library, and Asa Library and have been digitized with the help of the German government."
- "The manuscripts related to Ayurveda have hardly been explored, with only a few manuscripts edited and published."
- Method: recorded according to the manuscript detail form of the National Mission for Manuscripts, Government of India.
- Result: "During the study, a total of 2324 manuscripts were studied and they were written in seven different languages, eleven different scripts, in eight different materials and were related to eighteen different branches of Ayurveda."

## Interpretation for Loop 21 / book chapter
- Quantifies the Nepalese medical-manuscript corpus accessible through the NGMPP-era digitization program (the "German government" reference corresponds to the Nepal-German microfilming/cataloguing projects).
- Confirms medical manuscripts survive on multiple writing materials and scripts (relevant when discussing gloss placement across materials).
- Names the three principal holding institutions: National Archives (NAK) Kathmandu, Kaiser Library, Asa (Asha) Archive.
