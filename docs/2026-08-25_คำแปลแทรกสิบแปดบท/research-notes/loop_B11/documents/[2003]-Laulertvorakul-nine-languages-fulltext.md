## Key verbatim content extracted

### Abstract (p. 11)

"Pathamasambodhi is widespread in at least 5 countries in Southeast Asia. Hundreds of manuscripts and not less than 10 versions have been discovered. They are composed in 4 languages: Pali, Mon, Cambodian and Tai, including Tai vernaculars: Siamese Thai, Northern Thai Northeastern Thai, Lao, Tai Lue, and Tai Khoeun. The styles of composition dramatically vary between detailed enumeration and concision, prose and verse, translation in the style of nissaya and non-nissaya, single language and dual languages - Pali and another language."

### Version taxonomy (author's abbreviation list)

LPL = Lanna Pali version; LPR/LPRa/LPRb/LPRc = Late Pali Recensions (16-19 / 22 / 30 chapters); LAO = Lao version; LVV = Lao Verse Version; MON = Mon version; NET = Northeastern Thai version; PLN = Pali-Lao nissaya version; SMT = Siamese Thai version composed by Prince Paramanujitjinorot; TKN = Tai Khoeun; TL/TLC/TLE = Thai Lue (full/concise/enumerated); TY/TYC/TYE = Thai Yuan (full/concise/enumerated dual-language).

### Evolution thesis (usable directly in the book)

"The Pali version found in the Lanna region is the oldest complete version. It is probably the original version, which not only is rendered to Tai Lue, Tai Khoeun, Lao and Northern Thai, but evolved over centuries to become the three newer Pali recensions which later were rendered to northeastern Thai, Mon and Siamese Thai. The latter version was finally rendered to Cambodian."

Content expanded "in three phases with different genres": (1) legend of the Siddhattha Bodhisatta (Lanna Pali, Northern Thai, Lao, Tai Khoeun, Tai Lue versions); (2) legend of Gotama Buddha (two late Pali recensions, Mon, NE Thai); (3) legend of the sasana (Pali and Siamese Thai by Supreme Patriarch Prince Paramanujitjinorot, and the Cambodian version). The Northern Thai version is called "the transition link" between phases 1 and 2.

### Section 8.a — THE PALI-LAO NISSAYA VERSION (item-level Lao nissaya evidence)

- Surveyed by: Louis Finot (1917), Pierre-Bernard Lafont (1959), Thao Kene (1958), National Culture Research Institute/NCRI (1988), Royal Institute of Lao (cited in NCRI 1988), and PLMP (1995-6, 1996, 1998) — "a large number of manuscripts."
- Author's own examination of Wat Mai Suvannabhumaram (Luang Prabang) holdings, copied from PLMP microfilm: "the manuscripts consisting of 10 bundles are Pali-Lao Nissaya. The oldest manuscript is the one written in CS 1188 and is kept in the Museum at Luang Prabang."
- Appendix listing includes: "The Pali-Lao Nissaya version of Pathamasambodhi kept at Wat Mai Suvannabhumaram, Luang Prabang, 10 bundles (complete), CS 1195."
- Note appended: manuscripts of 30-31 bundles written in Lao found in the former kingdom may be the same version; not yet investigated.

### Adjacent appendix and section 8.b items (same list)

- Section 8.b Lao Verse Version (LVV): Prah Maha Jana Dhammadhajo found a manuscript "written in BE 2411 in Lao but in the form of poetry at a Laotian monastery in Brisbane, Queensland, Australia"; he transliterated and published it in AD 1999 (= Dhammadhajo, Prah Maha Jana. 1999. Lan Xang Version of Klong Slin Pathamasambodhi. Bangkok: Dhammasabha). LVV resembles the Vientiane edition of AD 1970.
- Two-group genre split used by the author: group 1 versions (LPL, TYC, PLN, TKN, TLE, TLC) recount the Buddha's life only up to the first preaching to the pancavaggiya; group 2 (LPR recensions, MON, CBD, LVV, NET, SMT) add the life after the first preaching.
- Bangkok-held witnesses listed: Pali Pathamasambodhi kept in the Vajiranana-dhamma Chapel, the Royal Palace, Bangkok, 17 bundles (complete), BE 2331; Pali version at the National Library, Bangkok, 18 bundles (complete), undated.
- Tai Khoeun version: 26 bundles (complete), undated; Tai Lue version at Min Wui, Sipsongpanna, Yunnan, PRC, 8 bundles.

### Scholarship history noted in the paper

Cœdès wrote on the Thai Yuan/Northern Thai version and another Pali version; Anatole-Roger Peltier on the Tai Khoeun version (1987); Nattinee Phromkerd on the Mon version (1987); Canya Khongcarern discovered/studied the Isan or NE Thai version (1989); Bampen Rawin published the Thai Yuan version (1989) and its concise Pali-Thai Yuan dual-language subversion (1997); CMU Center for Promotion of Arts and Culture discovered the very old Lanna Pali version (1992); Kumiko Kato & Isra Yanatan reported the Tai Lue version (1999/2001); EFEO discovered the Cambodian version (Les Leçons Khmères de la Pathamasambodhi dans les Bibliothèques du Cambodge, unpublished ms 2001).
