## A. Page-format rule (gap: double-small-character commentary columns)

From the Format section (p. 222):

> "The upper and lower margins of the leaf are called respectively the book eyebrow (shu mei) or heavenly head (thien thou), which is usually wider than the lower margin or the earthly foot (ti chiueh). The page on each side of the leaf is ruled into columns and spaces (hang ko) with border lines (chieh) to divide individual columns and marginal lines (pien lan), single or double, on four sides. **The characters of the text are usually arranged in one vertical line within a column, and notes or commentaries in smaller characters in two lines. Each page may contain from five to ten columns, with from ten to thirty characters in each column.** This basic format of the printed sheet and some of the bibliographical terms used for referring to the traditional format of the Chinese book, have been continued into modern times."

Other format terms defined on pp. 222-223 (Wade-Giles as printed): pan mien (block face), pan hsin (heart of block, centre fold), hsiang pi (elephant trunk fold-line), yu wei (fish tail folding marks), running title/leaf number/cutter name in narrow column at the fold, shu erh ("book ear", square sign on upper left margin in some Sung editions, thumb index for butterfly binding).

Calligraphy styles used for dating Sung editions (pp. 224-225): Ou-yang Hsun (+557-641), Yen Chen-Chhing (+709-85), Liu Kung-Chhiuan (+778-865); "Northern Sung editions follow closely the Yen style ... those of Southern Sung imitated the Ou-yang style if produced in the Hangchow area and the Yen and Liu styles if produced in Fukien"; early Yuan continues Yen-Liu then shifts to Chao Meng-Fu (+1254-1322). Footnote cites Poon Ming-Sun (2), pp. 204 ff. for criteria of dating Sung editions.

## B. Five Dynasties/Sung official printing of the classics (context for interleaved-commentary editions)

Feng Tao project (p. 210 area):

> "...it began in +932 when the chief counsellor recommended the recruiting of learned scholars from the National Academy to make a collation of the text based on the version of the Stone Classics of the Thang dynasty, and of good calligraphers to transcribe the correct text on paper in standard style, and this to be cut on wood blocks. Five sheets of text were to be prepared each day. The text included the eleven classics authorised in the Thang, plus two supplementary works on the forms of characters used in the Five [Classics] and Nine Classics. The task took some twenty-two years; the collection was completed and printed in 130 volumes in +953. This was the first time that the Confucian classics were printed, and the beginning of official publications for sale by the National Academy."

Jingdian shiwen printing (next paragraph):

> "Two years later, in +955, a lexicon of classical terms, Ching Tien Shih Wen [= 經典釋文, hanzi verified in loop_07], was entrusted to Thien Min for printing. Four years later (959), the Shang Shu Shih Wen, a section of the above, was prepared for printing by Kuo Chung-Shu (+918?-77). It was twice revised and reprinted, in 972 and in 999."

Sung National Academy programme (p. 214 area):

> "From the late 980s, about thirty years after the founding of the Sung, the printing of Confucian classics and standard histories by the National Academy began. This included the new commentaries on the twelve classics printed in +988-96 and the re-engraving in 1005 of the standard Feng Tao edition of the twelve classics, while with the addition of the Meng Tzu in 1011, the standard [...]"

Block-count datum (p. 369-370 area):

> "...when a large printing project was started by the Sung government in +988, only some 4000 wood blocks were kept in the National Academy, but by 1005, when the emperor inquired about the project, Hsing Pin (+930-1010), then Director of the National Academy, reported that the wood blocks at the Academy numbered 100,000, representing all kinds of classics, histories, and their commentaries."

(Hsing Pin = 邢昺, hanzi verified in loop_07 via 論語注疏 e-text.)

## C. Jianyang printers of classics with commentaries (late Sung)

Yu family of Jianyang (p. 218 area):

> "The most prominent printer of this family known to us in the Sung dynasty was Yi Jen-chung (fl. +1130-93), who held a chin-shih degree and collected over 10,000 volumes; his studio was therefore called Wan Chuan Lou (Tower of Ten Thousand Rolls). He printed many titles we still know, including the famous Nine Classics and Three Commentaries published at the end of the +12th century."

Liao Ying-chung, Shih Tshai Thang (p. 220 area):

> "Another noted Fukien printing house was the Shih Tshai Thang (Hall of Colourful Generations) of Liao Ying-chung (+1200?-75), a scholar-official from Shao-wu (in Fukien), who published a de luxe edition of the Nine Classics and Three Commentaries around +1270. This edition was noted for its careful collation, refined execution, its excellent ink and paper, and its rich decoration; unfortunately it was soon destroyed by the Mongol invasion of southern China. However, a facsimile of this edition was reprinted in about +1300, together with a manual of collation and printing, the Chiu Ching San Chuan Yen Ko Li (Manual for the Transmission of the Nine Classics and Three Commentaries), which provided specifications for the selection of editions, the style of calligraphy, its emendation, pronunciation, punctuation, and collation. This work has been praised as a classic model of textual criticism and printing ever since."

(The manual = 九經三傳沿革例; full text harvested separately in Song-Anonymous-JiujingSanzhuanYangeli-yinshi.md.)

## Relevance to Loop B01

1. Direct typological statement that Song-era book pages set main text one character per column and commentary in small characters over two sub-columns (= 小字雙行 convention described in Western-language scholarship).
2. Chronology anchoring official classic+commentary printing (953; 988-1011) against which commercial interleaved formats (附音/音釋 editions of Jianyang and Shu) must be placed.
3. Names and floruits for Yu Renzhong (余仁仲; Wade-Giles Yi Jen-chung) and Liao Yingzhong (廖瑩中) whose editions are the core 附釋音 lineage.
