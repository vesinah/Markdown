## Status quaestionis reconstructed by Herren

- Traube and Rand identified three strands of Carolingian Martianus commentary: oldest assigned to Dunchad, then Eriugena, then Remigius of Auxerre.
- Cora Lutz edited the two older commentaries from a single manuscript (Paris, BN lat. 12960) in the 1930s-40s; Remigius circulated widely (bipartite tradition).
- Claudio Leonardi, "I codici di Martianus Capella" (Aevum 33-34, 1959[-1960]) revealed many more early copies of commentaries; promised sequel never materialized.
- Jean Préaux (Latomus 12, 1953): twelve of these belong to the Oldest Gloss Tradition (OGT).
- Édouard Jeauneau (1978 per Herren) edited the second manuscript of the Eriugena commentary (O version; Oxford, Bodleian Auct. T.2), comprising Book 1 only.

## Teeuwen's four results (as summarized by Herren) — core evidence for palace-court attribution debate

1. The earliest Martianus scholia predate the activity of Dunchad.
2. The oldest manuscript is Leiden Voss. lat. fol. 48, re-dated by Teeuwen to ca. 830 — "some 20-30 years earlier than Bernhard Bischoff's dating (saec. IX2)" — and localized at Corbie.
3. "that the OGT was the work of several scholars active in the reign of Louis the Pious (rather than Charles the Bald, as previously thought)".
4. VLF 48 reflects the original FORMAT of the commentary: "a text of Martianus accompanied by marginal and interlinear scholia rather than a continuous commentary which had been 'marginalized,' to use James Zetzel's term."
- Teeuwen does not claim VLF 48 is a codex archetypus; divergences across the tradition are considerable.

## O'Sullivan's stemma (her chapter "The Stemmatic Relationship between the Manuscripts Transmitting the Oldest Gloss Tradition", pp. 35-54)

- Three main groups A, B, C, each with posited subarchetype; group A contains the oldest manuscripts (first half of the ninth to tenth century).
- Groupings established by scholia, confirmed by accompanying Martianus text groupings and diagrams.
- "All together there are fifteen manuscripts of OGT, of which only two are written continuously and lack the source text: Pd (Paris 12960 ...) and Or (Orléans 191), both ... group B."
- Later additions flagged, e.g., those by Rather of Verona.
- Transmission centres (in her other chapter, pp. 99-121): "Auxerre-Tours-Fleury, Corbie-Rheims, and Lorsch-Cologne". Two Insular witnesses: Cambridge CCC 153 (Wales) and CCC 330 (England) share a running commentary drawing on Eriugena and the oldest tradition.

## Dunchad evidence and its limits

- Only contemporary witness: leaf published by Mario Esposito 1909, misplaced in London BL Reg. 15. A. XXXIII (saec. IX): a certain Duncaht, Irish bishop teaching at Saint-Remi (Rheims), wrote a commentum on the "astrologia" of Martianus for his students there.
- Herren: the notice gives no floruit, so Teeuwen's argument from redating is moot; but this hardly proves Dunchad authored Lutz's text either.

## Palace-school statements found here

- Ilaria Ramelli (pp. 245-271): "She regards the De nuptiis as a prime teaching text used by Eriugena at the schola palatina." Also: the Annotaciones (P) are excerpta — called "Martiani expositio a Iohanne excerpta" in the twelfth-century Corbie catalogue; Remigius cites Eriugenian scholia not preserved by Corbie.
- Teeuwen's own paper "Writing Between the Lines: Reflections of Scholarly Debate in a Carolingian Commentary Tradition" (11-34): underscores lack of unity of OGT, repeats collaboration hypothesis.

## Herren's critical reservations (useful counterweight)

- If Bischoff was right (VLF 48 second half of ninth century, Corbie), Eriugenian interpolations could have entered it (Eriugena working on Martianus by 851).
- On the collective model: "I continue to be puzzled by the democratic model proposed for the composition of OGT. Did scholars sit at a common table and devise explanations to passages, then hammer out a common gloss?" Alternative: single-editor model like Bern 363 where an editor-scribe assembles scholia with author identifications. "Still, someone had to conceive of and direct the project. ... We may need more evidence for glossing by committees."

## Other chapters noted (for context map)

- Calvin Bower, quadrivial reasoning/allegorical revelation (Boethius foundational Neoplatonic treatises; secular curriculum).
- Luhtala on Alcuin's De vera philosophia and divisions of knowledge; liberal arts innate in soul (also in VLF 48 dialectic book).
- Lozovsky, perceptions of the classical past; historia vs fabula; Euhemerism.
- Garrison on Books IV-V: commentators likely highly learned, writing from memory; possible Italian colleague (PALES dea papulorum emendation).
- Guillaumin: monad/dyad; commentator corrects Martianus with Augustine/Boethius ("Ternarius primus numerus est").
- Eastwood: power of diagrams in Carolingian astronomy; descriptive rather than explanatory stance.
- McCluskey: astronomy study traditions (computus, solar horizon, zodiac prediction).
- Lendinara: Scholica Graecarum Glossarum, three growth stages, forthcoming edition.
- Godden/Jayatilaka: Boethius Consolatio gloss project; earliest known glossed Consolatio manuscript Vat. lat. 3363 (France, first half ninth c.).
