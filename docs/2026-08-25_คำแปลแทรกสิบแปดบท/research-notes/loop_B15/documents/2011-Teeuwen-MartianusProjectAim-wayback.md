## Introduction section — verbatim anchors

Carolingian context: Admonitio generalis (789) and Epistola de litteris colendis (790s) "issued in the name of Charlemagne ... intended to establish a kingdom-wide policy"; proper Latin, liturgy, copying. Under Charlemagne, Louis the Pious, Charles the Bald "the schools of Carolingian Europe flourished". "Education became a booming bussiness in the large monastic centres of the time."

Court activity: "The Carolingian kings not only promoted education; their courts played a more active role in the pursuit of knowledge. At the royal palace, monarch and masters engaged in intellectual conversations about a wide range of theological subjects: the cult of images, predestination, the Trinity, etc. (Freeman 1985; Chazelle 2001). Secular subjects ... seem to have been treated in a similar manner. Classical texts were subjected to textual criticism, corrected and provided with annotations; these enriched texts were brought into circulation."

Music-of-the-spheres debates: consensus not achieved in commentaries (Teeuwen 2002; Eastwood 2001).

The anonymous commentary: added to De nuptiis "roughly around 830"; explains grammar/syntax/vocabulary plus accessus, mythology, numerical speculation, heavens, cosmic harmony.

Transmission: "This commentary survives in sixteen manuscripts, twelve of which are dated in the ninth-century; and it is only in part available in a modern edition (Lutz 1944)."

Dating and localization: "an analysis of the manuscript tradition of text and commentary has led to the conclusion that it can be dated roughly between 820 and 840. Most manuscripts are dated to the reigns of Louis the Pious and Charles the Bald." Places suggested: "Auxerre, Corbie, Reims, and North(east) France" (Leonardi 1959-1960; Préaux 1978; Teeuwen 2002).

Authorship candidates rejected: Dunchad (Lutz 1944); Martin of Laon (Préaux 1953); links with Lupus of Ferrières (Shanzer 1986). "In contemporary sources, however, the commentary has remained anonymous."

Format: "Text and commentary were almost always copied together, in manuscripts especially laid-out for this purpose (with ample space around the text), which strongly suggests that the commentary was perceived as an integral part of the text."

THE PALACE-COURT HYPOTHESIS (verbatim): "In a previous study I have suggested that the commentary on De nuptiis, which fits so well within the educational ideal as animated by Alcuin, was a collective scholarly venture, perhaps produced by intellectual circles at the courts of Louis the Pious or Charles the Bald (Teeuwen 2002)."

Final interpretive stance (verbatim): "The commentary traditions gathered in single, exemplary manuscripts are to be interpreted not as school texts, but as encyclopedic collections of secular learning, created by and for the intellectual elites of the great Carolingian monasteries and courts."

## Edition section — method

- Semi-diplomatic edition based on critical reading of one central manuscript: Leiden, UB, Vossianus Latinus Folio 48.
- Checked/corrected against three others: Besancon, BM, Ms. 594; Leiden, UB, BPL 88; Vatican, BAV, Reg.lat. 1987.
- Also checked against editions of 'Dunchad' (Lutz), John the Scot, Remigius of Auxerre.
- Rationale for fluidity: "No two manuscripts have the same corpus of glosses ... The fluidity of their transmission, even when they form a coherent and official corpus, seems to be an essential part of their literary status (Zetzel 2005)."
- Specialist network per book: Thomas Brouwer (book 4 correction), Bruce Eastwood (book 8 Astronomia per this page), Mary Garrison (initial transcription books 4-5), Jean-Yves Guillaumin (book 7 Arithmetica per this page), Natalia Lozovsky (book 6 Geometria), Arpad Orbán (advisor book 3), Sinéad O'Sullivan (books 1-2), Aleksander Sroczynski (book 5 correction), Mariken Teeuwen (book 9 Harmonia/Musica; initial transcriptions books 3,4,5,7; overall coordinator).
- NOTE internal inconsistency between this page's numbering (Eastwood=VIII, Guillaumin=VII) and Fischer's RIDE review (Eastwood=Buch VII Astronomie, Guillaumin=Buch VIII Arithmetik); flag when citing team assignments.

## CCCM publication plan (status statement c. 2010-2012)

Verbatim: "In 2010-2012, the commentary tradition will also be published in books: we plan to publish two volumes in the Brepols series Corpus Christianorum Continuatio Medievalis. The first volume has now come out: a critical edition of the commentary tradition on books I-II of De nuptiis (ed. S. O'Sullivan, CCCM Vol. 237), the second volume will be a semi-diplomatic edition of books 3-5 (trivial arts) and 6-9 (quadrivial arts)."
