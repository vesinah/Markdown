## Content notes (from the fetched excerpt)

### Manuscript identification

- Object: Old Uyghur translation of the Guanwuliangshoujing (Apartment Sutra of Visualizing Amitayus, Taisho no. 365).
- Fragments discussed: Berlin U 1499 + St Petersburg SI 1748 (Serindia Collection, IOM RAS). The smaller fragment of U 1499 must be placed after a lacuna of two lines; SI 1748 joins directly with the larger fragment of U 1499 on the left side; the smaller fragment forms the lower part of lines 8-11.
- Provenance clue: Berlin fragment U 1499 bears old signature T I alpha, so one can assume SI 1748 was found at the same ruin alpha in Kocho (Gaochang).
- Kogishi Kudara's fundamental assessment: the sheet represents "a rather free rendering of the Chinese text by the Uighur translator" (Zieme & Kudara 1985, 21-23).

### Translation behaviour relevant to glossing/bilingual practice

- When a Sanskrit name is transcribed, the Old Uyghur follows the Chinese; but in the buyan avirmak (merit-transferring) section of the Altun Yaruk Sudur the same mountain is freely rendered: "kara kush sangirlig arig ol grtirakut tagta" = "on the mountain with the vulture peak", where the pronoun ol functions as an inline gloss for Grdhrakuta (Suv. 682/22-23); occurrence not recorded in UWN II.1.
- The opening list gives 32,000 bodhisattvas; the extant list names 15 bodhisattvas beginning with Manjushri (the only one named in the Chinese as first), then Samantabhadra, Avalokiteshvara, Mahasthamaprapta. Kudara suggested an Old Uighur Buddhist compiled this list himself, wanting to give a more vivid picture than the simple Chinese statement.
- Comparative data: the Old Uighur Dunhuang version of the Larger SukhAvativyUha-sUtra has only 15 bodhisattvas with different names (Kudara 1995; Kudara & Zieme 1997, Studies on the Inner Asian Languages XII, 73-82).

## Relevance to project

Documents the Uyghur habit of embedding Chinese-derived names and short Chinese passages inside running Old Uyghur prose, with occasional native-language appositions functioning as internal glosses - a counterpart of interlinear glossing inside continuous translation.
