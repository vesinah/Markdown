## Content notes

### The trilingual 1149 block-print edition from Khara-Khoto

- During the reign of Emperor Renzong of Western Xia (1139-1193), Buddhist publication peaked; Tangut colophons state that various Buddhist texts were printed "in Tangut, Chinese and Tibetan in thousands of copies for distribution among the participants of the national Dharma assemblies" (citing Solonin 2015, 849).
- The only surviving example of one edition extant in all three languages: the Avalokiteshvara dharani-sutra and the Ushnishavijaya dharani-sutra with a postscript by Emperor Renzong himself, published in 1149 (first year of reign period "Heavenly Prosperity"; Tibetan date probable).
- Call numbers (all IOM RAS, Kozlov Khara-Khoto collection unless noted):
  - Tibetan block print: Kh. Tib. 67, joined by Kh. Tib. 63, 64, 68 (Zorin & Sizova established all four belong to one edition); fragments published/fixed by Shirai 2004, Stoddard 2010, Zorin & Sizova 2019a-b.
  - Stein collection British Library: Or. 8212/1914 (Takeuchi 1997-1998: No. 674) identified as part of the same edition.
  - Chinese block prints: TK-164 and TK-165 (catalogued in Menshikov 1984).
  - Tangut printed book: Tang. 109 (old inv. Nos. 6796, 6821), identical to TK-164/165.
- The connection between Tang. 109 and Chinese TK-164/TK-165 was revealed by Tatsuo Nishida in the introduction to Kychanov's 1999 Catalogue of Tangut Buddhist Texts; colophon of Tang. 109 dates distribution "in the first year under the reign title of Heavenly Prosperity under the cyclic signs of Snake-Earth (in 1149)" (Kychanov 1999: 581). Shen Weirong (2010) had connected Chinese and Tibetan editions without knowing the dated Tangut counterpart; Schaeffer (2009) reports Shen's earlier date 1153 as unclear.
- The Tibetan edition matters for print history: until recently no earlier specimen of Tibetan printing was known; Shi Jinbo (2005) discussed Kh. Tib. 67 / Kh.Tib. 63/68 as butterfly-binding ("hufengzhuang") examples; translator JayAnanda is named in the colophon of the second text.

### Interlinear element (scholarly, not manuscript-internal)

- Lin Ying-chin (2011, Xixia xue 8, 23-61) produced a collation of the Tangut and Chinese postscripts "and interlinear translation into Chinese" - i.e., a modern interlinear bilingual presentation of the Tangut Ushnishavijaya material; she did not use the Tibetan text. Hamanaka supplied an English translation of the Tangut imperial postscript.
- The dhAranI-sUtras themselves were analysed by Shirai Satoko (2004) and Duan Yuquan (2010, "comparison research on SarvatathAgatoShNIshavijaya in its Tangut, Chinese and Tibetan versions", Xixia xue 5, 29-37).

## Relevance to project

Provides concrete Khara-Khoto/IOM RAS call numbers for a trilingual parallel print culture around Tangut dharani literature, documents emperor-authored postscripts as colophons, and records the modern scholarly interlinear collation practice (Lin Ying-chin) applied to Tangut-Chinese ritual texts.
