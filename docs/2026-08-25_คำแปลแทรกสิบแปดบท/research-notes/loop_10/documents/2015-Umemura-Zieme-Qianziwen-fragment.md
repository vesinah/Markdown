## Content notes (from fetched full text)

### The Old Uighur Qianziwen corpus (Thousand Character Classic as bilingual teaching text)

The authors edit fragment SI 1850 (Kr IV 312) of the Serindia Collection, Institute of Oriental Manuscripts RAS, St Petersburg, joining it to previously published pieces. Five distinct Old Uighur versions/manuscripts of the Qianziwen are distinguished:

1. Berlin Ch/U 8152 verso (studied by Tsuneki Nishiwaki 2002). Its verso carries the Old Uighur phrase "[miNG uzh]iklarniNG chizigi" ("The Essay of a Thousand Characters"); compare strophe 21 of the Old Uighur Qianziwen: shi zhi wen zi = shu chi [transcribed] + wenzi [written as characters] = ashnu-cha yarati-p uzhik chizig "First they created syllables and lines".
2. Second manuscript: SI 4000 (4bKr 182), SI 4006 (4bKr 194), SI 4084 (4bKr 185) (joins supplied by Akihito Yoshida; also SI 3924 (4bKr 155) and SI 3864 (4bKr 38)).
3. Third manuscript: SI 4083 (4bKr 181).
4. Fourth: mentioned by Zieme 1999.
5. Fifth manuscript: SI 5778 (3Kr 14) + SI 5836 (3Kr 15) + SI 1787 (Kr IV 260) + new SI 1850 (Kr IV 312); covers the first 38 four-character units (152 characters), lines 37-52.

Coverage across all fragments: sections 1-38, 82-101, 104-117, 114-146, 224-241, i.e. about 440 of the 1000 characters - nearly half of the Qianziwen attested in Old Uighur translation, albeit in different manuscripts.

Layout fact important for interlinear/bilingual studies: the Chinese recto has 17 characters per line; the Uighur translation stands on the verso, following the four-character group as structural unit.

### Mixed-script quoting practice (key for glossing typology)

The edition presents each section with: Chinese characters in the Old Uighur manuscript quoted as "characters only / transcription only / mixed". The authors state there is NO fixed arrangement: "We guess that the translator chose the method of transcription in the case of difficult characters, but, of course, there is no proof of that." Examples from the edition:

- Section 27 zuo chao wen dao: "The scribe quotes the text half in transcription, half as characters."
- Section 28 chui gong ping zhang: quotation given only in transcription; commentary notes: "There is no rule for quoting Chinese text, laying down which words were quoted as in the original Chinese text and which were transcribed."
- Section 31 xia er yi ti: only the third character is written as a Chinese character (yi instead of the variant yi); the scribe encircled a wrongly omitted passage with ((...)) and inserted the skipped translation.
- Section 33 ming feng zai shu: two characters quoted as Chinese, two in Old Uighur transcription; feng "phoenix" rendered by circumlocution yuy Kush "king of the peacock birds", paralleled in the Old Uighur Xuanzang Biography.
- Section 34 bai ju shi chang: translator replaced "white pony" with kilin (qilin/unicorn); the word urtgun "threshing-floor" is attested in Brahmi script (per Maue 2015, VOHD documents in Brahmi and Tibetan script).
- Section 36 lai ji wan fang: all characters quoted; wanfang circumlocuted as tumen ulush-dakilar-ka "those in ten thousand countries".

Translation technique observations: free interpretive renderings (chao "court" = kozunchlug orun "precious throne"; dao = toru yol "way of the law"; zhi "to govern" = bashla-, compared with SuvarnabhAsottamasUtra Suv 565/3-6 where zhi is likewise bashla-); additions such as kamag "whole"; comparison throughout with Erich Hauer 1925 Mongolian Qianziwen translation.
