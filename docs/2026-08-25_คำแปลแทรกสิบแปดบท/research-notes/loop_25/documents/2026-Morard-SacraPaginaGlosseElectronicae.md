## Key facts and passages (verbatim where quoted)

### What the edition is

> Le programme collaboratif Gloss-e.2.0 prends le relai de glossae.net, lance en 2009 pour rendre interrogeable en ligne la premiere edition imprimee de la Bible glosee parue en 1480/1481. L'edition electronique en propose une version referencable, enrichie de textes inedits. Sous le nom de Sacra Pagina CNRS, le site procure des editions electroniques natives dynamiques.

> Le site accueille desormais huit corpus complementaires, 500'000 sentences exegetiques associees au texte la Bible, a ses prologues et paratextes majeurs, verifiees sur manuscrits ou destinees a l'etre, accompagnees d'introductions originales, soit l'equivalent de 46'000 pages A4 ou 12050 pages web indexees par les moteurs recherche.

### Corpus list (site map)

1. Biblia communis: medieval Vulgate (BLAMAT) established from the Glossa ordinaria 1481 print collated with the critical Hieronymian Vulgate, plus correctoria and Paris booktrade bible witnesses; over 350 prologues and major paratexts.
2. Glossa ordinaria: "Martin Morard, ed., Biblia latina cum glossa ordinaria aucta, edition scientifique amendee, enrichie de prologues, sentences et collations inedites, d'apres l'edition princeps d'Adolf Rusch pro Antonio Koberger, Strasbourg 1480/1481, 2415 p. [ISTC n. ib00607000]"
3. Glossa media in Psalmos et epistolas Pauli of Gilbert de la Porrée (c. 1120-1140), ed. Morard 2024, after Karlfried Froehlich's transcription of ms Zwettl, Stiftsbibl. 58.
4. Glossa magna of Peter Lombard (Psalms and Pauline epistles), with prefaces by Herbert of Bosham.
5. Philip the Chancellor, Distinctiones/Summa super Psalterium.
6. Hugh of Saint-Cher, Postille super totam Bibliam — presented as "un apparat ad Glosam" (an apparatus built on the Gloss).
7. Thomas Aquinas, Catena aurea in quattuor evangelia; and Postilla super partem Psalterii (Ps. 1-54) — whose editorial file includes a section titled "Les lacunes textuelles de la tradition directe, stigmates de la reportation?" (textual lacunae as traces of lecture transcription).
8. Nicholas of Lyra, Postilla litteralis et mystica.

### Manuscript infrastructure

- GLOSSEM manuscript inventory ("Manuscrits" section): sigles, codicology, "Mises en page et codicologie des bibles latines glosees" instrument; separate handlist at https://glossaordinaria.net/ which labels gloss families: 'parva' (Anselm of Laon or his school), 'ordinaria' (begun at Laon, completed/standardized in late-twelfth-century schools), 'media' (Gilbert of Poitiers), 'magna' (Peter Lombard), 'prisca' (proto-version).

### Related digital resource

- Pitts Theology Library note on the editio princeps copy: "Pitts' copy was printed in 1480 in Strasbourg by Adolf Rusch for the Nuremberg-based publisher Anton Koberger" — https://pitts.emory.edu/tag/glossa-ordinaria

## Notes for the book chapter

- The Rusch print is the textual backbone of the modern digital edition: the incunable that froze the medieval apparatus now serves as citable electronic text.
- The Aquinas postilla file explicitly connects reportatio (lecture transcription) with textual gaps — direct evidence for the lecture-culture link (research question 6).
