## Key extracted passages (verbatim)

> The latter half of the 12th century was the high-point of Giant Bible production in England, and the surviving volumes are characterised by their generous dimensions, their preference for the pandect format (i.e., the whole text of the Bible in a single volume), and their exquisite decorative schemes, which made extensive use of opaque polychrome illustration, historiated initials [...], and frequent use of gold as a ground and as a decorative embellishment.

> Famous giant bibles include:
> - The 'Bury Bible' (Cambridge, Corpus Christi College, MS 2, now bound in 3 parts)
> - The 'Dover Bible' (Cambridge, Corpus Christi College, MS 3)
> - The 'Lambeth Bible' (London, Lambeth Palace Library, MS 3)
> - The 'Winchester Bible' (Winchester, Cathedral Library, MS 17 and New York, Morgan Library, MS M.619)

> By the early 13th century, the highest grade of bible was still exquisitely decorated, but the fashionable format for bibles increasingly tended towards more compact volumes that prioritised comfortable consultation and transport over monumental effect.

> This manuscript is a composite volume and comprises a complete copy of the Bible written in Latin and made in England in the first half of the 13th century, accompanied by four later quires of exegetical material that helped readers to understand the text of the Bible. These quires include Stephen Langton's Interpretationes nominum hebraicorum ('Interpretations of Hebrew names') and Peter of Poitiers' Compendium historiae in genealogia Christi [...]. MS Dd.8.12 is one of the last examples of the 'Giant Bibles' produced in the Romanesque decorative style in England.

## Notes for the book chapter

- Distinguishes two large-format traditions around the rise of the Gloss: monastic giant pandect Bibles (display, lectern reading) versus the commercial Paris biblia glosata (multi-volume, study use); the thirteenth-century compact Paris Bible then displaced both for portability.
- Companion CUDL description (MS Dd.1.14) notes the development "from the mid-twelfth century onwards of glossed books of the Bible, in which the text was heavily annotated with theological interpretation," against intervals favouring single-volume bibles: https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/view/MS-DD-00001-00014/13
