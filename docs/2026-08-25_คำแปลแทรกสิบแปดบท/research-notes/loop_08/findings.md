## Key findings (with URLs)

1. Two works, one name — core data verified. Xuanying's Yiqiejing yinyi: oldest extant Buddhist sound-and-meaning dictionary, 25 juan, compiled c. 648 (Zhenguan 22) by imperial order at Daci'en temple; glosses hard words from 454 texts consulting Sanskrit sounds. Huilin's Yiqiejing yinyi (T2128, 100 juan confirmed in CBETA's official list): composed 783-807; covers 1220 texts from the Daborei jing onward; incorporates Xuanying plus Yun'gong/Ji/Huiyuan yinyi and lexicographic sources (Yupian, Shuowen, Zilin, Zitong, Gujin zhengzi, Kaiyuan wenzi yinyi); its phonetic glosses reflect contemporary Chang'an pronunciation. Sources: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%80%E5%88%87%E7%B5%8C%E9%9F%B3%E7%BE%A9 ; http://www.cbeta.org/cbreader/help/list_Taisho.htm

2. Institutional origin of the glosses (RQ4). Xuanying served as wenzi dade 文字大徳 ("great virtue of letters") inside Xuanzang's translation bureau (訳場) — the Yinyi is a bureau product, like Baggio's characterization of Zhu Weimojie jing T1775 as a "side-product" of Kumarajiva's enterprise. CHEN Jinhua 2005 shows the earliest catalogue (Chu sanzang jiji) recorded translations in interlinear notes whose date-formula chu 出 encodes a four-stage procedure: enunciation, oral translation, draft subject to editorial/doctrinal revision, completion. Sources: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%80%E5%88%87%E7%B5%8C%E9%9F%B3%E7%BE%A9 ; https://doi.org/10.2143/ja.293.2.2011780 ; https://doi.org/10.33612/diss.94589377

3. Layout of glosses (RQ1/RQ3). Within the Yinyi manuscript tradition itself two calligraphic formats coexist: (type 1) headword and annotation same size; (type 2) large headword characters with small-letter annotations — the East Asian small-note (xiaozhu-type) convention; choice varies volume-by-volume even within one codex (typology after Ishizuka & Ikeda 1991 covering Dunhuang AND Japanese manuscripts). A manuscript lineage (Japanese temple copies: Kongōji, Hōryūji, Nanatsudera, Saihōji, Kōshōji, Hiroshima U.) is distinct from a printed lineage (Goryeo, Fuzhou, Tōzenji, Qisha, Sixi). Source (English full text): https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ibk/71/3/71_1128/_pdf/-char/en

4. Glosses as textual criticism (RQ1). LI Naiqi demonstrates that formulae like "經文作X" make Yinyi entries function as emendation proposals — an apparatus of variant readings for sutras circulating c. 650 — and uses old Japanese Yinyi copies to compare word usage between the old (Paramartha) and new (Xuanzang) Kośa translations. Source: https://doi.org/10.4259/ibk.69.1_268

5. Commentary genre already contains sound-glossing (RQ2). Kanno Hiroshi's study of Daosheng's Miaofa lianhua jing shu (earliest substantial jing shu) enumerates its formal repertoire — raii 来意, section division 分科, lemma citation, presentation of alternative views — explicitly including interpretation of transliterated words (音写語の解釈); Zhiyi's Tiantai practice is documented in his 2020 comparison with Sanlun Jizang; Sengzhao's commentary tradition is covered by Baggio's T1775 monograph. Sources: https://doi.org/10.4259/ibk.39.74 ; https://doi.org/10.4259/ibk.69.1_1 ; https://doi.org/10.33612/diss.94589377

6. Dunhuang manuscript practices around sutras (RQ3). Keyworth compares cave-17 Dunhuang colophons (e.g., P.ch.2056) with Nara-period canon-copying colophons (Shōgozō, Nanatsudera, Matsuo shrine), showing shared yiqie jing / issaikyō vow-copying and proofreading-oriented colophons (okugaki 奥書 / shikigo 識語); R. Chen (T'oung Pao 2020) shows colophon motives matched scripture content for three indigenous sutras. Sources: https://brill.com/view/book/9789004687066/BP000013.xml ; https://doi.org/10.1163/15685322-10656p03

7. Japan bridge confirmed (RQ5). The Yinyi reached Japan together with the canon and was copied repeatedly in temples (Li 2023); Huilin's version survived only through the Koryo-Korea route to Japan and was re-imported to China during Guangxu (kotobank/Sekai daihyakka); a 2024 De Gruyter chapter treats exactly this propagation-to-Korea/reintroduction path; a 2019 Japanese study ties Xuanying's Yinyi to the Kamakura Pure Land triple-sutra glossary (Jōdosanbukyō ongishū). Liao monk Xilin continued the series in 987 (Xu Yiqiejing yinyi). Sources: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ibk/71/3/71_1128/_pdf/-char/en ; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%80%E5%88%87%E7%B5%8C%E9%9F%B3%E7%BE%A9 ; https://doi.org/10.1515/9783111322599-003 ; https://doi.org/10.5845/bukkyobunka.2018.27_111

8. Digital canon and authorities (RQ6). CBETA (cbeta.org; CBETA Online at cbetaonline.dila.edu.tw built by DILA; open XML at github.com/cbeta-org/xml-p5; release 2026.R1) hosts T2123/T2128; SAT Daizojo DB (Univ. Tokyo, est. 1998) digitizes Taisho vols 1-85 with IIIF images and OCR alignment projects; DILA Authority databases (authority.dila.edu.tw) provide time/person/place/catalogue control used inside CBETA Online. Sources: https://cbeta.org/ ; https://21dzk.l.u-tokyo.ac.jp/SAT/ ; https://authority.dila.edu.tw/
