## Verified facts extracted from the entries
1. General definition: books explaining pronunciation and meaning of difficult words/phrases in the Buddhist canon (一切経の難語句について、発音と意味を解説した仏書). Two Tang works bear this title.
2. Xuanying's Yiqiejing yinyi (玄応音義):
   - Daijisen: by Tang monk Xuanying 玄応, 25 juan, formed c. 648 CE; also called 玄応音義.
   - Nipponica (signed): compiled c. 648 (貞観22) by Xuanying of Daci'en Temple 大慈恩寺 by imperial order (勅により撰述); glosses compound words (熟語) from 454 sutra-vinaya-sastra texts consulting Sanskrit sounds (サンスクリット音を参照して注解); oldest extant yinyi; accurate interpretation; precious for quoting many lost old books (古逸書).
   - Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (seisenban): 25 juan; mid-7th c.; selects hard words from 458 jing-lun texts.
   - Heibonsha Sekai Daihyakka (signed Keiya Toshinobu): "Xuanying served as wenzi dade 文字大徳 ('great virtue of letters') in Xuanzang's translation bureau 訳場" (玄応は文字大徳として玄奘の訳場に列した). Qing scholars put his yinyi to philological use.
3. Huilin's Yiqiejing yinyi (慧琳音義):
   - Daijisen: by Tang monk Huilin 慧琳, 100 juan, formed between 783 and 807 CE.
   - Britannica International (compact): written 建中4 (783) to 元和2 (807); 100 juan; synthesizes Xuanying's 25-juan work and earlier yinyi types; extracts and precisely annotates hard words from 1225 Mahayana/Hinayana sutra-vinaya-sastra texts; shows that the Qieyun phonological system had already changed by then; perished in China and survives only in Japan.
   - Nipponica: composed between 783 (one account 788) and 807 (one account 810); starts from the Daborei kyō 大般若経 and ends with Gomyō hōshō kyō 護命放生経; annotates compounds from 1220 texts while consulting Yupian 玉篇, Shuowen 説文, Zilin 字林, Zitong 字統, Gujin zhengzi 古今正字, Wenzi dianshuo 文字典説, Kaiyuan wenzi yinyi 開元文字音義; distinctive merit = inheriting Xuanying's work and incorporating the yinyi of Ungong 雲公, Ki 基, and Eon 慧苑 (Huiyuan); most precise of the yinyi.
   - Heibonsha Sekai Daihyakka: Huilin was a Kashgar native (カシュガルの人) and disciple of the translator Amoghavajra 不空; his phonetic notes are said to reflect contemporary Chang'an pronunciation, with analytical observation of syllable components especially visible; the Huilin Yinyi was lost in China, transmitted via Korea (Koryŏ 高麗) to Japan, and re-imported to China during the Guangxu era 光緒年間 (1875-1908); Liao dynasty monk Xilin 希麟 produced the Xu Yiqiejing yinyi 続一切経音義 in 987.
