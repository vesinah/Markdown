## Abstract (verbatim from J-STAGE)
"The Yiqiejing yinyi 一切経音義 compiled by Xuanying 玄應 (7th c.) of the Tang dynasty is the earliest extant text on the sounds and meanings of Buddhist texts. It is an important work that reflects the characteristics of Buddhist texts in the process of formation at the time of its compilation. Descriptive expressions such as "the scripture gives (經文作) . . ." in the Yiqiejing yinyi function to propose emendations and to explain and analyze the usage of words in Buddhist texts. Some entries in the Yiqiejing yinyi constitute Xuanying's proposals for emending terms found in Buddhist texts. On the basis of Old Japanese manuscripts, this paper surveys the differences in word usage between the two Chinese translations of the Abhidharmakosabhasya, and attempts to explain the changes these reveal."

## Extracted content notes (from PDF OCR; Japanese vertical text partially garbled, only reliably legible elements recorded here)
- The article works through entries of Xuanying's Yinyi on the Abhidharmakosabhasya, contrasting the old translation (舊譯, associated with Paramartha) and the new translation (新譯, Xuanzang), using Old Japanese manuscript copies of the Yinyi as witnesses.
- A comparison table pairs headwords across the two translations (large table with 大字 headwords and variant graphs; e.g. forms written with 扌/木 radicals differing between translations), demonstrating that Xuanying's glosses preserve variant readings of specific scripture copies current around 645-650 CE.
- The PDF text explicitly connects Xuanying's work to the translation office: the passage states that to translate scriptures Dao'an 道安 established the "譯場" (yichang, translation bureau), and that Xuanying 応 participated in the 譯場, which makes it easy to imagine he corrected readings against the new translation (新訳で修正したことが容易に想像できる). (OCR of this passage is partly corrupted; the sense recorded above is conservative.)
- Note on numbers visible in OCR: "450部以上の経典" (more than 450 scriptures) and "全25" (25 juan total), with commentary surviving in the first 20 juan — these figures are consistent with other sources (454-458 texts; 25 juan).

## Notes for the book chapter
- Demonstrates that Yinyi entries citing "經文作X" are textual criticism: Xuanying records which graph his exemplars used, effectively an apparatus of variants for sutras in circulation c. 650.
- Bridges China-Japan: Old Japanese manuscript copies (古写経) of the Yinyi transmit Tang-era word forms and are themselves evidence for the transmission of the gloss tradition to Japan (RQ5).
- Methodological model: glosses used as evidence for the history of two competing translations of one Indian treatise (Kośa).
