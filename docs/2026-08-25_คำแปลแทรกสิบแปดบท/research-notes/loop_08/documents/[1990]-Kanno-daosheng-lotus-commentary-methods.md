## Extracted content notes (from PDF; vertical Japanese OCR, conservative paraphrase only where OCR is clean)
Opening thesis (translated from legible OCR): Chinese Buddhists for the most part developed their understanding of Buddhism in the form of commentaries on scriptures (経典の注釈); China preserves an enormous number of sutra commentaries (経疏). Among these, Daosheng's Miaofa lianhua jing shu 『妙法蓮花経疏』 is one of the earliest works, and its various methods of commentary were, in Kanno's view, essentially carried on by later Sui-Tang sutra commentaries.

Formal features of Daosheng's commentary that the article organizes and analyzes:
1. "Raii" 来意 — a statement before each pin 章/品 of its position and significance within the whole sutra; a practice that later becomes standard in Jizang 吉蔵 and other 経疏 under the name 来意.
2. Bunka 分科 — division of the scripture into sections; Daosheng divides the whole Lotus into three sections (三因を一因となす段 etc.) and performs finer sub-division within chapters; his layered division of the parable-of-the-burning-house passages (長行・偈頌) receives special attention.
3. Kyōmon hyōshutsu 経文標出 — citing/setting out the lemma (sutra phrase) before explaining it.
4. Onshago no kaishaku 音写語の解釈 — interpretation of phonetically transcribed (transliterated) words: explicitly listed among the formal aspects of Daosheng's commentary examined in the article. This shows sound-glossing of transliterations was already embedded inside sutra commentary, not only in specialized yinyi works.
5. Isetsu teiji 異説の提示 — presenting alternative interpretations.
6. Interpretation of verses (偈頌) and the pairing terminology for parables: Daosheng already speaks of 以内義合之 ("matching with the inner meaning"), anticipating Fahyun's later technical terms 開譬/合譬/外譬/内合.

## Notes for the book chapter
- Key source for RQ2: establishes the formal repertoire of early Chinese sutra commentaries (jing shu 經疏) via its earliest substantial witness (Daosheng, d. 434).
- The inclusion of 音写語の解釈 (glosses on transliterated words) as one of Daosheng's regular procedures links the commentary genre directly to the sound-gloss tradition treated in this chapter's Yinyi sections.
