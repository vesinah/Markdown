## Key content relevant to Lanna typology (verified excerpts)

1. The crucial secondary summary of McDaniel's typology with exact gloss densities:
   "Unlike in Burma, McDaniel explains (2008, 131), in Thailand and Laos nissaya, namasadda and vohara genres overlap in style, pedagogical methods and social use. He describes how the nissaya and namasadda manuscripts are characterized by close, word-for-word glosses, whereas the vohara cite longer passages of twelve to twenty-five words from Pali source texts and offer extensive and creative vernacular translations and commentaries. Generally, nissaya fall between the two genres and often cite four to ten words before offering glosses and creative asides."

2. Three genre labels for Northern Thailand/Laos: "Three variations of literary genres: nissaya ('support'), namasadda ('glossary'), and vohara ('lifting words') are popularly used in Northern Thailand and Laos as pedagogical texts while students and teachers conduct 'languaging'."

3. Etymology/usage notes: nissaya = "guidance/reliance/support", traced to Mahavagga Vinaya rule that new ordinands live under a preceptor (upajjhaya); namasadda from Sanskrit nama-sabda ('noun-word'); von Hinüber (1997: 100–101): Thai nissayas may be considered vohara or namasap word-for-word explanations and "the difference between vohara and nissaya is not clear."

4. Thai vs Burmese scope: per interview with Gandhasarabhivamsa (6 Feb 2020), Burmese nissaya cover canonical texts + commentaries + sub-commentaries; Thai nissaya are limited to canonical texts and commentaries; Thai nissaya known as gambhi-nisai (gambhira-nissaya "deep support"), e.g., Kaccayana Gambhi-nisai, Saddasaratthajalini nisai. Nissaya is absent from current Thai Sangha examination curricula (Pathompong 2016: 30–32).

5. Seven Burmese interpretive methods (Karunika 2008: 10) useful as comparative grid: pathanusari (plain translation), pathasesa (added authoritative words), saddattha (literal), sanketattha (conventional precedent), pannattyattha (conventional terminology), voharattha (conventional usage), adhippayattha (direct meaning).

6. Regional parallels: Sri Lanka sannaya (earliest: Suttanipata-sannaya, 12th c.); Pyu Sanskrit nissaya at Sriksetra (7th c.); Pali-Burmese nissaya extant from late 13th c.; 1442 Thaungdwingyi governor inscription lists Pali/Sanskrit works with their nissayas (Walker 2020: 678).

7. Living practice: classroom photo caption — "A session of nissaya class with instructor and a repeater (zaso) ... student-monks interlineating nissaya's interpretation in their printed textbook" (Veluvan Monastery, Taunggyi) — i.e., interlinear glossing survives in printed form today.
