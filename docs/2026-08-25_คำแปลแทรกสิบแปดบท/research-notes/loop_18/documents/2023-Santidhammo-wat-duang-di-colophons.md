## Key content (from abstract, verified)

1. Corpus: palm-leaf manuscripts in Lanna tham script dated CS (จุลศักราช) 1180-1220 range cited as B.E. 2343-2358 (= CE 1800-1815), i.e., the reign of Kawila (ruler of Chiang Mai under Siamese alliance, d. B.E. 2358), surveyed at Wat Duang Di, Chiang Mai.
2. Four recurring colophon (บันทึกท้ายคัมภีร์) patterns found across the corpus:
   (1) time of incision — scribes state they carved when light was sufficient (daylight-dependent work);
   (2) place of incision — including named Pariyatti education centres such as วัดสวนดอก (Wat Suan Dok);
   (3) participants — both direct scribes and donors honoured as "bucha dhamma" supporters; mostly senior monks led projects;
   (4) aspiration/dedication — wishes that Buddhism endure, homage to teachers (ครูบาอาจารย์), plus conventional self-deprecation about the ugliness of one's handwriting.
3. Interpretation: analysis of 8 Kawila-era manuscripts links colophon patterns to post-war urban recovery in Chiang Mai; shows team-based incision projects headed by ranked monks with lay support; mass copying driven by merit-making among commoners, while rulers used sponsorship to project dhammaraja legitimacy and to honour charismatic monks with ecclesiastical titles.
