## Key content (verified from fetched page)

1. Four collections:
   - PNTMP: 3,914 manuscripts / 159,564 images (greytone from microfilm). PNTMP ran 1987-1991, implemented by CMU Center for the Promotion of Arts and Culture, initiated by Harald Hundius while building the Lan Na language and literature MA programme at CMU Thai Department (1983-1992), project leader M.R. Rujaya Abhakorn. Over 400 rolls of microfilm including the entire Wat Sung Men (Phrae) collection of over 1,700 manuscripts, largest in Northern Thailand. Microfilm copies also at National Archives of Thailand, CMUL, and Akademie der Wissenschaften, Mainz.
   - DLNTM direct digitisation: 1,051 manuscripts / 39,547 colour images, shot in situ in 39 temples (2013-2017) to cover roughly 2,000 important texts the 1980s survey selected but never microfilmed (no microfilming had been done in Chiang Rai and Lamphun provinces).
   - DELMN (Dokumentarische Erfassung literarischer Materialien in den Nordprovinzen Thailands): 991 manuscripts / 13,419 images. First comprehensive survey of Lan Na manuscripts in the eight northernmost provinces, conducted by Harald Hundius 1971-1974, funded by DFG, with Ajarn Singkha Wannasai; about 100 locations, about 25 rolls of microfilm.
   - HHHWC (Harald Hundius Handwritten Collection): 181 manuscripts / 21,222 colour images of notebook copies made by local scribes, mostly Lamphun monks selected by Singkha Wannasai, in the early 1970s during DELMN.
2. History sections on the page: oldest extant manuscripts are monolingual Pali from the late 15th c.; earliest dated manuscript = Timsati nipata section of Jataka-atthakatha-vannana, copied CS 833 / CE 1471 (Wat Lai Hin, Ko Kha, Lampang); few manuscripts survive from 17th-early 18th c. Burmese-suzerainty period; resurgence under Khruba Kancana who built Wat Sung Men into a Pali study centre and mobilised manuscript copying across the north and Luang Prabang.
3. Preservation history: Siam Society survey of Wat Lai Hin completed 1966 by Singkha Wannasai; SRI Chiang Mai University project (from 1981) catalogued and microfilmed over 4,000 manuscripts, mostly secular genres (law, customs, astrology, history, medicine); early 20th-century Bangkok administrative and monastic reforms "effectively supressed the use of the local Lan Na script and its related manuscript culture".
4. Related projects named: EFEO Lanna Manuscripts site (since 2005); Palm Leaf Studies Centre and Institute of Lanna Studies at Chiang Mai Rajabhat University (cataloguing/digitising Tai Khuen in Shan State and Tai Lue in Sipsongpanna); CMUL Digital Heritage Collection.
5. Technical practice: Zeutschel roll-film and book scanners; Nikon D610 in-temple photography with no manuscripts removed; TIFF archival masters plus JPEG derivatives; four redundant archives (Berlin, CMUL, Penn, National Library of Laos).
