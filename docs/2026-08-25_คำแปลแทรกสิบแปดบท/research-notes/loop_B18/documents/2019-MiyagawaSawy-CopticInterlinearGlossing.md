## Full abstract as displayed on the fetched Academia.edu page

"This paper explores the interlinear glossing in Coptic. Coptic is the last stage of the Egyptian language, and many important religious texts were written in this language, especially during the Late Antiquity period. Coptic Christians continue to use Coptic in their liturgies to this day. We commence the paper with an exploration of the interlinear glossing on Coptic texts that can be observed in medieval manuscripts. The most representative attestation is MS Copt. 14 (Vatican, Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana), which contains the translation of Pauline Epistles, Catholic Epistles, and Acts in the Bohairic dialect of the Coptic language. However, Arabic interlinear glosses were written above the Coptic words or phrases at a later date. In addition, MS Or. Quart. 159 (Berlin, Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin) contains interlinear glosses of Arabic on Bohairic Coptic translations of the Psalms. The interlinear glosses of both manuscripts contain Arabic translations above every Coptic word or phrase. Next, we discuss more contemporary examples of interlinear glossing of Coptic text. More recent publications on Coptic have frequently contained glossings that consider Leipzig Glossing Rules. However, the glossing methods employed by linguists in these papers vary greatly. The latter part of this paper will analyze the modern linguistic glossing on Coptic observed in selected papers from volumes of the journal Lingua Aegyptia and the volume of Grossman, Eitan, Martin Haspelmath, and Tonio Sebastian Richter (eds.) (2015) Egyptian-Coptic Linguistics in Typological Perspective, Empirical Approaches to Language Typology 55, Berlin: De Gruyter Mouton. The multilayered Coptic corpus of the Coptic SCRIPTORIUM project contains glossings of parts of speech, lemmata, and syntactic information displayed on an online multilayered corpus platform called ANNIS; however, it does not contain the glossings for the meaning of morphemes. One approach by which this is possible is through the addition of a further layer of standardized glossing to the corpus. To achieve this, it will be essential to standardize the Coptic glossing. Thus, this paper explores interlinear glossings of Coptic from the medieval to modern times with the objective of developing a standard that can be applied to govern future glossing for Coptic."
