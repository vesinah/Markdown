## 2. Content relevant to supralinear glosses (verified via open-access secondary source)

From Jørgensen 2014 PhD thesis (University of Copenhagen), full text at https://curis.ku.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/462038803/Ph.d._2014_J%C3%B8rgensen.pdf :

1. On the Tebtunis priestly onomasticon: "the onomasticon also functions as a dictionary with a section devoted to nouns and verbs. The script is hieroglyphic but with supralinear comments made in Demotic and old Coptic."
2. On the phrase wHa itnw used for this onomasticon (thesis p. 188-189 + note 761): the phrase is applied to the onomasticon from the temple library of Tebtunis, "but here it is uncertain whether it refers to the general text that orders and classifies a wide range of phenomena, or to the running supralinear additions in demotic and proto Coptic that provided the reader with both translation and a pronunciation aid."
3. The underlying citation given in note 761: Osing, The Carlsberg Papyri 2: Hieratische Papyri Aus Tebtunis I (1998), 68.
4. Same thesis passage also cites von Lieven's commentary typology: von Lieven, Grundriss des Laufes der Sterne (2007), 258-267 and 263-264.

## 3. Notes for the chapter

- Safe formulation: Osing 1998 publishes five Roman-period hieratic papyri (four certainly from the Tebtunis temple library, fifth probably Fayum/Tebtunis), among them the priestly onomasticon whose hieroglyphic main text carries running suplinear additions in Demotic and proto-Coptic serving both translation and pronunciation guidance (Osing 1998: 68, as cited by Jørgensen 2014).
- Four of the five papyri are stated by the publisher description to have belonged to the Tebtunis temple library.
