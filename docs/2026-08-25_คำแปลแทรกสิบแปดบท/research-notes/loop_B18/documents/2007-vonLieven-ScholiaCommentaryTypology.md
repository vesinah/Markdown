## Verified statement 1: von Lieven's commentary typology (Grundriss des Laufes der Sterne, 2007)

Verbatim from Jørgensen 2014, p. 189, note 761:

"According to von Lieven (Grundriss (2007), 263-264) commentaries should be divided into primary and secondary comments, in which the first are those comments that are already found in the original text, and secondary those added in the course of the text's transmission."

- Full reference: Alexandra von Lieven, Grundriss des Laufes der Sterne: Das sogenannte Nutbuch, The Carlsberg Papyri 8, CNI Publications 31, Copenhagen: Museum Tusculanum Press, 2007; 456 pp. (bibliographic data corroborated by the University of Chicago Press journals listing DOI 10.1086/666918 [review by Anthony Spalinger] and by a JSTOR-listed review of 2007-2008 describing it as von Lieven's re-edition of the so-called Book of Nut.)
- The same volume documents the New Kingdom Book of Nut transmission into hieratic/Demotic temple copies with commentary material (the Carlsberg papyrus tradition), per the above reviews' descriptions.

## Verified statement 2: BD ch. 17 as the commented funerary spell with variant markers

From the same thesis (chapter on commentaries): Book of the Dead spell 17 is found both with and without commentary; trend toward larger, more fixed commentary culminating in certain BD versions; interpretations revolve around the Heliopolitan Ennead; Assmann characterized these glosses as operating "on exactly the same level as the 'main text'" producing possible references rather than explanations; the phrases ky-Dd / ky-sp ("another saying", "variant") served to introduce semantically interesting variants found when collating papyri for copying. For the commentary literature see Rössler-Köhler, "Text oder Kommentar", in Assmann & Gladigow (eds.), Text und Kommentar (1995), 111-139.

## Verified statement 3: Amduat apparatus (from loop_26 sample, re-checked)

Hornung & Abt, The Egyptian Amduat: The Book of the Hidden Chamber (2007; German Leseprobe archived) documents: two recensional formats (long illustrated version vs short version without illustrations functioning as an abstract/catalogue); Thutmose III's KV34 catalogue of 741 deities; red rubrics for introductions and instructions; continuous deity numbering going back to Hornung's German edition with translation and commentary of 1963 (Das Amduat, Aegyptologische Abhandlungen 7 and 13); complete edition of all New Kingdom versions = Hornung, Texte zum Amduat (Geneva 1987-1994).
Caveat kept honest: this accessible material documents rubrication, catalogue abstracts, and image-caption units inside the Amduat, but does NOT define formal "Scholien" as a phenomenon; no open-access source stating "the Amduat has formal scholia" could be verified this loop.

The title consulted by Phase A as "Hornung 1967" corresponds to the Amduat edition trilogy completed 1963-1967 (Das Amduat I-III, AEgAbh 7/13); the synthesis volume is Hornung, Die Unterweltsbuecher der Aegypter (Artemis 1972; English: The Ancient Egyptian Books of the Afterlife, Cornell University Press 1999).
