## Supplementary verified context (Brian T. German, Toronto PhD thesis 2014, open access)

From German_Brian_T_201411_PhD_thesis.pdf (UTScholaris bitstream d846b618-1d00-4992-a0c7-5d447588cff6):

1. On glossing practice in Luther's world: "The act of glossing a printed text with the exegesis of the past was stock-in-trade in Luther's day, the massive Glossa ordinaria and Glossa interlinearis serving as prime examples of the time. Originality ... was not the objective: 'the aim was rather an industrious reproduction of the accepted teachers of the Church.'"
2. On measurable use of the two glosses in Luther's first Psalms lectures (Dictata super Psalterium): German reports (n.28) that J. S. Preus checked five psalm texts (89:40, 119:81, 119:123, 122:1, 123:1) in nine medieval commentaries — Augustine, the Glossa Interlinearis and Ordinaria, Lombard, Hugo Cardinalis, Lyra, Burgensis, Doering, Faber, Perez — for the phrase "faithful synagogue"; no occurrence found; the earliest occurrence of the phrase in the Dictata is at WA IV 78, 34, a gloss for Ps 92:2. This demonstrates modern scholarship treats both layers of the Gloss as checkable sources behind Luther's lecture glosses.
3. Froehlich's earlier hermeneutics study also cited by German with content: K. Froehlich, "'Always to Keep the Literal Sense in Holy Scripture Means to Kill One's Soul': The State of Biblical Hermeneutics at the Beginning of the Fifteenth Century," in Literary Uses of Typology (Princeton 1977), 20-48: traces heightened fifteenth-century interest in the literal sense back to "Aquinas' restatement of [its] fundamental role."
