## Per-corpus coverage (from editorial 2026)

1. Biblia communis: medieval Vulgate text (BLAMAT), established from the Rusch Glossa ordinaria collated with the critical Hieronymian Vulgate; ongoing extension with exemplars, correctories and received-text witnesses between 1160 and the Sixto-Clementine Bible (1595); over 350 prologues and major paratexts.
2. Glossa ordinaria: Martin Morard ed., Biblia latina cum glossa ordinaria aucta — amended scientific edition from the editio princeps of Adolf Rusch pro Antonio Koberger, [Strasbourg 1480/1481], 2415 pp., ISTC ib00607000. (2019 editorial adds precise imprint date: "[Strasbourg 23.IX.1481], 4 vol."; transcription based on the Erfurt copy checked against the facsimile and punctually the Graz copy; biblical citation identification then at 54 percent; each column break flagged with facsimile pagination and link to the Erfurt exemplar.)
3. Glossa media in Psalmos et epistolas Pauli of Gilbert de la Porrée (c. 1120-1140), ed. Martin Morard, 2024: Psalms (excerpts) and full new edition of the Pauline glosses based on Karlfried Froehlich's transcription of ms Zwettl, Stiftsbibliothek 58.
4. Glossa magna in Psalmos et epistolas Pauli of Peter Lombard (c. 1160): PL text collated and completed from Herbert of Bosham manuscripts (c. 1172); in progress. Sub-item 4.1: Thomas Aquinas, Postilla super partem Psalterii (Ps. 1-54): prolegomena to a critical edition plus preparatory-survey edition (PDF, January 2025).
5. Summa super Psalterium of Philip the Chancellor (c. 1230): from the princeps Paris 1523/1533?
6. Postillae super totam Bibliam of Hugh of St-Cher (c. 1230-1240): from the Venice 1703 edition, restructured to biblical order. Includes the related Petrus Remensis "Glossa dominicana" file (siglum HSC2) and his 'Stabat super XII boves' commentary on the Twelve Prophets (2025).
7. Catena aurea super quattuor Evangelia of Thomas Aquinas (1262-1272): first critical edition in progress; 12,840 sentences; progress rate stated as 65 percent.
8. Lyra electronica: Nicholas of Lyra, Postillae on the whole Bible (1322-1339), based on the Paris 1590-1603 Feuardent/Descousu/du Puys edition. Forthcoming 2026: Tractatus de differentia nostrae translationis ab hebrea veritate; Guillaume le Breton's Commentary on the prologues of the Bible.
9. Base GLOSSEM: repertoire of Latin manuscripts of the glossed Bible — "Recense a ce jour plus de 14100 livres bibliques glosés, transmis par quelque 7500 manuscrits."

## Methodological statements usable for the book (page id=190, verbatim translations)

- "The number of preserved manuscripts informs less about the success of texts than about that of the preservation of their codicological vehicles."
- GLOSSEM "is not intended for the collection of quantitatively material-codicological data, nor an analysis tool of ruling schemes, but an instrument seeking to grasp the relations of texts among themselves and with their social context."
- Stegmüller's Repertorium biblicum (finished 1980) had renounced recording witnesses of the ordinary Gloss; SACRA PAGINA/GLOSSEM intend to fill this gap.

## Print-history facts relevant to gaps 25.3/25.5 (2019 editorial)

- The Rusch incunable is called "le dernier état de l'évolution médiévale de la Glose ordinaire" (the final state of the medieval evolution of the Gloss).
- "L'usage et l'autorité de la Glose s'étant prolongés jusqu'au 18e siècle, ses éditions imprimées ont eu autant, sinon plus, d'influence que leurs ancêtres manuscrits." (Use and authority extended into the eighteenth century.)
- PL 113-114 (Migne) print only often-incomplete marginal sentences under Strabo's name, omitting the interlinear gloss and the biblical text.
- No mention of Melanchthon anywhere in this long editorial (checked by keyword search).
