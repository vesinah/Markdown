## Key content notes

### The manuscript

- Single sheet of a Tangut Buddhist manuscript, BL pressmark Or.12380/3495 (originally K.K.II.0280.s), collected by Aurel Stein at Khara-Khoto during his 1913-1916 expedition; digitized at IDP.
- Tangut text: five columns of neat characters inside a double-lined frame, "the appearance of being a faithful manuscript copy of a printed text".
- TIBETAN PHONETIC GLOSSES: written in untidy cursive headless Tibetan script, IN LIGHTER COLOURED INK, "do not appear to be contemporary with the copying of the Tangut text". Glosses cover only the inner three columns - 27 characters total; each gloss aligned to one Tangut character position (transcribed by position codes such as 02:01 gzhi གཞི for Tangut L1262, reconstructed źjị).
- Additional Tibetan line top-left (... ?se 'dzwar 'jo ste) and a wish-fulfilling-jewel drawing top-right, same late ink.
- Prior studies cited: Arakawa Shintaro 2008 ("Daieikan shojo sa-zō tai'on shiryō Or.12380/3495 ni tsuite", Kyoto University Linguistic Research 27: 203-212, OA PDF at repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/dspace/bitstream/2433/73220/1/09_Arakawa.pdf); Tai Chung Pui 2008 PhD dissertation "A Study of Tibetan Phonological Transcription in Tanguit Buddhism Fragments" 西夏文佛经残片的藏文对音研究, CASS.
- Blog context: five Tangut-Tibetan manuscripts from Khara-Khoto are transcribed on BabelStone (Or.12380/1842, /3495, /3909, /3910, /3911) with bidirectional gloss indexes; the phenomenon was first reported by N. A. Nevsky in 1926; Tibetan glosses often imperfectly match reconstructed Tangut readings (e.g. *mja transcribed ma, *ŋwu as ŋu); Tai Chung-pui 2018 also published "Tibetan Buddhism practice of inner fire meditation as recorded in Tangut fragments with Tibetan phonetic glosses" (Journal of Chinese Writing Systems 3.2, doi:10.1177/2513850218781514).

### Significance for interlinear-gloss typology

This is Khara-Khoto manuscript evidence of a SECOND script used to gloss pronunciation character-by-character on a Buddhist text - functionally parallel to interlinear glossing, though the exact physical placement (between columns vs above characters) is not spelled out in the source text captured; the alignment is per-character and the annotation layer was added after the main copying, by a different hand and ink.

## Relevance to project

Documents phonetic-glossing practice on Tangut manuscripts using Tibetan script - the direction "foreign phonetic script annotating local logographs" that Loop B04 sought for the Tangut side; complements the Pearl-in-the-Palm four-part entries and the trilingual parallel editions. A specifically CHINESE-Tangut interlinear blockprint arrangement remains unverified "(ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน)".
