## Key content notes

### The five texts partly written in Brahmi script

- Context list of Old Uyghur Abhidharma texts includes (item 10): "Sanskrit and Old Uyghur bilingual text of the Abhidharmakosabhasya in Brahmi script".
- "Apart from the last text (10), most of them were probably copied during the Mongolian period, which we can infer from their use of Uyghur cursive script as well as the fact that some of them contain Chinese and Tibetan characters."
- The five texts are preserved in the Berlin Turfan collection with Ch/U signatures, e.g. Ch/U 6698 [T II S 26/56], Ch/U 8083 (MIK 031764) [T II S 26.57] (+ joins Ch/U 7519, Ch/U 6829, Ch/U 6172), Ch/U 8159 (MIK 030514) [T III 62], Ch/U 8151 (MIK 030490) [T II 755], Ch/U 8177 (MIK 031770) [T I D 1004a, b].

### Function of Brahmi inside Old Uyghur texts

- "Brāhmī script has been used for a few single words in this text... The Brāhmī script is typically used for Sanskrit proper names, but not all of them have been written in this script."
- Some common nouns appear in Brahmi with their Sanskrit forms provided throughout; the choice "was surely left to the Uyghur translator/author to decide", reflecting his level of Sanskrit knowledge; the Chinese reference text cannot explain the arbitrary use.
- In one text Tibetan appears only for numbers while Uyghur and Brahmi carry the running text ("because they are almost identical in Tibetan and Brahmi, they could be interpreted as either or both").
- One attestation shows imperfect Sanskrit: antarakalpa written in Brahmi as "antalakalaba" - evidence of limited scribal competence rather than mechanical copying.
- Historical frame: "Because Brāhmī script itself was probably borrowed by Uyghurs under Tocharian influence, it is assumed that those texts also show a close relationship to the Tocharian texts." (citing von Gabain 1964 on the scribes of the Old Turkic Brahmi texts; Sander 1986; Maue on Brahmi varieties along the Northern Silk Road).

### Mixed-script conclusion (directly relevant to the book chapter)

- "The Uyghur Abhidharma texts not only partially contain Chinese or Tibetan characters but also bilingual Sanskrit and Old Uyghur scripts, and those partly written in Brāhmī script were also composed."
- "Regarding the fact that a comparable partial use of Chinese characters, Tibetan and Brāhmī script can be observed amongst the Old Uyghur Abhidharma texts it can be assumed that those communities were in contact with each other, although they would appear to have kept their own cultural tradition."

## Relevance to project

Direct manuscript-level evidence that Old Uigher Buddhist scholastic books mixed scripts on the same page: Uyghur-script Old Uygher prose carrying embedded Chinese characters (read as Uyghur per Shogaito), occasional Tibetan numerals, and Sanskrit items in Brahmi script. This is the Central Asian analogue of interlinear/marginal glossing practice and closes Phase A loop 10 remaining gap no. 1 partially: concrete Brahmi-bearing manuscripts exist (Berlin Turfan Ch/U signatures listed above), although a specifically "Chinese word transcribed in Brahmi" gloss system was still not attested verbatim in this paper.
