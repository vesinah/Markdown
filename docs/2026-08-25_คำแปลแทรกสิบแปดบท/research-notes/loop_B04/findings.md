## Key findings (with URLs)

1. GAP 1 CLOSED - Shoogaito's ondoku/kundoku publications fully verified. Original: 庄垣内正弘「文献言語と言語学―ウイグル語における漢字音の再構と漢文訓読の可能性―」, Gengo Kenkyu No. 124 (year-volume 2003), pp. 1-36, doi:10.11435/gengo1939.2003.124_1. English version (free): Gengo Kenkyu Supplement 1 ("Anthology Vol. 1", Presidents' Inaugural Lectures), pp. 155-185, 2021, doi:10.11435/gengo.Supplement.1.0_155, full PDF harvested. The LSJ abstract page found in Phase A (ls-japan.org .../abstract/127-2/) carries the society's internal archive number, not the journal issue. His monographs are also pinned down: Studies in the Uighur Version of the Abhidharmakosabhāṣya-ṭīkā Tattvārthā I-III (Shokado 1991-1993), The Uighur Abhidharmakosabhasya Preserved at the Museum of Ethnography in Stockholm (Harrassowitz Turcologica 99, 12 Jan 2014, ISBN 978-3-447-10204-9), and The Berlin Chinese Text U 5335 (Brepols BT 34, 2015) whose third chapter explicitly contrasts two ways Uighurs read Chinese characters: the inherited Uighur pronunciation of Chinese (ondoku-type) versus the so-called "Uighur reading" (kundoku-type). New quotable detail from the 2021 PDF: "because Uighurs did not develop kunten, the guiding marks for rendering Chinese into Japanese, the use of Chinese-like word order was a convenient way of reading in Uighur at a constant rate" (p. 184 area) - i.e., Old Uighur achieved kundoku WITHOUT kunten marks, unlike Japan.

2. GAP 2 SUBSTANTIALLY CLOSED - Brahmi elements in Old Uighur manuscripts are real and catalogued. Kasai 2019 (BuddhistRoad Paper 1.1, doi:10.13154/rub.br.112.98, full text harvested) identifies five Old Uyghur Abhidharma texts partly written in Brāhmī script among Berlin Turfan Ch/U fragments (Ch/U 6698, 8083+joins, 8159, 8151, 8177 etc.), plus a Sanskrit-Old Uyghur bilingual Akosabhāṣya text entirely in Brahmi (her item 10). Brahmi typically renders Sanskrit proper names and some common nouns; the choice was the translator's own; one text uses Tibetan numerals while Uyghur and Brahmi carry the prose; most were copied in the Mongol period and "contain Chinese and Tibetan characters". Her conclusion: "a comparable partial use of Chinese characters, Tibetan and Brāhmī script can be observed amongst the Old Uyghur Abhidharma texts". Full catalogue: Kasai, Die alttürkischen Fragmente mit Brāhmī-Elementen (Berliner Turfantexte XXXVIII, Brepols 2017). Still unverified: a system where CHINESE words are transcribed in Brahmi within Uighur mss "(ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน)".

3. GAP 3 PARTIALLY CLOSED - the physical form of Chinese-Tangut bilingualism at Khara-Khoto is now sharply characterized, but TRUE interlinear alignment inside one blockprint remains unverified. Verified forms: (a) separate PARALLEL language editions - the 1149 dharani-sutra set exists as Chinese blockprints TK-164/TK-165, identical-content Tangut print Tang. 109, and Tibetan butterfly prints Kh.Tib. 63/64/67/68 (Hamanaka-Sizova 2020; the linkage revealed by Nishida in the introduction to Kychanov's 1999 catalogue); no source fetched describes Tangut glosses between/under Chinese lines there "(ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน)"; (b) reverse-transcription glossary entries (Pearl in the Palm, finding 4); (c) TIBETAN phonetic glosses added character-by-character onto Tangut Buddhist manuscripts in a later hand and lighter ink (BL Or.12380/3495 and four siblings; Nevsky reported the genre in 1926; Arakawa 2008 and Tai Chung-pui 2008/2018 studied them) - the closest Khara-Khoto analogue to interlinear glossing, but in Tibetan script; (d) Tangut-script phonetic transcriptions of Chinese proper names embedded inside translations (108 items in the Tangut Mengzi, Peng Xiang-qian 2012); (e) bibliographic existence of a combined Tangut-Chinese calendar from Khara-Khoto (Peng & Li 2009, Xixia xue 4: 52-56) whose layout could not be checked "(ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน)".

4. GAP 4 CLOSED - Pearl-in-the-Palm layout documented precisely. Compiled by Gule Maocai 骨勒茂才, finished 1190 at Renzong's court; the Kozlov copy is a woodblock print of 37 pages in butterfly binding with a bilingual preface urging mastery of both languages for administering the state. Every entry gives the word twice with REVERSE-SCRIPT phonetic transcriptions: Tangut headword transcribed with Chinese characters alongside the Chinese equivalent transcribed with Tangut characters. Pages are divided into three panels with two columns each (no dividing line between columns); contents run through nine semantic spheres (heaven ... human activities, the last filling almost half the book). Edition history: Soviet facsimile first, Luo Fucheng's Chinese edition 1924, Huang Zhenhua et al. facsimile 1989 (chinaknowledge.de). Independent cross-checks: Kwanten 1982 (doi:10.2979/12484.0) analyzes it as a Heaven/Earth/Man glossary with catalogue of Chinese glosses; Peng's Mengzi study confirms its transcriptions match 12th-c. northwestern Chinese phonology. IMPORTANT for the book: the Pearl is a four-part columnar glossary, NOT interlinear glossing between lines.

5. GAP 5 CLOSED WITH THE BEST AVAILABLE PRINT EXAMPLE - Yuan 'Phags-pa paired-script pedagogy is concretely attested in blockprint culture, though strictly INTERLINEAR placement was not found. The Baijiaxing Mongguwen 百家姓蒙古文 (every surname given with its square-script spelling, characters written very large) is "the only surviving example of a Chinese literary text written in the Phags-pa script" and survives inside Yuan/early-Ming woodblock editions of the encyclopedia Shilin guangji: Taiding 1325 ed. (via Genroku-1699 reprint), Chunzhuang shuyuan 1330-1333 ed., Jicheng tang 1340 ed., Cuiyan jingshe 1418 ed.; its preface markets Mongolian writing as "a shortcut to a career as an official"; textual corruption shows later editors repaired spellings from dictionaries like the Menggu ziyun (BabelStone Baijiaxing page, citing Junast 1981; Luo Changpei & Cai Meibiao 1959; Ligeti 1956). Companion object: Menggu ziyun 蒙古字韻, revised Zhu Zongwen (Bayan) 1308 - 9,000+ ideographs arranged under 813 square-script syllables in fifteen rhymes; sole witness BL Or. 6972 (Qianlong copy); facsimiles Luo & Cai 1959 and Junast/Yang Naisi 1987. Bibliographic sources also name Mongolian Phags-pa prints of the Xiaojing, Daxue/Zhongyong and Zizhi tongjian - none survive (BabelStone Overview). The 1345 Juyongguan Cloud Platform six-language inscription remains the monumental parallel-text counterpart. A verified case of Phags-pa glosses physically BETWEEN the lines of a continuous Chinese text was not found "(ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน)".

6. Bonus for the comparative chapter: Shoogaito's negative comparison - Uighurs never developed kunten guiding marks; their solution was inserting Chinese characters INTO Uighur lines (read as Uighur) plus Chinese-like word order in the Uighur rendering. This sharpens the typological contrast Japan (kunten marks) / Korea (gugyeol) / Uighur (interlinear insertion without marks) used elsewhere in the project.
