 The extensive literature of Buddhist legends may be divided
 into three groups: (1) the legends on the rebirths of the
 Buddha anterior to his last life in this world, these are
 the * J?takas ' (proper) ; (2) legends of the Buddha in his
 last, historical, existence ; (3) legends of the Buddhist
 disciples, these are the 'Avad?nas/ which last name, how
 ever, is also applied to legends in general.
 We confine our remarks chiefly to the J?takas, which
 class occupies a specially prominent position in this litera
 ture, thanks to the manifold themes and * motifs ' touched
 upon therein. In them Buddha appears in the most various
 shapes, from god to beast; and, accordingly, the outward
 form varies between stories, moral tales, fairy tales, and
 fables.
 The J?taka, like most productions of Indian literature,
 has its fixed pattern, strictly preserved in the Buddhist
 (P?li) canon, which in general distinguishes itself from the
