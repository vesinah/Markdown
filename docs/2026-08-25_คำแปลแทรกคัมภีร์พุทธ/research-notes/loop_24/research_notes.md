## 1. Vikṛti recitation systems (krama/jāṭā/ghana/mālā) — secondary scholarship

### 1.1 Core facts verified
- Eight vikṛti-pāṭhas enumerated in the traditional verse: *jaṭā, mālā, śikhā, rekhā, dhvaja, daṇḍa, ratha, ghana* — cited from the **Vikṛtivallī** (1.5): "jaṭā mālā śikhā rekhā dhvajo daṇḍo ratho ghanaḥ / aṣṭau vikṛtayaḥ proktā kramapūrvā manīṣibhiḥ". Saṃhitā/pada/krama = prakṛti ("natural") pāṭhas; the eight vikṛtis are permutation recitations designed for error-proof transmission (e.g., ghana pattern: ab ba abc cba abc...).
- Source: Besra, Sala. "Memory Techniques in the Vedic Oral Tradition and Their Application in Education." *IJFMR* 7(6), Nov–Dec 2025.
  URL: https://www.ijfmr.com/papers/2025/6/59645.pdf

## 3. Chāyā tradition (Prakrit → Sanskrit shadow gloss)

### 3.1 Definition & lexicographic verification (non-Wikipedia)
- Cologne Digital Sanskrit Dictionaries (Cappeller): *chāyā* = "translation ([especially] from Prākrit into Sanskrit)" — the gloss-sense is lexicographically attested.
  URL: https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/chaya (aggregating Cappeller/Benfey/MW scans at sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de)
- Hindi *chāyānuvāda* = "shadow translation, a translation carrying only an overall impression of the original" (DSAL Caturvedi dictionary): https://dsal.uchicago.edu/dictionaries/caturvedi/
- Function: word-for-word Sanskrit rendering inserted after each Prakrit word/line in dramas (kāvyas of Kālidāsa etc.), Jain Prakrit texts, and commentaries; resolves Prakrit homonyms; scribe-dependent marginal practice from ~10th c. onward.

## 4. Theory primary texts

### 4.1 Jerome, Epistle 57 (Ad Pammachium, De optimo genere interpretandi)
- Date: A.D. 395. Canonical formula (§5, Latin text verified verbatim):
  **"non verbum e verbo, sed sensum exprimere de sensu"** — with the crucial exception clause "absque Scripturis sanctis, ubi et verborum ordo mysterium est" (except Holy Scripture, where even word-order is a mystery).
  NOTE: brief's phrasing "verbum e sensu / sensus e verbo" is inverted/garbled; correct canonical phrase recorded above.
- Online Latin + English (Fremantle, NPNF 2/6, 1893; Latin from Migne PL 22):
  http://www.bible-researcher.com/jerome.pammachius.html
- English translation (New Advent Fathers): https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3001057.htm
- Bilingual teaching PDF (Ca' Foscari HiTRADE): https://open.unive.it/hitrade/books/Kelly.pdf

### 4.2 Luther, *Sendbrief vom Dolmetschen* (1530)
- Facts verified: written September 1530 at the **Veste Coburg** ("die Wilderness"; during Diet of Augsburg year); printed Nürnberg 1530. ## 5. Gershevitch alloglottography — original usage verified
- Term coined by **Ilya Gershevitch (1979)**, *"The Alloglottography of Old Persian"* (study of how Darius' Bīsūtūn inscription was written). Semantic Scholar entry: https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/THE-ALLOGLOTTOGRAPHY-OF-OLD-PERSIAN-Gershevitch/dc617c806782e28cf502797b37aa6ce783e0a433
- Definition as coined: representing an utterance of one language through the writing system/language of another such that the original can be recovered (Brill chapter statement "'Alloglottography', a term coined by Gershevitch (1979)"): https://brill.com/display/book/9789004548459/BP000011.xml
- Authoritative secondary restatement: Rubio, "Alloglottography and Scribal Antiquarianism in the Ancient Near East" (abstract PDF, ISAC/OI): "The term alloglotography was coined by Ilya Gershevitch in his study of how the inscription of Darius at Bīsūtūn was written."
  URL: https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/Rubio_abstract.pdf
- Extension studies (chaining): Häberl, Charles G. (2019). "Writing in a Sacred Tongue: Inter-Aramaic Alloglottography." *WORD* 65(3): 164–178. doi:10.1080/00437956.2019.1611705 — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00437956.2019.1611705
- Iranica Online on heterograms/huzwāreš (Middle Iranian Aramaeograms = downstream alloglottography): https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ideographic-writing-i-terminology-and-conventions/
