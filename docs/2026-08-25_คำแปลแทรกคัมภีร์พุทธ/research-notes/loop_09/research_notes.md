## 2. รายการแหล่งที่พบ (raw data)

### 2.1 Interlinear Chinese in Old Uyghur texts (KEY evidence)

**[09-S01] Mirkamal, Aydar (2020). "Studies on a Fragment of the Old Uighur Abhidharmakośabhāṣya Preserved at the National Library of China." Acta Orientalia Academiae Scientiarum Hungaricae 73(4): 551–565.**
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1556/062.2020.00028
- สาระ (จาก full-text excerpt):
  - Leaf No. 15-58, NLC: pothi-type, red marginal line, red binding threads, "sūtra type script"; pagination säkiz-inč ülüš tört '8th volume, leaf 4'
  - Connects with Stockholm MS (Shōgaito published)
  - **"A remarkable feature of our text... is that a large number of Chinese characters are interlined"** — most selected from the original Chinese source (e.g., 應知兼有頂, 前七識住)
  - **Special interlinear characters added by Uighur translators**: 問 wen 'question', 答 da 'answer', 經 jing '(masters of) Sautrāntika', 婆 po '(masters of) Vaibhāṣika' — structuring labels NOT present in the Chinese original; "maybe they were temporarily added to the Chinese original texts that were going to be translated by Uighur translators"
  - Also 世友 shiyou + translated name vasumitri baxšï pair; zhu 主 = lunzhu 論主 (Vasubandhu); shi 室 = Śrīlāta (per Shōgaito 2014)
- Quote: bold script used to indicate interlinear Chinese characters in the Uighur text.

**[09-S02] Mirkamal, Aydar (2024). "Two Old Uighur Vajracchedikā Sūtra Fragments from the National Library of China." Acta Orientalia Hungaricae 77(2): 243–252.**
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1556/062.2024.00403 | record: https://real.mtak.hu/209586/
- สาระ: >10 block-printed + hand-copied Vajracchedikā versions in Old Uighur (Berlin, Paris, St. Petersburg); two new NLC blockprint fragments same origin as Berlin's; **"translated from a Sanskrit version while referencing the Chinese translation by Dharmagupta."**

**[09-S03] Tuersun, Ayixiemuguli (2025). "On Old Uyghur Fragments of the Lotus Sutra in the Berlin Turfan Collection." Religions 16(7):899. MDPI.** [CORRECTED by Unbiased Agent 2026-08-25: Crossref DOI 10.3390/rel16070899 ยืนยันผู้แต่ง = Ayixiemuguli Tuersun, School of Minority Languages and Literatures, Minzu University of China; published online 2025-07-13; abstract ยืนยัน two translation lineages + Kumārajīva collation ตามโน้ต]
- URL: https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/16/7/899
- สาระ:
  - Complete corpus typology of Old Uyghur Lotus Sūtra frags.; collation vs Kumārajīva's Chinese
  - **Two translation lineages**: Version I pre-10th c., Sogdian mediation/hybrid sources, free strategies (omissions, paraphrase); Version II 11th–14th c., near-verbatim syntactic fidelity to Kumārajīva, standardized terminology, Yuan-era copying
  - Sogdian-script frags.: MIK III 195, MIK III 199; Radloff 1911 edition = Version I witness
  - Hypothesis history: Maue & Röhrborn 1980, Elverskog 1997 (Sogdian intermediary — no Sogdian Lotus version found yet)
  - **Zieme 2025**: block-printed concertina booklet U 4780 (TM 31) + Mainz 490 (T III D) = chapter titles + representative quotations of chapters 11–24 → study/reference guide based on Kumārajīva
  - Example of Version II fidelity: Mainz 366 recto line 1 inčip ötrü ulug üč y(a)vlak yolta tüškäy ≈ 即當墮惡道 (with doctrinal addition triduḥkha concept)

**[09-S04] "Unedited Old Uighur Buddhist Literature." Acta Orientalia Hungaricae 75(4) (2022): 563–592.** [Author UNVERIFIED — cites Yakup 2015, Aalto 1954, Moriyasu 1980, Hidas 2012, Zhang 2017]
- PDF: https://real.mtak.hu/168903/1/1588-2667-article-p563.pdf (fetch blocked >5MB; content from search excerpts)
- สาระ:
  - Editions of Mahāpratisarā Mahāvidyārājñī booklets in Old Uighur incl. Fig. 78 + **Pelliot ouïgour grotte 181 (= P.O.181): 174–180, 182 Dunhuang printed booklet** (blockprint, northern grottoes; Moriyasu identified as dhāraṇī text)
  - Method: transcription per folio side + **reconstruction of the Sanskrit Vorlage** with parallels to Sanskrit (Hidas critical ed. 2012), Tibetan (Derge Kanjur No. 561, Pha.117b–138b), Chinese (T.1153) — sections marked TRANSLATION vs Transcription
  - **Tangut translation noted as direct translation of the Tibetan**, same method of treating dhāraṇī; edition: Zhang 2017: 1–195
  - Old Uighur keeps only 'pure' dhāraṇī portions (omits passages that Tibetan translates and Chinese transcribes)

**[09-S05] "Two Fragments of the Old Uyghur Saṃyuktāgama from the Berlin Turfan Collection." Religions 16(4):473 (2025). MDPI.**
- URL: https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/16/4/473
- สาระ:
  - U 5464 + U 5501: Old Uyghur Saṃyuktāgama written on verso + **interlinear between lines** of a discarded Manichaean manuscript (recto Manichaean kičig bačag kün recitation, Zieme 1975)
  - **Interlinear Buddhist text on recto oriented opposite direction to Manichaean text**
  - Verso = sūtras 588–594 (T 99 fasc.22); recto interlinear = sūtra 604 Aśoka-Avadāna (fasc.23)
  - Comparison w/ Manuscript N (Tokyo Museum of Calligraphy, 373 lines, sūtras 559–603, studied by Shōgaito 1982/2003) shows different translation choices (e.g., 因緣 = tıltag basutčı vs avant tıltag; 息 = öčür- vs amırtgur-) → multiple Old Uyghur versions
  - **"dots as omission markers... identified here for the first time in Old Uyghur Āgama manuscripts"**
  - Selective excerption of Chinese source + incorporation of Chinese character quotations as shared characteristics

### 2.3 Translation technique studies (Old Uyghur)

- สาระ: Maitrisimit nom bitig = rather faithful translation of Tocharian A Maitreyasamitināṭaka with significant discrepancies; most important Indo-European→Turkic translation before turn of first millennium CE; related: Wilkens 2008 in Zieme ed. *Aspects of Research into Central Asian Buddhism* (Brepols, pp.407–433).

**[09-S10] Ye, E. & Jumabay, U. "Conditional clauses in Old Uyghur translations of Chinese Buddhist texts." Orientalia Suecana (Uppsala).**
- PDF: https://journals.uu.se/orientaliasuecana/article/download/605/863 (fetch returned raw binary — content from search excerpt)
- สาระ:
  - Corpus: (i) Mait-H Hami Maitrisimit (from Tocharian, Geng 2008); (ii) Säkiz Yükmäk (Oda 2015, earliest from Chinese, four revisions); (iii) Suv = Suvarṇaprabhāsa (Kaya 1994, 2021); (iv) Guruyoga Tantric text **translated from Tibetan (Kara & Zieme 1977)**
