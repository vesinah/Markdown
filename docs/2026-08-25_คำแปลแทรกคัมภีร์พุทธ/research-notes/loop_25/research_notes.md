## 1. Thai-language scholarship: nissaya / Lanna Pali glossing

### 1.1 Primary evidence of a Lanna nissaya text (Thai)
- **อภิธานัปปทีปิกานิสสยะ** (Abhidhānappadīpikā-nissaya; "นิไสยอภิธานศัพท์") — a nissaya-style rendering of the Pali grammar-dictionary Abhidhānappadīpikā, preserved in **อักษรธรรมล้านช้าง** (Tham script). Documented with manuscript images by the Lanna literature preservation page "ธัมม์ล้านนา" (2019).
  URL: https://www.facebook.com/Lannaliterature/posts/589182744913042/
- Significance: concrete attestation that *nissaya* (word-by-word Pali→vernacular glossing) operated in Lanna manuscript culture on non-canonical scholastic texts too.

### 1.2 International scholarship covering Thai/Lanna nissaya (open access)

  - Chula OA full text: https://digital.car.chula.ac.th/manusya/vol26/iss1/22/ (PDF: https://digital.car.chula.ac.th/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1543&context=manusya)
  - Brill mirror: https://brill.com/view/journals/mnya/26/1/article-p1_004.xml
  - Key content for project: defines nissaya = word-to-word translation of canonical Pali or Aṭṭhakathā; cites Tin Lwin (1961), Pruitt (1992), McDaniel (2008), Clark (2015), Braun (2013); quotes McDaniel: in Thailand & Laos the genres **nissaya / nāmasadda / vohāra** overlap; nissaya typically cites 4–10 Pali words before vernacular glosses; vohāra cites 12–25 words with expansive creative vernacular commentary; used by monastic teachers 16th–19th c.
- McDaniel, Justin.   URL (library record + abstract): https://buddhism.lib.ntu.edu.tw/en/search/search_detail.jsp?seq=127314
- JIABS article (Heidelberg OA) confirming premodern Dhammapada teaching via "the nissaya method which is a vernacular gloss and explanation of Pali words and phrases":

## 3. Lydia H. Liu, *Translingual Practice* (1995) — core argument from publisher/reviews
- Imprint verified: Liu, Lydia H. ***Translingual Practice: Literature, National Culture, and Translated Modernity—China, 1900–1937***. Stanford University Press, January 1995. xx+474 pp. ISBN 0804725349 / 9780804725347.
  - SUP page: https://www.sup.org/books/asian-studies/translingual-practice
- Core argument (publisher synopsis, Google Books/Open Library — non-Wikipedia):
  - "Translingual practice" = "the process by which new words, meanings, discourses, and modes of representation arose, circulated, and acquired legitimacy in early modern China as it contacted/collided with European/Japanese languages and literatures."
  - Framing problem: Are languages incommensurate? How are **hypothetical equivalences** between words and meanings established and maintained?
  - Three questions: how "modernity"/"the West" became legitimized in May Fourth discourse; what happened to native agency; how national culture imagined its own unfolding.
  - Opening line quoted in ProQuest review: "Strictly speaking, comparative scholarship can do nothing but translate" (p. 1). https://www.proquest.com/scholarly-journals/translingual-practice-literature-national-culture/docview/216880470/se-2
- Reviews (non-Wikipedia): S.F. Daruvala, *BSOAS* 61(1) 1998: 182–183, doi:10.1017/S0041977X00016347; Ban Wang, *Comparative Literature* 49(3) 1997: 283–285 (JSTOR https://www.jstor.org/stable/1771286)

## 4. Sheldon Pollock — cosmopolitan/vernacular orders — key statements
- Main work verified via publisher page: Pollock, Sheldon. ***The Language of the Gods in the World of Men: Sanskrit, Culture, and Power in Premodern India***. Berkeley: University of California Press, 2006 (pb 2009). 703 pp. ISBN 9780520245006.
  - UC Press page (full TOC + reviews + awards): https://www.ucpress.edu/books/the-language-of-the-gods-in-the-world-of-men/paper
  - Structure: Part 1 **The Sanskrit Cosmopolis**; Part 2 **The Vernacular Millennium**; Part 3 Theory/Practice of Culture-Power; Epilogue "**From Cosmopolitan-or-Vernacular to Cosmopolitan-and-Vernacular**". Awards: Coomaraswamy Book Prize 2008 (AAS), Lionel Trilling Award 2007.
  - Publisher thesis statement: two great moments — (1) beginning of CE: Sanskrit reinvented from sacred language into code for literary-political expression, spreading Afghanistan→Java ("Sanskrit cosmopolis"); (2) second millennium: vernacularization producing regional politico-cultural orders defined relative to Sanskritic heritage.
- Preceding programmatic article: Pollock, Sheldon. 1998. "**The Cosmopolitan Vernacular**." *Journal of Asian Studies* 57(1): 6–37. doi:10.2307/2659022 — establishes Sanskrit cosmopolis across South/Southeast Asia + Kannada interaction. JSTOR: https://www.jstor.org/stable/2659022
- Independent review articulating the thesis (Ashley Thompson, Bryn Mawr Review of Comparative Literature 10.1, 2012 — open access): Sanskrit conquered "without armies, invasions or coercion"; replaces colonial "Indianization/importation" model with "transregional emergence"; vernaculars localize features of cosmopolitan culture whose lack had constituted their inferiority ("Grammar Envy" discussion).
  URL: https://repository.brynmawr.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1136&context=bmrcl
- Early self-statement PDF: Pollock, "'The Language of the Gods'... " (1996 paper, author archive): https://sheldonpollock.org/archive/pollock_sanskrit_1996.pdf
- Reception/comparative extension (chaining): King, Ross (ed.) 2023. *Cosmopolitan and Vernacular in the World of Wen 文: Reading Sheldon Pollock from the Sinographic Cosmopolis*. Brill, 612 pp. ISBN 9789004437692. https://parnassusbooks.net/book/9789004437692
