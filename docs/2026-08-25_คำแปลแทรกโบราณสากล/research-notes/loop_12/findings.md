## Key scholarly findings (with sources)
1. Corpus: the Tibetan Dunhuang manuscripts (mainly 8th-9th c.) are dispersed across BnF (Pelliot tibétain), British Library (IOL Tib J / Or.15000), St Petersburg, Japan, and Gansu institutions; van Schaik reconstructs the original eleven proto-canonical Prajñaparamita pothi volumes (3 Paris / 1 Lanzhou / 7 Dunhuang Museum) and documents extensive correction layers and non-sequential marginal pagination (earlytibet.com whereabouts).
2. Annotation ecology: beyond formal texts, the collections preserve scribal scrap sheets (glegs tshas), practice writing, doodles (PT 1164), and post-imperial contract-writing habits, showing a living scribal economy around the sutra-copying bureaus (Scriptorium III).
3. sa bcad: the topical-outline system is an indigenous structural device; Nishizawa shows its canonical six-topic format was innovated by gSang phu ne'u thog scholars (11th-12th c.) and functioned as doctrinal boundary-marker between schools; Fukuda studies its structure in early logical works; dictionary evidence defines sa bcad as chapter/division/outline/table-of-contents (rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sa_bcad, found via DDG).
4. Standardized glossary: the Mahavyutpatti with its companion sGra sbyor bam po gnyis pa is preserved and searchable as a paired volume in Oslo's Bibliotheca Polyglotta; it represents the imperial decree-level standardization of Sanskrit-Tibetan terminology (Toh. 4346 in Tengyur per standard catalogs); modern critical edition Ishihama & Fukuda 1989.
5. Script development: dbu can and dbu med emerged early (traditional accounts credit Thonmi Sambhota 7th c.; Dungkar also reports the Zhangzhung/Pungyig hypothesis), were regularized under Trisong Detsen (Lilug vs Denlug proportions), and the language underwent three decree-backed orthography revisions (8th c., mid-9th c. Ralpachen, late 10th c.) — the same reform climate that produced the terminology standardization.
6. Punctuation as dating tool: double tsheg usage drops after Senaleg's 812 reforms; Dotson's quantified study of IOL Tib J 740 shows environment-dependent tsheg choice (~75% single after nga/na/da/ra); mid-line and long tsheg variants have hypothesized chronological distributions (van Schaik punctuation tutorial).
7. Interlinear evidence exists but is under-studied in English: Goodman's P3861 (Sanskrit prayer in Tibetan script with interlinear Chinese notations) is a documented case; systematic surveys of interlinear/marginal annotation in Pelliot/Stein Tibetan collections were not located in this pass — gap flagged.
