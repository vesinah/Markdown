Content notes:
- Standard orthography (10th c. onward): single dot (tsheg) level with tops of main letters separates syllables. Early inscriptions often use a double dot (colon-like double tsheg) instead; the double tsheg fell from use in the early 9th century: consistent in the 8th-c. Zhöl and Samye pillars; by early 9th c. reduced to line-end marking/decoration between shad.
- In Dunhuang MSS the double tsheg appears in Old Tibetan Annals, historical texts, legal and divination texts. Brandon Dotson's study of legal document IOL Tib J 740 tabulated occurrence: single tsheg about 75% after final nga/na/da/ra but only ~5% after other endings (double tsheg preferred elsewhere) - explained by interference of descending letters; same pattern (less regular) in Zhöl pillar.
- Dating utility: sporadic at line-ends in 805/6 Temple of the Hat inscription, absent in 812 inscription at same site - suggesting discouragement in Senaleg's 812 language reforms; not found regularly after; absent from post-imperial (post-mid-9th c.) dated Dunhuang MSS; caveat: copyists may reproduce obsolete punctuation.
- Mid-line tsheg: sits mid-height rather than top; occurs in pillar inscriptions and some MSS (e.g., Pratimoksa sutra fragment IOL Tib J 13); advantage: letters written closer together; hypothesis: absent from 10th c. Dunhuang MSS (unproven).
- Long tsheg: tsheg lengthened toward shad; irregular, likely scribal quirk, possibly double tsheg without pen lift; commenters note survival in cursive (and Bhutanese mgyogs yig similarity raised by Chris Fynn).
- Sources listed: Dotson 2007 "Divination and Law in the Tibetan Empire" (in Kapstein & Dotson eds., Contributions to the Cultural History of Early Tibet, Brill, 3-77); de la Vallée Poussin 1962 catalog; Unicode proposal docs N3032/N3033 (Andrew West) encoding old-Tibetan punctuation characters (Unicode 5.1).

Relevance: punctuation systems functioned as dating evidence and textual organization devices in Tibetan manuscript culture; complements sa-bcad studies for Loop 12's "section markers" sub-topic.
