Content notes:
- Dunhuang sutra scribes were given paper bundles to write Tibetan sutras; they were allowed to keep a few sheets called lektsé (glegs tshas); losing more than allowance brought punishment — per the linked Scriptorium II post, ten lashes per incomplete bundle, property impounded, relatives imprisoned.
- Surviving glegs tshas scrap sheets show handwriting practice, letter drafts; scribes moonlighted writing letters/contracts, a practice that kept Tibetan document-writing alive after the end of Tibetan rule at Dunhuang.
- Pelliot tibetain 1164 = scribe's scrap paper covered in animal doodles, signed by scribe Jeu Taklek (Rje'u Stag legs); comment thread resolves an inscription as referring to Chinese inhabitants of Shazhou, suggesting the doodler was Chinese.
- Comparison drawn with soldier's otter sketch on a woodslip from Miran fort (IOL Tib J 404 verso).
- Comment thread points to IDP database advanced search keyword "illustrated" for more doodled manuscripts (e.g., Chinese scroll S.387).

Relevance: documents informal annotation/doodling layer of imperial-period Tibetan manuscript culture; complements formal interlinear/marginal annotation study; names the scribal economy (glegs tshas) behind Dunhuang sutra production.
