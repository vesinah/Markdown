Content notes:
- Library cave discovered at Dunhuang; Stein arrived 1907, estimated 1,050 bundles of Chinese and 80 bundles of Tibetan scrolls plus eleven large volumes of Tibetan pothi pages (~2'5" x 8", nearly 1.5 ft high).
- Stein took "over thirty compact bundles" of scrolls plus pothi packets; most scrolls carried repeated Aparimitayur-nama sutra; Pelliot (1908) took three of the eleven large pothi volumes hoping for an early bKa' 'gyur — they proved to be Prajñaparamita copies (Śatasāhasrikā dominant); also many scroll bundles.
- Post-1909 dispersal: 1909 Beijing exhibition led to partial recall of Chinese manuscripts only; 1911 Otani expedition Japanese explorers obtained hundreds of scrolls; 1914 Stein's third expedition bought 570 Chinese scrolls; Oldenburg (Aug 1914) took scrolls to St Petersburg (Institute of Oriental Studies): 212 Tibetan scrolls (202 Aparimitayur-nama, 10 Prajñaparamita-hrdaya).
- Gansu holdings (Huang Wenhuan 1978 survey): Dunhuang Museum 8,780 pothi leaves + 224 scrolls; Dunhuang Academy 42/43; Lanzhou Library 1,117/30; Jiuquan 0/19; Zhangye 0/1; Wuwei 7/0. Van Schaik's own 2000 cataloguing of Dunhuang Academy found 47 scrolls + 87 Śatasāhasrikā pothi leaves incl. marginal page numbers.
- Reconstruction: of the original eleven proto-canonical Prajñaparamita volumes, three to Paris (Pelliot tibétain collection has >3,800 large Śatasāhasrikā leaves), one to Lanzhou, seven to Dunhuang Museum. Pages carry page numbers/letters in margins, often non-sequential; many Paris pages extensively corrected; Marcelle Lalou judged them draft versions superseded by corrected copies in the same collection.
- Paris/London scroll counts: ~950 Aparimitayur-nama scrolls each; ~600 Prajñaparamita scrolls in Paris vs ~100 London; miscellaneous Buddhist and secular scrolls scattered through both catalogues (Pelliot tibétain 3500-4450 = Aparimitayur scrolls; 1495-2105 = Prajñaparamita scrolls per Lalou catalogues; BL IOL Tib J 310 and 109 groupings).
- Antiquity: manuscripts date mainly 8th-9th centuries (imperial Tibetan period), key sources for origins of Tibetan Buddhism and early development of the language.
- Bibliography includes Lalou inventories, Fujieda 1966, Takeuchi catalogs, de la Vallée Poussin 1962, Savitsky catalogs.

Relevance for interlinear-gloss project: establishes the physical corpora (Pelliot tibétain, IOL Tib J / Or.15000, Gansu collections) within which interlinear and marginal annotations occur; documents scribal correction culture (drafts, corrections, page numbering) in imperial-period Tibetan scriptoria.
