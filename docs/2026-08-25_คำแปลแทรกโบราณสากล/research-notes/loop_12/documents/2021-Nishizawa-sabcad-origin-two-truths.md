Abstract findings:
- The two-truths (satyadvaya / bden gnyis) discussion in later dGe lugs pa and Sa skya pa texts shared a standard Sa-bcad format of six topics:
  (1) base of division (dbye gzhi); (2) meaning of division (dbye ba'i don); (3) literal meaning (ming gi don); (4) number-determination (grangs nges pa); (5) definition (mtshan nyid); (6) valid cognition ascertaining a definition (mtshan nyid nges byed kyi tshad ma).
- The six-topic Sa-bcad format was created by early Tibetan scholars of gSang phu ne'u thog around the 11th-12th centuries.
- Pa tshab lo tsā ba Nyi ma grags (ca. 1070-1140) and his followers did NOT adopt this format and may not have treated the two-truths theory itself as an essential Madhyamaka topic; they placed dependent-arising (rten 'brel) at the core instead.
- Conclusion: two rival interpretations of Madhyamaka were established among early Tibetan scholars (11th-12th c.), differing precisely in their use of the sa-bcad outline format.

Relevance for interlinear-gloss project: demonstrates sa-bcad as an indigenous Tibetan system of structural/textual organization (topical outlines embedded in commentaries), historically traceable to specific scholarly communities — a section-marker practice comparable in function to European capitula/rubrication rather than interlinear glosses.
