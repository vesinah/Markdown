## Key scholarly findings (with sources)
1. Definition and function: kunten is a Japanese system of text markings used to clarify syntax and meaning of Chinese texts for Japanese readers; kunten systems arose across ancient East Asia to let non-native readers read Chinese documents directly (Tsutsumi et al. 2023, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10521107/).
2. Typology of marks: major components include wokototen/okotototen ヲコト点 (dots/lines/hooks whose shape + position encode particles, auxiliary verbs, endings), word-order/inversion marks kaeriten such as re-point レ and one-two points 一二点, katakana glosses, furigana-type readings, and tone glosses (sando 声点, Middle Chinese tone marks); the NINJAL database enumerates exactly these searchable categories (https://cid.ninjal.ac.jp/kunten-syousyo3/).
3. Quantitative typology: in 199 actual-material charts digitized by Tsutsumi et al., the dot is overwhelmingly dominant (2,199/6,411 instances); single-stroke shapes (・, |, ─, ＼) account for most marks; readings te, wo, ni occur in nearly every chart; positions cluster at four corners and center, with external marks predominantly right of characters, same tendency as furigana (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10521107/).
4. Periodization: kunten-glossed documents existed from the Heian through Edo periods, with wokototen especially common in Heian-Kamakura materials (Chinese classics, Buddhist scriptures) (Tsutsumi et al. 2023); Zisk reports evidence that kundoku practice began mid-to-late 8th century (Academia.edu abstract/QA, https://www.academia.edu/105841005/...).
5. Sociolinguistic value: kunten materials serve as historical sources for characters, pronunciation, vocabulary and usage of Japanese; NINJAL frames them as core evidence of language history (project page https://www.ninjal.ac.jp/english/research/cr-project/project/c/kunten/); Kono 2021 adds that the kanji-kana mixed writing system itself resulted from the kundoku assignment process (Waseda Pure record).
6. Digitization projects: NINJAL runs a family of resources — Structured Description of Kunten-Shiryo project (2009-2012, prototype digital interpretation aligned to images), Shousho Kunten DB (test release 2020-01-31), Wokototen charts Database (https://cid.ninjal.ac.jp/wokototendb/) — under NIHU "comprehensive bibliology" and JSPS KAKENHI 17K18506 funding statements on those pages.
7. Internationalization: English-language scholarship remains thin; referee Hmeljak Sangawa notes most kunten research is in Japanese; Whitman et al. 2010 proposed an international vocabulary ("vernacular reading", "gloss"); Zisk 2023 proposes a three-tier transcription standard adapted from the Leipzig Glossing Rules (Academia.edu pages above).
8. Cross-traditional link: Tsutsumi et al. cite Korean gugyeol studies using a comparable coordinate-grid method for point positions, conceptually identical though data-incompatible (F1000 article refs 14-18) — direct bridge to Loop 11.
