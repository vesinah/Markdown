## 3. Key findings (with URLs)
1. Nurtawab 2020 bibliographic record confirmed (IMW 48[141]:169-189, published online 2020-03-12); abstract recovered from OpenAlex: argues interlinear translations reveal pedagogical practice and differing user skill levels (A.51 high-level recitation users vs W.277 basic students), and that these Qur'ans prove continuation of Malay exegetical activity through the 18th century after the Tarjuman (late 17th c.). Note: the task-brief title "A Qur'anic Exegesis in Interlinear Translation..." does not match Taylor & Francis title; actual title as above. Sources: https://doi.org/10.1080/13639811.2020.1724469 ; https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1080/13639811.2020.1724469
2. Gallop (RAS blog, 25 Nov 2022) states verbatim that RAS Arabic 4 is "the only known single-volume Qur'an with a full interlinear translation in Malay", fully digitised Nov 2022 (RAS Digital Library) in honour of Peter Riddell; sponsor Gallop/Merdeka Award 2022. Source: https://royalasiaticsociety.org/a-quran-manuscript-with-full-malay-translation/
3. Codicology of Arabic 4 per Gallop: nine lines Arabic/page in wide hand, Malay sloping beneath in much smaller fine hand; red rules/panels; small gold roundel verse separators (very rare in SEA, recorded also in two other Banten Qur'ans); laid Asian paper without chainlines/watermarks; first folio reversed during rebinding after damage.
4. Textual family: Arabic 4's Malay translation is almost identical to PNRI A.51 and W.277 (worked example Q.18:11 quoted in blog, incl. marginal addition in A.51); corpus claim: only four other known interlinear-Malay Qur'an copies exist, all multi-volume, two from Banten; three of eleven known Javanese-interlinear Qur'ans also from Banten -> Banten hub of pedagogical interlinear tradition; likely early 18th c.
5. Nurtawab 2023 (IMW, DOI 10.1080/13639811.2023.2213588, abstract via Semantic Scholar) adds qira'at + verse-division comparison of A.51/W.277/Arabic 4 via Surat al-Kahf samples: copyists combined methods, referring to older copies and their own qira'at knowledge.
6. Tarjuman al-Mustafid: composed in Aceh second half 17th c., first full Malay Qur'an commentary (per Gallop blog citing Riddell 2017); 1901 printed scan on archive.org (identifier tarjuman-al-mustafid, creator Abd al-Rauf ibn Ali al-Fansuri al-Sinkili; description: mostly from tafsir al-Jalalayn, first complete Malay interpretation). Recent Indonesian scholarship confirms Jawi-script status and philological ms/print differences (Al-Tadabbur 2025) and defends its identity against duplication claims re Jalalayn/al-Khazin/al-Baydawi (Javano Islamicus 2025).
7. Regional spread beyond Banten: Palembang manuscript of al-Sanusi's Umm al-barahin uses interlinear translation as paratext (Iankovskaia 2025, Acta Linguistica Petropolitana, DOI 10.30842/alp230657372132643) - shows the interlinear technique applied to didactic Arabic texts in Sumatra.
