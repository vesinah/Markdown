Content notes:
- Filed April 23, 2024 by Victor Mair; reports Si Nae Park's Penn talk on kugyeol/gugyeol/kwukyel 구결 口訣 ("oral glossing").
- Mair's two points: (1) sharp difference between oralization and vernacularization; (2) kugyeol belongs to the former; beonyeok 번역 / hon'yaku 翻訳 / fanyi 翻譯 to the latter.
- In gugyeol the original Classical Chinese text is not modified; markers are inserted between phrases; parts read out of sequence to approximate Korean SOV order; compared with Japanese kanbun kundoku.
- Comment by Jongseong Park (April 26, 2024) gives a worked example from the Analects opening (學而時習之面不亦悅乎牙) showing inserted -(eu)myeon 'if' and -a interrogative elements.
- Park's terminology: eumdok gugyeol 音讀口訣 (uses Sino-Korean readings), sundok gugyeol 順讀口訣 (follows original reading order without modification); earlier seokdok gugyeol 釋讀口訣 could indicate replacing characters with native equivalents and changing reading order; word-order type also called yeokdok gugyeol 逆讀口訣; complex systems rediscovered in the 1970s; huge variation in sign forms (ink or blind-stylus/dot-stroke or full characters).
- Park: National Hangeul Museum (Seoul) displays a replica of a 13th-14th century woodblock print of Yuga saji ron 瑜伽師地論 (Yogacarabhumi-sastra) employing gugyeol signs for reading-order changes; transition to later gugyeol types around that time.
- Park: after invention of Korean alphabet, eonhae 언해 諺解 (full vernacular translation, cf. Japanese genkai 諺解) took over the translation role while gugyeol simplified into reading aids.
- Related sources named in thread: Kin Bunkyo, Literary Sinitic and East Asia: A Cultural Sphere of Vernacular Reading (Brill); W.H. Medhurst 1832 Hokkien dictionary examples of analogous Sinitic vernacular paraphrase; Mair, "Buddhism and the Rise of the Written Vernacular in East Asia" (JAS 53.3, 1994).
