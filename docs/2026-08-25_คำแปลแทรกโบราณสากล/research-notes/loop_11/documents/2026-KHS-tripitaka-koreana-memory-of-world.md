Content notes:
- The Goryeo Daejanggyeong ("Tripitaka Koreana") was carved onto 81,258 wooden printing blocks in the 13th century under commission by the Goryeo Dynasty (918-1392 CE); stored at Haeinsa Temple; also called Palman Daejanggyeong ("Eighty-thousand Tripitaka").
- Buddhist scriptures were translated from Indian/Central Asian languages into classical Chinese, described as the lingua franca of educated discourse throughout East Asia including Korea.
- It is "the only complete canon still extant on the mainland of Asia" and is known as the most accurate of the Tripitakas written in classical Chinese, serving as a standard critical edition for East Asian Buddhist scholarship.
- 5,987 miscellaneous scripture woodblocks at Haeinsa supplement the canon (scribing dates 1098-1958).
- The woodblocks embody a complete "knowledge system" producing and distributing knowledge; Haeinsa printed copies for research and education of the ordained.
- Relevance to gloss research: this is the canonical corpus within which Goryeo-period reading aids (gugyeol) circulated; per Language Log (Mair 2024 comment by Jongseong Park), a 13th-14th century woodblock print of Yuga saji ron 瑜伽師地論 with word-order gugyeol signs is displayed at the National Hangeul Museum, showing gugyeol practice contemporaneous with the second Tripitaka carving.
