Content notes:
- Korean Studies Colloquium, April 18, 2024; speaker Si Nae Park, Associate Professor, East Asian Languages and Civilizations, Harvard University.
- "Language contact between Literary Sinitic and Korean gave rise to the practice of vernacular reading, whereby Literary Sinitic texts can be read as Korean using glossing practice."
- Kugyeol glossing attributed to Sol Ch'ong 薛聰 (655-?), who is said to have "read the Nine Classics by means of the local language and instructed younger scholars"; kugyeol names both the method and the glosses.
- Park's argument: kugyeol was not only a linguistic intermediary easing access to Literary Sinitic and assimilating it into Korean, but also "a vehicle for acquiring eloquence and for adorning texts"; attention to visual and acoustic interplay in surviving Choson specimens.
- Speaker bio: author of The Korean Vernacular Story (Columbia UP, 2020); works on yadam, sosol orokhae fiction glossaries, kyongso onhae vernacularized Confucian classics; book project Writing as Utterance: The Culture of Reading, Script, and Literature in Choson Korea.

Sociolinguistic value for the project: positions gugyeol as reading culture practice (memory, recitation, textual adornment), not merely a parsing device.
