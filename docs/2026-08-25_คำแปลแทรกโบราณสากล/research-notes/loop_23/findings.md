## Key findings (with URLs)
1. Codex Bezae is FACING-COLUMN/PAGE bilingual, NOT interlinear: Parker states it is "a bilingual manuscript, with a Greek text and a Latin version on facing pages"; single scribe trained primarily in Latin copying; dated c. end of 4th-beginning of 5th c.; Parker's proposed origin Berytus (Beirut); 9th-c. replacement leaves written at Lyon; Beza gave it to Cambridge in 1581. https://cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/view/MS-NN-00002-00041/1
2. Its Latin is pre-Vulgate ("one of the small handful of manuscripts which are the most important witnesses to the development of a Latin version before Jerome's Vulgate of 382") and its Greek-Latin relationship is a free restyling/harmonisation rather than mechanical equivalence — i.e., not a construe-style bitext. Same URL.
3. The interlinear impulse in the biblical psalm tradition is instead documented later/northward: Haelewyck's manuscript census describes BL Cotton Vespasian A.i as an 8th-c. southern-England Roman Psalter carrying "a later Anglo-Saxon interlinear translation (9th century)", plus Regius, Cambridge Ff.1.23, Bosworth psalters with Anglo-Saxon translations — showing interlinear vernacular gloss layered onto Latin psalter text. https://ojs.uclouvain.be/index.php/babelao/article/download/78353/69243
4. Vetus Latina character: Haelewyck organises Old Latin Daniel into textual types X (origins), K (ancient African), C (recent African), D/I (European), A (Augustine), H/V (Jerome's revision) — confirming that early Latin Bible versions are a family of free, evolving renderings revised toward the Greek, with Jerome's ad verbum ideal coming last, not first. Same URL.
5. Verona Psalter: only thin confirmations retrievable this session — Tregelles 1860 N&Q note "Dominus regnavit a ligno: psalterium graecum veronense" proves sustained scholarly engagement with a Greek Psalter manuscript at Verona since at least 1860. Detailed codicological description NOT verified from accessible sources. Status: partial; do not cite layout claims yet. https://doi.org/10.1093/nq/s2-ix.216.127a
6. Word-for-word pedagogy precedent: the Hermeneumata Pseudodositheana (Greco-Latin school glossaries/colloquia, ed. Dickey CUP 2012/2015, incl. P.Lond. II.481 phrase-and-word glossary) provide the documented ancient apparatus of paired Greek-Latin lemmata used in language learning — the functional ancestor of interlinear study formats. https://doi.org/10.1017/cbo9781139096706
7. Lemma+gloss micro-format in antiquity is best evidenced by papyrus Scholia Minora in Homerum (P.Köln Od. 1.45-116 = Lundon 2001; Ilias 22.184-256 = Sijpesteijn 1987; alphabetische Iliasglossare = Fontanella 2023): short lemmata followed by explanatory glosses, i.e., the Greek school form available for Latin transmission; the Latin mirror appears in Priscian-linked "scholia" on principal Aeneid verses preserved in vetusti codices (Lindemann 1818). DOIs in table.
8. Transmission continuity into medieval glossing is now citable via Grifoni 2017 (Wissembourg glossing practices on Catholic Epistles) and the bilingual/glossed psalter strand (Toswell 2014; Porck 2021), bridging late-antique Greek school forms to Carolingian Latin gloss corpora.
