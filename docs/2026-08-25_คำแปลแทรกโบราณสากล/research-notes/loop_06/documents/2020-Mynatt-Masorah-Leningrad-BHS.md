- Author: Daniel Mynatt
- Published: Ancient Jew Review, September 16, 2020 (panel first presented at SBL 2019, San Diego)
- URL: https://www.ancientjewreview.com/read/2020/9/11/introduction-to-the-masorah-the-masorah-of-the-leningrad-codex-in-the-biblia-hebraica-stuttgartensia-bhs-edition

## Key content (full argumentative text preserved)

- A circule (small circle) over a word tells the reader it should be matched with a Masoretic note; example: yid'eh with circule matched to note b, "occurs two times."
- The Leningrad Codex (L) is the basis of BHS; oldest complete manuscript of the Hebrew Bible, dating from around 1008; its colophon claims copying from exemplars written by the esteemed Masorete Aaron ben Asher.
- Masorah Parva (Mp): brief notes in the side margins of columns; generally count word or phrase occurrences; written in Aramaic shorthand or abbreviations; e.g., l with circle = occurs only once (hapax); d with dot = four times; may add spelling info, position in verse, or book distribution.
- Masorah Magna (Mm): lengthier notes in upper and lower margins; give location of occurrences by citing short verse "snippets", because Masoretes lived before concordances and chapter/verse divisions.
- Innovation #1 of BHS: editor Gerard E. Weil "completed" Mp notes so each occurrence carries a note (in L only some occurrences carry them), merging multiple rubrics into one note when needed (example Gen 26:3 immekha note "12 in the Torah" occurring with only 4 of 12 references in L).
- Innovation #2: all Mm notes exported to separate volume Weil, Massorah Gedolah, Vol. 1 (Rome: Pontifical Biblical Institute, 1971); lists heavily edited with book abbreviations, chapter/verse locations added, emendations such as restoring missing snippets.
- Other BHS-only notations: "sub loco" and "contra textum".
- Works cited: Kelley, Mynatt & Crawford, The Masorah of Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia (Eerdmans 1998); Weil 1971; Wurthwein, The Text of the Old Testament (2nd ed. 1995).

## Relevance to project

Documents the mechanics of the Masoretic apparatus (circulus/circule reference system, simanim/catchwords) that constitutes the Hebrew analogue of interlinear/marginal glossing traditions.
