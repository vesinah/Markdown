# Cairo Codex of the Prophets (Codex Cairensis) - colophon, dating dispute, contents

Compiled from web sources consulted 2026-08-25 (full-page fetch of CSIC page failed with transport error; content below is from indexed excerpts of the cited pages).

## Primary descriptive source

CSIC digital edition, "Home. The Cairo Codex of the Prophets" - https://biblioteca.cchs.csic.es/Codice-Profetas-Cairo/en/Descripcion.php

Excerpt: "According to its colophon, the Cairo Codex of the Prophets dates from 894/5 and was copied, vocalized, and provided with Masora by Moshe ben Asher in Tiberias. It is written on fine gazelle-skin parchment... three columns... 23 lines... includes vowels, accents, and annotations of Masora Parva (MP) and Masora Magna (MM), some of which are occasionally arranged in geometric patterns." Justification letters at line ends include mem, shin, bet, nun, aleph, peh - which except peh form part of the scribe's name Moshe ben Asher. Contains Former and Latter Prophets plus lists (number of verses of all books, middle words, Seder ha-Mikra, rulers of Israel), eleven carpet pages in gold and colour combining ornament with figurative text resembling MP/MM annotations; second colophon for commissioner Yabez ben Solomon.

"The Cairo Codex of the Prophets occupies a central place in the history of the Hebrew biblical text. It is one of four manuscripts associated with the Ben Asher family... According to the date recorded in its colophon, this manuscript is the earliest dated partial Hebrew illuminated Bible. Although the dating of the colophon has been the subject of scholarly debate, even if assigned a later date, it would still rank among the oldest surviving biblical manuscripts."

## Colophon text (excerpt via Posen Library)

Posen Library, "Cairo Codex: Colophon" (Moses Ben Asher, 895) - https://www.posenlibrary.com/entry/cairo-codex-colophon
"I, Moses Ben Asher, wrote this holy book of the Bible, dictated by myself, in accordance with the good hand of the Lord upon me [Nehemiah 2:18], very clearly (Deuteronomy 27:8)..."

Same entry notes: while the colophon states 895, carbon dating and other techniques indicate the extant manuscript is likely an eleventh-century copy of Moses ben Asher's work; remained in the Cairo Karaite community for centuries; originally copied for a Jerusalem-based Karaite, Ya'bets ben Solomon.

## Modern critical assessment

- Open Book Publishers (Outhwaite, B.), "The Cacophony of Colophons in the Cairo Codex of the Prophets" (2021) - https://www.openbookpublishers.com/books/10.11647/obp.0464/chapters/10.11647/obp.0464.03 : "modern scholarship has largely rejected this claim, favouring a later date of production, likely in the eleventh century"; palaeographic and linguistic analysis questions authenticity of several dedication colophons possibly added later to enhance status.
- Hebrew University Bible Project note (quoted in Wikipedia article, keyword source only): scribe and naqdan cannot be identified as Moshe ben-Asher; date rather 11th century CE; cf. Beit-Arie et al., Codices Hebraicis litteris exarati (1997) 25-29; Lyons, The Cumulative Masora (1999).
- Lipschuetz observation: within the masoretic tradition Codex Cairensis seems closer to ben Naphtali than to Aaron ben Moses ben Asher.
- Beiler, V., "Does the Cairo Codex Represent a Scribal School?" (2025, Festschrift Khan) - https://www.academia.edu/128067974/ : layout and Masoretic peculiarities align it with a group of model Hebrew Bibles that is NOT that of the Aleppo Codex.

## Provenance narrative (traditional)

Given to the Karaite community in Jerusalem; looted by Crusaders 1099; redeemed; came to Karaite community of Cairo; deposited at Hebrew University of Jerusalem in 1983.
