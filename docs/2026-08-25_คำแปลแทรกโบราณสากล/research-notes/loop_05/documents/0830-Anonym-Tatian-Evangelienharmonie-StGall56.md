## Manuscript data

- Manuscript title: Evangelienharmonie des Tatian
- Date of origin: saec. IX erste Haelfte (first half of the 9th century)
- Support: Pergament (parchment)
- Extent: 342 Seiten
- Format: 2 degrees (34 x 26 cm)
- Page layout: zu 32 Zeilen, zweispaltig von Mehrern, auf starkem grauen Pergament
- Binding: original binding with spine title: Plenarium
- Main language: Althochdeutsch

## Contents

1. S. 1-2: Praefatio Victoris episcopi Capuae ("Cum fortuito in manus meas...")
2. S. 3-18: Canones X concordantiae evangeliorum (said to derive originally from Ammonius, author of a gospel harmony)
3. S. 19-24: Elenchus 181 capitum (Codex Fuldensis has 182, Cassel 184)
4. S. 25-342: "Quoniam quidem multi ... Bi thiu wanta manage ..." - Latin and Old High German text of the gospel harmony in 181 chapters, without headings; the Latin in the left column, the German opposite.

## Filiation and edition history (as stated by Scherrer/e-codices)

- Only manuscript of this Old German translation, besides an Oxford copy made by Junius of the lost codex of B. Vulcanius, lacking chapters 76-153. First printed from the Oxford copy by Palthenius (Greifswald 1706), then by Schilter (Thesaurus II, p. 59); then from the St Gall manuscript by J. A. Schmeller (Matthew only, Stuttgart 1827; whole, Vienna 1841); and by Eduard Sievers with glossary (Bibliothek der aelteren deutschen Literatur Denkm. V, Paderborn 1872). Sievers distinguishes seven scribes of the codex and supposes several successive editors of the German translation, whose linguistic form is High Frankish (the Fulda dialect).
- The Latin text is attributed by Victor of Capua (6th century), in his preface to the oldest manuscript, to Tatian (2nd century), known as author of a lost Greek Diatessaron; according to more recent views it derives from Ammonius (3rd century), probably a mere compilation from the Vulgate.
- Origin note: inside front cover words (13th c.): "Hunc librum cum diligentia quaesitum diu et vix inventum resumite sancte pater Erneste in nom. dom." (Pater Ernestus appears as St Gall dean as late as 1271).
- Cod. 56 is not mentioned in any old St Gall catalogue, neither that of the 9th century nor that of 1461.
