# Vocabularius (Keronis) et Alia - the "Abrogans" manuscript (St. Gallen, Stiftsbibliothek, Cod. Sang. 911)

- Source fetched: e-codices description page https://www.e-codices.ch/en/description/csg/0911/
- Basis: Scherrer Gustav, Verzeichniss der Handschriften der Stiftsbibliothek von St. Gallen, Halle 1875, S. 329-330
- License: Public Domain Mark

## Manuscript data

- Date of origin: s. VIII (8th century)
- Support: parchment
- Extent: 323 pages
- Format: 8 degrees (17 x 10.5 cm)
- Page layout: 14-21 lines per page
- Writing and hands: by several hands
- Main language: Althochdeutsch

## Contents (per Scherrer description)

- p. 4-289: "Incipiunt closas ex uetere testamento. - Abrogans dheo modi ... alienatus irfremidid. finit closas." The so-called Keronisches Woerterbuch (Kero's dictionary), in which however at least three scribes can be recognized. Script description: pre-Carolingian minuscule, letters upright with low body; 18 large black-red hollow initials at section starts (A-Z). "Die deutschen Woerter sind nicht zwischen die Zeilen sondern neben die lateinischen gesetzt" - the German words are placed NOT between the lines but beside the Latin ones, with very imperfect word separation.
- p. 290-291: blank except two runic words.
- p. 292-319: Gennadius of Marseille, De ecclesiasticis dogmatibus (new quaternio of lesser parchment).
- p. 320-322: Altdeutsches Vaterunser (Lord's Prayer without commentary/exposition) and Credo according to the apostolic formula, likewise without explanation. Earliest German translation of the Lord's Prayer and Credo. Per Goldast these formulas were also attributed to Kero; however the hand is different ("die Hand ist jedoch verschieden").
- Note: the oldest library catalogue (No. 728, p. 17) lists no dictionary separately but only "libri glosarum Vol. 8", i.e., eight volumes of glossaries.

## Corroborating sources (from search results)

- Handschriftencensus entry (https://www.handschriftencensus.de/3692): Codex in 2 parts; p. 4-289 "Abrogans deutsch"; p. 319-322 "St. Galler Paternoster und Credo"; the last two quires (p. 291-323) with Gennadius and the OHG Paternoster/Credo were added within a short time after the Latin-OHG dictionary. Facsimile edition: Das aelteste deutsche Buch. Die 'Abrogans'-Handschrift der Stiftsbibliothek St. Gallen, ed. Bernhard Bischoff, Johannes Duft, Stefan Sonderegger (St. Gallen 1977).
- BStK Online Nr. 253 (https://glossen.germ-ling.uni-bamberg.de/manuscripts/12329): Autopsy-based record; script = pre-Carolingian minuscule by several insular-influenced hands from the end of the 8th century; contents listing identical to above; identified as StSG. 220.
- e-codices St Gall OHG subproject (http://e-codices.unifr.ch/en/list/subproject/stgall_oldhigh_german): "The oldest book in the German language, the so-called 'Abrogans' manuscript from around 790, containing the earliest German translation of the Lord's Prayer and Credo."
- historyofinformation.com (https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=2857): dates the surviving codex 775-790 CE; describes it as a dictionary of synonyms/glossary from Latin into Old High German, named after its first word abrogans ("humble"); preserved in Abbey Library of St Gall (Codex 911).
- Koebler overview PDF (http://www.koeblergerhard.de/Altdeutsch/AlphabetischeUebersichtueberaltdeutschbearbeiteteWerke.pdf): lists "Abrogans (Hrabanisch-keronische Sippe)" with question mark "(Freising zwischen 740-757?)" for the archetype family; main edition Steinmeyer/Sievers, Die althochdeutschen Glossen Bd. 1 (1879), pp. 2-270.
- Wikipedia (keyword use only, not cited as source): states ~3,670 OHG words in over 14,600 entries; Latin-Latin glossary base possibly compiled in Italy; OHG translation likely made in Freising under bishop Arbeo (764-783). These figures require verification against Splett/Bischoff scholarship before citing.
