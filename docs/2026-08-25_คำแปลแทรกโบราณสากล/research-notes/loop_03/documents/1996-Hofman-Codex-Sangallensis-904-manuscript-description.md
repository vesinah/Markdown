Source: e-codices standard description = Hofman Rijcklof, The Sankt Gall Priscian Commentary. Part 1. Volume 1: Introduction; Book 1-5, Munster, 1996, S. 12-31 (plus Scherrer 1875 additional description)
Manuscript: St. Gallen, Stiftsbibliothek, Cod. Sang. 904 (Priscianus, Institutiones Grammaticae)

## Key facts (all from the fetched page)
- Date of origin: a. 851; vellum; ff. II + 120; format 390 x 285 mm; two columns of 36-55 lines.
- Text by two scribes: first scribe wrote pp. 1-156 (quires 1-9 + 6 folia of quire 10); named via second scribes tribute "hucusque calvus patricii depinxit" (= Mail Patricc) at p. 157.
- Helpers: monk Finguine wrote col. 182b; monk Don(n)gus wrote four small portions; a correcting hand "amicus Donnguso".
- Second scribe tentatively named Coirbbre(?) per Lindsay 1910:41 based on margin note p. 194a "do inis maddoc dun .i. meisse 7 choirbbre" - but Hofman argues this refers to glossator A and a brother monk from Inis Maddoc.
- Glosses: commentary consisting of 9412 glosses; ca. 3478 in Old Irish = 36.95%; most in hand of glossator A; glossator B entered glosses in some columns; later hands C-G and H-L plus one Carolingian remark (Z).
- Glosses copied from an exemplar similar in layout to MS G (two columns); displaced subscript glosses prove it.
- Dating demonstration: marginal entries Feast of Diarmait ua hAedo Roin (21 June), feast of Mochaoi (23 June), "satharnn samchasc" (Summer Easter on 28 June): in 9th century Summer Easter fell on 28 June only in 845 and 856; palaeography (Brown 1993) prefers early 9th c.; proposal: year 845 (confirming Guterbock 1895).
- Localization: Ireland (palaeographers Traube, Bischoff, Brown); possibly Nendrum or Bangor (Co. Down) based on Mochaoi/Nendrum entries, Vinniane fave entry, northern-Irish cursive minuscule; Maíl Gaimrid (M.G., died 839 abbot of Bangor per Annals of Ulster) quoted as authority in both St Gall (Sg.) and Milan (Ml.) glosses; McCone (1985: 96-7) suggests "a northern locale for development of a written Old Irish standard".
- Comparison: MS G puncta delentia occur more often/more systematically than in the (earlier) Milan and Würzburg glosses, which were certainly written in Ireland (Thurneysen, Gramm., § 33).
- Old Irish gloss counts across Irish Priscian MSS: Karlsruhe MS 104 Old Irish glosses, Paris MS ca. 65, Leiden MS 19, St Gall ca. 3478; continental MSS contain only one-two-word vernacular glosses.
- Innovation: punctum delens over s, f to denote lenition first attested in MS G.
- Viking poem "Is acher in gaith innocht..." written in upper margin p. 112a in hand of second scribe.
- Later history: poem praising Archbishop Gunther of Cologne (850-863) added on p. 89 by Carolingian hand corrected by Irish hand - testifies presence on Continent in circle of Sedulius Scottus between 855 and 863.
- Arrival in St Gall unknown but after 888 (not in list of libri scotice scripti compiled 884x888); rebound 13th c.; no use-traces after 9th century.

## SELECTED QUOTES (from full fetched text)

"The text was written by two scribes." [...] The name of the first scribe is revealed in the upper margin of p. 157 (f. 79r), where the second scribe has paid him tribute by writing "hucusque calvus patricii depinxit", "Mail Patricc has written until here".

"Priscians text in MS G is elucidated by a commentary consisting of 9412 glosses. Ca. 3478 of these glosses are in Old Irish, i.e. 36.95 % of the total number."

"In the 9th century, Summer Easter was on 28-06 in the years 845 and 856 only. Since Brown (1993: 219) prefers a date early in the 9th cent. for MS G on palaeographical grounds, it seems not unlikely to propose 845 as the year in G was written."

"[I]n MS G these puncta delentia occur more often and more systematically than in the (earlier) Milan and Würzburg glosses, which were certainly written in Ireland (Thurn., Gramm., § 33)."

"...identify the scholar quoted in MS G with Mailgaimrid quoted in the Milan glosses (46b12, 56b33, 68c15, 85b11, 14, cf. McNamara 1973: 259), written early in the 9th century in Ireland, perhaps in Bangor (Brown 1993: 218-9...). As Maelgaimrid is a very uncommon name, he must be the Mailgaimrid who died in 839 as abbot of Bangor according to the Annals of Ulster..."

"The Karlsruhe MS has 104 Old Irish glosses, the Paris MS ca. 65, the Leiden MS 19, but the St Gall MS has ca. 3478 glosses in Old Irish."

"A further argument supporting localization of MS G in the northern part of Ireland could perhaps be derived from the fact that McCone (1985: 96-7) suggests a northern locale for development of a written Old Irish standard."
