## Key points (from official abstract, full text below)
- Comprehensive view of the oldest gloss tradition (OGT) on books I-II of De nuptiis.
- Descriptions of all extant manuscripts transmitting these glosses + stemmatic relationship; centres of glossing activity indicated.
- Apparatus of variants to text and gloss, source apparatus, traditio textus; glosses organized by lemma and categorized by content type; additions of second/third hands noted.
- Findings: layering of annotations over time, close cooperation between scribes, presence of a "core" corpus of annotations.
- Glosses show how Martianus blend of astral religion, classical mythology, pagan tradition impacted Carolingian commentators; reception of pagan culture question.

## Related (search result, QUB pureadmin PDF "Problems in Editing Glosses", URL: https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/94002386/Problems.pdf):
- OGT date ca. 825-900; witnesses used: twenty manuscripts (14+6) from early ninth to tenth centuries; 24 MSS transmitting OGT identified total.
- Methodological problems: how to organize glosses; identification/variation/corruption of lemma; accretion and correction. Solution: categorization + tree structure; multi-word lemmata before single-word; recording different gloss hands.
- OGT once attributed to Dunchad, later Martin of Laon, now multiple scholars; influenced later commentaries of Eriugena and Remigius.
- Spread: Loire valley and northern France eastwards to Lorsch and Cologne, northwards to Flanders, Low Countries, Wales and England (O'Sullivan CMCS 62, 2011).
- Textual variants/corruptions became bedrock: e.g. librico (intellege lubrico) in Cologne-Lorsch family; variants often became vulgate readings furnished with glosses.
