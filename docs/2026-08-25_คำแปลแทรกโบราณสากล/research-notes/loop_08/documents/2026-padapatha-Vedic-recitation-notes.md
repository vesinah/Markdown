## Extracted content

1. Transmission character (Goettingen SPW): Vedic transmission predominantly oral; Vedas handed down in different recitations (patha); includes grammatical analysis; uses various mnemotechnical devices; tradition survives today in Patha-Shalas; transmission 'unmatched in detail and accuracy'; written sources of little import compared with other traditions.

2. Padapatha definition (ACL 2026): 'To understand and preserve the samhitas, the acaryas split them into the Padapatha that is the word segmentation marking the word entries, splitting the Sandhi and compound.' Example RV 1.1.1 samhita 'agnimile purohitam...' vs padapatha 'agnim | ile | purah-hitam | yajnasya | devam | rtvijam | hotaram | ratna-dhatamam'. The padapatha is used to perform eight Vikrtipathas (jata, mala, shikha, rekha, dhvaja, danda, ratha, ghana; krama precedes them) per the traditional verse 'jata mala shikha rekha dhvajo dando ratho ghanah | ashtau vikrtayah prokta kramapurva manishibhih'.

3. Shakalya attribution (Staal excerpt): 'The Padapatha was the work of a great scholar and scientist, the first great linguist in human history, known as clever (vidagdha) Shakalya.' The fixation of the precise form of the Rigveda was based on linguistic analysis separating words (pada); padapatha dissolves sandhi of continuous speech. Shakalya 'faithfully preserved early forms of the language no longer in use at his time', attributing them to regional dialects; his analysis stands at core of the third and final stage of the centuries-long canonization of the Rik-Samhita.

4. LINGUINDIC (Oxford): 'The Rigvedapadapatha (i.e. the padapatha of the Rigveda), ascribed to Shakalya, undoes sandhi and gives grammatical information about the words in the Rigvedasamhita.'

5. Markers system (Franceschini): In the northern 'orthodox' manuscript tradition of the Rigveda Padapatha basically only four markers are used to analyse and rearrange the text of the Samhitapatha: dandas (separating words), avagrahas (separating compound members), circles between dandas (marking galitas), and the particle iti. A Grantha-script manuscript (Cambridge University Library MS Or.2366) uses a full additional marker system flagging peculiar or 'irregular' euphonic modifications, possibly to provide the reciter all information needed to accurately convert Padapatha back into Samhitapatha.

6. Transformations encoded by pada markers (ACL 2025): avagraha separates stem/suffix (haribhyam -> hari'bhyam RV 1.35.3), compound components (puruvasuh -> puru'vasuh RV 2.1.5), or before following iva (pragardhini iva -> pragardhini'iva 10.142.4); iti marks hiatus or pragrhya endings (pati -> pati iti 1.23.3); compounds ending unchangeable vowel repeat after iti (vajinivasu iti vajini'vasu 5.74.6); pluti vowels shortened; euphonic nasal removed; word order changed where necessary (shunashcicchepam -> shunashchepam | cit 5.2.7). Precedence of avagraha usage: iva > compound > suffix > prefix. 'Padapatha is possibly the first attempt to segmentation in Sanskrit literature.'

7. Function vs recitation (Eichler edition intro): The Rigveda-Padapatha contains 163,557 words, among them 22,209 compound words; original pada text does not mark end of internal pada or hemistich (added editorially); accents udatta and independent svarita marked in his transliteration.

8. Manuscript evidence of learner copies (OPenn Ms. Coll. 390 Item 2027): a padapatha manuscript presenting hymns of the Rigveda broken into individual words without sandhi, including 'an unidentified commentary before each section of the Rigveda'.

9. Yaska's use (wisdomlib Nirukta study page): Yaska knew the Padakaras (N.I.17); criticizes Shakalya at N.VI.28; 'without etymology analysis of words is impossible'; padapatha helpful many times (e.g., rodasi RV V.56.8 shows singular), though he is not a blind follower of the Padapatha.

## Relevance to interlinear gloss research

- Padapatha is an analytic re-segmentation layer parallel to interlinear glossing: a word-by-word decomposition transmitted alongside the continuous samhita text, functioning simultaneously as mnemonic device, grammatical analysis, and textual-critical anchor.
- The four-marker system (danda, avagraha, circle, iti) is a graphic apparatus whose function parallels interlinear gloss conventions: converting between analytic and synthetic forms of the same text.
