## Key academic findings

1. **Padapatha as analytic gloss-layer**: The Rigveda-padapatha, ascribed to Shakalya ('the first great linguist'), undoes sandhi and provides word-by-word grammatical segmentation of the samhita; it anchors the true form of the text and serves as base for eight vikrti-recitations functioning as error-checking cross-references (Staal; LINGUINDIC; ACL 2026; URLs above).

2. **Graphic marker apparatus**: Orthodox northern RV Padapatha mss encode analysis through just four markers (danda, avagraha, circle for galita, iti); regional traditions (Grantha script CUL Or.2366) expand this into fuller systems to support conversion between pada and samhita forms — a manuscript-level graphic convention directly comparable to interlinear gloss signs (Franceschini).

3. **First systematic annotation study**: The Hamburg DFG project (Isaacson/Formigatti 2008-11) produced the first monograph-length treatment of marginal/interlinear annotation in Sanskrit mss from N. India and Nepal, arguing such annotation gives near-direct access to premodern scholarly thought processes and can capture oral-to-written transmission moments (teacher's explanations noted down by student-scribe) (GEPRIS; project page; dissertation).

4. **Five-fold purpose taxonomy**: Nearly all examined annotations map onto the five canonical services of a running commentary: (1) word division, (2) syntax analysis, (3) compound resolution, (4) glossing meanings, (5) content explanation; philosophical texts attract categories 3-5, kavya mostly 3-4 with supra/sublinear reading aids replacing prose glosses (Formigatti dissertation abstract).

5. **Annotation-commentary continuum**: Marginal/interlinear notes in Isvarapratyabhijnavimarsini mss are excerpts of independent running commentaries (Abhinavagupta's Vrittivimarsini; Vallabhadeva's Raghupancika) treated as a fixed exegetical apparatus copied together with root text across mss — i.e., commentary migrating into gloss position (Formigatti dissertation).

6. **Material determinism of layout**: Only Kashmiri birch-bark/paper codices offer page space permitting numerous interlinear annotations; palm-leaf pothi format restricts glossing to margins or reading marks — writing medium shapes gloss genre (Formigatti dissertation).

7. **Genre fluidity in naming**: One work can self-describe as vrtti, tika, and tippani simultaneously (Manikya's Amarakoshavivrti, 1386 CE, which glosses Sanskrit lemmas in Newari — itself a bilingual interlinear-like commentary); Rajashekhara's Kavyamimamsa lists eight types: vrtti, paddhati, bhashya, samiksha, tika, panjika, karika, varttika (CUDL Add.1698; RHIMRJ).

8. **Learner-copy evidence**: NGMCP A 375-13 Kumarasambhava carries interlinear and marginal glosses plus word-division/sandhi markings identifying it as a learner's copy; OPenn padapatha item embeds commentary sections — classroom function parallels the Syriac school booklets of Loop 07 (NGMCP; OPenn).
