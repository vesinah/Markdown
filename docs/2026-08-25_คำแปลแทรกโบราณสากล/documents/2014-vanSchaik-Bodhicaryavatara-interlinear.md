## Key passages on interlinear notes (verbatim)
"The four manuscripts are actually three:
(1) A complete copy comprising IOL Tib J 628 and Pelliot tibetain 794.
(2) A copy with several missing pages, now representing about 60% of the text (IOL Tib J 629).
(3) A copy of the last chapter, on the dedication of merit (IOL Tib J 630)."

"In manuscript culture in particular, it is clear that texts were constantly trimmed, augmented and supplemented. Commentary added in interlinear notes might be inserted into the main text in a later copy. Whole sentences might disappear due to a copyist's eye's skipping too far down the page."

## Other verified facts in the post
- The Dunhuang Bodhicaryavatara version is shorter (roughly 700 verses vs 1,000), nine chapters instead of ten; chapters 2-3 combined.
- Early 9th-c. catalogue Ldan dkar ma records the text at 600 verses.
- Colophon of IOL Tib J 629 names the author as Akshayamati, translators Sarvajnadeva (Indian) and Bande Paltseg (Tibetan).
- References cited: Saito Akira 1993 and 2000 reports; Crosby & Skilton 1996.
