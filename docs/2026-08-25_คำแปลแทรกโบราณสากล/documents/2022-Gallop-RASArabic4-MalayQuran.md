## Key facts (verbatim-grounded)
1. "The Royal Asiatic Society holds a Qur'an manuscript (Arabic 4), which is of exceptional importance as being the only known single-volume Qur'an with a full interlinear translation in Malay."
2. Digitised in honour of Prof. Peter Riddell (Riddell 2001; Riddell 2017) alongside the Riddell Festschrift; sponsorship by Gallop supported by Merdeka Award 2022.
3. Provenance: inscribed "Presented by Admiral C. M. Pole, Bart., June 19, 1830"; Sir Charles Moris Pole (1757-1830) served in the East Indies Station 1773-1778, likely acquisition period.
4. Layout: first two folios severely damaged (first folio reversed at rebinding); each page nine lines of Arabic in careful wide hand with "the Malay text sloping beneath in a much smaller, very fine, hand"; one or two ruled red text frames; surah headings red in rectangular panels; verse separators small gold roundels outlined in black.
5. Paper: laid paper probably Asian manufacture; hatched mold marks, no chainlines/watermarks.
6. Textual identity: comparison shows Arabic 4 contains almost exactly the same Malay translation as PNRI A.51 and W.277 (Banten Qur'ans studied by Nurtawab 2020); worked example quoted for Surat al-Kahf v.11 including marginal addition in A.51.
7. Corpus claim: only four other known Qur'an copies with interlinear Malay translations exist, all in multiple volumes; two of them are also from Banten; additionally three of eleven known Javanese-interlinear Qur'ans are from Banten -> Banten as hub of this pedagogical tradition.
8. Dating: early 18th century, when Banten flourished as an Islamic scholarship centre; gold verse markers very rare in Southeast Asia but recorded in two other Banten Qur'ans; Asian paper + layout + Pole chronology support early dating.
9. Nurtawab (2020) proposal reported: interlinear-translated Qur'ans should be regarded as tafsir, created with explicit intent to explicate meaning, with elements of interpretation and variant views beyond pure translation, independent of Abd al-Rauf's Tarjuman al-Mustafid.
10. Exegetical timeline framework (via Riddell): mid-17th c. Malay world producing Qur'an mss, partial translations, surah-level exegesis; 2nd half 17th c. Aceh: first full Malay commentary = Tarjuman al-Mustafid by Abd al-Rauf; next major work absent ~250 years until Tafsir Nur al-Ihsan by Muhammad Said bin Umar of Kedah, 1925-1927.
