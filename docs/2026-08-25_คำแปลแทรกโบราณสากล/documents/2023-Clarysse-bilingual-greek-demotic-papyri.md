## Key extracted facts

1. Basis: Leuven database Trismegistos (https://www.trismegistos.org), ca. 50,000 documentary texts from Egypt (all languages/materials) dated 3rd c. BC - 3rd c. AD. Bilingual Greek-Demotic documents = about 5% of total [2,626 texts; figures accessed August 2016]. Written within ERC GESHAEM project (Horizon 2020 grant agreement No 758907).

2. Definition of "bilingual texts" follows Depauw 2009, 114-115: "Greek and demotic are both used on a single writing surface and there is an intentional relation between the texts using these different languages and scripts."

3. Chronological spread (absolute figures):
   - Greek: BC3 5397 | BC2 4819 | BC1 2602 | AD1 6467 | AD2 19698 | AD3 12598
   - Demotic: BC3 1728 | BC2 2506 | BC1 1392 | AD1 1244 | AD2 469 | AD3 177
   - Bilingual: BC3 362 | BC2 318 | BC1 160 | AD1 258 | AD2 603 | AD3 744

4. Bilinguals never exceed 5%; numbers rise in 3rd c. AD mainly due to hundreds of bilingual mummy labels (late 2nd/early 3rd c.; >1,600 labels published after Arlt 2011 and Vleeming 2011; more than half bilingual).

5. Typology:
   (a) Two languages equal: trilingual priestly decrees (11 preserved); mummy labels; P.Count 2/3 salt-tax register (229 BC) mixing Demotic entries with Greek totals; SB 28 16943 letter starting Greek continuing Demotic; Narmouthis ostraca archive with complete linguistic mixture (Greek words inserted in Demotic and vice versa).
   (b) Primary Greek + secondary Demotic [881 items]: Greek receipts with full Demotic first-person text; Greek tax receipts followed by Demotic summary/signature; archival filing notes in Demotic; Demotic receipt confirming repayment of a Greek loan.
   (c) Primary Demotic + secondary Greek [819 items]: Demotic contracts with Greek abstracts on back (brewer/oil-seller sureties, nomarch Aristarchos archive); Demotic tax receipts with Greek summary (tax, year, sum); Greek autograph subscriptions under Demotic contracts (ca. 25 known, 12 BC-AD 71, Fayum: Dime, Tebtynis); Greek registration subscriptions (peptoken eis kiboton) from 259-240 BC; from 145 BC rule that all Demotic contracts receive a Greek hypographe.
   (d) "Pseudo-bilinguals": later Greek dockets below originally monolingual Demotic contracts.

6. End of Demotic (Theban tax-receipt sequence): Demotic receipts usual up to AD 31; sporadic AD 32-43; disappear completely after AD 43. Last dated Demotic papyrus document Chrest.Wilck. 86 (AD 210/211, bilingual linen receipt for Apis mummification); last bilingual ostraca P.Zauzich 33-34 (AD 203/204), O.Zurich 54 (AD 223); last Demotic inscriptions: two graffiti at Philae AD 452.

7. Bibliographic anchors noted: Depauw 2009 (bilingual papyri and hellenization); Mairs 2012 (bilingual tagging of financial accounts, ZAS 139); Fournet 2018 (documents bilingues greco-coptes dans l'Egypte byzantine: essai de typologie); Blasco 2015/2018 (Narmouthis bilingualism); Vandorpe & Clarysse 2008 (bankers Menodoros adding taxpayer names in Greek in margins of Demotic receipts).

## Relevance to interlinear-gloss research
Demonstrates ancient Egyptian translation/annotation practice adjacent to glossing: word-level insertion of Greek into Demotic (and vice versa), marginal summaries, and registration subscriptions - functional precursors of interlinear bilingual layout in documentary culture.
