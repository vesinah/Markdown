# Full-text notes: Coptic glosses in Greek Bible manuscripts (Feder)

- Source: Feder, Frank, "Old Testament, Coptic Versions of the: Update", Claremont Coptic Encyclopedia (update dated 22/27 November 2023).
- URL: https://ccdl.claremont.edu/digital/api/collection/cce/id/2188/download
- Fetched: 2026-08-25 (webfetch; PDF binary - notes based on extracted search text of same PDF)

## Key extracted facts

1. Section 2.1 "The oldest witnesses of Coptic biblical translations: Coptic glossing to Greek biblical texts": the earliest witnesses of Coptic biblical translations - or more precisely of biblical "text work" by Egyptian "philologists" - are Egyptian glosses or "annotations" in the margins of Greek manuscripts. The custom may have been adopted from a longstanding pharaonic Egyptian tradition still active in the 2nd c. CE (Feder 2020c: 644-646).

2. Chester Beatty papyrus codex VII with the Greek book of Isaiah (TM 61951; LXX MS 965), dated mid-3rd to early 4th century, contains a significant number of Coptic "annotations" (ca. 30; Wagner 2021: 293-295) written in Greek letters - still without the signs borrowed from Demotic (Wagner 2021: 406-407). The fragmentary state makes it likely many more glosses were lost.

3. The glosses show a barely standardized form of Fayyumic, not free of Mesokemic influence, arguably typical for the region between the Fayyum Oasis and the nearby Nile Valley; if MS 965 was written in the 3rd c., glosses could have been added still in the 3rd or early 4th c.

4. The Psalter is the only book of the Sahidic Bible with a long tradition of bilingual Greek-Sahidic manuscripts; P.Bodmer XXIV (Greek Psalter, publ. 1967) shares important variant readings with the Sahidic version - Greek Vorlage of the Sahidic Psalter similar to P.Bodmer XXIV.

5. Keywords of the entry include "Manuscripts, Coptic Glosses in Greek Bible Manuscripts" and "Digital Edition" - evidence that Coptic-gloss studies are now framed within digital edition projects (Göttingen Academy context).

## Relevance
Primary confirmation for Loop 24 gap (1): Coptic glosses inside Greek manuscripts from Egypt, pre-dating full Coptic Bible translations; situates glossing as the first stage of Egyptian biblical translation work.
