## Three-tier typology of Tibetan Dunhuang manuscripts (paraphrase of post)
1. Top tier: thousands of carefully copied and corrected sutras from organized scriptoria of the Tibetan imperial period; variation minimized by editorial process.
2. Middle: careful non-scriptorium copies, usually unrevised, personal use (short treatises, prayers, rituals); individual sometimes stylish hands.
3. Bottom: scruffy manuscripts with poorly formed letters and notably variable spelling; hypothesis: products of teaching situations / student note-taking (possibly by non-native learners of Tibetan).

## Manuscript examples named
- IOL Tib J 754: a "passport" (letters of introduction in Tibetan, 10th c.) for a Chinese monk travelling to Nalanda.
- Pelliot tibetain 849: long scroll ending with account of Indian teacher Devaputra's journey India-China via Tibet; "contains some written notes perhaps taken by a Tibetan student of this Devaputra."
- Pelliot 5386: Khotanese-Sanskrit bilingual phrasebook with teacher-student dialogues.
