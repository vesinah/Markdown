## Abstract (verbatim extract)

"This paper explores the interlinear glossing in Coptic... We commence the paper with an exploration of the interlinear glossing on Coptic texts that can be observed in medieval manuscripts. The most representative attestation is MS Copt. 14 (Vatican, Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana), which contains the translation of Pauline Epistles, Catholic Epistles, and Acts in the Bohairic dialect of the Coptic language. However, Arabic interlinear glosses were written above the Coptic words or phrases at a later date. In addition, MS Or. Quart. 159 (Berlin, Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin) contains interlinear glosses of Arabic on Bohairic Coptic translations of the Psalms. The interlinear glosses of both manuscripts contain Arabic translations above every Coptic word or phrase."

## Key points

1. Medieval Arabic-on-Coptic interlinear glosses: every Coptic word/phrase carries an Arabic translation written above it - true interlinear (word-by-word) technique, attested in at least two major manuscripts:
   - Vat. Copt. 14: Bohairic Pauline Epistles, Catholic Epistles, Acts + later Arabic interlinear glosses.
   - Berlin Or. Quart. 159: Bohairic Psalter + Arabic interlinear glosses.
2. Modern linguistic glossing of Coptic frequently follows Leipzig Glossing Rules but methods vary greatly among authors (survey of Lingua Aegyptia articles and Grossman, Haspelmath & Richter eds. 2015, Egyptian-Coptic Linguistics in Typological Perspective, EALT 55, De Gruyter Mouton).
3. Coptic SCRIPTORIUM project: multilayered online corpus (platform ANNIS) includes POS, lemma, syntax layers, but no morpheme-meaning gloss layer; proposal to add standardized glossing layer.
4. Goal: develop a standard governing future Coptic glossing, bridging medieval interlinear practice and modern linguistic glossing.

## Relevance
