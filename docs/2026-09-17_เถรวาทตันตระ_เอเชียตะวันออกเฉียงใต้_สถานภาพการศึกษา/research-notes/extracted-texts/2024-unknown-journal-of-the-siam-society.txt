Editorial
This final issue of the Journal of the Siam Society (JSS) for 2025 brings together a rich 
and wide-ranging collection of scholarship that reflects the Society’s enduring 
mission: to support rigorous research on Thailand and its regional and global 
contexts, while honoring those who have shaped the field.
	
We open with remembrance and a tribute to French architect Pierre Pichard 
(1936–2024), former honorary member of the Siam Society, whose monumental 
restoration and documentation of Phimai, Phanom Rung, and Bagan remains a 
landmark achievement in Southeast Asian architectural history. His passing marks 
the loss of a meticulous scholar whose curiosity, discipline, and generosity of spirit 
continue to inspire.
	
Our Research Highlights section presents five studies that illuminate diverse 
aspects of Siam’s past and present. Hiroshi Kawaguchi revisits Siam–Vietnam 
relations during the reign of King Rama I, offering new perspectives on diplomacy 
and regional politics. Chris Baker & Pasuk Phongpaichit reconstruct Nai Mot’s 
printing of the Three Seals Law, shedding light on evolving law, new technologies 
and royal authority in the mid-19th century. Pakdeekul Ratana examines the roles of 
Lan Na women across key historical periods, foregrounding labor, moral authority, 
and cultural agency. Tinakrit Sireerat explores nationalism and continuity in 
Siamese forestry history, tracing tensions between stewardship and sovereignty. 
Finally, Saranpat Boonhok & Tongchen Hou analyze how contemporary television 
drama reframes the historical relationship between Siam and Burma, revealing 
popular media’s role in shaping collective memory. 
	
This issue also features a Special Edition dedicated to George Cœdès (1886–1969), 
former President of the Siam Society and a foundational figure in Southeast Asian 
studies. Published to coincide with the forthcoming Bangkok colloquium on HRH 
Prince Damrong Rajanubhab and Prof. Cœdès, these articles revisit his intellectual 
networks, early field experiences, and enduring influence. Jean Baffie offers a 
biographical overview situating Cœdès within the institutional world he helped 
build. Thissana Weerakietsoontorn examines his first encounter with Angkor in 
1912; Pierre-Yves Manguin reassesses his seminal contributions to Śrīvijaya studies; 
and Bernard Cros, Cœdès’s grandson, highlights the significance of his brief 1928 
visit to Batavia (modern Jakarta). Michel Lorrillard publishes and contextualizes 
correspondence between Prof. Cœdès and Paul Pelliot regarding the name Xianluo 
found in the Chinese annals, identified with Siam, while Gregory Kourilsky analyzes 
Jean-Yves Claeys’s 1929 mission to Siam in the orbit of Cœdès. Together, these 
contributions present a multifaceted portrait of Cœdès as scholar, administrator, 
and participant in the intellectual and geopolitical currents of his time.

In the Museum Spotlight, I examine a small pre-Angkorian bodhisattva in 
bronze at the Art Institute of Chicago, situating it within broader questions of style, 
iconography, and provenance. The Events & Exhibitions section reviews two recent 
cultural initiatives: Natasha Reichle reflects on Moving Objects at the Asian Art 
Museum, engaging contemporary debates on looting and restitution to Thailand; 
and Anne Håbu discusses Temples and Elephants, an exhibition in Bangkok and 
Oslo revisiting Carl Bock’s travels and their place in the Western imagination of 
Southeast Asia.
	
We conclude with an extensive Book Reviews section that underscores the 
vitality of Southeast Asian studies across archeology, legal and colonial history, 
anthropological and religious studies, maritime networks, and the history of 
education. From Ban Chiang to colonial law and spirit possession, the breadth of 
works reviewed attests to continuing scholarly innovation and engagement.
	
We extend our warm thanks to all authors, reviewers, and collaborators 
who have contributed to this year’s issues and to The Siam Society Under Royal 
Patronage and our global readership for their continued support. As we look ahead 
to 2026, JSS remains committed to fostering open, critical, and evidence-based 
scholarship grounded in respectful debate and a spirit of inquiry. May these pages 
serve both as a record of achievement and an invitation to further research.
Nicolas Revire 
Managing editor
