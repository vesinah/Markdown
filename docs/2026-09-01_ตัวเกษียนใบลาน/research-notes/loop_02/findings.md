## S-2023-Pannabhoga-01

- **Bibliography:** Pannabhoga Herngseng, Ven. "Burmese Nissaya Literature: A Mainstream Monastic Education Learning Method and its Salient Features". *MANUSYA: Journal of Humanities* 26.1 (2023), 1–23. DOI: 10.1163/26659077-25010027 (ยืนยันผ่าน Crossref)
- **Language:** English (วัตถุศึกษา: Pali + Burmese)
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1163/26659077-25010027
- **Local Path:** `research-notes/loop_02/documents/2023-burmese-nissaya-literature-manusya.pdf`
- **Quote 1** (นิยาม nissaya word-by-word, abstract, p. 1): "In some of the earliest Buddhist literature found in Myanmar, a type of translation in which each Pali word is followed by its relevant annotative interpretation, known as nissaya, can be found."
- **Quote 2** (ต้นกำเนิด — nissaya ที่เก่าที่สุด, p. 2): "Such a literary genre, consisting of a word-to-word translation, was already popular in Sri Lanka and found among Pyu and Mon literature. Archaeological excavation in Myanmar found that the Sanskrit-Pyu nissaya at Srikṣetra in the 7th ce, and Kyansittha's inscriptions at Pagan in 12th ce were the earliest forms of nissaya."
- **Quote 3** (nissaya กับสันสกฤต-พม่า ผ่าน Pruitt/Braun, p. 7): "Pruitt states, '[the] texts are given word-by-word translation into Burmese, a Tibeto-Burman language, in the texts called nissayas'. According to Erik Braun, nissaya is a Burmese text that translates and explains Pali works, often on a systematic word-by-word basis."
- **Quote 4** (nissaya = ต้นฉบับของคำแปล Tripiṭaka มาตรฐาน, pp. 8–9): "The current standard translation of the Tipiṭaka in Burmese language clearly derives from the original nissaya with a minimum of adjustment... if the nissaya has added words, called pāṭhasesa ('extra text'), the translation has included the additional words in parentheses."
- **Quote 5** (sannaya สิงหล + การเทียบภูมิภาค, p. 8): "According to McDaniel (2008, 119), a similar genre of Pali literature also developed within Theravada Buddhist countries, i.e., in Sri Lanka, as sannaya... The Sutranipāta-sannaya, a Pali-Sinhala word-to-word translation written in the 12th ce, is the earliest example of a nissaya, or sannaya literary genre found in Sri Lanka."
- **Quote 6** (nissaya ไทย vs พม่า + 2 methods, pp. 11–12, 17): "While Burmese nissaya include canonical texts, commentaries, sub-commentaries, and so on, Thai nissaya are limited to canonical texts and other commentaries." + "1. pāṭhānusārī nissaya method – a simple translation of Pali sentences... 2. pāṭhasesa nissaya method – words may be added in order to better..." + (p. 20) "Burmese nissaya has become not only a tool of interpretation, but a resource of reference, a handbook for students, and a syntactical text, apart from merely a word-for-word translation or bitext as Walker (2020) puts it."
- **ข้อสังเกต:** ผู้เขียนเป็นนักบวชพม่า (SSBU Taunggyi) — เน้น nissaya เป็น "mainstream monastic learning method" ที่มีชีวิตถึงปัจจุบัน (ห้องเรียน nissaya ที่ Veluvan Monastery, Taunggyi — figure 3); ยืนยันว่าพม่าใช้ nissaya ครอบคลุมกว่าไทยมาก

## S-2023-Scott-01

- **Bibliography:** Scott, Anthony. "The Politics of Pali Commentary: Canon-making, Monastic Reform, and Religious Revival in the Mingun Jetavana Sayadaw's Milindapañhā-aṭṭhakathā". PhD Thesis, Department for the Study of Religion, University of Toronto, 2023.
- **Language:** English (วัตถุศึกษา: Pali + Burmese)
- **URL/DOI:** ไม่พบ DOI ในตัวไฟล์ (proQuest T/repository thesis) — ตรวจไม่ได้ผ่าน Crossref
- **Local Path:** `research-notes/loop_02/documents/2023-politics-of-pali-commentary-toronto-thesis.pdf`
- **Quote 1** (นิยาม nissaya + seamless text, p. 78): "Bilingual bitexts that have dominated the literary culture of second-millennium Burmese Buddhism, a nissaya is an interphrasal or interlinear bilingual gloss of Pali into Burmese, where a single or a set of Pali words from the root text is given, then explained with Burmese equivalents in a type of running translation-cum-commentary. The result is a seamless text where the reader pivots from the target text, such as Burmese, to understand the source text, usually in Pali, and vice versa."
- **Quote 2** (ทฤษฎี bitext ของ Harris, p. 78 fn. 67): "Harris describes a bitext as when a source text and a target text are 'sewn firmly together like a piece of cloth and its lining is to be used as one fabric'... a bitext is 'not two texts but a single text in two dimensions, each of which is a language'... there are multiple techniques to visually 'sew' these two together on manuscripts in Southeast Asia, but perhaps more important is the psycholinguistic component, the ways that readers in what is now Burma, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia, and Sri Lanka are able to read Pali-vernacular texts as one"
- **Quote 3** (nissaya กับ Milinda-nissaya เป็นตำราสอบ, p. 110): "since the Milindpañha was then a book prescribed for translation in the government pathama pran exam... the nissaya that was compiled by the sayadaw also came to be treated as a manual for learners of Piṭaka literature and [by those] people taking part in the exam"
- **Quote 4** (อ้าง Walker กรอบ 3 ขั้น, p. 111): "Walker provides the most expansive account of the techniques of nissaya bitexts, distilling them to three main stages: 'selection, analysis, and presentation' (Walker 2020, 678)... In terms of presentation, Walker lists four ways that an author tailors their nissaya for a specific audience, either for philological and language training, to provide a specialised scholastic exegesis, as a homiletic narrative, or to give a 'poetic aesthetic expansion' of the root text in verse (Walker 2020, 677–78)."
- **Quote 5** (การใช้คำว่า padaso 'line by line' ใน Vinaya, p. 36–37): "a monk commits an offence requiring expiation (P. pācittiya) 'for making one who is not ordained [as a monk] speak Dhamma line by line' (anupasampannaṃ padaso dhammaṃ vāceyya) (Vin IV 14,29-30)"
- **ข้อสังเกต:** ประโยชน์เชิงวิธีวิทยา — เชื่อม "bitext" (Harris 1988, translation theory) เข้ากับ nissaya; แสดงว่า aṭṭhakathā ยุค 1949 (Mingun Jetavana) ยังดูดซับเทคนิค nissaya — หลักฐานว่าเทคนิค bitext เป็น living tradition
