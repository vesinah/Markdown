# Loop 19 Summary: Research on Manuscript Annotation Studies (2021-2026)

**Date**: 2026-09-02  
**Research Question**: What are the recent scholarly trends (2021-2026) in manuscript annotation studies, especially related to interlinear gloss, digital humanities approaches, and comparative manuscript studies?

---

## Executive Summary

This research loop identified **~60 sources** across **9 major search queries**, focusing on recent scholarship (2021-2026) in manuscript annotation studies. The investigation reveals four major trends:

1. **AI/ML Revolution in HTR**: Transformer-based models have replaced traditional segmentation approaches for handwritten text recognition
2. **Human-AI Hybrid Workflows**: Recognition that fully automated systems require human expertise in the loop
3. **Infrastructure Gaps**: Critical discussions about misalignment between DH tools and manuscript scholars' actual workflows
4. **Global/Comparative Turn**: Growing emphasis on non-European manuscript traditions and cross-cultural methodologies

### 2. Computational Approaches: HTR & Machine Learning

**Major Sources Identified**:

1. **"An Indirect Evidence Synthesis of AI Methods for Low-Resource Chagatai Manuscripts"** (2026)
   - MDPI Information 17(7), 681
   - Systematic review of 50 studies (2020-2026)
   - Covers: HTR, OCR, script classification, dataset creation
   - **High priority but download failed**

2. **"Human-in-the-Loop Mass Transcription and Ground Truth Annotation for Challenging Historical Documents"** (2026)
   - Fischer, N. & Puppe, F., LREC 2026
   - Methodologies for ground truth creation with human expertise
   - **Status**: PDF successfully downloaded → documents/Fischer_Puppe_2026_Human_in_the_Loop_LREC.pdf

3. **"Compact Multi-Domain HTR with Evidence-Aware Review for Arabic Manuscripts"** (2026)
   - ArXiv 2608.19385
   - Cross-domain adaptation, Phoenix model
   - 81-symbol codec, document-aware replay

4. **"Transformer-based HTR for Historical Documents"** (2022)
   - ArXiv 2203.11008
   - Marks shift from RNN to transformer architectures

5. **"From Codicology to Code"** (2025)
   - ArXiv 2506.20326
   - Comparative study of Transformer vs YOLO detectors for Document Layout Analysis
   - Bridges traditional codicology with ML

6. **"Classifying Medieval Manuscripts by Pen and Support"** (ACH)
   - Machine learning for material attributes (paper vs parchment, quill vs reed pen)
   - Shows computational analysis can aid/surpass manual paleographic methods

7. **"From Genomes to Algorithms"** (2026)
   - ArXiv 2606.06889
   - Neural networks for palimpsest detection
   - Biocodicology: manuscripts as textual AND biological artifacts

**Key Trend**: Move from fully automated systems to human-in-the-loop workflows. Recognition that low-resource manuscripts (underrepresented scripts/languages) require specialized approaches.

### 6. Interlinear Gloss: A Disciplinary Divergence

**Computational Linguistics Approach** (IGT = Interlinear Glossed Text):

1. **"Massively Multilingual Joint Segmentation and Glossing"** (2026, ArXiv 2601.10925)
   - Neural networks for automated gloss prediction
   - 1000+ languages, morpheme-level annotation

2. **"Multilingual Pretraining for Low-Resource Interlinear Glossing"** (2024, ArXiv 2403.06399)
   - IGT for language documentation

3. **"Automated interlinear gloss prediction"** (ACL 2026)
   - GlossLM models

**Key Finding**: Recent "interlinear gloss" scholarship is dominated by **computational linguistics** (endangered language documentation) rather than **historical manuscript studies** (medieval glosses). However, the computational methods may be adaptable.

## Gaps Identified

1. **Interlinear Gloss in Historical MSS**: NLP work on IGT doesn't address medieval interlinear annotations (glosses between lines of text)

2. **Southeast Asian Manuscripts**: Limited representation compared to European, Arabic, Chinese traditions

3. **Integration Bottlenecks**: Multiple standards (TEI, IIIF, W3C Web Annotation) exist but aren't smoothly integrated

4. **Annotation Semantics**: Tools focus on spatial/visual annotation; less on semantic/scholarly layers

5. **Palmleaf Manuscripts**: Very limited coverage in recent DH literature

## Conclusion

Loop 19 successfully identified recent scholarly trends in manuscript annotation studies. The field shows:

1. **Technical maturity**: HTR and ML methods advancing rapidly
2. **Methodological reflection**: Critical awareness of DH limitations
3. **Global expansion**: Move beyond Euro-centric perspectives
4. **Integration challenges**: Multiple standards not yet harmonized
5. **Human-AI collaboration**: Recognition that automation requires expertise

For the **interlinear-gloss-palmleaf project**, these findings suggest:
- Adopt **human-in-the-loop** HTR workflows
- Use **IIIF + TEI standards** but be aware of integration challenges
- Position within **comparative manuscript studies** framework
- Highlight **Southeast Asian manuscript** contribution (addressing field gap)
- Learn from **computational codicology** methods for material analysis

The downloaded Fischer & Puppe (2026) paper provides a strong methodological foundation for ground truth annotation workflows, directly applicable to Thai Buddhist palmleaf manuscripts with interlinear annotations.
