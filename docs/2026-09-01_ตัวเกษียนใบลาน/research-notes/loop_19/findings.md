# Loop 19 Findings: Recent Trends in Manuscript Annotation Studies (2021-2026)

**Research Question**: What are the recent scholarly trends (2021-2026) in manuscript annotation studies, especially related to interlinear gloss, digital humanities approaches, and comparative manuscript studies?

**Date**: 2026-09-02

---

## Executive Summary

Analysis of recent scholarship (2021-2026) reveals four major trends in manuscript annotation studies:

1. **AI/ML Revolution in HTR**: Shift from traditional segmentation-based methods to transformer-based models for handwritten text recognition
2. **Computational Linguistics Approaches**: NLP methods applied to interlinear glossed text (IGT) for language documentation
3. **Infrastructure Critique**: Growing awareness of gaps between DH tools and manuscript studies workflows
4. **Multimodal Integration**: IIIF, TEI, and AI systems increasingly integrated in annotation platforms

- **Focus**: GlossLM models for accelerating documentation
- **Relevance**: Methodological interest for automating gloss annotation

### Note on Interlinear Gloss Literature
Most recent work on "interlinear gloss" focuses on computational linguistics and endangered language documentation (IGT = Interlinear Glossed Text format for linguistic analysis). This is distinct from historical manuscript studies' use of "interlinear gloss" (medieval translations/explanations written between lines). However, the computational methods may be adaptable.

## Gaps Identified in Current Research

1. **Interlinear Gloss in Historical MSS**: Recent work on IGT is computational linguistics-focused; little on historical manuscript interlinear annotations (glosses in medieval texts)

2. **Southeast Asian Manuscripts**: Limited representation in recent DH literature compared to European, Arabic, Chinese manuscript traditions

3. **Integration Bottlenecks**: Multiple standards (TEI, IIIF, W3C) not smoothly integrated in practice

4. **Annotation Semantics**: Tools focus on spatial/visual annotation; less on semantic/scholarly annotation layers

5. **Scholarly Workflows**: Gap between tool capabilities and actual scholarly needs (echoes 2018 DHQ workshop findings)
