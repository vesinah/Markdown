**Manuscript Identified**:
- **Title**: *dbu ma de kho na nyid rnam par spyod pa* (Analysis of the Essence of Madhyamaka)
- **Author**: Gyamarwa Jangchupdrak (rgya dmar ba byang chub grags), 12th century
- **Repository**: Néchu temple collection, Drepung ('bras spungs) monastery
- **Catalogue ID**: Drepung catalogue No. 015397
- **Format**: 31-folio manuscript (codex unicus)
- **Publication**: *Collected Works of the Kadampas* (bka' gdams gsung 'bum), vol. 31, pp. 7–67 (Chengdu: Si khron mi rigs dpe skrun khang, 2007)
- **Digital Edition**: https://www.oeaw.ac.at/ikga/forschung/tibetologie/materialien/the-dbu-ma-de-kho-na-nyid-of-rgya-dmar-ba-byang-chub-grags-12th-c/

**Annotation Content**: The notes identify scholars and their philosophical positions in Madhyamaka debates — names include Métikpa, Khyung, and other 11th-12th century Tibetan scholastics.

**Layout Evidence**: Hugon consistently uses BOTH terms together: "marginal and interlinear notes" (repeated at least 4 times in the article). This confirms that mchan annotations in this manuscript occupy **both positions**: margins AND between lines of text.

## Answer to Research Question

### Is mchan interlinear or marginal?

**ANSWER: BOTH**

Mchan (མཆན་) annotations in Tibetan manuscripts are **BOTH interlinear (between lines) AND marginal (side notes)**.

### Evidence Summary:

1. **Hugon 2020** (peer-reviewed, primary manuscript evidence):
   - Explicitly describes Drepung ms. No. 015397 as having "marginal and interlinear notes"
   - Uses both terms together repeatedly (4+ instances)
   - Actual 12th-century manuscript with documented annotations

2. **van Schaik 2014** (expert blog, Dunhuang manuscripts):
   - Confirms "interlinear notes" in IOL Tib J 628/629/630 and Pelliot tibétain 794
   - 9th-10th century Dunhuang evidence

3. **S-mchanbu snippet** (unverified but consistent):
   - States mchan bu placed "in the margins of the page or in between the lines"

4. **Rangjung Yeshe dictionary**:
   - Metaphor "armpit/side" suggests adjacent/beside placement, consistent with both positions

### Comparison with Other Traditions:

| Tradition | Layout | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **Tibetan mchan** | **Interlinear + Marginal (both)** | Mini-commentary, scholar IDs, annotations |
| Chinese jiazhu 夹注 | Interlinear (same line, smaller) | Inline annotations |
| Thai nissaya | Interlinear (alternating lines) | Translation gloss |
| Sinhala sannaya | Interlinear (alternating lines) | Translation gloss |
| Japanese kunten | Marginal + inline marks | Reading marks, pronunciation |
| Latin glossa ordinaria | Interlinear + marginal | Biblical commentary |

**Conclusion**: Tibetan mchan shares the **dual-position layout** with Latin glossa ordinaria — both systems use interlinear AND marginal space. This differs from pure interlinear systems (Thai nissaya, Sinhala sannaya) and pure inline systems (Chinese jiazhu).
