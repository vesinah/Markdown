## Major Breakthroughs

### 1. Zhang Yongquan (2024) - Most Significant Find ⭐⭐⭐

**Publication**: "Alignment of double-line annotation" in *Manuscript Philology of Dunhuang*  
**Publisher**: Springer Singapore (Oct 2024)  
**Pages**: 415-448 (34 pages)

**Why This Matters**:
- **First systematic technical study** of jiazzhu layout mechanics in Dunhuang manuscripts
- Confirms jiazzhu = 双行小字 (double-line small characters)
- Documents **scribal calculation process**: scribes must count characters to distribute evenly between two annotation lines
- Provides specific technical details about aesthetic requirements
- Based on earlier 2011 article in *Dunhuang Turpan Research*

**Impact**: This single source transforms understanding of jiazzhu from vague concept to technically precise practice with documented methods.

### 2. Li Naiqi (2022) - Confirmation Source ⭐⭐⭐

**Publication**: "On the 'Small Characters Double-line' in Yiqiejing yinyi"  
**Journal**: *Journal of Indian and Buddhist Studies* Vol. 70, No. 3  
**Language**: Japanese

**Why This Matters**:
- Confirms 小字双行 (xiǎozì shuāngháng) = standard term for jiazzhu format
- Studies oldest Buddhist dictionary (Tang dynasty, c. 649 CE)
- Shows jiazzhu used in lexicographical works
- Connects to Loop 05 findings on Yiqiejing yinyi

**Impact**: Validates terminology and extends evidence to Buddhist reference works.

### 3. Imre Galambos (2020) - Authoritative Context ⭐⭐

**Publication**: *Dunhuang Manuscript Culture: End of the First Millennium*  
**Publisher**: De Gruyter  
**Pages**: 299 pages

**Why This Matters**:
- Major authority on Dunhuang manuscripts
- Comprehensive coverage of manuscript layout conventions
- Focus on 9th-10th century (key period for jiazzhu development)
- Multiple related articles on punctuation, manuscript format

**Impact**: Provides scholarly framework and context for understanding jiazzhu practices.

## Technical Terminology Confirmed

### Chinese Terms for Jiazzhu
1. **夹注** jiāzhù = "inserted annotation" (primary term)
2. **双行小字** shuāngháng xiǎozì = "double-line small characters" (descriptive)
3. **小字双行** xiǎozì shuāngháng = "small characters double-line" (alternative)
4. **细注** xìzhù = "fine annotation" (variant)
5. **双行注** shuāngháng zhù = "double-line annotation" (abbreviated)

### Format Characteristics (from Zhang 2024)
- Two lines of smaller characters between main text lines
- Equal character distribution required for aesthetics
- Pre-calculation necessary before writing
- Used in sutras, commentaries, dictionaries
