## What We Now Know About Jiazzhu

### Confirmed Terminology
- **夹注** jiāzhù = "inserted annotation" (primary term)
- **双行小字** shuāngháng xiǎozì = "double-line small characters" (technical term)
- **小字双行** xiǎozì shuāngháng = alternative form (same meaning)

### Technical Details (from Zhang 2024)
- Annotation written in two lines of smaller characters between main text lines
- Scribes must **pre-calculate** character count to distribute evenly
- Goal: maintain neat, balanced appearance
- Requires skill: avoiding too many blanks or cramped spacing
- Used in Buddhist sutras, commentaries, and dictionaries

### Historical Context
- Evidence from Tang dynasty (618-907 CE) onward
- Found in Dunhuang manuscripts (Cave 17 library)
- Used in major works like Yiqiejing yinyi 一切経音義 (oldest Buddhist dictionary, c. 649 CE)
- Deliberate practice with aesthetic standards, not casual notation
