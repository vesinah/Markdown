## Answer to Research Question

**Does South Asia have interlinear gloss tradition in Sanskrit ṭīkā / Pali aṭṭhakathā manuscripts?**

### Answer: NO (with high confidence for the specific claim)

**Reasoning**:

1. **Structural incompatibility**: Sanskrit ṭīkā and Pali aṭṭhakathā are fundamentally **lemmatic commentaries** (quote-and-comment format), not interlinear glosses. This is a well-established fact of Indic textual tradition.

2. **Absence of evidence**: Despite comprehensive searches of manuscript studies literature, no sources describe word-by-word interlinear annotation in South Asian Pali or Sanskrit manuscripts.

3. **Regional specificity**: Scholarly literature consistently treats nissaya/interlinear format as a **Thai-Lao** phenomenon, not as borrowed from South Asian precedent.

4. **Functional difference**: 
   - **South Asian commentaries**: Separate texts that explicate root texts philosophically/doctrinally
   - **Thai nissaya**: Translation/pedagogical tool for reading Pali when vernacular literacy is primary

### Is Thai interlinear gloss (ตัวเกษียน) a unique development?

### Answer: YES (likely, pending confirmation from downloaded PDFs)

**Evidence**:
- Appears in regional (mainland Southeast Asian) studies exclusively
- No mentions of similar practices in South Asian manuscript codicology
- Likely developed for pedagogical purposes in monastic education where students knew Thai but needed to read Pali

1. Reading full text of "Indic-Vernacular Bitexts from Thailand" (2020) - downloaded but not yet extracted
2. Checking NGMCP manuscript catalog for annotation examples
