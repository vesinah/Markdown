### Preliminary Analysis

**Evidence for South Asian interlinear gloss**: 
- **NONE FOUND YET** - No sources in the search results explicitly mention interlinear glossing in Sanskrit ṭīkā or Pali aṭṭhakathā manuscripts from India, Nepal, or Sri Lanka

**Evidence against (suggesting Thai uniqueness)**:
1. The 2020 JAOS article title "Indic-Vernacular Bitexts from Thailand" suggests this is a Thai phenomenon specifically
2. The "Forgotten Chapter" article (2016) on Sanskrit book production doesn't appear in nissaya/interlinear searches, suggesting these weren't standard practices
3. Most relevant hits come from Thailand/Laos regional studies, not South Asian codicology

**Key Observation**:
Traditional Sanskrit commentaries (ṭīkā) and Pali commentaries (aṭṭhakathā) are **lemmatic** - they quote a portion of the root text (lemma) and then provide extensive commentary. This is different from word-by-word interlinear glossing where the translation/gloss appears directly between or above/below each word of the source text.
