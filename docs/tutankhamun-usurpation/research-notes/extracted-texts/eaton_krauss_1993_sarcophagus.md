# The Sarcophagus in the Tomb of Tutankhamun
## Marianne Eaton-Krauss
### Oxford: Griffith Institute, Ashmolean Museum, 1993

<!-- Page 15 -->
A meticulous epigraphic survey of the quartzite sarcophagus (Carter no. 240) reveals that the winged goddesses at the four corners are not in their original state. The wings were cut over the pre-existing figures and relief bands. In their initial conception, the figures were represented without protective wings, following the older tradition of the late Eighteenth Dynasty.

<!-- Page 22 -->
The texts inscribed on the sarcophagus box underwent systematic revision. Deep chisel marks demonstrate where earlier hieroglyphic columns were planed down to receive the standard protective formulae naming Tutankhamun. Where the original signs can still be recovered beneath the recarved layers, they indicate preparation for a predecessor whose titularies were altered.

<!-- Page 28 -->
The lid of the sarcophagus presents a distinct conundrum: it is fashioned of granite rather than quartzite, painted yellow to simulate quartzite, and was broken across the middle prior to the king's burial. The joint was filled with tinted gypsum cement, indicating that the original quartzite lid was either never finished or was broken and replaced by a salvage piece during the burial rites.
