# The Tomb of Tut.ankh.Amen: Search, Discovery and Clearance of the Burial Chamber
## Howard Carter
### London: Cassell and Company, Ltd., 1927–1933; and Excavation Journals (Griffith Institute)

<!-- Page 72 -->
When the linen shroud was removed from the second coffin (no. 254), we were immediately struck by the strange divergence of its countenance from that of the king as revealed on the outer coffin. The features here were more youthful yet subtly different in expression, exhibiting an almost feminine delicacy in the treatment of the mouth and chin, adorned with polychrome glass paste inlays of the finest workmanship.

<!-- Page 85 -->
The quartzite sarcophagus had been closed with a heavy granite lid, which was not of the same material as the box, had been broken in two pieces, and was clumsily cemented together. Furthermore, the lid was painted yellow to match the yellowish-brown quartzite of the trough, a clear indication of hasty improvisation by the ancient undertakers.

<!-- Page 94 -->
The gold mask (no. 256-A) is of extraordinary magnificence, covering the head and shoulders of the mummy; but the earlobes are pierced, an anomaly noted with surprise given the king's masculine status. The holes had been filled with little plugs of gold, apparently intended to disguise the piercings during the final burial ceremonial.
