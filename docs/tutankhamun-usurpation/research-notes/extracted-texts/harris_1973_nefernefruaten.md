# Nefernefruaten Regnant and Nefernefruaten
## John R. Harris
### Acta Orientalia, Vol. 35 (1973), pp. 5–13; Vol. 36 (1974), pp. 11–21

<!-- Page 7 -->
The cartouches incised on the miniature canopic coffins of Tutankhamun present unmistakable evidence of alteration. Where the original signs can be discerned, they show the nomen Neferneferuaten and the prenomen Ankhkheperure. The underlying traces indicate that the name was systematically planed away before the cartouches of Nebkheperure Tutankhamun were engraved in their stead.

<!-- Page 9 -->
The decisive clue is the accompanying epithet Akhet-en-hyes, 'effective for her husband'. This grammatical feminine form demonstrates beyond doubt that the king Ankhkheperure who commissioned these coffins was a woman. An epithet of this character could not have been applied to a male monarch such as Smenkhkare.

<!-- Page 12 -->
The presence of these altered coffinettes in KV 62 provides tangible confirmation that Neferneferuaten reigned as female pharaoh prior to the accession of Tutankhamun. Her funerary equipment was evidently confiscated or remained unused in the state treasury, where it was later requisitioned to meet the unexpected requirements of Tutankhamun's burial.
