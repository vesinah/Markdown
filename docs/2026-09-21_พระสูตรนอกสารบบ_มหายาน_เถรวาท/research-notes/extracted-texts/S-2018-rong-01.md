## Academic Structure and Core Content Relevant to Buddhist Apocrypha

1. **Lecture 1: The Discovery of the Library Cave and the Dispersal of Dunhuang Manuscripts**
   - Discovery of Cave 17 at Mogao by Wang Yuanlu in 1900.
   - Expeditions of Aurel Stein (1907), Paul Pelliot (1908), Zuichō Tachibana (Otani Mission, 1910), and Sergei Oldenburg (1914–1915).

2. **Lecture 2: The Nature of the Library Cave and Reasons for Its Sealing**
   - Analysis of why over 50,000 manuscripts were sealed in Cave 17 around 1006 CE (fear of Karakhanid invasion vs. sacred waste depository / Genizah theory).

3. **Lecture 5 & 6: Dunhuang Buddhist Manuscripts and Non-Canonical Texts**
   - The coexistence of standard canonical scriptures (*Kaiyuan Shijiao Lu* system) and indigenous apocrypha (*weijing* 偽經) in daily monastic and lay life.
   - Distinctive Dunhuang apocrypha: *Tiwei Poli Jing* (提謂波利經, T2875), *Fumu Enzhong Jing* (父母恩重經, T2887), *Xiangfa Jueyi Jing* (像法決疑經, T2870), and esoteric dharani manuals.
   - The role of popular sutras in ritual protection, funeral rites, and trans-Eurasian trade caravans along the Silk Road.
