## Topic 1 — Biblical studies: the great "versions" and their critical editions

### 1.1 Septuagint (Göttingen Septuaginta; Rahlfs/Hanhart)
- The Göttingen *Septuaginta-Unternehmen* was founded in 1908 (following Paul de Lagarde's plan for an edition based on all manuscripts) and ran until 2015; since 2016 a research commission ("Kommission zur Edition und Erforschung der Septuaginta") continues the work. Its aim is to reconstruct "the oldest attainable, i.e. pre-recensional text form" of the Greek OT and to document later revisions.
  - Source: https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/ — Göttinger Septuaginta project site (Akademie der Wissenschaften zu Göttingen); German description at https://www.uni-goettingen.de/en/262432.html
- The critical series: *Septuaginta. Vetus Testamentum Graecum Auctoritate Academiae Scientiarum Gottingensis editum* (Göttingen: Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht, 1931– , 20+ vols). Key editors per volume: Alfred Rahlfs (*Psalmi cum Odis*, 1931); W. Kappler (1–2 Maccabees, 1936/1959); J. W. Wevers (Genesis 1974 through Deuteronomy 1977); Robert Hanhart (Esther 1966, Judith 1979, Tobit 1983, Esdras, 2 Chronicles 2014); Udo Quast (Ruth 2006).
  - Source: IOSCS, "Critical Editions of Septuagint/Old Greek Texts" (statement adopted 4 April 2005): https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/ioscs/editions.html
- IOSCS officially recommends the Göttingen edition as the only "fully critical" LXX text; Rahlfs's *Handausgabe* (Stuttgart 1935) is classified as "semi-critical." Cambridge (Swete 1909–1922; larger Cambridge ed. Brooke–McLean–Thackeray from 1906) took a "diplomatic" approach and lapsed c. 1940.
  - Source: same IOSCS statement, https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/ioscs/editions.html
- Rahlfs's manual edition was revised by Robert Hanhart as *Septuaginta, Editio altera* (2006), correcting/extending text and apparatus "in well over a thousand instances" (Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft, ISBN 9783438051196).
  - Source: https://www.die-bibel.de/en/septuagint-lxx-publications (German Bible Society)
- Since 2020 a new long-term Academy project prepares the *Editio critica maior of the Greek Psalter* to replace Rahlfs's outdated 1931 Psalms; Module 1 described ~1,300 Psalter manuscripts.
  - Source: https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/

### 1.2 Vetus Latina (Beuron Vetus Latina Institut)
- "Vetus Latina" = collective name for all pre-Jerome Latin Bible forms differing from the Vulgate; preserved in few manuscripts (many fragments/palimpsests, 4th–13th c.), in biblical quotations in Latin Fathers, and in liturgical tradition. Thomas Johann Bauer is current Academic Director.
  - Source: https://vetus-latina.manuscriptroom.com/ (Vetus Latina Project / INTF manuscriptroom)
- Institut founded 1945 at the Benedictine Archabbey of Beuron by Bonifatius Fischer OSB, building on Josef Denk's ~400,000 index cards of Old Latin verse evidence; directors: Fischer (1945–73), Hermann Josef Frede (1973–98), Roger Gryson (1998–2013). Edition title: *Vetus Latina: Die Reste der altlateinischen Bibel nach Petrus Sabatier neu gesammelt und herausgegeben von der Erzabtei Beuron* (Freiburg: Herder, 1949– ). About half the Bible is published; NT instalments include Frede on Ephesians/Philippians–Colossians/Thess–Tim/Titus–Phlm–Heb, Thiele on Catholic Epistles, Gryson on Revelation (2000–03), Burton–Houghton–MacLachlan–Parker on John (2011–), Haelewyck on Mark (2013–).
  - Sources: H. A. G. Houghton, *The Latin New Testament: A Guide to its Early History, Texts, and Manuscripts* (Oxford University Press, 2016), full-text PDF: https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/files/101039771/9780198744733.pdf ; https://itseeweb.cal.bham.ac.uk/vetuslatina/
- Five classes of witnesses for the Old Latin are enumerated: (1) patristic quotations (Cyprian, Lucifer of Cagliari, Jerome, Augustine; inventory in Frede 1981/84/88; all material on file at Beuron); (2) early biblical manuscripts; (3) Carolingian/medieval Bibles preserving archaic readings even inside Vulgate codices; (4) glosses/additions to Jerome's translation; (5) lessons, canticles, antiphons in liturgical books ("Liturgy is conservative").
  - Source: "Latin versions of the Bible" (encyclopedic overview reprinted by Serbian Orthodox Church archive): http://arhiva.spc.rs/eng/latin_versions_bible.html
- Significance claim: most Old Latin translations were made from Greek manuscripts that no longer exist; the original layer "preserves a witness to the Bible... which would otherwise be lost."
  - Source: https://itseeweb.cal.bham.ac.uk/vetuslatina/

### 1.3 Peshitta and Syriac versions
- Syriac recensions of the Gospels: Old Syriac (*vetus syra*) in Codex Sinaiticus (syrs, mid/late 4th c.) and Curetonianus (syrc, early 5th c.), each an independent translation of a Greek archetype; Peshitta (early/mid-5th c., 350+ mss), a consistent rendering of its Greek base; Harclean (616 CE, Thomas of Harqel's revision of the lost Philoxenian of 507/508). Tatian's Diatessaron (~172 CE) is the oldest Syriac gospel text.
  - Source: William L. Petersen, review of Kiraz, *TC: A Journal of Biblical Textual Criticism* 2 (1997): http://rosetta.reltech.org/TC/v02/Kiraz1997rev.html (full extract saved as documents\1997-petersen-syriac-versions-review.md)
- George Anton Kiraz, *Comparative Edition of the Syriac Gospels: Aligning the Sinaiticus, Curetonianus, Peshitta and Harklean Versions*, 4 vols., NTS 21 (Leiden: Brill, 1996).
  - Sources: https://syriaca.org/work/71 ; https://bethmardutho.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Kiraz_1996_Comparative-Edition-of-the-Syriac-Gospel.pdf
- Münster's *Das Neue Testament in syrischer Überlieferung* (B. Aland/A. Juckel, ANTT 7, de Gruyter 1986– ) displays the full Syriac evidence for the Catholic Letters incl. patristic citations.
  - Source: Petersen review bibliography (above); NA28 introduction PDF: https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783438072399_A33839551/preview-9783438072399_A33839551.pdf
- Methodological point (Petersen §10): when a Syriac variant is paralleled in earliest fathers and other early versions (e.g., vetus latina), "one can be certain that one has recovered a second- or third-century Greek reading"; singular Syriac variants remain ambiguous between local Syriac development and their lost Greek base.

### 1.4 Vulgate (Weber–Gryson)
- Robert Weber (ed.), *Biblia Sacra iuxta vulgatam versionem* (Stuttgart: Württembergische Bibelanstalt/Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft, 1st ed. 1969; 5th revised ed. prepared by Roger Gryson, 2007, ISBN 978-3-438-05303-9, xlix + 1980 pp.), with collaborators B. Fischer, J. Gribomont, H.F.D. Sparks, W. Thiele. A "manual edition" condensing the Oxford and Rome Vulgates with independent examination of manuscripts; apparatus repeatedly rewritten using new Beuron Vetus Latina research.
  - Sources: WorldCat record https://search.worldcat.org/title/182757062 ; https://www.die-bibel.de/en/home/scholarly-editions/vulgate
- Parker's handbook treats the Stuttgart Vulgate together with the Oxford Vulgate as the two principal editions whose chief witnesses are catalogued; the Vetus Latina Institute's register supplies the early Latin evidence.
  - Source: Houghton 2016 (PDF above).

### 1.5 Targums
- The Aramaic Bible series translated all traditionally-known Targums into English with apparatus/notes (Wilmington: Michael Glazier / Collegeville: Liturgical Press, 1987– ): e.g., vol. 1A McNamara (Targum Neofiti 1: Genesis), vol. 1B Maher (Pseudo-Jonathan: Genesis), vol. 12 Hayward (Targum of Jeremiah), etc.
  - Source: Internet Archive publisher metadata for vols 8 & 12: https://archive.org/details/aramaicbibletarg0008unse and https://archive.org/details/aramaicbibletarg0012unse
- Standard critical introduction: Paul V. M. Flesher & Bruce Chilton, *The Targums: A Critical Introduction* (Leiden: Brill, 2011, ISBN 9789004217690) — argues the major Targums date to the fifth century or earlier.
  - Source: cited in Gavin McDowell, "The Date and Provenance of Targum Pseudo-Jonathan," *Aramaic Studies* 19 (2021) reference list, DOI 10.1163/17455227-bja10018 (via Wikipedia-linked bibliography; book itself verified via Brill listing in that citation trail)
- Methodological caution for targumic evidence: Targum Onkelos is comparatively restrained/literal and therefore more usable; Palestinian targums (Neofiti, Fragment-Targums, Pseudo-Jonathan) are expansive, use reverential circumlocutions (Memra, Shekhinah) and midrashic additions; "a targum cannot automatically be treated as a transparent window into a Hebrew Vorlage." Divergence from MT must be tested against the targum's habitual translation technique before positing a variant Vorlage.
  - Source: Edward D. Andrews, "Targums and Peshitta: Aramaic Translations of Old Testament Texts," Updated American Standard Version blog, 22 Dec 2025: https://uasvbible.org/2025/12/22/targums-and-peshitta-aramaic-translations-of-old-testament-texts/ (secondary synthesis; consistent with Tov/Flesher-Chilton)

### 1.6 Origen's Hexapla and retroversion/retranslation
- The Hexapla: six parallel columns — (1) Hebrew, (2) Hebrew transliterated in Greek letters, (3) Aquila, (4) Symmachus, (5) Septuagint (with Aristarchian signs), (6) Theodotion. Origen marked LXX pluses over against Hebrew with obeli and supplied minuses from other Greek versions (normally Theodotion) with asterisks; his purpose was apologetic/textual alignment of the church's Greek Bible with the synagogue Hebrew.
  - Sources: John D. Meade, "The First Parallel Bible," Text & Canon Institute, Aug 2024: https://textandcanon.org/the-first-parallel-bible/ (full extract saved); Catholic Encyclopedia "Hexapla": https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/07316a.htm ; Swete, *An Introduction to the Old Testament in Greek* ch. III (Hexapla and recensions): https://mail.biblehub.com/library/swete/an_introduction_to_the_old_testament_in_greek_additional_notes/chapter_iii_the_hexapla_and.htm
- The signs derive from Alexandrian Homeric philology (Aristarchus). Origen, Comm. Matt. 15.14: "I marked with an obelos some passages not in the Hebrew... And I added some passages with asteriskoi... from the rest of the editions agreeing with the Hebrew."
  - Source: Meade 2024 (above), quoting Origen; cf. Joachim Schaper, "Origen's Hexapla and Fragments," preview PDF in Mohr Siebeck volume: https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783161587863_A40613274/preview-9783161587863_A40613274.pdf — notes Origen's decisions rest entirely on "outward" criteria (other versions, the Hebrew).
- Material remains: Cairo Geniza palimpsest Taylor-Schechter 12.182 (multi-column Hexapla copy of Psalms); P. Grenf. 1.5 (Ezekiel with asterisks, 250–350 CE); the Syro-Hexapla of Paul of Tella (616 CE) preserves the fifth column in Syriac.
  - Source: Meade 2024; also https://textandcanon.org/tag/hexapla/
- Frederick Field, *Origenis Hexaplorum quae supersunt* (Oxford, 1875) is still the standard collection of fragments; a new Hexapla Project (originally Greenspoon/Salvesen/Norton at Oxford from 1994; now hosted by the Text & Canon Institute) is producing new editions. Colloquium papers: *The Forerunners and Heirs of Origen's Hexapla* (Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht, 2024, open access).
  - Sources: Meade 2024; Christian Publishing House Blog essay citing Field and the Oxford project: https://christianpublishinghouse.co/2020/05/29/origens-hexapla-a-sixfold-text-in-parallel-columns-of-the-old-testament/
- The Jewish revisers (kaige-Theodotion, Aquila ca. 130, Symmachus ca. 200) show graded literalism: Aquila uses stereotyped one-word-one-Greek-equivalent renderings; Symmachus is freer. Their literalism makes them valuable retroversion tools but they attest "the conservatively copied Hebrew text," while occasionally preserving non-Masoretic readings.
  - Source: John D. Meade, "The Legacy of the First Revised Bible Translations," Text & Canon Institute, Oct 2022: https://textandcanon.org/the-legacy-of-the-first-revised-bible-translations/ (extract included in documents\2024-meade-hexapla.md)

## Topic 2 — Emanuel Tov, *Textual Criticism of the Hebrew Bible*

- Emanuel Tov, *Textual Criticism of the Hebrew Bible* (Minneapolis: Fortress / Assen: Van Gorcum; 1st ed. 1992; 2nd ed. 1992/2001; 3rd ed. 2012; 4th ed. Fortress 2022, ISBN 9781506483481). The standard handbook; classifies Qumran biblical scrolls relative to MT, LXX-Vorlage, and Samaritan traditions (proto-MT, pre-Samaritan, close to the Hebrew of the LXX, non-aligned), with percentages shifting across editions (e.g., proto-MT 60% → 35% → lower as categories were refined).
  - Sources: Fortress product page https://www.fortresspress.com/store/product/9781506483481/Textual-Criticism-of-the-Hebrew-Bible ; Anthony Ferguson, "Emanuel Tov and His Evolving Categories for the Biblical Dead Sea Scrolls," Evangelical Textual Criticism blog, 10 Mar 2023: http://evangelicaltextualcriticism.blogspot.com/2023/03/the-evolution-of-biblical-dead-sea.html
- Tov's companion methodology manual: *The Text-Critical Use of the Septuagint in Biblical Research* (Jerusalem Biblical Studies 3; Jerusalem: Simor, 1981; 2nd rev. ed. 1997; 3rd completely rev. ed.: Winona Lake: Eisenbrauns, 2015, ISBN 9781575063287).
  - Sources: Hebrew University CRIS record: https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-text-critical-use-of-the-septuagint-in-biblical-research-3/ ; Eisenbrauns TOC page: https://www.eisenbrauns.org/books/titles/978-1-57506-328-7.html ; Richard S. Hess review, *Denver Journal* 20 (2017): https://denverjournal.denverseminary.edu/the-denver-journal-article/the-text-critical-use-of-the-septuagint-in-biblical-research/ (full extract saved)
- Key concepts verified in TOC and review:
  - Retroversion criteria: Greek–Hebrew equivalents; intuition; textual probability; linguistic plausibility; external support (Qumran scrolls such as 4QJer^b/d, 4QSam^a corroborating LXX against MT).
  - Reliable retroversions require literal/stereotyped translation technique, quantitatively equal representation, preserved Hebrew word order; free translations (e.g., Job, Proverbs) defeat retroversion.
  - Non-variants vs pseudo-variants: grammatical substitutions (waw/καί, article, pronouns, singular/plural) are not variants; letter-confusions (d/r, y/w, b/m...) generate pseudo-variants unless externally supported.
  - Vocalization and word division can sometimes be reconstructed; theological tendencies (de-anthropomorphizing) must be discounted.
  - Programmatic quote: "The ultimate purpose of the textual-critical analysis of the LXX" is "to isolate deviations in that translation that presumably were based on a Hebrew Vorlage different from the MT and, accordingly, to reconstruct elements in that Vorlage."
    - Source: Hubner review of Tov, *TC: A Journal of Biblical Textual Criticism* 21 (2016): http://rosetta.reltech.org/TC/v21/TC-2016-Rev-Tov-Hubner.pdf
- Translation technique studies: James Barr, *The Typology of Literalism in Ancient Bible Translations* (MSU 15; Göttingen: Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht, 1979) — foundational taxonomy of literalism levels; Anneli Aejmelaeus, "What We Talk About When We Talk About Translation Technique," in *On the Trail of the Septuagint Translators* (CBET 50; Leuven: Peeters, 2007 [2001]).
  - Sources: Bryan Beeckman, "Trails of A Different Vorlage and A Free Translator in Proverbs," *Old Testament Essays* 30/3 (2017), reference list: https://ote-journal.otwsa-otssa.org.za/index.php/journal/article/view/155 ; Tov, *The Greek and Hebrew Bible: Collected Essays on the Septuagint* (VTSup 72; Leiden: Brill, 1999) contents incl. ch. 16 "The Nature and Study of the Translation Technique of the Septuagint": https://brill.com/display/title/6779?language=en

## Topic 5 — Terminology: versions, secondary witnesses, retroversion, Vorlage

- **"Versions" vs "witnesses":** In NT scholarship "versions" = translations of the Greek NT into other languages (Latin, Syriac, Coptic...); they function as *witnesses* in the apparatus but are not original-language witnesses. Waltzmn's survey: "we now call these translations 'versions'... When properly used, the versions are one of the best and leading tools of textual criticism... they cannot testify to small variant readings, they usually will testify to major differences between texts."
  - Source: "Versions of the New Testament," The Encyclopedia of New Testament Textual Criticism (R. Waltz): https://www.skypoint.com/members/waltzmn/Versions.html
- **Secondary witnesses:** NA28 distinguishes consistently cited witnesses (Greek mss) from others; versional and patristic support is recorded separately (sigla lat/syr/cop/arm...). Petersen: using versions requires immersion "in the peculiarities and particularities of that version: its manuscripts, syntax, vocabulary, and transmission history."
  - Sources: NA28 intro PDF (pageplace link above); Petersen 1997 (rosetta link above)
- **Vorlage** (German, "that which lies before"): the exemplar text actually before a translator/copyist; central to LXX studies ("Hebrew Vorlage of the LXX") and to Buddhist philology (Chinese/Tibetan Vorlage).
  - Sources: Tov 2015 chs. 3–6 (Eisenbrauns TOC); Beeckman 2017 abstract ("a different Vorlage, which had another verse order than MT and 4QProv^b")
- **Retroversion / back-translation:** reconstructing the source-language reading by reverse-translating a version. Biblical paradigm: Tov's five criteria (see Topic 2). Buddhist parallels: Tibetan "translationese" led scholars to attempt Sanskrit back-translations (Gummer); Silk's "funhouse mirror" warns translations distort more than reflect; Harrison: reconstruction of originals now considered futile in principle.
  - Sources: Hess/Denver Journal 2017 (saved extract); Natalie Gummer, "Buddhist Books and Texts: Translation," *Encyclopedia of Religion* (Gale, 2005): https://www.encyclopedia.com/environment/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/buddhist-books-and-texts-translation ; Jonathan A. Silk, "Peering Through a Funhouse Mirror..." (Topic 6); Heuman/Tricycle 2011 (saved extract)
- **Translation technique:** measurable habits of a translator (segmentation, quantitative correspondence, stereotyping, word order). Barr 1979; Aejmelaeus 2007; Tov 1999 ch.16; van der Louw, *Transformations in the Septuagint* (Ph.D. Leiden 2006; Peeters).
  - Sources: Beeckman 2017 reference list (OTE link above)

### 6.2 Jonathan Silk

### 6.5 Ernst Waldschmidt and reconstruction of lost Prakrit/Sanskrit Āgama originals
- Ernst Waldschmidt (1897–1985, Göttingen) established the Turfan method: diplomatic transcription of the fragmentary Central Asian Sanskrit manuscript followed by "critical reconstruction of the literary work on the basis of parallel versions in Pāli, Chinese, and Tibetan whenever available. The juxtaposition of transcription and reconstruction became the model for all later editions."
  - Source: Thomas Oberlies, "WALDSCHMIDT, ERNST ii. Research," *Encyclopaedia Iranica* (published 2000, updated 2016): https://iranicaonline.org/articles/waldschmidt-ernst-02/
- Major editions: *Die Überlieferung vom Lebensende des Buddha* (2 vols, 1944–48) and *Das Mahāparinirvāṇasūtra* (3 parts, 1950–51); *Das Catuṣpariṣatsūtra* (3 vols, 1952–62); *Das Mahāvadānasūtra* (2 vols, 1952–56) — Abhandlungen der Akademie der Wissenschaften in Göttingen; *Bruchstücke buddhistischer Sūtras aus dem zentralasiatischen Sanskritkanon* (1932, Madhyamāgama fragments); *Sanskrithandschriften aus den Turfanfunden* pts 1–5 (1965–1985).
  - Sources: Oberlies/Iranica (above); German Wikipedia-derived bio confirmed by Iranica data: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Waldschmidt (keyword discovery only)
- Waldschmidt, "Central Asian Sutra Fragments and their Relation to the Chinese Agamas," in *Die Sprache der ältesten buddhistischen Überlieferung* (Symposien zur Buddhismusforschung II; Göttingen: Vandenhoeck & Ruprecht, 1980), pp. 136–174; repr. in *Ausgewählte kleine Schriften* (Wiesbaden: Franz Steiner, 1989), 370–408. Direct statement of the principle that Chinese Āgamas witness lost Indic recensions.
  - Sources: NTU Digital Library of Buddhist Studies entries: https://buddhism.lib.ntu.edu.tw/DLMBS/en/search/search_detail.jsp?seq=274174&comefrom=authorinfo and seq=332819

### 6.7 Back-translation discourse in Buddhist philology
- Gummer (Encyclopedia of Religion, 2005): Tibetan normalization created "a highly artificial style, a kind of 'translationese'... some modern scholars of Buddhism have thought that Tibetan translations could be used to produce 'back-translations,' reconstructions of Sanskrit texts that are no longer extant."
  - Source: https://www.encyclopedia.com/environment/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/buddhist-books-and-texts-translation
- Paul Harrison, "Experimental Core Samples of Chinese Translations of Two Buddhist Sūtras Analysed in the Light of Recent Sanskrit Manuscript Discoveries," *JIABS* 31 (2008); conference paper PDF: https://www.oeaw.ac.at/fileadmin/Institute/IKGA/PDF/events/Harrison_2007.pdf — compares all Chinese versions of the Vajracchedikā and Vimalakīrtinirdeśa passages against newly found Sanskrit mss; concludes the fluidity of both Sanskrit and Chinese traditions and reaffirms the importance of the OLDEST Chinese translations (Kumārajīva, Zhi Qian). Quote: "their work is more art than science. There are simply too many imponderables..."
- Salomon/Cox/Harrison/von Hinüber (Tricycle interview, Summer 2011): Gandhari manuscripts proved the canons are siblings, not parent/child; "none of the existing Buddhist collections... can be privileged as the most authentic"; braided-river replaces tree model; horizontal conflation defeats pure stemmatics.

## Topic 7 — Kalila wa-Dimna transmission chain (translations forming the stemma)

- François de Blois, *Burzoy's Voyage to India and the Origin of the Book of Kalīlah wa Dimnah*, Royal Asiatic Society Prize Publication Fund Vol. XXIII (London: Royal Asiatic Society, 1990, x+101 pp., ISBN 0947593063/Routledge 9780947593063).
  - Sources: Routledge: https://www.routledge.com/Burzoys-Voyage-to-India-and-the-Origin-of-the-Book-of-Kalilah-Wa-Dimnah/DeBlois/p/book/9780947593063 ; JSTOR review (S. Leder, 1994): https://www.jstor.org/stable/4183324 ; SOAS repository record: https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/output/405075/burzoys-voyage-to-india-and-the-origin-of-the-book-of-kalilah-wa-dimnah
- Verified chain (per Davidson 2023, tracking de Blois's inventory):
  1. Sanskrit *Pañcatantra* (conventional estimate c. 300 CE per IIS; prosimetrum fables).
  2. Middle Persian (Pahlavi) rendering by Borzōy/Burzuy under Khosrow I Anōshirvān (r. 531–579) — NOT attested; known only through aetiologies.
  3. Older Syriac *Qalīlag w Damnag* (6th c., attributed to Bōd) — the oldest attested text; independent of the Arabic.
  4. Arabic *Kalīla wa-Dimna* by Ibn al-Muqaffaʿ (d. c. 757); oldest Arabic MSS: ʿAzzām MS (1220/21) and Shaykhū MS (1338); poetic Arabic versions by ʿAbān al-Lāḥiqī (d. c. 815) and Ibn al-Habbārīyah (11th–12th c.).
  5. Later Syriac version (10th–11th c., from Arabic, "rather free," padded with biblical quotations).
  6. Greek *Stephanites kai Ichnelates* by Symeon Seth (late 11th c.) — reduces expansions "to a bare minimum."
  7. New Persian versions (Balʿami commission; Naṣrallāh Munshi 12th c.; al-Bukharī 12th c.; later derivatives incl. Kashifi's *Anvar-i Suhayli*).
  8. Hebrew versions (older 12th c.; newer 12th c. direct from Arabic) → John of Capua's Latin *Directorium humanae vitae* (13th c.; printed Strasbourg ca. 1489) → Renaissance European vernaculars.
  9. Old Spanish *Calila e Dimna* (mid-13th c., direct from Arabic; word-for-word correspondences allow it to be called a true "translation").
  - Sources: Olga M. Davidson, "On the Kalīla wa-Dimna," Classical Continuum (Harvard CHS), 2 Nov 2023: https://continuum.fas.harvard.edu/davidson-on-the-kalila-wa-dimna/ (full extract saved); Muslim Heritage/FSTC overview incl. BNF arabe 3465 and John of Capua images: https://muslimheritage.com/kalila-wa-dimna/ ; Institute of Ismaili Studies synopsis (Pancatantra c. 300 CE; Burzuya under Khusraw Anushirwan; de Blois listed in further reading): https://www.iis.ac.uk/scholarly-contributions/kalila-wa-dimna
- Davidson's critique: de Blois treats multiformity as scribal corruption ("padding"), whereas performance/oral-tradition analysis explains expansion and contraction organically; the Near Eastern versions may reflect "a different and perhaps older recension of the Sanskrit text" than extant Pañcatantra manuscripts (de Blois quoting earlier orientalists). Lesson: in long translation chains, the stemma is made OF translations, and each link can preserve older content lost in sibling lines.
- Digital corpus of Kalīla manuscripts (FU Berlin, citing de Blois p. 70 for MS P13): https://kalila-and-dimna.fu-berlin.de/description/
