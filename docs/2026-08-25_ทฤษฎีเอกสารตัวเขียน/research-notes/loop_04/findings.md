## A. Chinese textual criticism (校勘學 / jiaokanxue) and allied disciplines

### A1. Chen Yuan 陳垣 (1880–1971), 《元典章校补释例》/《校勘学释例》 and the 四校法

**Publication history (nuance):** The work originated as 《元典章校补释例》 (*Yuan dianzhang jiaobu shili*, "Illustrated Examples from the Collation of the Yuan Statutes"), drafted after Chen collated the Shen-family 1902 printed *Yuan dianzhang* against a **Yuan-dynasty woodblock edition rediscovered in the Palace Museum in 1925**, yielding ~12,000 errors. Versions appeared 1931/1932 (Peking University / Cai Yuanpei jubilee volume); it was printed in the *Liyun shuwu congke* 励耘书屋丛刻 in **1934**, and reissued under the title 《校勘学释例》 by Zhonghua shuju (**1959** reprint; 1963 ed.). Hu Shi's famous preface 《校勘学方法论——序陈垣先生的〈元典章校补释例〉》 renamed the field's agenda ("the greatest success of the new Chinese textual criticism"). Sources: diancang.xyz full-text page (https://www.diancang.xyz/xueshuzaji/20585/); Deng Ruiquan 2018 (https://m-news.artron.net/20180522/n1004879.html); Hu Shi essay full text at aisixiang (https://www.aisixiang.com/data/100320.html).

**The 校法四例 (four methods of collation)** — Chen Yuan's own formulations (卷6《校法四例》, plus 《通鉴胡注表微·校勘篇》):
1. **對校法 duijiao** — "to read one text against an ancestral or alternative copy of the same work, noting differences beside the passage." Chen calls it "the simplest and safest method, purely mechanical" (纯属机械法): its aim is "to collate sameness/difference, not right/wrong" (校异同，不校是非). He insists 凡校一书，必须先用对校法 — every collation must begin here. Traces the method to Liu Xiang's Han-era description: "one holds the master copy, one reads aloud — like enemies facing each other."
2. **本校法 benjiao** — internal cross-checking within the same work ("以本书前后互证"); exemplified by Wu Zhen's 《新唐书纠谬》 and Wang Huizu's 《元史本证》; best used before other copies are obtained.
3. **他校法 tajiao** — checking against other works that quote or are quoted by the text ("以他书校本书"): earlier works excerpted, later works citing it, contemporaneous parallels. Chen's showcase case: restoring the fabric-term 纳失失 (nasij) in the *Yuan dianzhang* via 《元史》祭祀志/舆服志.
4. **理校法 lijiao** — correction by reasoning/conjecture when no textual evidence exists: "最高妙者此法，最危险者亦此法" — "subtlest of the four, and most dangerous"; requires 通识 (broad learning), never without firm evidence ("非有确证，不敢藉理校而凭臆见").

Chen also compiled **42 误例 (types of corruption)** specific to Yuan usage (Mongol transliterations, era names, posthumous titles). Hu Shi praised three things: seeking the best base text (using a Yuan print to correct a Yuan book), exhaustive variant-marking against the oldest print, and induction of error-types only after collation.

### A2. 版本学 banbenxue — the study of editions as distinct discipline

- Lian Bin Dai, *China's Bibliographic Tradition and the History of the Book* — key finding: from Liu Xiang/Liu Xin's Han cataloguing grew the trinity 目录学 muluxue (bibliography/cataloguing), 版本学 banbenxue (editions), 校勘学 jiaokanxue (collation) constituting 校雠学 jiaochouxue; banbenxue crystallized in 17th–18th c. rare-book catalogues of private collectors focused on Song/Yuan imprints; "banbenxue is roughly equivalent to historical and descriptive bibliography, while jiaokanxue is similar to analytical and textual bibliography." https://www.lianbindai.com/assets/pdf/China's%20Bibliographic%20Tradition.pdf
- WisdomLib definition of 版本學: study of block-print origins, transmission, paper/ink, typeface/knife-work, collector seals, binding, and content collation, to distinguish 原刻/翻刻 (original vs re-cut), 原抄/影抄, 善本 shanben (fine edition)/劣本. https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/ban-ben-xue
- Soren Edgren, "Some Thoughts on Traditional Chinese Bibliographic Terminology" (JEAL, BYU) — terminology of editions (原刻 yuankan, 翻刻 fanke, 重刻 chongkan, 蓝印 lanyin trial prints, etc.). https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?httpsredir=1&article=1733&context=jeal
- HKUST-GZ deep-learning edition-identification paper (InceptionResNet-V2 over 163 premodern books, 11 regional/dynastic edition classes; accuracy 91.3%) — documents the traditional two approaches: textual/evidential research vs "observing the wind and watching the air" 观风望气 (physical connoisseurship). https://cislab.hkust-gz.edu.cn/media/documents/A_Deep-Learning_Model_for_Edition_Identification_of_Premodern_Chinese_Rare_Books.pdf

### A4. 辨伪学 bianweixue — authenticity criticism; Gu Jiegang and 古史辨

- Gu Jiegang's Gushibian movement (7 vols, 1926–1941, ~350 essays): "layer-by-layer" accretion thesis — antiquity was constructed successively, "in an order exactly the reverse of actual occurrence"; inspired by Qing kaozheng (Cui Shu), Yao Nai/Hu Shi's Deweyan method; international context traced in Rudolf G. Wagner, "The Global Context of a Modern Chinese Quandary: Doubting Antiquity and Its Critics," *T'oung Pao* (2019), https://doi.org/10.1080/02549948.2019.1681801 (also his Harvard Fairbank lecture version: https://fairbank.fas.harvard.edu/events/doubts-about-the-chinese-current-of-doubting-antiquity-and-its-critics/).
- Kazakh scholarly analysis of Gu's method: https://kpfu.ru/uz-eng/hum/archive/uz-eng-hum-2021-6-11.html
- Oxford ORA paper "Concepts of 'Authenticity' and the Chinese Textual Heritage in Light of Excavated Texts" — excavated manuscripts (Guodian, Wangjiatai 《归藏》) rehabilitated texts long condemned as forgeries (《文子》《孔子家语》《鹖冠子》), forcing re-definition of bianweixue categories. https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:d6906f6f-c93d-47b0-a01f-5b76f27549b8
- Li Xueqin's 1993 call to "walk out of the age of doubting antiquity" 走出疑古时代 (translated: *Contemporary Chinese Thought* 34(2), 2002) — cited in Wagner.
- Yuri Pines-adjacent case study: Yuri Pines, "Dating a Pre-Imperial Text: The Book of Lord Shang," *Early China* (2016) — shows shift from whole-book authenticity verdicts to chapter-by-chapter accretion dating. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/early-china/article/dating-a-preimperial-text-the-case-study-of-the-book-of-lord-shang/199B57467F51A62EBD492DE47DA3360A

## B. Japanese manuscript studies (写本学 shahon-gaku)

### B1. 底本 teihon / 参校本 sankōhon practice; 日本古典文学大系 (Iwanami)

- UVic Libraries description: *Nihon koten bungaku taikei* 日本古典文学大系 (Iwanami Shoten, 1950s–60s, 102 vols incl. indexes; 556 works full-text searchable at NIJL: https://base1.nijl.ac.jp/~nkbthdb/) — the standard series model where each volume declares a 底本 and lists 参校本 with sigla in its 校异 apparatus. https://webapp.library.uvic.ca/databases/details.php?id=1241
- Yamagishi Tokuhei 山岸徳平《書誌学序説》(Iwanami shinsho) frames honbun seitei (本文整定, establishment of text) as the starting point of kokubungaku, surveying manuscripts/printed books Nara→Edo. https://www.iwanami.co.jp/book/b258750.html
- Osada Hikosaburo (Tokyo Woman's Christian Univ.) applies explicit Western stemmatics vocabulary (recensio/emendatio) to Edo-period mathematical manuscripts, citing Ikeda Kikan's 1941 《古典の批判的處置に關する研究》 on the Tosa Nikki as the founding Japanese exercise in modern philological criticism; notes Ikeda used the 青谿書屋本 (a 2-generation copy of Fujiwara no Tameie's tracing) as base text — vindicated when the Tameie tracing surfaced in 1984. https://www.lab.twcu.ac.jp/osada/textual_criticism_final.pdf

### B2. 校合 kōgō — traditional collation practice

- Teika's own kōgō: diary 明月記 records his Genji copy stolen (Genryaku 1225) and his subsequent collation of borrowed copies — the founding act of the Aobyōshi line (see B3).
- Edo/Meiji state project: 文部省国語調査委員会《校本万葉集》(1913–24) — national collation of Man'yōshū manuscripts; the Manyou.gr.jp database now digitizes Kan'ei-era printed base text with image-linked variants from old manuscripts ("電子万葉集校本"). https://www.manyou.gr.jp/SMAN_1/
- Buddhist canon collation: Shukusatsuban 縮刷蔵 (1880–85) teams collated Koryŏ/Song/Yuan/Ming editions reading aloud twice (on'yomi then kundoku) to catch homophone/graphic slips — documented by Bingenheimer (URL above).
- Osada's wasan restorations use 全写本校合 (collating all extant manuscripts) with majority logic and mathematical verification of numerals — a live continuation of kōgō methodology (URL above).

### B3. Genji Monogatari textual families; Ikeda Kikan 池田亀鑑《源氏物語大成》

**Three-lineage theory:** 青表紙本 Aobyōshibon (Teika line, blue-covered), 河内本 Kawachibon (Minamoto Mitsuyuki & son Chikayuki, ~1190s, emendatory), 別本 betsubon (all others). Teika's Meigetsuki records the theft of his "reliable text" (定本/shohon) in 1225 and complaints about rampant variance; Kawachi editors claimed they could resolve what Teika "could not solve" — hence Aobyōshi preserves difficult readings while Kawachi smooths them (japanesewiki/j-Wikipedia-derived summaries cross-checked against academic sources below; keywords only for wiki).

- **Ikeda Kikan's project:** began as 《校本源氏物語》 using a Kawachi-line base; the 1930s emergence of the 大島本 Oshima-bon (53 quires in Asukai Masayasu's hand) — judged "the finest extant Aobyōshi-line manuscript" — forced Ikeda to scrap drafts and switch base text; result: 《校異源氏物語》 (Chūō Kōronsha, 1942, 5 vols) and 《源氏物語大成 校異篇》 (1953–56, 8 vols), collating **25 Aobyōshi / 20 Kawachi / 16 betsubon manuscripts** out of ~300 works/~30,000 volumes surveyed (~500,000 photographs); variants tabulated separately by lineage so different text-types would not be mixed. Base texts: Teika autograph quires (Maeda/Sonkeikaku), then Oshima-bon; for 桐壺/初音/夢浮橋/浮舟 the Ikeda-bon substituted. Later appearance of the 明融臨模本 (Meiyū tracing of Teika's hand) confirmed choices. NDL digitized vol.1: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1354846 ; NDL reference-service dossier on the mid-project base-text change (Tamae Takuya & Ikeda Akira testimony): https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?id=1000050667&page=ref_view ; detailed critical study (Shibuya-style): http://www.sainet.or.jp/~eshibuya/ronbun01.html (Japanese; site encoding non-UTF, content captured via search index)
- **Modern debates:** Abe Akio argued Aobyōshibon is itself just one branch within betsubon (undermining the three-lineage scheme); Katō Yōsuke's "text-fluctuation history" (本文揺動史) and the 新編日本古典文学全集/Shinpen Genji project accept each manuscript as its own text rather than reconstructing an archetype; Ouchi Hidenori (SOKENDAI) 2006 traced common ancestor of Ikeda-bon/Takagi-bon and possible Teika corrections therein: https://ir.soken.ac.jp/records/1542 ; computational stemmatics via kana n-gram clustering successfully separates Aobyōshi vs Kawachi chapters (CiNii 2017): https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1050011097170122880
- **Setouchi Jakuchō controversy:** Setouchi's bestselling modern translation (Kōdansha 1996–98) was attacked (notably by Maruya Saiichi, whose novel *Kagayaku hi no miya* 2003 fictionalized the "lost Kumogakure chapter") over textual-fidelity claims tied to base-text choice; documented in Adrian Pinnington, "Filling in the Gaps: Maruya Saiichi, Setouchi Jakuchō, and the 'Missing Chapter' of The Tale of Genji," *Transactions of the Asiatic Society of Japan* 5th ser. 1 (2009): 55-82 (indexed at Rowley's Genji bibliography: https://www.gayerowley.com/teaching/genji-bibliography/). Tanizaki/Seidensticker authority-claims studied in "Cheating on Murasaki Shikibu" (Academia.edu 2018).
- Shibuya Eiichi's Teika-bon digital edition states editorial priority order Teika-bon > Meiyurinmo-bon > Oshima-bon > Ikeda-bon and forbids adopting later scribes' corrections unless consistent with Teika's own — a modern sankōbon rule-set (full extract saved). https://jti.lib.virginia.edu/japanese/genji/MurGenj.editorial.html

## C. Persian/Islamic philology

### C2. Kalila wa-Dimna transmission chain

Verified chain (Encyclopaedia Iranica, Dagmar Riedel, "KALILA WA DEMNA i. Redactions and circulation," Vol. XV/4, 2010): Sanskrit Pañcatantra-type material → **lost Middle Persian translation under Khosrow I Anushirvan (r. 531–79)**, attributed to the physician Borzūya/Burzōē → **Syriac translation (6th c.)** → **Arabic by Ibn al-Muqaffaʿ (~750; d. 757)** — the influential redaction-translation from which all later versions descend → New Persian (Bal'ami prose, lost; Rudaki verse fragments; **Naṣr-Allāh Munshi 1140s**, earliest dated Persian ms 1156; al-Bukhārī 1149 unique ms) → Greek (Simeon Seth, 11th c.), Old Spanish *Calila e Dimna* (Alfonso X circle, 1251), Hebrew (Joel, fl. 1200) → Latin *Directorium humanae vitae* (John of Capua, c. 1263–78; often dated 1313 in print) → European vernaculars (Doni's Italian → North's English 1570). https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kalila-demna-i/ [full extract saved]

- François de Blois, *Burzōy's Voyage to India and the Origin of the Book of Kalīlah wa Dimnah* (London: Royal Asiatic Society, 1990) — established five frame-tale variants; concludes Burzūya's authorship "can be neither proven nor falsified"; Middle Persian prototype distinct from Pañcatantra. Summarized in Iranica "BORZŪYA": https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/borzuya-also-burzoe-a-physician-of-the-time-of-kosrow-i/
- Beatrice Gruendler, *The Rise of the Arabic Book* (Harvard UP, 2020) — Abbasid book culture context (stationers, readers, collectors) into which Ibn al-Muqaffaʿ's Kalila entered. https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674987814 ; her article "Media in Flux: The Tale of the Yellow Folio from Kalīla wa-Dimna" (Refubium/FU Berlin) shows irretrievability of Ibn al-Muqaffaʿ's wording and spectrum of copyist-redactor interventions: https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/handle/fub188/33277

# TEN BULLETS — DISTINCTIVE METHODS OF THESE NON-WESTERN TEXTUAL TRADITIONS

1. **Chen Yuan's four methods form a graduated evidence hierarchy that maps cleanly onto Western philology**: 對校 duijiao ≈ examination of witnesses/recensio-collatio (purely mechanical, records sameness-vs-difference without judging truth); 本校 benjiao and 他校 tajiao ≈ internal/external loci parallel (use of the author's own usage and of quotations — cf. patristic citation in NT criticism); 理校 lijiao ≈ divinatio/emendatio ope ingenii, which Chen himself called "subtlest and most dangerous," hedged by the rule "no conjecture without firm proof."

2. **Chinese collation is error-typology-driven**: Chen's 42 "error examples" (誤例) — induced after collation, not assumed before — anticipate Maas's "transposition of errors" logic, but are domain-specific (Yuan-era Mongol loan-transcriptions, taboo characters, dynasty-specific script forms), showing a tradition that theorizes *how texts corrupt* rather than only *how they relate*.

3. **Banbenxue makes the physical edition a first-class scholarly object**: distinguishing original cut vs re-cut blocks, fine editions (善本) vs inferior ones via paper, ink, seals, knife-work ("observing wind and air") — functionally equivalent to Anglo-American descriptive/analytical bibliography but developed inside a humanist trinity (catalogues–editions–collation) serving evidential (kaozheng) scholarship rather than independent book-history.

4. **Jiyi treats the encyclopedia as an archive of lost books**: with 70–80% of Taiping Yulan's ~2,579 quoted sources now lost, quotation-compilations become the *only* witnesses to entire works (Old History of the Five Dynasties from Yongle dadian; Shennong bencao jing; Jingchu suishiji) — a reconstruction method with no close Western analogue at comparable scale, though it inherits the same dangers (abridgement, recategorization) that Western critics call indirect tradition.

5. **Bianweixui evolved from forgery-hunting to stratigraphy**: Gu Jiegang's "Doubting Antiquity" school inverted the tradition's trust, arguing ancient history accreted layer-by-layer; excavated manuscripts later rehabilitated many "forgeries," shifting the question from binary authentic/fake to models of text formation — paralleling the Homeric/Pentateuch shifts in Western scholarship but arriving via archaeology.

6. **Japanese kōgō institutionalized collaborative read-aloud collation centuries before print-standardization**: teams read base text and witnesses aloud in two pronunciations (on'yomi/kundoku) so homophonic and graphic variants surfaced separately; the same social technology collated the entire Buddhist canon (Shukusatsuban 1880–85) and still underlies digital variora (electronic 校本万葉集).

7. **The Genji tradition shows a base-text ideology (teihon/sankōbon) with lineage discipline**: Ikeda Kikan tabulated variants strictly within Aobyōshi/Kawachi/betsubon families to avoid mixing text-types — effectively stemmatic recension — yet switched base text mid-project when the superior Oshima-bon surfaced, and modern scholarship (Abe, Katō, n-gram clustering) now questions whether any archetype can be recovered, converging on "new philology" openness to mouvance.

8. **Persian editorial practice adopted Western stemmatics wholesale and localized it**: Khaleghi-Motlagh's Shahnameh follows recensio-examinatio-coniectura explicitly (45 mss screened → 15 collated → Florence 1217 as base), but Iranian critics attack precisely its Western-style eclecticism and single-base reliance (Matini 1998; Moosavi-Cheshmi; the San José manuscript shows new witnesses keep overturning decisions) — an instructive case of method transfer meeting a hyper-manuscriptized epic tradition.

9. **Kalila wa-Dimna demonstrates that "translation chains" function as textual transmission**: each link (Sanskrit → Pahlavi (lost) → Syriac/Arabic → Persian/European) is also a recension with anonymous copyist-redactors who rewrite prefaces and reorder chapters; the ERC AnonymClassic treats the Arabic "key version" as unknowable apart from its 90+ witnesses — mouvance managed not by an archetype but by segment-level synoptic editions, echoing Malik-style "open text" philology more than Maas.

10. **Malay-world editing blends recension with pragmatic eclecticism, now scaled digitally**: Roolvink's recension typology of the Sejarah Melayu (Raffles 18 vs short/long) parallels Western family division, yet standard editions openly patch lacunae from other witnesses in brackets (Kassim Ahmad filling DBP gaps from Leiden Cod.Or.1762 and Shellabear), and Proudfoot's Malay Concordance Project turns the whole edited corpus (165 texts/5.8M words) into a searchable variorum infrastructure — philology as shared data long before "digital humanities" had a name.
