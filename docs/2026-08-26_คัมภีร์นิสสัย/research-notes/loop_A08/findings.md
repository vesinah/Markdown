## Sub-topic 10: Comparative origin claims (Siamese/Burmese models vs independent development)

[VERIFIED - strongest direct claim] de Bernon, BEFEO 1992 (doc #3): regarding the Phnom Penh Buddhist Institute tradition of transcribed/vernacularized canonical texts โ€” "ce que l'on sait maintenant de **l'extrรชme similitude de cette tradition avec celle du Siam**" (the extreme similarity of this tradition with that of Siam), which he uses to argue the urban tradition's destruction was partially mitigable because Siamese parallels survive.
[VERIFIED - corroborating] Walker (2018c) "Siamese manuscripts in Cambodian collections" (in *Inventaire provisoire II*) documents actual Siamese manuscripts held in Cambodia โ€” physical channel for influence.
Walker 2022 frames all such texts as instances of a "pan-Theravada technology of the bitext" (shared Indic-vernacular technology across Burma/Siam/Lanka/Cambodia rather than unique Khmer invention), while documenting Khmer-specific formats and performance practices (chanted leporellos/smot).
[VERIFIED - comparative baseline] John Okell, "Nissaya Burmese," *Lingua* 15 (1965): 186-227, DOI 10.1016/0024-3841(65)90013-6 โ€” classic linguistic study of Burmese nissaya; Pruitt, William (1994). *รtude linguistique de nissaya birmans.* Paris: EFEO (cited in Walker 2022 biblio).
