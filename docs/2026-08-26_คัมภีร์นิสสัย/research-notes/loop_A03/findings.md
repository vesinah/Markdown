## Sub-topic 1 — Herngseng Pannabhoga, MANUSYA 26(1) 2023 ✅ FULLY VERIFIED
- **Herngseng, Ven. Pannabhoga (2023). "Burmese Nissaya Literature: A Mainstream Monastic Education Learning Method and its Salient Features."** *MANUSYA: Journal of Humanities* 26(1): 1–23. DOI: 10.1163/26659077-25010027. CC-BY 4.0. Received 9 Jul 2020, accepted 15 Jun 2021.
- Author affiliation (Crossref + Brill): Acting Dean, Shan State Buddhist University; **Head, Department of Tipitaka Studies, SSBU, Taunggyi**.
- URLs: Brill https://brill.com/view/journals/mnya/26/1/article-p1_004.xml ; Chula repository (OA mirror, blocked 403 to bots) https://digital.car.chula.ac.th/manusya/vol26/iss1/22 ; ScienceDirect mirror pii S0859992023000027.
- Abstract (fetched): earlier studies (Tin Lwin 1961; Pruitt 1992; McDaniel 2008; Clark 2015) only asked *what* a nissaya is / its genre; this paper traces how Burmese nissaya became the mainstream monastic learning method, relation to official Tipitaka translation, thesis that nissaya ≈ encyclopaedic compilation.
- Key content mined from full text:
  - Earliest attestations claimed: Sanskrit-Pyu "nissaya-type" gloss at Srikṣetra (7th c.); Kyansittha-era inscriptions; **Saṃpyaṅṭīkā/Thanbyin Ṭīkā** composed by Minister Thanbyin at court of Narapatisithu, Pagan (1174–1211) — cited as oldest grammatical nissaya attestation.
  - **Classroom practice**: teacher + *zāso* ("repeater") read nissaya aloud; students do **ane'-de** ("marking annotation in the margin") interlineating interpretations into printed Pali/aṭṭhakathā textbooks. Variants: ane' kau' (interpretation), ane' pjan' (translation), ane' pwa: (extended meaning), ane' hpwin (definition), ane' hpo (elucidation). Photo: Veluvan Monastery, Taunggyi.
  - **Seven nissaya methods** after Kāruṇika (2008): pāṭhānusārī (plain), pāṭhasesa (inserted words), etc. (list begins at text line ~456).
  - Learning sequence (quoting Dhammasami 2004:42): memorize Pali → learn nissaya → meaning via Burmese poems. "Student's intellectual level measured by how accurately he reproduces"; pure Burmese translation considered inferior/"for ordinary people", nissaya superior/scholarly (Khamphorn, citing Pannabhoga 2020:26).
  - **Modern production**: Ashin Janakābhivaṃsa (1900–1977) as 20th-c. monastic-education reformer; his **Bhāsāṭīkā** series (incl. Nissaya of Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī "Ti Kar Kyaw", Silakkhan Bhāsāṭīkā) crafted for exam needs, two difficulty levels per text, "handbooks for modern students". 19th c.: >22 nissayas on Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha alone (History of the Abhidhamma 1965); many more in 20th–21st c.
  - Comparison: Thai/Lao nissaya overlap with nāmasadda/vohāra genres (McDaniel's "lifting words"); Thai Naktham/Pariyatti curricula contain **no nissaya interpretation** (Pathompong 2016) — nissaya is distinctively Burmese-mainstream.
  - Official Tipitaka translation link: current standard Burmese translations "obviously adopted from old nissayas".
- Metrics seen on Brill page: >2,900 full-text views, >3,500 PDF downloads.

## Sub-topic 10 — Nissaya usage / state Pali examinations (Pahtamapyan) ✅ HISTORY VERIFIED; CURRENT STATISTICS GAP
From Pyi Phyo Kyaw 2015 (full text downloaded, Contemporary Buddhism 16(2), DOI 10.1080/14639947.2015.1080925):
- **First formal monastic examinations: King Thalun, 1636** (to purge service-dodgers); **Bodawpaya promoted "Pahtama-sar-taw-pyan"→"Pahtamapyan"** as sangha-control tool; **Mindon reformed format into three levels: pahtamagne (primary), pahtamalat (intermediate), pahtamagyi (advanced)**; candidates recite from memory.
- Colonial suspension after 1885 → lay associations founded 1890s: **Cetiyaṅgaṇa Pariyatti Dhammānuggaha Association** (Yangon) & **Pariyatti Sāsanahita (Sakyasīha/That-kya-thi-ha) Association** (Mandalay) holding "national examinations" (*a-myo-tha-sa-mei-pwe*) aka abhivaṃsa exams; still active.
- **Tipiṭakadhāra Selection Examinations**: oral+written over 20 canonical books plus exegesis. Memorization-first pedagogy mirrors nissaya sequence (memorize→analyze via teacher/nissaya).
- Kyaw 2012 conference paper: "Burmese Monastic Education in Contemporary Sociopolitical Contexts…curricula, motivations and roles of monastic examinations" (10th Intl Burma Studies Conf.) — follow-up lead.
- Pannabhoga 2023: exam orientation drives nissaya design (Janakābhivaṃsa case); Dhammacariya syllabus discussed (citing Dhammasami 2004:134–147).
- **Official recent candidate pass/statistics for Pathamabyan (state exams, Ministry of Religious Affairs): ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน** — no official or scholarly table retrieved; websearch outage contributed.
