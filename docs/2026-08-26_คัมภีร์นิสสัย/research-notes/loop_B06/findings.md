## Sub-topic 1 — TERM HUNT "คำหา" (kham ha)

**Verdict: ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน — "คำหา" as a genre label for glossed Pali mss is NOT attested in any Thai scholarly source found.**

Evidence of absence (negative results after targeted queries: "คำหา ใบลาน", "คำหา ล้านนา บาลี แปล คัมภีร์", RID check):
- Royal Institute Dictionary portal (dictionary.orst.go.th, HTTP 200 but search requires POST/encoding; no public hit for คำหา surfaced in any search). Wiktionary entry for หา confirms the verb exists in Kham Mueang (คำเมือง ᩉᩣ = "to seek") — i.e., หา is a common verb, not a genre term. http://th.wiktionary.org/wiki/หา
- Nearest lookalike found: SAC Manuscripts DB record RBR003-222 (สุวรรณหอยสังข์, Tham script, Pali–Lan Thai) contains colophon verses "งำหานี้ อ้ายก็มาหาน้อง..." / "ชำหานี้ อ้ายก็ไปตีเผิ้ง..." — here งำ (ngam) is a poem type and หา = "to seek"; NOT a genre label. https://manuscripts.sac.or.th/manuscript-info.php?id=614
- No hit for "kham ha manuscript" as genre in English-language scholarship either; Walker (JAOS 2020) enumerates local bitext names WITHOUT any "kham ha" (see Sub-topic 3).
- Conclusion: Phase A gap stands. Recommend dropping or heavily caveating "คำหา" in the report; use documented terms below.

## Sub-topic 2 — Alternative Thai terms VERIFIED

**Verified terms (with sources):**

1. **"วรรณกรรมนิสสัย" / "การแปลแบบนิสสัย"** — the standard Thai scholarly label.
   Quote (วารสารปณิธาน, MCU, art. on พรหมชาลสูตต์สำนวนล้านนา): "วรรณกรรมพระพุทธศาสนาในล้านนามี 3 ประเภทด้วยกัน คือ วรรณกรรมบาลี เขียนเป็นภาษาบาลีทั้งหมด วรรณกรรมนิสสัย ยกบาลีขึ้นเป็นบทตั้งแล้วแปลเป็นภาษาล้านนา และวรรณกรรมโวหาร เขียนอธิบายเป็นภาษาล้านนาทั้งหมด"; also "การแปลจะเป็นการแปลแบบนิสสัย คือ ยกบาลีขึ้นตั้งแล้วแปล". https://so05.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/panidhana/article/download/272179/182934/1118310

2. **"แปลยกศัพท์" — IN THE ROYAL INSTITUTE DICTIONARY 2554 (official).**
   Longdo mirror of RID 2554: "แปลยกศัพท์ (ก.) ยกคำบาลีขึ้นมาแปลเป็นไทยไปทีละคำ. Ex.: กรมพระปรมา[นุ]...แปลยกศัพท์ร่ายยาวมหาเวสสันดรชาดก". https://dict.longdo.com/search/แปลยกศัพท์
   Used for Lan Na texts specifically: MCU-CM journal on ปัญญาสชาดก: "ฉบับล้านนา...แปลยกศัพท์ตลอดจนจบเรื่อง โดยใช้สำนวนภาษาถิ่นล้านนา...แปลเป็นสำนวนนิสัย" https://so02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JBS/article/download/246042/166454
   Trent Walker transliterates the same Thai term as "ṕlè yak śăbd ('translating by citing words')" — a bitext where vernacular matches word-by-word style.

3. **"นิสัย/นิสสัย" as Northern Thai usage** — Brereton (JSS 81, 1993), quoting Sommai Premchit: "The word **nisai**, in this context, is a specific Northern Thai term for religious treatises written in Pali with a phrase by phrase translation into Northern Thai." (re: ms "Nisai Malai Plai"). https://thesiamsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/1993/03/JSS_081_1k_Brereton_NorthernPhraMalaiText.pdf
   Hundius likewise refers to "nissaya texts" kept at Wat Sung Men. https://digital.crossasia.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/literaryheritageoflaos01_hundius_en.pdf

4. **"คำแผลง"** — documented BUT with a different meaning than hoped: it denotes orthographic/phonetic adaptation of Pali-Sanskrit words in Lan Na writing (e.g., การใช้คำแผลงที่ประกอบด้วยไม้กั๋งไหล; สงฆ์←สงฺฆ). Journal of Northern Buddhist studies (NBJ): https://so04.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/NBJ/article/download/271715/183506/1101975 . NOT the genre term → do not use for nissaya.

5. Additional technical Thai terms from Walker JAOS 2020 (footnote 1 + body): "nisrai/nissăy (Lanna, Lao, Siamese)", "nāmaśăbd/săpº (= นามศัพท์) (Lanna, Lao, Siamese)", "cuṇṇiyapad (= ชันนียบท?) (Siamese)", interlinear annotation called in Thai "khaam hvăt or tvă kṣien" (Thai spelling uncertain from transliteration — verify before citing in Thai script). https://lockwoodonlinejournals.com/index.php/jaos/article/download/679/532 (saved to documents\Walker_2020_IndicVernacularBitexts_JAOS.pdf)
   Also Walker (Routledge Handbook of Theravāda, 2022): contemporary Thai scholars distinguish ṕlè toy byañjanaḥ (แปลโดยพยัญชนะ 'translating the letters') vs ṕlè toy aŕthaḥ (แปลโดยอรรถ 'translating the meaning'); opening verse presented then dissected may be called cuṇṇīyapad. https://www.academia.edu/77686749/

## Sub-topic 3 — DLNTM catalog terminology

Fetched About page (full text saved in conversation):
- Confirms Phase A: "The vast majority of manuscripts are bi-lingual texts in Pali and Northern Thai." (Resources page wording) https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-northern-thai-manuscripts-resources/?lang=en
- Oldest dated Tham-script ms = monolingual Pali Tiṁsati nipāta, CS 833 / CE 1471, Wat Lai Hin, Ko Kha, Lampang. Oldest dated Tham document overall = bilingual gold-leaf inscription, Sukhothai, CE 1376. (Resources page)
- Genre labels used by DLNTM/PNTMP: categories like 'Monolingual Pali' exist ("'Monolingual Pali' was used as a manuscript category in the Preservation of Northern Thai Manuscripts Project"); NO dedicated genre label equivalent to "nissaya/kham ha" was found on the public pages. Old record URLs (lannamanuscripts.net/en/manuscripts/NNNN) now redirect to the CrossAsia landing page (new search at https://iiif.crossasia.org/s/lanna); per-record genre fields could not be inspected directly — note as limitation. However Walker cites actual DLNTM/SRI titles such as "**Nissayº ṭīkā abhidhammatthasańgaha**" (ms at Wat Lai Hin Luang, Lampang, CS 947/CE 1585) and DLNTM 010720003_01 (Chiang Saen, CE 1638, author-scribe Nandapañño) showing "Nissaya" appears in ms titles themselves. https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-northern-thai-manuscripts-about/?lang=en ; JAOS PDF above.

## Sub-topic 7 — Wat Sung Men (Phrae) library project

Confirmed via DLNTM About page + Hundius intro PDF:
- Entire Wat Sung Men collection (>1,700 mss, largest in Northern Thailand) microfilmed within PNTMP (>150 rolls); digitised 2012–13; online in DLNTM.
- Khruba Kancana developed Wat Sung Men as centre of Pali/Buddhist studies c. early 19c; mobilised copying across N. Thailand AND Luang Prabang (transregional Tham network — feeds Sub-topic 9).
- Collection includes Pali works AND "nissaya texts" (Hundius intro fn.3) and the Paññāsa-Jātaka brought FROM Luang Prabang; edition team: Udom Roongruangsri, Bampen Rawin, Harald Hundius (Toyota-supported), publ. 1999 (BE 2541).

## Sub-topic 9 — Lan Na kham mueang ↔ Lao nissaya shared domain

Evidence chain:
- Same script family: "The Lan Na, Lao, Tai Lue and Tai Khuen versions of the Tham script are very similar… adapted for vernacular languages not later than the 15th century, most probably in Chiang Mai, from where it spread to neighbouring Tai-Lao kingdoms." (DLNTM Resources page, fetched)
- Khruba Kancana's copying network linked Chiang Mai region temples with Vat Vixoun, Luang Prabang (Hundius intro PDF; Colophons JPTS XIV esp. pp.34–36). Paññāsa-Jātaka physically moved LP→Phrae.
- McDaniel, "The Curricular Canon in Northern Thailand and Laos" (Manusya 5.4, 2002, DOI 10.1163/26659077-00504003): documents Dhammapada Nissaya in 16 independent "mak" sections scattered between Chiang Mai and Luang Prabang temples; Aṭṭhakathāmātikā Nissaya, Saccasarikhepa Nissaya, and a **Nāma Nissaya from Chiang Mai** used to teach Pali grammar; notes grammatical nissayas largely NOT found in Laos proper — an asymmetry worth reporting.
- Walker 2020 (JAOS PDF): Lao monastic law code Garubhaṇḍa (PLMP 06018515002_02, copied 1683) partly structured as Pali-Lao bitext; Lanna exegetical bitexts of Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā (1585 Lampang; 1638 Chiang Saen) show identical technique family.
- Walker 2022 (Routledge chapter): "Tai Lü and Tai Khün bitexts follow a similar pattern to the Lao and Lanna examples."
- Thai Wikipedia (keyword-only use): อักษรธรรมล้านนา article exists as overview. https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/อักษรล้านนา

## Sub-topic 10 — Published facsimiles/editions of Lan Na bilingual texts

1. **Rawin Bampen, Udom Roongruangsri, Harald Hundius et al., A Critical Study of Northern Thai Version of Panyasa Jataka (ปัญญาสชาดกฉบับล้านนา)**, Chiang Mai University, BE 2541/1998 (in Thai) — Lan Na version is a full แปลยกศัพท์ (word-glossed) rendering. (DLLM Bibliography + JBS article)
2. **วิโรจน์ อินทนนท์, พรหมชาลสูตต์สำนวนล้านนา** (The Print Idea, 2563/2020) — critical edition of Phra Saddhammakitti's Brahmajāla-sutta Lan Na nissaya (12 ผูก, 529 leaves; mss at Wat Na Pang Nan, Wat Duang Dee & Wat Ram Poeng CM); composed before B.E. 2358. (panidhana article)
3. **Brereton (JSS 81, 1993)**: Malai Plai ms (Wat Kittiwong copy) C.S. 878 = AD 1516, Tham script, "dual language format of Pali and Northern Thai"; pair Malai Ton–Malai Plai read as preface to Vessantara; "nisai" term attested; SRI microfilm archived. https://thesiamsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/1993/03/JSS_081_1k_Brereton_NorthernPhraMalaiText.pdf
4. **Jālikañhā-bhiseka**: Damrong Journal (Silpakorn, 2026) reports 16 Pali-only vs **39 bilingual copies** (Pali + vernacular incl. presumably Lan Na/Khom) across Thailand — strong quantitative evidence of bilingual transmission; plus JBS Chula 2021 study of Wat Bowonniwet Khom copy. https://so01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/damrong/article/view/286039 ; https://so02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/jbscu/article/view/244988
5. Maṅgala(-sutta)-related Lan Na bilingual edition: specific published facsimile of a Pali–Kham Mueang Maṅgala paritta ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน this session; closest: von Hinüber's Siam Society catalogue lists a North-Thai-script paritta ms containing Maṅgalasutta (JSS 75, 1987); Maṅgalatthadīpanī (Sirimaṅgala, CM 1524) itself widely transmitted (Kourilsky JPTS). National Library D-Library has Khom-script มังคลัตถทีปนี palm leaf: http://digital.nlt.go.th/dlib/items/show/17630?collection=64
6. Paṭhamasambodhi multilingual study (Kanok Pang-Kham?, Manusya/brill 6.1, 2003): versions in Pali + Tai vernaculars incl. Northern Thai, "translation in the style of nissaya and non-nissaya, single and dual languages". https://brill.com/view/journals/mnya/6/1/article-p11_2.xml
