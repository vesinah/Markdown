**VERIFIED.** Multiple catalog descriptions confirm the genre is the *dominant* one in Laos.

- **DREAMSEA DS 0075 00006** (concrete single-manuscript record, saved to documents\DREAMSEA_DS0075_00006_Lao_nissaya_manuscript_record.html):
  - URL: https://www.hmmlcloud.org/dreamsea/detail.php?msid=3806 (fetched via curl.exe; direct TLS from PowerShell failed)

  - "The text is used for studying Pali language. It was written in **Nissaya style, which is Pali word first and followed by Lao word as translation word by word**. This study was running at the monastic schools and later in the Pali schools in Laos during the 20th century."
  - Title: ສັບສົນ (ຜູກ 5) "Sab son (phuk 5)"; Language: Lao; Pali. IIIF manifest: https://www.vhmml.org/image/manifest/628353
- **DLLM About Collection** (https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-lao-manuscripts-about-dllm-collection/?lang=en): "a significant portion of which are bi-lingual versions, often with elaborated Pali-vernacular translations, which shed light on the local interpretation of these texts. - **Wharton, "Manuscript Literature of Laos"** (https://www.academia.edu/40578466): "The vast majority of manuscripts (**6,203 texts or almost 75 percent**) are bi-lingual, containing Pali and vernacular languages." ## Sub-topic 3: EFEO missions to Laos documenting Buddhist manuscripts

**VERIFIED.**
- EFEO Vientiane Centre official history (https://www.efeo.fr/base.php?code=231&min=345): "**In 1951** the Laotian government entrusted the EFEO with the mission to preserve and restore its historical monuments… The School's official presence in Laos led to the arrival of the art historian and philologist **Henri Deydier**, the ethnologist **Charles Archaimbault**… and **Pierre-Bernard Lafont, who resided at the Centre until 1966**." ## Sub-topic 5: McDaniel, Gathering Leaves — Laos chapters, translation genres

**VERIFIED.** Book: Justin McDaniel, *Gathering Leaves and Lifting Words: Histories of Buddhist Monastic Education in Laos and Thailand*, University of Washington Press, 2008 (Critical Dialogues in Southeast Asian Studies). ISBN 978-0-295-98848-1 / 978-0-295-98849-8. JSTOR: https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctvcwnct8
- Publisher preview PDF downloaded → documents\McDaniel_2008_Gathering_Leaves_Lifting_Words_preview.pdf (URL: https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780295989228_A26577189/preview-9780295989228_A26577189.pdf)
- Structure: Part I "Structural Mechanisms" (chs 1–3), Part II "Proximate Mechanisms" (chs 4–6), Part III "**Vernacular Landscapes**" (chs 7–8) — the translation-genre chapters.
- Quote (preview, acknowledgments): "I realized that I still had much to learn about the way **nissaya manuscripts structured and sustained the oral and textual modes of expression in Buddhist Laos and Thailand**."
- Fieldwork bases in Laos named: "Vat Mai, Vat Xainyaphum, Vat Naxai, Vat Nom Lam Chan, Vat Ong Teu, Vat Inpeng, Vat Xiangthong" (Vientiane + Luang Prabang monasteries).
- Underlying dissertation abstract (NTU Buddhist Library record https://buddhism.lib.ntu.edu.tw/en/search/search_detail.jsp?seq=127314): McDaniel 2003 Harvard diss., 549 pp., advisor Leonard van der Kuijp — "**Nissaya texts are some of the oldest bi-lingual Buddhist texts in Burma, Northern Thailand and Laos… instead of merely translations nissaya texts are actually idiosyncratic vernacular lecture notes composed and used by Buddhist monks between the 16th and early 20th centuries**… close reading of six nissayas and their related voharas and saddas… how nissayas shifted from genre to modal entity in modern Thai and Lao didactic literature and homiletics."

## Sub-topic 8: Preservation of Lao Manuscripts Programme (PLMP) reports; funders; bilingual-text mentions

**VERIFIED.**
- Funders/history (local docs: Hundius Part 2 *manuscript cultures* Nl 2, pp. 21 ff.; Hundius & Wharton JLS 2011): PLMP began 1992 at Ministry of Information and Culture/National Library; "**The German Ministry of Foreign Affairs supported the project through its cultural assistance programme, with Harald Hundius from the University of Passau as advisor**." ## Sub-topic 9: Hundius — Lan Na/Lao textual studies (Phra Malai angle)

**PARTIALLY VERIFIED.**
- Verified Hundius works touching Lan Na↔Lao textual transmission: 'The Colophons of Thirty Pāli Manuscripts from Northern Thailand', *JPTS* XIV (1990): 1–173; Rawin Bampen, Udom Roongruangsri, Harald Hundius et al., *A Critical Study of Northern Thai Version of Panyasa Jataka* (Chiang Mai Univ., BE 2541/1998, in Thai); 'Tai Manuscripts in the Dhamma Script Domain' Parts 1–2 (*manuscript cultures* newsletters — Part 2 PDF downloaded); initiator of PNTMP (Chiang Mai, German Foreign Office funding 1987–91) whose methods/model fed directly into Lao PLMP; Hundius Handwritten Collection (181 mss) in DLNTM.
- Lan Na/Lan Sang text-flow evidence from his surveys (local doc): Khruba Kancana-era copying brought Luang Prabang texts to Wat Sung Men (Phrae) — "**Along with various rare works written in Pali and nissaya texts, a complete version of the famous Paññâsa-Jâtaka was brought to Phrae**" (Hundius 2005 intro, downloaded PDF); "three complete sets of bi-lingual (Pāli-Lao) Paññāsajātaka collections… offer some revealing clues about the transmission of texts between Lan Na and Lan Sang."
- **A Hundius publication specifically on Phra Malai**: ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน. Regional Phra Malai scholarship is Brereton: "Some Comments on a Northern Phra Malai Text Dated C.S. 878 (A.D. 1516)", *JSS* 81.1 (1993): 141–145 (https://www.academia.edu/20374865) — oldest Tai-language ms, Pali + Northern Thai vernacular; *Thai Tellings of Phra Malai* (ASU 1995); Wikipedia Phra Malai: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phra_Malai

## Sub-topic 10: Scholarship explicitly using "nissaya" for Lao texts

**VERIFIED — abundant.**
1. **Lafont 1962** (BEFEO 50-2: 395–405, 'Les écritures du pāli au Laos', https://www.persee.fr/doc/befeo_0336-1519_1962_num_50_2_1538), fn. 1: "Un simple regard sur l'inventaire des manuscrits des pagodes de Luang Prabang et de Vientiane montre que très rares sont les ouvrages rédigés en pâli et que **la quasi-totalité des textes religieux sont des Nissaya**." (Earliest explicit Western identification of the Lao corpus as nissaya.)
2. **McDaniel 2003 diss. & 2008 book** — entire works on Northern Thai AND Lao nissaya (see sub-topics 5).
3. **DLLM database** — "nissaya/nisai" as Ancillary Term; Wharton 2010 paper: "complete sets of bi-lingual (Pali-Lao) **nissaya versions** of Paññāsajātaka collections" (https://www.academia.edu/40578466).
4. **Hundius & Wharton 2011** (JLS, downloaded): "translated renderings such as śabda/sap, **nissaya/nisai**, vohāra/vohan."
5. **Hundius 2005** (downloaded): research agenda — "Studies on the relationship between monolingual Pali versions of non-canonical Buddhist Jatakas, their **nissaya or wohan versions** and their secular literary adaptations… across the whole Lao 'cultural domain'."
6. **DREAMSEA catalog record DS 0075 00006** — "written in **Nissaya style**, which is Pali word first and followed by Lao word as translation word by word."
7. Related terminology: Lao *nisai* (ນິໄສ), *vohara/vohan*, *sap/śabda* as parallel translatory genres (DLLM).
