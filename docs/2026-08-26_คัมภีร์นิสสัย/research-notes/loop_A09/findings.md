## Sub-topic 1: The Sinhala "saṇṇa/saṇṇaya" genre (word-by-word Sinhala glosses of Pali texts)

**Definition (verified quote).** Wisdomlib (reprinting *Journal of the European Āyurvedic Society* article on Ravigupta's Siddhasara, Sinhala version): the MSS "fall into the category of works known as **sanna or sannayan (plural), sanne or sannaya (singular), word-for-word translations interspersed with exegetical matter**." Method described: "Generally, a sannaya of a Sanskrit or Pali verse text takes the words of each strophe in the prose order and then gives the Sinhala meaning of each word. Some words are further explained... Sometimes only the Sinhala translation is given without citing each original word..." Example given: vertical strokes separate Sanskrit words culled from ślokas and the Sinhala meaning following them.
URL: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/journal-of-the-european-ayurvedic-society/d/doc1474809.html

**Genre scope.** Sanna is applied not only to Pali canonical/commentarial texts but also Sanskrit works (e.g., Amarakośa with Sinhalese sannaya, Batuwantudawe 1880 — archive.org item amarakosa-batuvantudave; Sūrya-śatakaya sannaya, ed. Kirialle Nanavimala, Colombo 1979, cited in the same wisdomlib article).

**Terminology variants:** saṇṇaya (sing.), sannayan/sannes (pl.), "sanne" in RAS catalogue entries; also called "Pali-Sinhalese sanne" for mixed-language gloss mss.

**Modern computational evidence of the code-mixed tradition:** SiPaKosa corpus (arXiv:2603.29221, LREC-CHiPSAL 2026, DOI 10.63317/4tbdbjqmtfj6) — 786K sentences from 16 historical Sinhala/Pali books (IFBCnet library) + Tripitaka; documents pervasive Pali-Sinhala mixing in Buddhist texts written in Sinhala script.
URLs: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29221v1 ; https://github.com/RanxduG/SiPaKosa-Dataset

## Sub-topic 4: The Dhampiyā-Aṭuvā-Gätapadaya (DAG / DhpAGp)

**Nature (verified):** JSTOR 45384838, "A Short Study of the Dhampiyā-AṬuvĀ-GÄṬapadaya": "**Dhampiyä-Atuvä-Gätapadaya (DhpAGp) is a glossarial commentary in Sinhalese on the Pāli Dhammapadattha-kathā (DhpA) of Buddhaghosa**, the great Buddhist Commentator," composed under King Kassapa V. Also: "In several instances, Pāli terms, commented upon by our author, do not occur in the present editions of the DhpA ... These divergences in the Pāli portions of the Glossary will be extremely useful in the textual criticism of the Dhammapadatthakathā."
URL: https://www.jstor.org/stable/45384838 (scribd mirror: https://www.scribd.com/document/335399812/)

**Date/attribution (verified):** Asiff Hussein, "A survey of Sinhalese prose literature" (lankalibrary): "The oldest extant Sinhala prose work we have dates back to the ninth century. This is the Dhampiya-Atuva-Getapadaya, a glossary to the Dhammapadatthakatha ... compiled by King Kassapa V (913-923 A.C.)."
URL: http://www.lankalibrary.com/books/sinhala7.htm
(Ariyapala's *Society in Mediaeval Ceylon* gives attribution "Kassapa V (a.d. 929)".)

**Ganthipada/gätapada character:** It is a **gätapada (= gaṇṭhipada, "difficult words") glossary**, i.e., selective explanation of obscure Pali terms — a sibling genre to the saṇṇa rather than a running word-by-word saṇṇaya. M.B. Ariyapala (via wisdomlib OCR of *Society in Mediaeval Ceylon*): "Thus arose a series of **gatapadas or glossaries**, which are of course not very valuable as literature. The main interest in this book lies therefore in its linguistic material." And: "The Dhampiya-atuva-gatapadaya bears evidence that these [old Sinhala] commentaries were in existence in the 10th century (D. E. Hettiaratchi—Introduction to Vesaturudd-sanne, pp. 9, 10)."
URL: https://www.wisdomlib.org/history/book/society-in-mediaeval-ceylon/d/doc1897568.html (full OCR PDF: https://archive.org/details/societyinmediaev0000mbar)

**Editions:** ed. D.B. Jayatilake, Lankabhinavavisruta Press, Colombo, 1933 (per Ariyapala bibliography); modern annotated ed. D.E. Hettiarachchi (Samayawardhana, 350 pp., ISBN 978955579620): https://emars.lk/dhampiya-atuwa-gatapadhaya-aa0105.html and https://online.buddhistcc.com/books/dampiya-atuwa-gatapadaya.html
Colombo Public Library record lists Samayawardhana 1974 printing: http://sci-opac.lib.cmb.ac.lk/cgi-bin/koha/opac-detail.pl?biblionumber=16472

**Relation to lost Sinhala commentary:** academia.edu paper "Dhammapada 195/196: Two Verses Added in Sri Lanka?": "The author of DAG had the unique advantage of having before him the original heëañuvà [heḷaṭuvā] or the Sinhala commentary on which DhA claims it is based. It was his habit to compare the original and the Pali version when he glossed..."
URL: https://www.academia.edu/487742/

## Sub-topic 8: Relationship between saṇṇa and aṭuvā (අටුවා)

- **Hierarchy of exegesis** (Sinhala traditional scheme): aṭuvā (aṭṭhakathā commentary) → ṭīkā → ṭippanī → sammatha; old Sinhala aṭuvās named in tradition: Budukeli/Budu-kali Aṭuvā (Rajagala), Heḷa Aṭṭhakathā, Mahā-Aṭṭhakathā, Sīhala-Aṭṭhakathā, Kurundī-Aṭṭhakathā.
URL: https://panhinda.sirisaddharmaya.net/atuwa-teeka-tippani-and-sammatha/ (devotional site; treat claims critically)
- **Historical chain (scholarly):** Old Sinhala aṭṭhakathās (Sīhalatthakathā) underlay Buddhaghosa's 5th-c. Pali commentaries (Mahāvaṃsa-ṭīkā/Vaṃsatthappakāsinī; Buddhaghosuppatti bonfire story; Malalasekera reads it as supersession — quoted via Ariyapala OCR, wisdomlib doc1897568). After Pali replaced Sinhala as commentary medium, the **gätapadas and sannas re-glossed those Pali texts back into Sinhala**: Ariyapala — "This book, and other works of a similar nature, indicate that Pali works were extensively studied and hence it became necessary to explain in Sinhalese obscure words and passages in them. Thus arose a series of gatapadas or glossaries."
- **Family of works modelled on DAG** (Ariyapala): "**The Jataka-atuva-gatapadaya, Vesaturuda-sanne and Maha-bodhi-vamsa-gatapadaya are all modelled on the Dham-piya-atuva-gatapadaya**, but are written in a more developed language." Also: "the abundance of sannes, gätapadas, translations and poems based on [Jatakas] testifies to their great popularity." Jātaka-aṭuvā-gätapadaya ed. D.B. Jayatilake 1943; contains "a stratum as old as the Dham-piya-atuva-gatapadaya" and Dravidian influence. Vesaturu-da-sanne shown to contain an older language stratum than Jātaka-aṭuvā-gätapadaya.
- **Distinction:** aṭuvā = full running commentary (originally Sinhala, later Pali); saṇṇa = interlinear word-by-word Sinhala rendering + short exegesis; gätapada/gaṇṭhipada = glossary of difficult terms only (DAG type). The three form a continuum of vernacular access-tools to Pali.

## Cross-cutting observations for the nissaya project
1. Sinhala sanna parallels Burmese nissaya as a vernacular interlinear glossing tradition, but the Sinhala case additionally preserves the older indigenous layer: the lost Sinhala aṭṭhakathās which nissaya-type Burma/Thailand never had.
2. Genre spectrum: saṇṇa (interlinear word-by-word) ↔ gätapada/gaṇṭhipada (difficult-word glossary, e.g. DAG) ↔ full aṭuvā translation (e.g., Amāvatura style) — same functional niche as Thai/Burmese nissaya + nissaya-glossary hybrids.
3. Strongest primary-source clusters: RAS London mss (Brahmajāla sannayas), John Rylands digitised mss (Rhys Davids provenance), archive.org printed sannas (1880 Amarakośa), PTS JPTS archive (colonial ms lists, Liyanaratne 2006).
