## Sub-topic 2: The gaṇṭhipada genre — word-note glosses

**Verified lexical evidence:**
- Wisdomlib (Pali section): "gaṇṭhipada : (nt.) an obscure word; a glossary." [Concise Pali-English Dictionary]. Pali-Myanmar dictionary: "gaṇṭhi+pada... (1) a difficult/knotted word; (2) a text that clarifies difficult words (gaṇṭhi-knot)". URL: https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/ganthipada
- Related compound titles attested there: *Vinayaganthipada*, *Mahaganthipada*, *Culaganthipada*, *Majjhimaganthipada*, *Ganthipadakkama*, *Ganthipadanaya*, *Anuganthipada*, *Padaganthi*, *Dhammapadatthakathaganthipadatthadipani*. The term also appears in M. B. Ariyapala, *Society in Mediaeval Ceylon* (Lankan medieval usage).
- The Burmese lexicographic definition ("text clarifying difficult words") matches the nissaya-style glossing function; in Sri Lanka the functional equivalent genre is the **saṇṇaya** (see sub-topic 5).
- **ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน** (gap): direct scholarly description using the exact term "ganthipada" for eighteenth-century Siyam Nikaya classroom practice was not retrievable in open sources; IA full-text check of Blackburn's book for "ganthipada" was blocked (403). The verified Lankan equivalents are saṇṇaya/sūtra-saṇṇaya commentaries and vīstara glosses.

## Sub-topic 5: Siyam / Amarapura / Ramanna Nikāyas & glossed-Pali teaching

- **Siyam Nikāya**: founded 1753 when Upali Thera of Siam performed upasampadā in Kandy at the initiative of Weliwita (Välivita) Saraṇaṅkara Sangharāja (1698–1778), reign of Kīrti Śrī Rājasiṃha; chapters Malwatta & Asgiriya. Sources: Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siam_Nikaya (citing Blackburn 2001 p. 34 and Malalgoda, *Buddhism in Sinhalese Society 1750-1900*, Univ. of California Press, 1976, pp. 84–91).
- Blackburn (1997 SLJH, full text downloaded): "A striking feature of Buddhist manuscript evidence from eighteenth and nineteenth-century Sri Lanka is the large number of Sinhala-language commentaries on Pali suttas... known as *satra sannayas* [sūtra-saṇṇayas] and/or *sūtra vistara sannayas*, composed in large numbers beginning in the middle of the eighteenth century"; they "helped students study Pali language, and to develop the command of specific suttas necessary for their work as preachers"; students under Saraṇaṃkara formed a "textual community" (concept borrowed from Brian Stock). Quote shows the word-gloss style: "The Sinhala commentary begins by providing a relatively simple word gloss and explanation of this passage..."
- Historical precedent: King Parakkamabāhu II wrote a Sinhala saṇṇaya (glossary) to the Visuddhimagga, 1234–1269 CE (Dhammawheel colophon thread citing chronicles).
- **Amarapura Nikāya**: founded 1800/1802, named after Amarapura (Burma capital); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarapura_Nik%C4%81ya . **Ramanna (Rāmañña) Nikāya**: founded 12 Jun 1864 by Ambagahawatte Indrasabhawara Gnanasami after ordination in Lower Burma (Neyyadhamma Munivara Sangharāja, Ratnapunna Vihara); HQ Mūla Mahā Vihāra, Payagala; https://slrnpss.org/about.php?lang=en , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanna_Nikaya .

## Sub-topic 6: Pirivena education system

- Wikipedia "Pirivena" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirivena): monastic college system; 753 piriveṇ under Ministry of Education as of 2018; **Mulika level = 5 years, six subjects: Pali, Sanskrit, Sinhala, English, Tipitaka studies, mathematics**; declined in coastal areas under Portuguese rule but "continued to flourish in the Central Kandy Kingdom until the British conquest."
- Key institutions: Vidyodaya Pirivena (Maligakanda, Colombo; founded 1873 by Hikkaḍuvē Śrī Sumaṅgala; became Vidyodaya University 1959→Univ. of Sri Jayewardenepura) https://vidyodayapirivena.com/ ; Vidyālaṅkāra Pirivena (Peliyagoda, founded by Ratmalane Śrī Dharma­loka; → Univ. of Kelaniya 1959); Parama Dhamma Cētiya Pirivena (Ratmalana, founded 1841 by Walane Siddhartha) https://paramadhammapirivena.org/en/pirivena/ ("syllabus spans from the basic to the fifth grade, oriental examination subjects and A-level subjects").
- NIE Piriven Education Unit established 16 Mar 2017 designs curricula: https://www.nie.ac.lk/departments/department-of-pirivena-unit/
- Current status quote (*The Island*, 28 Jul 2026, symposium report): "Pirivena education remains the centre of Pali knowledge transmission, while universities represent its highest level of academic learning" (Ven. Medagampitiye Vijithadhamma). India/ICCR reprinting classical Pali grammar texts *Nāmamālā* and *Balāvatāra*: http://island.lk/ancient-pali-gets-fresh-push-from-india-and-pathfinder/

## Sub-topic 7: Burma–Lanka influence (Chapaṭa/Saddhammajotipāla)

- Mabel Bode, "Early Pali Grammarians in Burma," *JPTS* VI/3, pp. 81–101 — **PDF DOWNLOADED**: "The works usually ascribed to Saddhammajotipāla, otherwise Chapata, are: The Suttaniddesa, or Kaccayanasuttaniddesa, a grammatical treatise explaining the sūtras..." URL: https://palitextsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/JPTS_VI_3.pdf
- Encyclopedia of Buddhism "Pali literature": "_Suttaniddesa_ or _Nyāsapradīpa_ by Chapata or Saddhammajoti-pala" (commentarial works on Kaccāyana); also lists Lankan 12th-c. grammatical school: Moggallānavyākaraṇa + auto-commentary Pañcikā; Abhidhānappadīpikā by Moggallāna Thera. URL: https://encyclopediaofbuddhism.org/wiki/Pali_literature
- Direction of influence: Chapaṭa (Śrī Laṅkā-trained) went TO Pagan Burma c. late 12th c. — i.e., **Lanka → Burma transmission** of grammar pedagogy; reverse flow came later via ordination missions (Kalyāṇī inscriptions 1476; 1753 Siyam; 1800 Amarapura; 1864 Ramanna). Gornall, *Rewriting Buddhism* (downloaded, open access CC BY-NC-ND): reform-era Lanka produced new Pali literature shaping "monastic intellectual life" across Theravāda Asia; DOI `10.14324/111.9781787355156`.
- Kaccāyana textual traditions differ regionally: "The Sinhalese and Siamese versions of the Kaccāyana have 675 rules whereas the Burmese version has 673 rules omitting Sutta no. 244–245" — https://ariyajoti.wordpress.com/2020/09/18/kaccayana-grammar-and-saddaniti-grammar/
- Kirichenko, "The Itineraries of 'Sīhaḷa Monk' Sāralaṅkā: Buddhist Interactions in Eighteenth-Century Southern Asia" (2019) flagged for follow-up (Lanka-Burma monk mobility).
