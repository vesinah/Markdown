However, strong verified evidence exists for the *phenomenon* kham ha refers to
(bilingual Pali–vernacular manuscripts in Tham script):

- DLNTM Resources (EN), https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-northern-thai-manuscripts-resources/?lang=en :
  "The vast majority of manuscripts are bi-lingual texts in Pali and Northern
  Thai... A considerable number of texts, including the oldest dated texts,
  are in monolingual Pali."
- DLNTM Resources (TH), https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-northern-thai-manuscripts-resources/?lang=th :
  "คัมภีร์ใบลานส่วนใหญ่เป็นแบบสองภาษาคือ ภาษาบาลีกับภาษาล้านนา"
  (Most palm-leaf scriptures are bilingual: Pali with Lan Na.)
- Script context (same TH page): "อักษรธรรมล้านนา...ธรรมเขิน ธรรมลื้อและธรรมลาว";
  oldest dated Tham manuscript = monolingual Pali *Tiṁsati nipāta* (Jātaka-
  aṭṭhakathā-vaṇṇanā), CS 833 = พ.ศ. 2014 / CE 1471, Wat Lai Hin, Ko Kha,
  Lampang; images at http://lannamanuscripts.net/en/manuscripts/3669
  (linked from DLNTM).
- Thai Wikipedia, https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/อักษรธรรมล้านนา :
  "ตัวธัมม์ หรือ ตั๋วธรรม...ใช้สำหรับเขียน ภาษาไทยถิ่นเหนือ (คำเมือง)...รวมไปถึง
  คัมภีร์ใบลานสำหรับพระพุทธศาสนาคือ ภาษาบาลี"; colloquial name "ตั๋วเมือง"
  (Tua Mueang). Oldest Tham inscription: gold-leaf inscription of
  Mahathera Jutamuni, พ.ศ. 1919 (CE 1376), Wat Mahathat Sukhothai — bilingual
  Pali (Tham script) + Thai (Sukhothai script).

## Sub-topic 2: บาลีคำเมือง / Pali-Kham mueang translations

Verified core facts (DLNTM Resources EN + TH, URLs above):
- Bilingual Pali–Northern Thai is the dominant manuscript type in the
  6,137-manuscript DLNTM collection: "The vast majority of manuscripts are
  bi-lingual texts in Pali and Northern Thai. Other vernacular languages found
  (mostly in bi-lingual Pali-vernacular texts) are Tai Yai, Tai Lue, Tai Khuen,
  Lao, Burmese, and Thai."
- Function of the vernacular layer: "in bilingual texts, the vernacular can
  provide important information about local understanding or interpretation of
  the Pali." (EN resources page) — i.e., exactly what nissaya/kham ha studies
  analyse.
- The Pali in these mss often diverges from Kaccāyana orthodoxy (EN page).
- Thai-language scholarly apparatus for reading such texts (DLNTM Resources TH
  bibliography): โครงการ ศูนย์ส่งเสริมศิลปวัฒนธรรม มช., ข้อตกลงในการปริวรรต
  อักษรธรรมล้านนาเป็นอักษรไทยกลาง (2529); อุดม รุ่งเรืองศรี, พจนานุกรมล้านนา-ไทย
  (มช., 2547); อรุณรัตน์ วิเชียรเขียว, พจนานุกรมศัพท์ล้านนา เฉพาะคำที่ปรากฏในใบลาน (2539);
  บาลี พุทธรักษา, วรรณกรรมบาลีในล้านนา : รายชื่อเอกสารตัวเขียน 89 ฉบับ (SRI มช., 2543);
  บำเพ็ญ ระวิน, อุดม รุ่งเรืองศรี, ฮารัลด์ ฮุนดิอุส, การศึกษาเชิงวิเคราะห์ปัญญาสชาดกฉบับล้านนาไทย (มช., 2541).

## Sub-topic 4: Harald Hundius

Verified via Crossref API (https://api.crossref.org/works?query.author=Hundius&rows=15)
and the DLNTM About page:

- Hundius, H., & Wharton, D. (2010). "The Digital Library of Lao Manuscripts:
  Making the Literary Heritage of Laos Available via the Internet."
  *Microform & Imaging Review*, https://doi.org/10.1515/mfir.2010.020
- Kindlers Literatur Lexikon entries on Thai literature (2020, De Gruyter DOIs).
- DLNTM About page (URL in header) documents his career directly:
  - DELMN project: "Dokumentarische Erfassung literarischer Materialien in den
    Nordprovinzen Thailands (DELMN)... conducted by Harald Hundius from 1971 to
    1974... first comprehensive survey of Lan Na manuscripts in the eight
    northernmost provinces of Thailand" (funded by DFG); ~100 sites microfilmed.
  - PNTMP: "initiated by Harald Hundius while he was a guest lecturer and
    research adviser to help establish a Master's Degree programme in Lan Na
    Language and Literature in the Thai Department of Chiang Mai University
    from 1983 to 1992"; implemented by CMU Center for the Promotion of Arts and
    Culture 1987–1991; M.R. Rujaya Abhakorn project leader; 400+ rolls of
    microfilm incl. whole library of Wat Sung Men, Phrae (1,700+ mss).
  - HHHWC: Harald Hundius Handwritten Collection — 181 notebook-copy
    manuscripts (21,222 images) made by monks in Lamphun during DELMN.
  - He is Professor Emeritus, University of Passau; honorary doctorate from CMU (2000).
- Bibliography items listed on DLNTM Resources EN: Hundius, 'The Colophons of
  Thirty Pāli Manuscripts from Northern Thailand', JPTS XIV (1990): 1–173;
  *Phonologie und Schrift des Nordthai* (Steiner, 1990); 'Notions of Equity in
  Lan Na' (1995).

## Sub-topic 5: Chiang Mai University palm-leaf manuscript databases

Verified via DLNTM About page (https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-northern-thai-manuscripts-about/?lang=en):
- The Digital Library of Northern Thai Manuscripts (DLNTM, began 2013) hosts
  6,137 manuscripts / ~233,000+ images across four collections:
  PNTMP (3,914 mss, 159,564 images), DLNTM direct digitisation (1,051 mss,
  39,547 images), DELMN (991 mss, 13,419 images), HHHWC (181 mss, 21,222 images).
- Partners: Chiang Mai University Library (CMUL), University of Pennsylvania
  (Justin McDaniel as Project Leader), National Library of Laos, Berlin State
  Library (hosting via CrossAsia); funded by Henry Luce Foundation, German
  Federal Foreign Office, Mellon Foundation.
- "CMUL has also directly digitised a number of manuscripts, and has an online
  catalogue, the CMUL Digital Heritage Collection"
  http://library.cmu.ac.th/digital_collection/digitalheritage/
- SRI-CMU (Social Research Institute, est. 1981) catalogued/microfilmed 4,000+
  secular manuscripts; Sommai Premchit, *Lan Na Literature: Catalogue of Palm-
  leaf Texts on Microfilm at the SRI* (1986) per Resources page.
- Chiang Mai Rajabhat University's Palm Leaf Studies Centre / Institute of
  Lanna Studies also active (per About page).
- Search interface: https://iiif.crossasia.org/s/lanna (blocked to bots by
  Anubis proof-of-work during this session — human browser needed).

## Sub-topic 9: Central Siam — royal Tipitaka translation projects vs nissaya model

Verified pieces:
- Vajiranana (Prince-Patriarch) — English Wikipedia
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajirananavarorasa (live-fetched):
  - 10th Supreme Patriarch (1910–1921), son of Rama IV; Dhammayuttika leader.
  - "Vajirañana has always been very critical about the methods by which
    students learned Pāli and would later rewrite the texts for Pāli studies."
  - Founded Mahāmakuṭa Royal Academy (opened 1 Oct 1893), wrote a Pāli grammar
    and several textbooks, designed curriculum; established *Dhammacakṣu*
    journal (1894); new Dhamma-study curriculum not requiring Pali (1912).
  - Pali exams held in two modes: Mahamakuta written format vs "at the Royal
    Chapel, where the traditional oral translations would be heard" — evidence
    that oral translation remained part of elite Pali training in Siam.
  - Reference cited there: Reynolds, Craig J. (1979). *Autobiography: The life
    of Prince-Patriarch Vajiranana of Siam, 1860–1921*. Ohio Univ. Press.
- Thai Wikipedia พระไตรปิฎก (live-fetched) gives the canon structure but does
  NOT document Ayutthaya/Rattanakosin royal Thai translation campaigns.
- DLNTM Resources EN points to the key comparative source: Skilling, Peter and
  Santi Pakdeekham, *Pali and Vernacular Literature Transmitted in Central and
  Northern Siam.* Materials for the Study of the Tripitaka Vol. 2. Bangkok:
  Fragile Palm Leaves Foundation & Lumbini International Research Institute, 2004.
- Gap remains: no session-verified source yet quantifying how Rattanakosin
  royal translation projects differ from the nissaya model. Status: partial /
