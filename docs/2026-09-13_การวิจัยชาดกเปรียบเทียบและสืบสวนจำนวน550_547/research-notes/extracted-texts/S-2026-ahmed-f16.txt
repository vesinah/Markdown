Cultural Consciousness and Narrative Representation in The Circle of
Karma
Dr. Jabbar Ahmed* (Assistant Professor)
Department of English, ISBM University
Abstract:
Kunzang Choden’s novel The Circle of Karma presents a substantial portrayal of Bhutanese culture
by addressing memory, tradition, and gendered circumstances. This research paper examines the
novel through the lens of cultural consciousness and narrative representation, exploring how The
Circle of Karma functions as a literary account and a cultural repository of Bhutanese existence. The
protagonist Tsomo’s expedition demonstrates the collective experiences, beliefs, customs, and social
order of Bhutanese culture. Choden’s narrative serve as a vehicle to safeguard oral traditions and
expressing the collective experiences of women marginalized by prevailing historical and cultural
discourses. Choden argues that memory and the act of retelling are strategies of securing a legacy.
She can record the history of Bhutan and its cultural practices in a unique and intensely personal
way by blending her beliefs with those of the community. The protagonist’s journey here is
analogous to the experiences of women who negotiate between individual desires and cultural
obligations. Going beyond the narrative representation, the novel interrogates the patriarchal
values embedded in Bhutanese society. Through depictions of cultural practices, the novel spotlights
the discrimination faced by lower-class woman across Bhutanese society as a whole. The customs,
Tsomo’s defiance of them, and the gender discrimination are portrayed as conventional element. In
the process, it becomes a form of reclamation and redefinition of Tsomo’s cultural identity. The
Circle of Karma challenges the patrilineal structures ingrained in traditional culture and
reconstructs Bhutanese cultural identity by fusing memories with shared experiences. Ultimately,
this study contends that Choden’s narrative not only documents social facts and cultural practices
but also establishes a forum for self-representation, resistance, and cultural preservation in the
contemporary Bhutanese literature.
Keywords:
Cultural Consciousness, Narrative Representation, Identity, Gender and Tradition,
Collective Memory.
