- **Quotes (verbatim):**
  1. "The core text of the Jinapañjara consists of 14 verses composed in the classical Anuṣṭubh (Śloka) metre, which are virtually identical across the Siamese, Sri Lankan, and Burmese traditions."
  2. "The diversification of the tradition occurs after verse 14: the Siamese version appends a concluding verse (verse 15) in a deficient Triṣṭubh metre; the Burmese tradition concludes firmly at verse 14; while the Sri Lankan Mahājinapañjara expands significantly from verse 15 to 22, employing elaborate classical Sanskrit-derived metres such as Upajāti, Vaṃśastha, Vasantatilakā, and Sragdharā."
  3. "This metrical diversification indicates that the Sri Lankan monks subjected the popular apotropaic verse to an ornate literary revision (Kāvya), whereas the Siamese and Burmese lineages preserved the practical, ritualistic core of the protective cage."
