## 1. Rin chen bzang po biography basics

- Dates: **b. 958 – d. 1055** ("b.958 - d.1055" in header; BDRC person ID **P753**).
- Dan Martin's article title cited in bibliography confirms epithet and dates: Martin, Dan. 2008.
  "Veil of Kashmir — Poetry of Travel and Travail in Zhangzhungpa's 15th-Century Kāvya Reworking
  of the Biography of the Great Translator Rinchen Zangpo (**958-1055 CE**)," *Revue d'Etudes
  Tibétaines* 14, pp. 13–56.
- Associated institutions tagged by ToL: **Toling Monastery, Tabo, Lamayuru, Nyarma,
  Vikramaśīla** (institution links in page metadata).

  Jñānaśīla, Dharmapāla, Nāropa, Jarāndana; Tibetan students incl. Nyima Sherab, Legpai Sherab.

## 2. Kālacakra citation evidence

Primary scholarly witness: Péter-Dániel Szántó, "Minor Vajrayāna Texts II: A New Manuscript of the
Gurupañcāśikā," in Mirnig–Szántó–Williams (eds.), *Puṣpikā* vol. 1, Oxbow, 2013, pp. 443–450.
Verbatim transcription verified in `documents/szanto2013-verbatim-transmission-excerpts.md`
(transcribed during earlier loop from page images; PDF fonts non-Unicode).

p. 443 main text (exact wording):
> "Despite its brevity it played a crucial role in the history of Vajrayāna as it was widely quoted
> and debated in several important commentaries belonging to the tenth and eleventh centuries."

fn. 3 (exact wording):
> "To name but a few: the *Catuṣpīṭhanibandha* of Bhavabhaṭṭa, (the earliest of testimonia I could
> find for this work), the *Vimalaprabhā* of Puṇḍarīka, and the *Piṇḍārthatīkā* of Vajragarbha."

p. 445 (exact wording):
> "...we are preparing a new critical edition of the work including the readings of this witness,
> the *pratīkas* of the anonymous commentary, and **the large body of testimonia from
> Kālacakra-exegesis and beyond**."

This confirms the KNOWN FACTS attribution to Szántó/Sferra: the Kālacakra commentarial corpus
(Vimalaprabhā of Puṇḍarīka explicitly named; "Kālacakra-exegesis" as a class) cites the
Gurupañcāśikā as authoritative. A dedicated Brill Encyclopedia of Buddhism entry by Sferra with
further exact wording was NOT retrievable online this loop (ยังไม่พบ full BEB text); the 2013
Puṣpikā wording above is the securest citable formulation.

## 3. Indian commentators citing it

Confirmed citing works (Szántó 2013, fn. 3):
1. **Bhavabhaṭṭa, *Catuṣpīṭhanibandha*** — earliest known testimonium.
2. **Puṇḍarīka, *Vimalaprabhā*** (Kālacakra commentary).
3. **Vajragarbha, *Piṇḍārthatīkā*** (Hevajra commentary).

Only known Indic commentary (Szántó 2013, fn. 4, exact):
> "The \**Gurvārādhanapañjikā* survives only in translation (Tōh. 3722) by Gzhon nu dpal, who
> mentions in the colophon Vanaratna (1384-1468) as his master in Sanskrit."

Tsongkhapa's closing remark quoted there (Tibetan): "gzhung 'di la Rgya gar pa'i 'grel pa mi snang
yang ..." ("although I have not seen an Indian commentary on this text...").

"To name but a few," such citations are plausible but must await the announced Sferra critical

## 5. 日稱 (Rìchēng / Sūryakīrti) and the Song translation bureau

Source: "譯經：8. 宋代" (Translation of Scriptures: 8. Song Dynasty), sutrapearls.org section 8.7
(saved: `documents/sutrapearls_song_translators_ri_sheng.html`):
> "日稱，譯經僧，中印度人。宋慶曆六年（1046）至汴京（河南開封），奉敕從事譯經，賜號宣梵大師。至和三年（1056）..."

= Ri Sheng was a **Central Indian monk** who reached Kaifeng in **Qingli 6 (= 1046)**, was
commissioned by imperial edict to translate scriptures, and received the title **宣梵大師**
("Master Who Proclaims the Sanskrit [Scriptures]"). Activity window matches T1687 (1046–1078).
Szántó 2013 fn. 2 independently gives the Sanskrit name: "**Taishō no. 1687 by Sūryakīrti**"
(日稱 = Sūryakīrti, "Sun-Fame").

Bureau context: NTU Buddhist Studies FULLTEXT article "宋代的譯經潤文官與佛教":
> "宋太宗於太平興國五年(九八0)於太平興國寺西院設立譯經三堂，並於太平興國七年(九八二)完成譯經院，
> 專司譯經。至神宗元豐五年(一0八二)譯經院廢置為止"

= Translation bureau (譯經院) completed 982 at Ximing-yuan wing of Taiping-Xingguo Temple;
abolished 1082 — bracketing Ri Sheng's career exactly.

Provenance of Ri Sheng's specific Sanskrit manuscript (梵筴) for T1687: ยังไม่พบ. General pattern
documented: Song-era translations were made from Sanskrit texts brought to court by Indian monks
(the bureau's 證梵義/證梵文 offices presuppose imported Indian pandits and palm-leaf manuscripts);
T1687's own exemplar history is not recorded in accessible sources.

## 7. Nyingma canon absence

Consistent with absence, though no explicit negative statement found:
- BDRC W21518 (*rnying ma'i rgyud 'bum*, Tingkye/Gting-skyes ed., 36 v., Thimbu, Dirgo/Dingo
  Khyentse, 1975) contents described as: "man ngag a ti yo ga'i chos skor / lung a nu yo ga'i
  chos skor / bskyed pa mahA yo ga'i chos skor / thor bu'i skor" — i.e., Atiyoga/Anuyoga/
  Mahāyoga inner-tantra cycles only; no sarma śāstra such as Toh 3721.
- Standard accounts (THL catalogs; Rigpa Wiki "Nyingma Gyübum"; wisdomlib glossary) define the
  NGB as Mahāyoga+Anuyoga+Atiyoga tantras, first compiled by Ratna Lingpa because Kangyur/Tengyur
  "omitted many of the Nyingma tantras."
- GP (Toh 3721) sits in the Tengyur rgyud 'grel section translated 10th–11th c. under the
  later propagation (phyi dar) — categorically outside NGB scope.
Verdict: absence structurally certain; an explicit scholarly statement of non-inclusion: ยังไม่พบ.
