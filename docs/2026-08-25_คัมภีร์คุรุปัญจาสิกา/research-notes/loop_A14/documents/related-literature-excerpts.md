## 1. Genre context — Szántó, "Tantric Prakaraṇas" (BEB vol. I, 2015, pp. 755-761)
File: papers_2015h_prakaranas.pdf/.txt (openphilology.eu)

On Gurupañcāśikā (p. 758):
> "The Gurupañcāśikā (Fifty [Stanzas] on the Guru; Bla ma lnga bcu pa; D 3721/P 4544) is a short but very influential work already cited in the 10th century, in actual fact for the most part an anthology of scriptural verses, describing the qualities of a tantric master and how disciples should interact with him."

> "At present we are aware of only two Sanskrit manuscripts, both fragments (Lévi, 1929, also contains a French translation; Szántó, 2013a; Pandey, 1997, 33–40 misleadingly prints the entire Sanskrit text, but the latter part is a back translation from Tibetan marked only by square brackets)."

> "The manuscript studied by S. Lévi also transmits two further small works, lists of the so-called fundamental (mūlāpatti) and gross trespasses (sthūlāpatti), essentially major and minor commandments for a tantric initiate. There are several other such works deserving closer attention (a preliminary overview is presented in Davidson, 2002, 322–327). In spite of their brevity, these texts allow valuable insights into the inner workings of a tantric community."

Genre definition (p. 755):
> "Prakaraṇas are usually short or mid-length treatises on a particular aspect of doctrine and/or practice, either tacitly endorsing and promoting a viewpoint or phrased in an apologetic style; in other words, they are descriptive or argumentative, essay-style exegetical writings."

Survey contents (texts covered): Padmavajra's Guhyasiddhi; Indrabhūti's Jñānasiddhi; Jñānapāda's Ātmasādhanāvatāra; Ārya School (Pañcakrama, *Caryāmelāpakapradīpa, Cittaviśuddhiprakaraṇa, Svādhiṣṭhānakramaprabheda); Śāntarakṣita's Tattvasiddhi; Anaṅgavajra's Prajñopāyaviniścayasiddhi; *Tripiṭakamalla's *Nayatrayapradīpa; *Dharmendra's *Tattvasārasaṃgraha; *Udbhaṭa's *Mantranayāloka; **Gurupañcāśikā**; Advayavajra corpus; Vāgīśvarakīrti; Jñānakīrti's Tattvāvatāra; Jñānaśrī's *Vajrayānāntadvayanirākaraṇa; Ratnavajra's Caturthasadbhāvopadeśa; *Jñānākara's *Mantrāvatāra(+vṛtti); Jinasujayaśrīgupta's Abhiṣekanirukti; Śrīlakṣmī's Advayasiddhisādhanopāyikā; anonymous Subhāṣitasaṃgraha; anonymous Yuktipradīpa.

Master-related passages in other prakaraṇas:
- Anaṅgavajra: "The chief idea of Anaṅgavajra's work … is the importance and indispensability of a master and the initiations and instructions provided by him in order to obtain success on the tantric path."
- Śrīlakṣmī: "The practitioner is exhorted to give up external methods and focus on worshipping the guru, sexual yoga with a consort, and the cultivation of nonduality."
- Yuktipradīpa: opponents were subitists "who claimed that liberation is possible merely by having received a guru's teaching."
- NO text titled "Guru-tattva-vimśikā" or "Guruguhyasādhana" appears in this survey.

## 5. Aśvaghoṣa's Vajrayānamūlāpattisaṅgraha (Toh 2478) — Lotsawa House
File: lotsawahouse-asvaghosa-root-downfalls.html (+ series page)

Series page description verbatim:
> "This short text, which is included in the Tengyur (Toh 2478), explains the fourteen root downfalls of the Vajrayāna and became an important source for later commentaries on the samaya commitments."

Confession-version description verbatim:
> "This version of Aśvaghoṣa's text outlining the fourteen root downfalls of the Vajrayāna differs from the version in the Tengyur; in addition to some minor variations, it contains additional lines to facilitate the confession of all fourteen forms of transgression."

Text (trans. Adam Pearcey, 2018) verbatim excerpts:
> "In the language of India: Vajrayānamūlāpattisaṅgraha / In the language of Tibet: rdo rje theg pa rtsa ba'i ltung ba bsdus pa"
> "At the lotus-feet of all the buddhas / And the gurus I bow down. / Here I shall explain the fourteen root downfalls / As they are taught in the tantras."
> "The Vajradhara said that accomplishments / Come from following the teacher, / So to disparage him or her / Is explained as the first root downfall."
> "To disparage women, who are by nature wisdom, / Is the fourteenth."
> "With whatever you can afford, / Make offerings to the guru in the Sage's presence."
> "This concludes Summary of the Root Downfalls of the Vajra Vehicle composed by the great ācārya Aśvaghoṣa."
Bibliography: Beijing dpe bsdur ma, Vol. 27: 682–683.
Series page name variants listed: "Durdharṣakāla", "Bhavideva (bha bi lha)".

## 8. Tibetan guru-yoga literature (task 4)

Gray (ENBO COM-0060, via Brill search snippet recorded in loop_A03):
> "The practice of guru yoga appears to be a Tibetan innovation. South Asian tantric Buddhist traditions generally encouraged devotion to one's guru. Many tantras prescribe a devotional attitude, and this is discussed at length in a late Indian Buddhist work, the Gurupañcāśikā"

Lama Chöpa/Guru Puja authorship (LYWA, Sun of Devotion, Lama Zopa Rinpoche, ed. Gordon McDougall; files lywa-sun-of-devotion.pdf/.txt):
> "The fourth Panchen Lama, Panchen Lobsang Chökyi Gyaltsen, who composed the Guru Puja, texts on mahamudra and many other collections of teachings on sutra and tantra, also explained, If you cherish yourself, don't follow just anyone you happen to meet, like a dog seeking food in the street. Examine well the lama who reveals the holy Dharma, then follow him with respect."
Footnote 39 verbatim: "Composed by the First Panchen Lama, Lobsang Chökyi Gyaltsen (1570-1662). The outline of this text is included in Path to Bliss, by the Dalai Lama."

Tibetan teachers' framing of guru-samaya (Karmapa 2025 Day Three summary, kagyuoffice.org; saved karmapa2025-day-three.txt):
> "When we talk about the samaya of the Secret Mantra, there are tens of millions of them. However, the main aspect of samaya comes down to the guru. Gampopa said, 'When following a qualified guru, this is the ultimate samaya of the path of the Secret Mantra.'"
