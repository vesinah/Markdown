## 2. Confirmed findings with citations

### 2.1 CIHTS Sarnath — RBTRD official publication record for BLGS 1997 (mission items 2, 3)

Source: Central Institute of Higher Tibetan Studies, Rare Buddhist Texts Research Department page, https://cihts.ac.in/rbtrd/ (fetched 2026-08-25; saved: `cihts-rbtrd-page.html`).

Exact quotes:

> "Beside, this Department publishes a research journal entitled Dhih devoted to Buddhist Tantric Studies. So far 63 volumes of this journal have been published."

On editorial method (context for the Sarnath re-Sanskritization criticized by Szántó 2013):

> "...the manuscripts are collated and the Sanskrit texts are carefully compared with and supplemented by the critical editions of their classical Tibetan translation available in as many as four versions of Kagyur and Tangyur."

BLGS as team-work publication no. 12 in the completed-projects list:

> "Bauddhalaghugranthasaṃgrahaḥ (Saṁskṛta And Tibetan), 1997
> (The following 11 minor texts related to Buddhist Tantra literature are compiled under this title)"

with item 3 of the 11 being exactly:

> "Mahācārya-Aśvaghoṣakṛtā Gurupañcāśikhā (Saṁskṛta And Tibetan)"

(The other ten: Kambalāmbarapāda Aṣṭasāhasrikāprajñāpāramitāpiṇḍārtha Navaślokī; Nāgārjuna Kriyāsaṃgrahakārikā; Śabara Guhyavajravilāsinīsādhanam; Dauḍi Guhyāvalī; Vāgīśvarakīrti Tattvaratnāvaloka with auto-commentary; Nānāsiddhopadeśaḥ; Ratnākaraśānti Pañcākāraḥ; Pañcākārābhisaṁbodhiḥ; Dignāga Yogāvatāraḥ; Āryadeva Svādhiṣṭhānaprabhedaḥ.)

Trade bibliography corroboration (Aditya Prakashan, book code 11312; saved: `adityaprakashan-blgs-record.html`):

> "Bauddha laghugrantha samgraha: a collection of minor Buddhist texts (Sanskrit and Tibetan), ed. by Janardan Pandey — Edition: Sarnath — Rare Buddhist Texts Sr.; 14 — Year: 1997 — Language: SANSKRIT AND TIBETAN — Binding: Hardbound"

### 2.2 NEW FINDING — 2021 CIHTS volume containing a Hindi translation of the Gurupañcāśikā commentary (mission item 4)

Same CIHTS page (`cihts-rbtrd-page.html`), under "Details of books published individually by members of the department", list item 19:

> "Ācārya-Losaṅ-Ḍākapākṛtā Śiṣya-Sarvāśā-Paripūraṇī Gurupañcāśikā-Vyākhyā (Saṁskṛta Mūla Sahita Bhoṭa Se Hindī-Anuvāda), Saṁskṛta, Tibetan and Hindi, 2021"

Reading: *Śiṣya-sarvāśa-paripūraṇī* is the Sanskrit title of the bla ma lnga bcu pa'i rnam bshad (slob ma'i re ba kun skong) tradition already documented in loops A01/A02 (Tsong kha pa's commentary). This 2021 Sarnath volume = Sanskrit root text together with a Hindi translation made from the Tibetan ("भोट से हिंदी अनुवाद"). Caveats recorded honestly: (a) the CIHTS page attributes the vyākhyā to "Ācārya Losaṅ Ḍākapā"; the identity of this name could not be further resolved from open web sources this loop (bookseller databases carry no record); (b) whether Tsong kha pa is meant or a later/other Tibetan master requires inspection of the physical book — ยังไม่พบ confirmation.

This is the **closest confirmed Indian/Hindi-language rendering** of the Gurupañcāśikā material found in any loop so far. A standalone direct Hindi translation of the fifty root verses themselves: **ยังไม่พบ**.

### 2.3 Dhīḥ journal — volume 13 year verified = 1992 (mission item 3)

Szántó 2013 fn. 7 (already archived loop A01) states the Sarnath text edition appeared "First in Dhīḥ 13, and then reprinted in the Bauddhalaghugranthasaṃgraha, 1997. pp. 33-40." The year of Dhīḥ 13 was an open gap (A01 gap #2). Now fixed by the Biblia Impex bookseller record (https://www.bibliaimpex.com/index.php?p=sr&Field=bookcode&String=30677 ; saved: `bibliaimpex-dhih-vol13-1992.html`):

> "Title: DHIH: Journal of Rare Buddhist Texts Research Project, Vol. 13, ed. by S. Rinpoche et al — Year: 1992 — Pages etc.: various pagings, 24cm. — Binding: Paperback — Place of publication: Sarnath — Publisher: Central University of Tibetan Studies"

(Vol. 14 likewise 1992 per adjacent record 30678 — i.e., two volumes/year pace, consistent with the CIHTS internal mapping seen on the same page: "Dhīḥ 10, 1990 … Dhīḥ 24, 1997".) Editors "S. Rinpoche et al." = Samdhong Rinpoche; DSBC bibliography independently shows Dhīḥ volumes edited by "S.Rinpoche and Janardan Pandey" / "Samten, Ngawang & Janardan Pandey".

Discrepancy noted: the official RBTRD "Details of Buddhist-Sanskrit minor texts published in different issues of Dhīḥ" list on cihts.ac.in/rbtrd/ does **not** include a Gurupañcāśikhā entry (it begins with "Amanasikāra-yathāśrutakramaḥ – Dhīḥ 10, 1990"), while Szántó cites Dhīḥ 13 as its first publication venue. The CIHTS list is evidently selective; resolution requires the physical Dhīḥ 13 — flagged below.

Journal identity (Stanford SearchWorks record in00001000909): "Dhīḥ : Durlabha Bauddha grantha śodha patrikā = Journal of rare buddhist texts research."

### 2.4 Full scan of BLGS 1997 located on Internet Archive — contents and page range verified from the book itself (resolves A01 gaps #1–3 partially)

Item: "Bauddha Laghu Granth Sangraha Edited By Janardan Pandey, Durlabh Bouddh Granth Mala 14 - Kendriya Uccha Tibbati Shiksha Samstha, Varanasi", uploader eGangotri, Jangamwadi Math Collection, uploaded 2023-06-16, CC0 1.0.
URL: https://archive.org/details/EfEc_bauddha-laghu-granth-sangraha-edited-by-janardan-pandey-durlabh-bouddh-granth-ma
Metadata JSON saved (`archiveorg_blgs_1997_metadata.json`, tesseract 5.3.0 OCR, Devanagari detected conf. 0.9197) and full OCR text downloaded (`blgs-1997-archiveorg-ocr.txt`, 326 KB).

From the OCR of the actual 1997 volume:

Title page:
> "महाचार्य अश्वघोषकृता / गुरुपञ्चाशिका / GURUPANCASIKA OF MAHACARYA ASVAGHOSA"

Opening (Sanskrit, Devanagari):
> "३% नमो बुद्धाय | [OCR garble of 'ॐ नमो बुद्धाय'] … श्रीवञ्रसत्त्वपदवीप्रतिलम्भहेतो- / नत्वा यथाविधि गुरोश्चरणारविन्दम्‌ | / तत्पर्युपास्तिरमला बहुतन्त्रगीता / संक्षिप्य कथ्यत इयं शृणुतादरेण ॥ १॥"

Running headers confirm printed pagination inside the composite volume:
> "३६ / बौद्धलघुग्रन्थसंग्रहे" (p. 36) … "३९ / बौद्धलघुग्रन्थसंग्रहे" (p. 39)

Colophon (p. ~39–40; OCR partially garbled):
> "॥ इति गुरुपञ्जाशिका समास्ता ॥ [read: इति गुरुपञ्चाशिका समाप्ता] / п कृतिरियं четата ат Il [read: कृतिरियं महाचार्याश्वघोषस्य]"

The Tibetan translation follows immediately (garbled Tibetan-script OCR, running header "४२ / बौद्धलघुग्रन्थसंग्रहे").

Consequence: the Sanskrit portion occupies printed pages 33 (title) through ~40 (colophon), confirming **Szántó fn. 7 ("pp. 33-40") for the Sanskrit text**, while DILA's "pp. 33-53" evidently spans Sanskrit + Tibetan. A01 gap #3 resolved to that effect; the exact Tibetan end-page still unverified (gap remains).

### 2.6 Hindi web circulation of the text (mission items 1, 4)

advocatetanmoy.com (Advocatetanmoy Law Library, "Buddhist & Jaina Texts" category), post dated 2025-02-07 (JSON-LD datePublished 2025-02-07T19:26:59+00:00):
URL: https://advocatetanmoy.com/guru-panchashika-by-asvaghosa/ (saved: `advocatetanmoy-guru-panchashika-hindi-intro-sanskrit.html`).

Content = short Hindi introduction paragraph + the **full Sanskrit Devanagari text of all 50 verses + colophon** ("॥इति महाचार्याश्वघोषस्य गुरुपञ्चाशिका समाप्ता॥"), i.e., a popular republication of the DSBC-type recension, not a Hindi verse translation. Exact quote of the Hindi introduction:

> "गुरुपञ्चाशिका, अश्वघोष द्वारा रचित एक महत्वपूर्ण बौद्ध ग्रंथ है, जिसमें गुरु की भूमिका और महत्व पर प्रकाश डाला गया है। इसे 5वीं शताब्दी CE में लिखा गया था। ग्रंथ में गुरु को ब्रह्माण्ड के विकास और बौद्ध साधना में आवश्यक तत्व मानते हुए उनकी पूजा और सम्मान का निर्देश दिया गया है। शिष्य-गुरु संबंध की महत्ता, गुरु की शिक्षाओं के प्रति श्रद्धा और उनके बिना ज्ञान की प्राप्ति की कठिनाई को रेखांकित किया गया है। इसके अलावा, गुरु का अपमान करने वाले व्यक्तियों को कभी सुख न मिलने और नरक में जाने की चेतावनी दी गई है।"

(Note its non-academic dating claim "5वीं शताब्दी CE"; tags "800 CE" contradict the intro internally.) The same site carries a related guru-yoga text in Hindi: "Gurukriyakrama by Atisha Dipankara with Hindi Translation" (https://advocatetanmoy.com/gurukriyakrama-by-atisha-dipankara-with-hindi-translation/) — evidence of a small Hindi devotional-web corpus around guru-vidhi texts, none of it scholarly.

General Hindi search results otherwise returned only Hindu/Jain guru-bhakti stotras (Guru Paduka Stotram etc.), not this text — the Gurupañcāśikā has negligible footprint in Hindi popular space.
