## 1. TRICYCLE (tricycle.org)

### 1.1 "Unmasking the Guru" - INTERVIEW WITH BERNHARD PORKSEN [CRITIQUE - PRIMARY FIND]
- Citation: Bernhard Pörksen, interview by Ursula Richard (Buddhismus aktuell), "Unmasking the Guru," Tricycle: The Buddhist Review (online, posted 2019).
- URL: https://tricycle.org/magazine/bernhard-porksen/
- Author status: Pörksen is professor of media studies, University of Tübingen (Germany); scholar of media/digital communication.
- Exact quotes (verified from full-page fetch):
  - "I see it as a media effect. In our age of digital networking, the authority of gurus or masters— something that was unassailable until recently—has become vulnerable. Suddenly anyone can compose a petition or post a description on blogs and forums of what has been done to them. What we are experiencing at the moment in Tibetan and Japanese Buddhist groups and at the same time in yoga schools and communities around the world is an implosion of spiritual authority."
  - Q: "So that we know too much . . ." A: "Too much to keep on worshiping blindly. The new dynamic as a whole is still poorly understood, because we're too shaken by individual cases to grasp the general direction of the evolution of media. We have to understand that aura, charisma, and both spiritual and secular authority are always in part the result of successful control of information and communication—something that is becoming less possible by the day."
  - "To sum it up: the holy man has become a broken shell, the guru is a sad or pathetic or—worst-case scenario—even criminal figure. And the image of the exalted being has to compete for attention with our personal experience and the online documentation of the guru's disgrace."
  - "This kind of transparency, and particularly the empowerment of abuse victims through media access, must be welcomed unconditionally. But the question remains whether a form of radically disenchanted idealism can survive, or whether the many scandal-ridden communities, monasteries, and centers will simply fall apart, leaving behind nothing but depression and disillusionment."
- Relevance: names Tibetan Buddhist guru cases explicitly (Sogyal Lakar open letter, Sakyong Mipham allegations, #metooguru); frames guru-devotion crisis as information-control breakdown. Strong citable scholarly-journalistic hybrid.

## 2. LION'S ROAR (lionsroar.com)

### 2.1 Rob Preece, "Our Teachers Are Not Gods" [CRITIQUE - PRIMARY FIND]
- Citation: Rob Preece, "Our Teachers Are Not Gods," Buddhadharma / Lion's Roar (2017).
- URL: https://www.lionsroar.com/teachers-not-gods/
- Author status: longtime Tibetan Buddhist practitioner (student since 1973) and Jungian psychotherapist; author of The Wisdom of Imperfection (Snow Lion).
- Exact quotes (verified from full-page fetch):
  - "My idealism not only blinded me to my teacher's human fallibility but was also reinforced by the teachings. I was even given the message that to see flaws in the guru, or to criticize him, would lead to dreadful suffering. In retrospect, I see how I was tied into a belief system that acted as a powerful snare using very skillful rationale."
  - "Occasionally this can lead to bullying and even cruel and abusive behavior with students. It does not then serve any of us to simply ignore this behavior or to go into a kind of naive denial that says, 'It is my obscuration; the teacher is perfect.'"
  - "This dynamic can lead to a kind of masochistic intoxication with a teacher's abusive behavior, with the devotee justifying it as something that is all part of his or her path. I am sometimes shocked when I hear students describe how the critical, bullying way in which they are treated is a necessary part of the destruction of the ego. So often this reflects the narcissism of the teacher rather than some kind of enlightened skillful means."
  - "Sadly, unquestioning devotion toward teachers has indeed sometimes turned things rotten."
  - "because there is a taboo against criticizing the teacher, students may then find they have no one within their community to speak to about it. They may also find that their community does not really want to know. In the end, the very heart of the student's spirituality has been betrayed."
  - "If teachers do not maintain appropriate boundaries in their relationship to students, then it is for students to hold the ethical ground when teachers do not."
- CRITICAL: This article reproduces the Dalai Lama, The Path to Enlightenment passage VERBATIM (independent print confirmation of the quote previously obtained via Preece's book):
  > "The problem with the practice of seeing everything the guru does as perfect is that it very easily turns to poison for both the guru and the disciple. Therefore, whenever I teach this practice, I always advocate that the tradition of 'every action seen as perfect' not be stressed. Should the guru manifest un-dharmic qualities or give teachings contradicting dharma, the instruction on seeing the spiritual master as perfect must give way to reason and dharma wisdom. I could think to myself, 'They all see me as a Buddha, and therefore will accept anything I tell them.' Too much faith and imputed purity of perception can quite easily turn things rotten."
  Plus second attributed quote: "Too much deference actually spoils the guru." (Dalai Lama, as quoted by Preece)
- Series context: filed under Lion's Roar "Confronting Abuse of Power" cluster (links: "No More Secrets" bdwinter2014; "Confronting Abuse of Power"; "Confronting Abuse: Be Proactive").

### 3.2 David B. Gray, "Compassionate Violence? On the Ethical Implications of Tantric Buddhist Ritual" [PRIMARY FIND]
- Citation: David B. Gray (Santa Clara University), Journal of Buddhist Ethics, ISSN 1076-9005, Volume 14, 2007.
- Landing: https://blogs.dickinson.edu/buddhistethics/2010/05/10/ethical-implications-of-tantric-buddhist-ritual/
- PDF: http://blogs.dickinson.edu/buddhistethics/files/2010/05/gray-article.pdf
- Abstract quote (verified): "Buddhism is often presented as a non-violent religion that highlights the virtue of universal compassion. However, it does not unequivocally reject the use of violence, and leaves open the possibility that violence may be committed under special circumstances by spiritually realized beings. This paper examines several apologetic defenses for the presence of violent imagery and rituals in tantric Buddhist literature. It will demonstrate that several Buddhist commentators, in advancing the notion of 'compassionate violence,' also advanced an ethical double standard insofar as these violent actions as justifiable when performed by Buddhists, but condemned them when performed by non-Buddhists."
- Relevance: peer-reviewed ethical critique of tantric-apologetic logic (realized-being exemption) underpinning guru infallibility claims.

### 3.3 Charles S. Prebish, "Mahayana Ethics and American Buddhism: Subtle Solutions or Creative Perversions?"
- Citation: Charles S. Prebish, JBE, ISSN 1076-9005, Volume 20, 2013.
- Landing: https://blogs.dickinson.edu/buddhistethics/2013/09/22/mahayana-ethics-and-american-buddhism/
- PDF: http://blogs.dickinson.edu/buddhistethics/files/2013/09/Prebish-final.pdf
- Abstract quote (verified): "...uses [Keown's] distinctly Mahayana interpretation of upaya to examine the presumably scandalous behavior of Chogyam Trungpa, Rinpoche and Richard Baker, Roshi ... if abused by less realized beings, we must recognize these acts as merely creative perversions of a noble ethical heritage."
- Relevance: peer-reviewed treatment of teacher-scandal apologetics (upaya / crazy wisdom) in convert Buddhism.
