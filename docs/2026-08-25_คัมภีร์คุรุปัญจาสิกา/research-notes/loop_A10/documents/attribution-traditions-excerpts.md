=== 1. LTWA printings credit "Aryashura" (1992 2nd rev. ed.) ===
Source: Centre for Applied Buddhism library catalog record
URL: https://www.appliedbuddhism.org.uk/book/fifty-stanzas-spiritual-teacher-gurupanchashika-bla-ma-lnga-bcu-pa-2nd-rev-ed
Verbatim:
"Title: Fifty stanzas on the spiritual teacher = Gurupanchashika = Bla-ma lnga-bcu-pa. 2nd rev. ed.
 Author: Asvaghosa.
 Statement: by Aryashura; commentary by Geshe Ngawang Dhargyey prepared by the Translation Bureau
 of the Library of Tibetan Works and Archives Geshe Ngawang Dhargyey ...[et al.].
 Publisher: Dharamsala: Library of Tibetan Works & Archives    Year: 1992    ISBN: 8185102872"
Compare the 1975 edition record (same catalog):
"Fifty verses of guru-devotion = Gurupancasika = La-ma nga-chu-pa / Author: Asvaghosa /
 by Asvaghosa; prepared by the Translation Bureau ... Year: 1975"
=> The LTWA's own 1992 reprint states "by Aryashura" on its statement of responsibility,
   while the 1975/76 first editions state Aśvaghoṣa. The Paljor 2002 reprint (same ISBN)
   likewise lists author "Arya Sura" (Open Library OL9090139W, verified Loop A02).

=== 2. Gelug traditional account conflating the names (Dhargyey intro, LYWA) ===
URL: https://www.lamayeshe.com/article/fifty-verses-guru-devotion
Verbatim:
"The Fifty Verses of Guru Devotion [Skt: Gurupancashika ; Tib: Lama Nga-chu-pa] was written in
 about the first century B.C. by Ashvagosha. This Indian poet was known by many names—such as
 Aryashura, Matriceta, Patriceta, Matichitra, and Bhavideva—and was a contemporary of King
 Kaniska of the Kusan Dynasty."
=> Traditional conflation Aśvaghoṣa = Mātṛceṭa = Āryaśūra = Bhavideva, with a dating
   ("first century B.C.") contradicted by all modern scholarship.

=== 4. Foguang dacidian (佛光大辭典), s.vv. — captured verbatim in loop_A05 ===
s.v. 事師法五十頌 (p. 3042): 「全一卷。馬鳴菩薩集，宋代僧日稱等譯。收於大正藏第三十二冊。全卷由五十頌組成…此外，本頌中所出現之儀軌、灌頂、真言、護摩等名相，顯系密教用語，故作者是否確為馬鳴菩薩，尚待研考。」
s.v. 事師法五十頌釋: 「據藏譯本載，原頌為拔毗天所集，然漢譯本則謂繫馬鳴所集。」
(Re-confirmed via fodizi.tw mirror page title 「事師法五十頌 【佛光大辭典】」.)

=== 5. Karmapa 2025 teachings (Kagyu Office) — Indian commentary named ===
URL: https://kagyuoffice.org/fifty-verses-on-the-guru-day-one/ (12 March 2025)
Verbatim excerpts:
"According to the Textual Commentary on Serving the Guru (Skt. Gurvārādhana-pañjikā Tib:
 བླ་མའི་བསྙེན་བཀུར་གྱི་དཀའ་འགྲེལ།), the commentary translated from Sanskrit into Tibetan by
 Gö Lotsawa Shonnu Pal, Fifty Verses on the Guru first teaches..."
"Lord Tsongkhapa's commentary on the Fifty Verses doesn't distinguish between common and
 particular characteristics. He probably hadn't read the Sanskrit commentary, which might not
 have been translated at that time."
"Tsarchen Losal Gyatso's commentary on the Fifty Verses on the Guru titled Opening the Gateway
 to Precious Siddhi..., and Jamgön Kongtrul's Treasury of Knowledge... say there are six common
 characteristics and nine specific ones"
=> Confirms an INDIAN commentary (Gurvārādhana-pañjikā) existed and reached Tibet via
   Gö Lotsawa (15th c.), contradicting the Dhargyey-era claim of no Indian commentary;
   relevant to authorship debate because Szántó 2013 also documents Indian testimonia.
