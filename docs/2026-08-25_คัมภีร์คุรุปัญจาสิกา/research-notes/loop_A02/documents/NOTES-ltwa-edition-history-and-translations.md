=== 1. The English translation team ===
Source: Dhargyey-Fifty-Verses-Guru-Devotion-long-commentary-LYWA.txt, lines 1477-1480
("Colophon to this commentary"):
"The root text was composed by Ashvagosha in the first century BCE, translated into English by
Sharpa Tulku, Khamlung Tulku, Alexander Berzin and Jonathan Landaw, and published by the Library
of Tibetan Works and Archives in 1975. Gen Rinpoche Geshe Ngawang Dhargyey gave this commentary
at the LTWA in Dharamsala July-August 1976. It was translated [from oral Tibetan] by Losang
Gyaltsen and edited by Nicholas Ribush from his notes of the class."

Source: short commentary PDF text, lines 546-550:
"Gen Rinpoche Geshe Ngawang Dhargyey gave this oral commentary at the Library of Tibetan Works
and Archives, Dharamsala, about 1973. The translation and explanation of the root text are based
on commentaries by Lama Tsong Khapa and Pabongka Rinpoche. The root verses are not included here
but provided with the 1976 commentary. Translated and edited by Sharpa Tulku, Khamlung Tulku,
Alexander Berzin and Jonathan Landaw; published by the LTWA in 1975. This version lightly edited
by Nicholas Ribush."

=> Task-brief attribution "Geshe Ngawang Dhargyey et al." = Dhargyey is the commenting teacher;
the English translators are Sharpa Tulku, Khamlung Tulku, Alexander Berzin, Jonathan Landaw.

=== 3. Content claims from Dhargyey's commentary (traditional views, for contrast w/ scholarship)===
- p.2 (long comm.): "In Tibetan, the title of this text, Lama Nga-chu-pa, literally means
  'Fifty Lamas' because it is composed of fifty verses..."
- p.2: "There's no Indian commentary on the Fifty Verses but we have the Tibetan one by Lama
  Tsong Khapa..." (NOTE: contradicted by modern scholarship - Szántó 2013 discusses Indian
  commentaries; Rigpa Wiki: text "widely cited and debated in several important commentaries
  written in the tenth and eleventh centuries")
- Colophon p.31: "This Fifty Verses of Guru Devotion was rendered into Tibetan by the great
  translator Rinchen Zangpo and the great Indian Pandit, Padmakaravarma."
- Colophon p.31: Tsongkhapa wrote his commentary "at Reting... at the request of two great Kagyu
  lamas"; lineage of Dhargyey's transmission: Trijang Dorje Chang <- Pabongka <- ... <- Vajradhara.
- Outline appendix sourced from: "Lama Tsong Khapa's The Fulfillment of All Hopes, translated by
  Gareth Sparham" (line 1549).

=== 5. Online full translations compared (rendering choices) ===
(a) viewonbuddhism.org (saved html): verse 1 "Bowing in the proper way to the lotus feet of my
Guru... I shall condense and explain in brief what has been said in many stainless tantric texts
about Guru-devotion." Verse 3: "Three times each day... pressing your palms together, offering a
mandala as well as flowers and prostrating (touching) your head to his feet."
(b) LYWA root text inside long-commentary PDF: verse 1 identical wording ("Bowing in the proper
way...") => viewonbuddhism reproduces the LTWA 1975/76 four-translator rendering.
(c) Site checks: studybuddhism.com does NOT host it (Original Texts index checked 2026-08-25);
lotsawahouse.org Aśvaghoṣa series has only 3 other texts (Dedication Toh 4390; Root Downfalls
Toh 2478 + confession version). Note Lotsawa House lists name variants "Durdharṣakāla,
Bhavideva (bha bi lha)" for its Aśvaghoṣa page.
