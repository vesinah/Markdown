## 2. Group-by-group doctrinal summaries

**G1 (v.1) — Maṅgala and authorial commitment.**
After the external homage "Homage to Bhagavan Vajrasattva" (a prose maṅgala present in the Tibetan witness; the Sarnath Sanskrit editor substituted om namo buddhāya — see loop_A01 §8.2), v.1 bows to the guru's lotus feet as the cause of attaining the state of glorious Vajrasattva and announces that the work condenses what "many stainless tantric texts" say about guru-devotion. It functions simultaneously as namaskāra, abhidhāna (statement of purpose) and pratijñā (commitment to compose), and disclaims originality — the content is drawn from scripture, not invention.

**G2 (vv.2-3) — The universal cult of the vajra master.**
V.2 grounds the entire manual in an authority claim: all Buddhas of the three times in the ten directions have worshipped the vajrācāryas from whom they received the highest abhiṣeka (Skt incipit abhiṣekāgralabdho hi vajrācāryas tathāgataiḥ). V.3 prescribes the concrete daily protocol: thrice daily (trikālam), with supreme faith, joined palms holding flowers, maṇḍala offering, and prostration touching the head to the guru's feet. Dhargyey reads v.2 through the doctrine of three initiations (causal, pathway, resultant) ripening the mindstream.

**G3 (vv.4-5) — Vinaya-sensitive exception.**
These verses solve a normative collision: a monk or nun must not prostrate publicly to a lay (gṛhin) or newly ordained (navaka) guru, lest worldly scorn (lokāvadya) arise. The compromise is prostrating instead before such objects as the guru's scriptural texts while prostrating mentally, and performing all other services (seating, rising, obedience) fully — only prostration and unorthodox acts like foot-washing are withheld in public. Dhargyey illustrates with the Chandragomin/Chandrakirti procession story and stresses that in private the full protocol applies regardless of the guru's status.

**G4 (vv.6-9) — Mutual examination; selecting guru and disciple.**
V.6 requires prior mutual testing (prāk śiṣyācāryasaṃbandhaḥ ... parīkṣya) so that neither party's "word of honour" degenerates — Dhargyey glosses this pledge explicitly as Skt samaya. V.7 lists disqualifying traits (compassionless, wrathful, cruel, arrogant, possessive, undisciplined, boastful — niṣkṛpaṃ krodhanaṃ krūraṃ stabdhaṃ laghum asaṃyatam svotkarṣakaṃ); the Tibetan renders it disciple-side (a wise disciple should not take such a person as guru, loop_B03 §2). Vv.8-9 give the positive profile: stability, cultivation, wisdom, patience, honesty, expertise in tantra and rituals, compassion, scriptural knowledge, mastery of "both ten fields" (daśatattva), skill in drawing maṇḍalas, ability to explain tantras, faith, senses controlled. This is the block Dhargyey expounds most extensively (ten Mahāyāna qualities; ten inner and ten external fields of the vajra guru).

**G5 (vv.10-15) — Consequences of despising the guru.**
V.10 states the principle: despising one's protecting nātha equals disparaging all Buddhas, reaping continual suffering; vv.11-12 enumerate the visible calamities — epidemics and spirit-caused disease, death by demons, plague, poison, kings, fire, snakes, water, ḍākinīs, bandits, savages — culminating in hell. V.13 forbids ever disturbing the guru's mind on pain of boiling in hell; v.14 names the unobserved consequence, residence in the fearsome hells beginning with Avīci (avīcyādyā bhayānakāḥ narakā); v.15 concludes: never belittle the master who conceals his virtues (pracchanna-vara-kalyāṇa). Dhargyey supports v.14 with three tantric citations (Ornament of Vajra Essence Tantra; Heruka Root Tantra; Guhyasamaja Root Tantra — the last contrasting the five immediate negativities with belittling the guru).

**G6 (vv.16-21) — Offering, dakṣiṇā, samaya.**
V.16 gives the remedy for past disrespect: reverent offering (yatheṣṭa-dakṣiṇā) and seeking forgiveness, after which fevers and harms (jvarādayas tāpā) cease — the Chinese generalizes this verse (loop_B04 §3, PART row). V.17 escalates: for the samaya guru (Tib dam-tsig bla ma, glossed by Dhargyey) one gives wife, children, life itself, "how much more fleeting wealth." V.18 supplies the rationale: such giving confers buddhahood in one lifetime though it would otherwise be hard to attain in koṭis of kalpas; v.19 binds the disciple's triple daily duty — keep one's own samaya, worship the Tathāgatas, give to the guru who equals all Buddhas (sarvabuddhasamaḥ); v.20 directs everything pleasing, best and highest to the guru for the inexhaustible (akṣaya) state; v.21 seals the logic — what is given to the guru is given to all Buddhas, and from that merit-collection (puṇya-saṃbhāra) supreme bodhi arises.

**G7 (vv.22-23) — The guru as Vajradhara.**
V.22 defines the qualified disciple (compassion, generosity, moral self-control, patience) as one who never perceives the guru and Vajradhara as different (na anyatvaṃ kalpayanti); Dhargyey anchors this in bodhicitta motivation and the drama-mask image of one enlightened nature in many faces. V.23 extends reverence behaviorally: treading even the guru's shadow carries the same fearfulness as breaking a caitya (caityabhaṅgādibhītyā), hence a fortiori his shoes, seat and mount.

**G8 (vv.24-26) — Obedience and stewardship.**
V.24 commands joyful, wholehearted hearing of the guru's ājñā, with polite explanation when unable; v.25 states the reward-triad — from the guru come siddhi, higher rebirth (sarga/svarga) and happiness — therefore never transgress his advice. V.26 widens the object of care: guard the guru's goods like one's own life, regard his household and circle as dearest kin (the Sanskrit tad-aṅganām is softened to "retainers/attendants" in Chinese and partially in Tibetan; loop_B04 §3).

**G9 (vv.27-32) — Deportment in the guru's presence.**
A dense etiquette code: not sharing bed/seat, not walking ahead, no top-knot (uṣṇīṣa), no hands-on-hips (kaṭihasta), no outstretched feet (v.27); never sitting/lying while he stands, always ready to serve (v.28); no spitting/phlegm (śleṣman), no stretching legs, no pacing (caṅkramaṇa) or arguing before him (v.29); no rubbing limbs, singing, dancing, instruments, or loud chatter within earshot (v.30); rising, slight bow, respectful sitting, and walking ahead only at night, rivers, dangerous paths with permission (v.31); no twisting around, leaning on pillars, cracking knuckles in his sight (v.32). Dhargyey divides the block into "field of sight" (v.27-30a) and "range of hearing" (v.30b).

**G10 (vv.33-34) — Service and address.**
V.33 sanctifies physical ministrations — washing the guru's feet and body, drying, massaging — which must be framed by prostrations before and after. V.34 regulates speech: if the guru's name must be spoken, attach honorifics ("Your Presence," i.e., pūjyapāda-type epithets), and use further respectful qualifiers so that others too generate respect. (This verse survives in Sanskrit only in the re-Sanskritized portion; the new manuscript NAK 5-135 attests v.34 region textually — see loop_A01 §6.2.)

**G11 (vv.35-37) — Communication protocol and requesting teaching.**
V.35 scripts the audience: announce the purpose, listen with palms joined and undistracted mind, then answer "I shall do exactly as you have said"; v.36 requires reporting results in gentle words and covering the mouth when yawning, coughing or laughing; v.37 prescribes requesting a teaching three times, palms joined, on one knee, then sitting humbly in modest dress. The unit trains humility through formulaic speech and gesture.

**G12 (vv.38-39) — Humility and anti-display.**
V.38's celebrated simile: serve with a mind free of arrogance, timid, bashful and subdued "like a newly-wed bride" (navavadhūriva; trapayā, pāpabhītyā). V.39 bans coquettish behavior (vilāsamayīṃ ceṣṭām) before the teacher and boasting about one's services, which must be examined and discarded.

**G13 (vv.40-43) — Delegated ritual agency and reciprocity.**
V.40 forbids performing pratiṣṭhā (consecration), maṇḍala (initiation), homa (fire offering), gathering disciples or discoursing while one's own guru resides nearby, without his prior permission — the Tibetan and Chinese agree against the bsa045 reading here (loop_B04 §3, v42 row). V.41 orders all gains from such rites (e.g., "eye-opening") handed to the guru, who takes a token and returns the rest; v.42 bars acting as guru to one's own disciples, and them honoring one, in his presence; v.43 mandates two-handed giving and receiving, head slightly bent, in either direction of exchange.

**G14 (vv.44-45) — Co-disciple ethics and the sick-disciple exemption.**
V.44 demands vigilant mindfulness never to forget one's pledges and friendly mutual correction among fellow disciples (premṇā hṛṣṭena cetasā) — the community dimension Dhargyey reads as vajra-brothers/sisters. V.45 grants the single explicit exemption: illness excuses bowing and normally prohibited acts without penalty provided the mind remains virtuous (rugṇas tv akuśale citte naivaṃ bhavati pāpabhāk).

**G15 (vv.46-47) — Summary and warrant.**
V.46 compresses the whole practice: do whatever pleases the guru, avoid whatever displeases, diligently. V.47 attaches the scriptural warrant, quoting Vajradhara: "Powerful attainments follow from doing what your Guru likes" (sarvathā sarvadā siddhir ācārya-anugāminī) — making guru-pleasing the causal basis of siddhi and closing the practical core of the text.

**G16 (v.48) — Admission threshold.**
Only a disciple of pure intention (śuddhāśayo) who has taken refuge in the triratna may be given this text to internalize and follow the guru's example. Structurally this verse shifts the work from describing practice to licensing its transmission; the Chinese adds a deathbed-teaching clause not found in Sanskrit/Tibetan (loop_B04 §3, v50 row).

**G17 (v.49) — Entry into tantra.**
Having become a "vessel of the pure Dharma" (saddharma-pātra) through the preliminary trainings, the disciple receives the gift of mantra (mantrādi-dāna) and must recite aloud, retain and take to heart the fourteen root downfalls (mūlāpattīś caturdaśa; the Chinese drops the number fourteen — loop_B04 §3, v51 row). Dhargyey ties this to lam-rim prerequisites (renunciation, bodhicitta, emptiness) before initiation, and warns that initiations given to vow-breakers create hell-causes.

**G18 (v.50) — Dedication.**
The author denies having inserted personal interpretation, dedicates limitless accumulated merit so that all beings may quickly attain buddhahood, and wishes infinite benefit (kuśalapadam anantam) to disciples who follow their guru blamelessly. Metrically anomalous in the Sarnath Sanskrit (~30-31 akṣaras vs anuṣṭubh elsewhere; loop_A01 §7), it corresponds to the Tibetan longer-quarter closing stanza.

## 3. Cross-reference: the commentators' own structural remarks

### 3.1 Dhargyey long commentary — full hierarchical outline (LYWA txt lines 108-114, 455, 606-654, 1413, 1485-1547)

The extended commentary preserves a complete topical outline (chandoh-style saṃgraha) evidently inherited from the Tsongkhapa-Pabongka-Trijang lineage (the preface names Trijang Dorje Chang and Khenchen Losang Thubgyen as the transmission; line 68: "There's no Indian commentary on the Fifty Verses but we have the Tibetan one by Lama Tsong Khapa"). Its skeleton:

1. Introduction to the explanation: 1.1 Homage (v.1a); 1.2 Commitment to the undertaking (v.1b)
2. Presentation of the explanation
   - 2.1.1.1.1 BRIEF EXPLANATION: correctness of reverence (v.2); general teaching (v.3); exception (vv.4-5); reason for mutual investigation (v.6); guru to be avoided (v.7) / to be relied upon (vv.8-9)
   - 2.1.1.1.3 EXTENSIVE EXPLANATION OF HOW TO BE DEVOTED TO A GURU:
     - A. Giving up irreverence: despising, general (v.10) and specific (vv.11-12); not upsetting the guru (v.13); invisible bad consequence (v.14); summary (v.15)
     - B. How to go about being reverent — EIGHT DIVISIONS (explicit list, lines 635-644):
       (a) offering material (vv.16-21); (b) perceiving him as buddha (vv.22-23); (c) acting according to his word (vv.24-25); (d) looking after his materials and entourage (v.26); (e) purifying temporal behavior (vv.27-32, split sight/hearing); (f) offering body, speech and mind (v.33 physical; v.34 verbal); (g) abandoning pride (vv.35-39, three sub-divisions); (h) not acting according to your own wishes (vv.40-43)
   - 2.1.1.2 Discussion (v.44); 2.1.2 An exception (v.45); 2.1.3 Summary (vv.46-47)
   - 2.2 Time for explaining how to be devoted (v.48); 2.3 How to be a suitable receptacle (v.49)
3. Bringing the explanation to a conclusion (v.50): dedication verses; colophon

This indigenous outline validates my macro-grouping almost completely: G5 = its division A; G6-G13 = its eight reverences (my G9 splits its (e) only at the v.31/32 seam; my G10-G12 unpack its (f)-(g)); G14-G15 = Discussion/Exception/Summary; G16-G18 = closing frame. My only departures: (i) G2/G3 kept separate (the outline folds vv.3-5 under "brief explanation"); (ii) G7 separated vv.22-23 from the obedience pair vv.24-25, which the outline also does ((b) vs (c)).

### 3.3 Functional-structural remarks

- The text "outlines specifically how disciples should act with their guru" and "it is customarily taught before a tantric initiation is given" (short comm., p.2).
- It "was written to be recited daily so that disciples wouldn't forget the important points" (short comm., on vv.48-49) — matching Szántó's description of fifty "mnemonic stanzas."
- Tsongkhapa (quoted in both commentaries) says the work was composed for disciples who take teachings from many gurus without cultivating devotion to any — a stated rhetorical occasion for the whole composition.
- Scriptural sources named for individual verses: Samputa Tantra (v.17), Long Paramadya Commentary (v.16), Guhyasamaja/Heruka/Vajra Essence tantras (v.14) — per Tsongkhapa's Fulfillment of All Hopes as reported by Dhargyey.

## 5. Most-emphasized / most-quotable verse: assessment from local evidence

Method constraint: modern citation frequency cannot be measured directly from saved files; the best local proxies are (i) amount of commentary exposition, (ii) external testimonia reported locally.

Findings:
1. **Largest single-verse expositions in the long commentary**: v.9 (with v.8 forming a ~175-line block on the ten inner/external fields, lines 280-455) and **v.14** (~56 lines, lines 554-610, carrying three independent tantra citations plus Maitreya's Uttaratantra, Tilopa-Naropa and Gampopa quotations). By contrast v.25 receives ~23 lines (lines 891-914).
2. Between the two task-hinted candidates, therefore, **v.14 (the Avīci warning) is far more heavily emphasized than v.25** in this commentary tradition. The v.14 cluster (despise-the-guru → no siddhi even with practice → hell) is the passage the commentators arm with the most scriptural artillery, and the Guhyasamaja contrast (five immediate negativities forgivable, belittling the guru not) is its sharpest formulation.
3. At block level, vv.7-9 (qualifications) receive the greatest total exegesis — consistent with these verses being the most operationally cited in living tradition (checking a teacher before bond).
4. External datum (loop_A01 §4.1, Szántó 2013, p.443): the text "was widely quoted and debated in several important commentaries belonging to the tenth and eleventh centuries" (Catuṣpīṭhanibandha, Vimalaprabhā, Piṇḍārthatatkā); the saved extracts do not isolate WHICH verse dominates those testimonia — flagged as a Phase-C gap.
5. For the structure chapter, the safest sentence: *within the Tibetan commentarial reception preserved locally, v.14 is the most heavily amplified single verse, vv.7-9 the most amplified block, and v.47 the formal proof-text (Vajradhara quotation) for the whole discipline.*

## 6. Structural observations for the chapter (cross-tradition caveats)

1. Ring/composition logic: frame (G1 maṅgala … G18 dedication) encloses (i) worship protocol (G2-G3), (ii) contract formation (G4), (iii) prohibition + sanction (G5), (iv) positive duties in escalating order — material (G6), conceptual (G7), volitional (G8), behavioral (G9-G13), communal (G14) — then (v) recapitulation (G15), and finally (vi) a pedagogical coda regulating WHO may receive the text and WHEN (G16-G17). The commentators themselves mark the G15 joint as "Summary of the way to be devoted" and G16-G17 as the timing/receptacle section — i.e., the tradition itself reads vv.48-49 as meta-instruction, not further practice.
2. Meter: vv.1-49 anuṣṭubh throughout (all witnesses); v.50 metrically expanded in the Sarnath Sanskrit (back-translated), regular anuṣṭubh in the new manuscript and mirrored by longer Tibetan quarters (loop_A01 §7; loop_B03 §3).
3. Restoration boundary: in bsa045, vv.34-50 exist only via re-Sanskritization from Tibetan (bracketed in the edition); the new NAK 5-135 witness independently attests up to 35b and resumes at v.48 (Szántó, pp.444-445). Any verse-level claims about G10-G17 in Sanskrit phrasing must cite this status.
4. Chinese recension: 52 quatrains — vv.11-12 expanded to three (calamity list, G5), one translator-side insertion (Ch v41, "do not seek faults in the guru," thematically extending G7/G12 logic), otherwise slot-identical (loop_B04 §3). The insertion shows the fault-seeking prohibition was felt to be structurally under-represented in the Indic 50.
5. Attribution frame affects the structure chapter's framing only marginally: colophons divide between Aśvaghoṣa (NAK 3-715 + translations) and Vāpilladatta (NAK 5-135; Tib Bha bi lha) (loop_A01 §4.3).
