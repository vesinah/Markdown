## 1. Hevajra Tantra parallels (84000 Toh 417/418 — LOCAL FILE READ)

Source: local file loop_A14/documents/84000-toh417.txt (print generated 21 May 2026, v1.0.0; translation team, Wiesiek Mical trans.; CC BY-NC-ND 4.0). Degé-based; Tibetan by Gayādhara + Drokmi/Śākya Yeshé.

Terminology note: in this translation the word "guru" occurs only twice (1.6.22 and n.434, where Skt *gurum* means "thick canvas" for painting). The master is rendered "master," "teacher," "sagely master," "vajra holder/master." Numbering below follows 84000's Second Sovereign Section (= Snellgrove's Part II with different chapter mapping).

### 1a. HT 2.3.17–20 (ch.3 "The Foundation of the Entire Tantra and the Allusive Language") — disciple praises the master when requesting initiation

> [2.3.17] "The teacher should command him, 'Perform kunduru, vajra holder!' He should explain the duty of a disciple In properly requesting initiation.
>
> [2.3.18] "After seeing the master with the mudrā, The disciple should offer appropriate praise and worship: 'O Blessed One, great peace, Wholly intent on vajra yoga!
>
> [2.3.19] "'You arise from the unbroken vajra yoga That accomplishes the mudrās. Just as you are, great being, Make it so also for me, lord!
>
> [2.3.20] "'I have sunk in the thick mud of saṃsāra. Protect me, who has no refuge!' The disciple should offer delicious food and drink As well as madana and potent bala."

Parallel to GP: petitionary devotion ("protect me who has no refuge"), praise of master as refuge/bestower ("Just as you are... Make it so also for me"), offerings to the master. Register = liturgy of initiation, not a free-standing devotional poem.

### 1b. HT 2.5.65 (ch.5 "The Arising of Hevajra") — worship precedes initiation

> [2.5.65] "The initiations, as already explained, Are categorized into the master initiation and the rest. A good disciple should know that prior to that Is the prescribed praise and worship."

Confirms the ritual sequence: praise/worship of master BEFORE the four initiations — the same sequence GP presupposes.

### 1c. HT 2.8.6 (ch.8 "Discipline") — aspiration to be devoted to the master

> [2.8.6] "'In birth after birth may I be born in a good family, Keep my samaya, maintain my senses, Teach the Hevajra Tantra, be compassionate, And be devoted to my master."

Direct thematic parallel: guru-devotion tied to samaya-keeping (cf. GP v.6 samānasamayabhraṃśo doṣo hi guruśiṣyayoḥ).

### 1d. HT 1.6.22 — honor the guru or fall into Avīci

> [1.6.22] "A disciple who has obtained accomplishment And displays correct knowledge Should honor their guru with their accomplishment, To avoid falling into the Avīci Hell."

Parallel: offering one's accomplishment to the guru; hell-sanction motif (GP has similar sanction logic around disrespecting the guru).

### 1e. HT 2.9.3 — killing to protect a "buddha-like master"

> [2.9.3] "After informing one's sagely master, One should kill out of compassion Someone who harms the teachings Or brings ruin upon a buddha-like master."

Shows the master's quasi-buddha status within the Yoginī-tantra milieu contemporary with GP.

Negative finding (from loop A14, reconfirmed): the fourteen-root-downfall stanza is NOT contained in Toh 417/418 nor in Snellgrove 1959 (full-text checks for fourteen/apatti/mūlāpatti/skur-pa patterns).

## 4. Atiśa's Bodhipathapradīpa — FINDING

Full text captured: documents/lotsawahouse-atisha-lamp-path-enlightenment.raw.md (tr. Patrick Dowd 2021, Toh 3947).

**Answer to the brief:** vv.3–5 do NOT contain guru-devotion verses — they define the three scopes of beings (inferior/middling/superior). The guru material is vv.61–63 (+64–67):

> [62] "then, in order to receive empowerment from a vajra master, you must please the noble spiritual teacher through veneration, offerings of wealth and the like, and endeavouring to carry out all commands.
>
> [63] Through the complete bestowal of the vajra master empowerment by a spiritual master whom you have delighted, you will be completely purified of all sins and gain the fortune of accomplishing the siddhis."

Similarities with GP: pleasing the master before empowerment via veneration/offering/service; master as gateway to siddhis; obedience to commands (cf. GP anuvartana, v.48–50).
Differences: no guru-as-buddha deification, no etiquette catalogue, no mūlāpatti list; guru devotion appears only instrumentally, as prerequisite for tantric entry inside a lamrim frame. Atiśa (982–1054) is roughly contemporary with GP's terminus ante quem (Tibetan tr. Rin chen bzang po); BPP shows that mid-11th-c. Indian masters assumed guru-veneration norms WITHOUT citing GP — i.e., shared tantric koine rather than direct dependence. (No evidence in BPP of quotation from GP.)
