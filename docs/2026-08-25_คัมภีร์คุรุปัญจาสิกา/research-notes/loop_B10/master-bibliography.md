### 1.4 Tibetan Commentaries

1. Tsong kha pa Blo bzang grags pa (1357–1419). *Bla ma lnga bcu ba'i rnam bshad slob ma'i re ba kun skong* (Fulfilling All the Student's Hopes). No Tohoku number exists (catalogue ends at 4569; claim "D4577" REFUTED); transmitted in gsung 'bum collections only: W30084 (Lan kru'u: Kan su'u mi rigs dpe skrun khang, 1999; commentary opens vol. ka), W22273 (Labrang brang par ma blocks), W635 (New Delhi: Guru Deva, 1978), W8LS76588 (2004, root + commentary, 277 pp.). Composed at Reting (rwa sgreng) at the request of Kagyu patrons; scribe Dar ma rin chen. Scans: archive.org bdrc-W22273, bdrc-W635, bdrc-W30084 (OCR-grade Tibetan only). [loop_A11, loop_B05]
2. Grags pa rgyal mtshan (Jetsun Drakpa Gyaltsen, 1147–1216, Sakya). Earliest known Tibetan commentary (*Elucidation of the Fifty Verses of the Guru*). Modern print: *Bla ma lnga bcu dang rtsa ltung bcu bzhi'i rnam bshad*, with Chinese translation by Sonam Chozom. Lhasa: Bod ljongs mi dmangs dpe skrun khang, 2012. xii+366 pp. BDRC WA3CN6661/W3CN6661 (text lending-restricted). [loop_A11, loop_A14]
3. *Gurvārādhanapañjikā* (D3722, immediately following D3721 in the Derge Tanjur). Only Indic commentary known; survives solely in Tibetan by Gzhon nu dpal (Gö Lotsawa Zhönnu Pel, 1392–1481), who names Vanaratna (1384–1468) as his Sanskrit master. [loop_A01, loop_A13]

BDRC W3CN26522. [loop_A11, loop_A12]
