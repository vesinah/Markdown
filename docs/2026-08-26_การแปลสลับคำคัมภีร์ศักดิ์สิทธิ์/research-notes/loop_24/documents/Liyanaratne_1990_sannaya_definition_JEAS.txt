Journal: Journal of the European Ayurvedic Society, Vol. 1 (1990), pp. ca. 74-83

KEY PASSAGE (genre definition of sanna/sannaya):

"The two MSS fall into the category of works known as sanna or sannayan (plural),
sanne or sannaya (singular), word for word translations interspersed with exegetical
matter. Generally, a sannaya of a Sanskrit or Pali verse text takes the words of each
strophe in the prose order and then gives the Sinhala meaning of each word. Some words
are further explained. When this method of translation is strictly followed, one has only
to remove the Sinhala phrases and sentences to obtain the words of each strophe ... in
the prose order'. However, this is not always the case, as evidenced by the two sanna K
and W. Sometimes only the Sinhala translation is given without citing each original word,
while sometimes the original word is used without a Sinhala equivalent."

NOTES:
- Confirms sannaya applies beyond Buddhist canonical texts: here to Sanskrit Ayurvedic
  verse texts (Siddhasara/Siddhisara of Ravigupta), i.e. the genre is a general
  Indological glossing technology in Sri Lankan manuscript culture.
- Word-by-word interlinear-style glossing: source-language lemma followed by Sinhala
  equivalent; variable density of exegesis ("Some words are further explained").
- Reconstructability test: strict sannaya allows recovery of the original strophe by
  removing Sinhala glosses - analogous to nissaya conventions (Burmese) where Pali
  word order is rearranged into vernacular syntax while preserving lemmas.
- Footnote 9 cites another example: the Surya-satakaya, ed. Kirialle Nanavimala,
  Colombo 1979 (a Sanskrit panegyric with Sinhala sannaya).
- Liyanaratne was a leading scholar of Sinhala medical manuscripts (also published
  'Sinhalese medical MSS in Paris', BEFEO LXXVI, 1987).
