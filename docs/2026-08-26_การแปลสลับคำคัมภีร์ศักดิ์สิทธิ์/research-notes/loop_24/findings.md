(B) Sannaya genre definitions - OBTAINED from scholarly sources.
- Liyanaratne 1990 (JEAS 1): sanna/sannayan (pl.), sanne/sannaya (sing.) = "word for word translations interspersed with exegetical matter"; verse-text words taken in prose order then Sinhala gloss per word; strict form is reconstructible by deleting Sinhala; genre used even outside Buddhism (Ayurveda, Surya-sataka).
- Blackburn 1997 (primary theoretical treatment): sūtra sannaya defined by object (Pali suttas) and method; combines arthavivaraṇaya (elucidation of meaning; parallels padavannaṇa) and vistara kathanaya (exposition of detail; parallels atthavannaṇa); positioned between arthavyakhyanas (word-for-word) and dharmavyakhyanas (free narrative, e.g., Saddharmaratnavaliya); word-by-word/phrase-by-phrase translation PLUS narrative amplification; genre began 12th c., dormant after 15th, revived mid-18th c. by Saraṇaṃkara's Sararthadipani (1739-1747).
- Sri Lankan scholars cited within these sources as authorities on the genre: Srī Dharmakīrti (Siṅhala Sāhityaye Svarṇayugaya, Vidyalankara, 1961 - linguistic-change explanation for 12th-13th c. sannayas); Sannasgala, Siṅhala Sāhitya Vaṃsaya (1964, 492 - links sannayas to preaching); Sorata's dictionary sv. san̆na (1963); Somadasa's Nevill catalogue (1987, ix-x: "minor literary genre prior to the eighteenth century").
- Kelaniya venue found but not mined this loop: Sarada - Journal of Pali and Buddhist Studies (Dept. of Pali and Buddhist Studies, Univ. of Kelaniya; articles in Sinhala or English). Flagged as follow-up for Sinhala-language definitions.

## Key Findings

1. Structural analogy between sannaya and andemta is documentable from primary descriptions on both sides: canonical/sacred source language text (Pali / Ge'ez) followed closely by a vernacular gloss (Sinhala / Amharic), plus expanding exegetical matter (stories, etymologies, applications). Both are pedagogical instruments inside institutional curricula: sannaya in Saraṇaṃkara's four-class monastic education and preacher training (Blackburn 1997); andemta/mahafte teregwame in the EOTC traditional schools where memorizing the commentary marks the highest theological stage (Senbetu 2022).
2. Both genres sit on a spectrum between literal gloss and free vernacular exposition. Sinhala side: arthavyakyana (word-for-word) vs dharma-vyakyana (idea-only) vs sannaya as intermediate (Blackburn 1997:77-78). Ethiopian side: close tergwame (translation-commentary) vs andemta (verse-by-verse multi-option exposition) (Villa 2019, cited in Jezek 2022). Burmese side mirrors this with nissaya/namasadda (close gloss) vs vohara (extended creative commentary) (McDaniel via Herngseng 2023).
3. Distinctive feature of andemta absent from sannaya/nissaya descriptions: the formulaic andem ("and there is one who says") introducing multiple alternative interpretations without ranking them (Lee 2011; Cowley 1988; Mohammed Girma 2011; Pedersen 1995 compares Hebrew exegetical formulae). Sannaya/nissaya conventionally preserve one authoritative gloss line; andemta institutionalizes plurivocity ("four-eyed" practitioners; five contradictory readings of John 1:48 recorded).
4. Chronological asymmetry: sannaya floruits 12th c. (Sutanipata-sannaya) and mid-18th c. onward (revival under Saraṇaṃkara, tied to Siyam Nikaya formation and "indigenous modernity" before colonialism); written andemta corpus takes definitive shape in Gondarine era (17th-19th c.), printed piecemeal 19th-20th c., oral roots claimed earlier.
5. Institutional stakes parallel: Blackburn shows sutra sannayas created a "textual community" (Stock) consolidating monastic authority; Senbetu/Belay show the AC functioning as the sole authorized interpretive tradition whose questioning is treated as heresy. Both traditions tie commentary to preaching performance (bana/preaching vs church teaching).
6. Blackburn's "indigenous modernity"/anti-decline argument (18th-c. reformulation without colonial stimulus) has an Ethiopian analogue in Jezek's characterization of Ethiopian exegesis as original, contextually alive, and unsystematized - useful comparative frame for the project.
