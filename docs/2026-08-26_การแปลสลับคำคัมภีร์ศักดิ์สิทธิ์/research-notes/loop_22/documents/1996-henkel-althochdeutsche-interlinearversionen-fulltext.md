## Content summary (all from fetched text; page numbers = printed pp. 46-72)

Opening device: Henkel opens with a deliberately literalistic English interlinear-style rendering of Luke 2:8-11 ("And shepherds were in the country same...") from a 19th-c. English interlinear Bible, then cites Otfrid-connected interlinear tradition, to show that interlinear rendering is a culture-technique "Universalie" not confined to the Middle Ages (pp. 46-47). He notes modern printed interlinear Hebräisch-Deutsch/Griechisch-Deutsch Bibles (Steurer; Dietzfelbinger) still serve Bible study (n. 2).

Corpus overview (p. 47): "in einer ersten Phase, vom ausgehenden 8. bis zum beginnenden 13. Jahrhundert": two Benediktinerregel manuscripts (St. Gall and Zwiefalten), ten to the Psalter/biblical Cantica, two to Latin hymns (Murbacher Hymnen), one Interlinearversion of Sedulius' Carmen paschale (fragment found/published by M. Stählin? [footnote 5: Bodleian Auct. 2Q Ins. 1.18, dated um 1200]), one to a Brevier. Later interlinear versions exist but are not even approximately catalogued.

Core corpus used, with sigla (pp. 48-49):
- (B) Benediktinerregel, St. Gallen Cod. 916, written early 9th c., probably St. Gallen (ed. Steinmeyer, Kl. althochdt. Sprachdenkmäler)
- (APs) 'Altalemannische Psalmenübersetzung', fragments, second third of 9th c.
- (RhC) 'Rheinfränkische Cantica-Fragmente', recorded ca. 1000
- (MH) 'Murbacher Hymnen', written by a Reichenau and a Murbach scribe, first quarter of 9th c. (ed. Sievers, 2nd ed. Firchow 1972)
- (LG) St. Pauler Lukasglossen — dense, nearly continuous glossing; included because it is the one monument in this circle treated with detailed reference to its transmission context (Voetz 1985 edition)
- additionally evaluated where relevant: (C) Carmen ad Deum; (T) the OHG Tatian translation (St. Gall Cod. 56 bilingual)
- late witnesses cited: (WPs) Windberger Psalter (late 12th c.), (ZB) Zwiefaltener Benediktinerregel (early 13th c.)

Central thesis shift (pp. 49-50):
- Communis opinio (Sonderegger quote, p. 49): "Die Interlinearversion bleibt die erste und älteste Stufe in der Geschichte der deutschen Übersetzung, der erste Wegbereiter für einen zusammenhängenden volkssprachlichen Übersetzungstext, die erste tragfähige Brücke vom Lateinischen zum Deutschen."
- Counter-position: Bischoff 1971 on the Murbacher Hymnen: "Auch dienen Interlinearversionen dem Verstehen des Lateinischen, nicht dem Übersetzen in die deutsche Muttersprache."
- Henkel's own approach starts NOT from translating but from how Latin manuscripts of the 9th-13th c. were read/worked: glosses AND interlinear versions are parts of a comprehensive differentiated instrumentarium for Erschließung (expository access) of Latin texts in medieval education; glossing's primary goal is explaining (erklären), translating only one function among several. This corrects older Germanist gloss research which defined Glosse simply as translation of a Latin lemma (Vokabelübersetzung vs Kontextübersetzung).
- Comparative dimension (p. ~53): the phenomenon also appears in insular manuscripts with Old English interlinear versions (cites Gernot Wieland / Michael Lapidge-influenced functional results; footnote references an altenglische Interlinearversionen Studien-und-Textausgabe title).

Conclusion (pp. 71-72, verbatim key moves):
1. Manuscripts with OHG interlinear versions have their profile within monastic book production and education: two large groups — Sacra pagina study manuscripts (patristic/medieval exegetes, professional theologians) vs school-operation manuscripts; glossing (Latin and vernacular) clusters in the latter. A third group: the biblical text itself plus texts preserved with interlinear versions.
2. All interlinear versions up to the early 13th century are, as far as localizable, monastic, mostly Benedictine. They open up (erschließen) the central fundus of monastic liturgy (Psalter, Cantica, hymns for Stundengebet) and the Rule as the prescriptive text of cloister life.
3. The interlinear versions do NOT intend to translate these texts into the vernacular for the illitterati (as with Vaterunser, Glaube, Beichte); they aim at precise word-understanding of the normative Latin text. The Latin base text remains a syntactically coherent sense-unit; the interlinear version's elements do NOT form a horizontally coherent "text" but attach vertically, interpretament by interpretament, to each lemma.
4. From the standpoint of linguistic and literary pragmatics, interlinear versions are only the writing-fixed part of an appropriation process that included oral mediation.
5. Literary-historical evaluation must largely abandon the categories of translation performance (source/target language) and put the functional category of Erschließung des lateinischen Grundtextes at the center; literary and linguistic history are factors of an overarching bildungsgeschichtlicher process. Signed: Regensburg, NIKOLAUS HENKEL.

Methodological detail worth keeping (p. 50 n. 19): the perspective shift was already sketched in Henkel, Deutsche Übersetzungen lateinischer Schultexte (München 1988, MTU 90), esp. pp. 65-86 (on glosses, interlinear versions, Notker's Bearbeitungen).
