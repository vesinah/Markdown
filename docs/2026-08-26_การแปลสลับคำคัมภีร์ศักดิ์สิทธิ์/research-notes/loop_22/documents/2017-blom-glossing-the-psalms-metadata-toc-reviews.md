Part II = INTERLINEAR VERSIONS:
- Chapter 6: Introduction to Interlinear Versions, pp. 131-140 (DOI ...-006): 6.1 Introduction; 6.2 West Germanic Interlinear Versions; 6.3 Layout, Structure and Function of Interlinear Versions. Definition quoted by Dekker from p. 131: interlinear versions are "more or less complete word-for-word, or better form-for-form transfers into another language or register, provided interlinearly to a, generally Latin, principal text".
- Chapters 7-13: seven West Germanic test cases in chronological order of production: Ch. 7 Old Alemannic Psalter Fragments (ca. 820; OHG); Ch. 8 Vespasian Psalter (OE); Ch. 9 'Lublin/Wittenberg' Fragments (OS); Ch. 10 Regius Psalter (OE); Ch. 11 Paderborn Fragment (OS); Ch. 12 Lambeth Psalter (OE, incl. construe marks sections); Ch. 13 Old Frisian Psalter Fragment (ca. 1200).
- Chapter 14: Interlinear Versions: Conclusion, pp. 241-242 (DOI ...-014): per Dekker, concludes that chronological development did NOT cause the observed variety; cultural and geographical variables matter more; the question of 'the' purpose of interlinear psalters (read vertically vs horizontally) is rendered obsolete.

## The horizontal/vertical debate (from Dekker review, fetched in full)

Blom's Ch. 6 presents the scholarly dispute over status/function of interlinear versions: either PROTO-TRANSLATIONS whose glosses should be read 'horizontally' (appraisal via a reading process), or AIDS FOR READING the principal text, where lemma-gloss relations are 'vertical' and an important ORAL component of explanation belongs to the paratext though invisible. This maps directly onto Henkel 1996's Bischoff-derived position (Erschließung not Übersetzung) — Blom cites neither Henkel 1996 nor Bischoff 1971 in the passages Dekker summarizes (open question: check Ch. 6 footnotes for Henkel citations when library access available).
