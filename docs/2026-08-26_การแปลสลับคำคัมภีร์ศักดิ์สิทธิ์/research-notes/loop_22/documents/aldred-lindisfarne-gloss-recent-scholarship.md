## Brookes chapter — content notes (from fetched text)

Argues from script analysis that Aldred's gloss shows systematic VARIATION AND INTERVENTION: experimentation with letter-forms (incl. revival of archaic scripts such as proto-Square minuscule features and Phase II Insular minuscule traits), unusual abbreviations (4-shaped -um), haste vs neat consistency, layout constrained by Eadfrith's single-column model; engages T.J. Brown 1969, Roberts 2005, Dumville 1987, Jolly 2012, M.P. Brown 1990. Methodological relevance for gloss-as-translation studies: scribal/graphemic choices are part of Aldred's response to the Latin text, i.e., glossing practice includes visual/paratextual decision-making, not only lexical substitution.

## Assessment for the sacred_text_gloss project

Recent work frames Aldred's gloss NOT as mechanical word-for-word transcription but as layered practice: substitution/supplement/multiple glosses, construe-like interventions, script experimentation, marginal maxims, and agenda (Brown's reform reading). This converges with Henkel 1996's functional view (gloss as Erschließungsinstrument with oral dimension) and Blom 2017's vertical/horizontal discussion - useful triangulation for the project's typology.
