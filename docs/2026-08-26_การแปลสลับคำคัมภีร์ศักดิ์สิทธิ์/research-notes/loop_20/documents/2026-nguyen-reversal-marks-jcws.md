## Abstract (verbatim from Crossref deposit, ASCII-fallback for diacritics)
Mirroring the glossing traditions of literary Sinitic in Japanese kanbun kundoku and Korean seokdok kugyeol, Vietnam has also been theorized to have a similar system for vernacular interpretation of classical Chinese texts. While Japanese and Korean glossing practices have been extensively studied, evidence for Vietnamese equivalents has remained understudied. This article provides an examination of reversal marks in Vietnamese Han Nom manuscripts from the Canh Phuc Collection currently held at the University of Kyoto. Several of these manuscripts have extensive glossing in the Nom script. The reversal marks provide evidence of gloss-based reading practices, indicating a method for reordering classical Chinese syntax to match Vietnamese word order.
