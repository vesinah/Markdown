## Extracted facts (from abstract/previews seen 2026-08-25)
- "In Old Armenian translations from Greek the same Greek words and expressions could be rendered in considerably different ways."
- Core conclusion quoted in abstract: translators of the Classical period regarded the whole sentence as the unit of translation, whereas Hellenizing translators tried to translate word by word, sometimes even morpheme by morpheme.
- Chronological debate noted: "Only A. Terian, following Akinean's later opinion that all translations of the School were made in Constantinople between 590 and [610]..." placed them after 570, distinguishing a fourth group made in the first third of the 6th century connected with Step'anos Siwnec'i and his companions.
- Terian "pointed to differences in syntax between the Armenian versions of Aristotelian and pseudo-Aristotelian writings"; syntax of David the Invincible and Nonnus versions closer to second-group translations.
- Keywords listed: A.Terian; Aristotelian writings; Constantinople; Greek words; Hellenizing translators; morpheme; Old Armenian translations; pseudo-Aristotelian writings; Step'anos Siwnec'i; syntax.
- Chapter opens with comparative examples of Classical vs Hellenizing renderings "of the same Greek texts (Bible, and biblical citations in Philo of Alexandria and Timothy Aelurus)" (Brill preview text visible in search results).

## Notes (loop 16 relevance)
- This is the standard current scholarly treatment of the Armenian Hellenizing School's literal (interlinear-like, word-by-word) translation technique from Greek into Old Armenian, relevant as a parallel for any claimed "Armenian interlinear rendering of Greek scripture."
- Confirms the school's activity involved biblical texts and biblical citations, not only philosophical works.
- Author is the leading specialist on Greek loanwords/grecisms in ancient Armenian (Muradyan, Grecisms in Ancient Armenian, referenced in secondary lists seen this session).
