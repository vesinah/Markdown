## Abstract facts captured

- "Most Old Polish texts are in fact sixteenth century copies with accompanying inscribed or reinscribed glosses (interlinear and marginal) introduced into the text."
- Aim: to show how to recognize these gloss types "using elements of semantics, syntax, rhetoric and source studies" and propose a typology of adequate research tools; single-perspective analyses are easily undermined from another perspective.
- Main examples from four Old Polish apocrypha including Rozmyslanie przemyskie ('The Przemyśl Meditation'), "the most extensive monument of Polish and Slavic medieval prose"; in surviving copies "glosses are incorporated into the main body of the text."
- Claim: proper recognition of glosses "leads to a complete change of thinking about these texts and about Polish religious language in the Middle Ages in general."

## Notes

1. Documents the medieval-to-early-modern gloss practice in vernacular religious manuscripts - glosses as interpretation embedded in sacred texts - providing the historical baseline against which Lehmann-style linguistic interlinear glossing can be contrasted.
2. Methodological point relevant to the project: distinguishing genuine glosses from main-text material requires multi-dimensional analysis; glosses are editorial/interpretive layers, echoing the Leipzig Rules' dictum that glosses are analysis, not data.
