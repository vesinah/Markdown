## Notes

1. This is the canonical founding article of modern diglossia theory as referenced across the other sources gathered in this loop (e.g., Jean-Noel Robert explicitly says hieroglossia was inspired by "the neologism diglossia, coined by Charles Ferguson in 1959").
2. The standard characterization of Ferguson's H/L framework (HIGH variety used for formal/religious/literary functions vs LOW vernacular) is widely repeated online but was NOT directly quoted from the article text this session; specific internal claims about the nine defining features or the four classic case languages (e.g., Classical Arabic/colloquial Arabic, Swiss German, Modern Greek, Haitian Creole) remain UNVERIFIED until the full text is obtained.
3. Citation count (2,101 CrossRef) confirms its status as one of the most-cited papers in sociolinguistics, relevant to the sacred-text gloss project's framing of functional differentiation of language varieties.
