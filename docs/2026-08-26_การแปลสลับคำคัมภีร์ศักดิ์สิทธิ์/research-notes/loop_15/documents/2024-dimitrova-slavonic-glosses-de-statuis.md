## Abstract facts captured

- Manuscript RM 3/6, Rila Monastery: complete Church Slavonic translation of John Chrysostom's Homilies on the Statues made on Mount Athos by Serbian monk Antonije, copied by Vladislav the Grammarian in 1473; earliest extant copy of a revision of the first (10th-century Preslav) translation.
- All three preserved copies "contain a number of explanatory glosses"; the article analyzes "the 21 interlinear and marginal glosses as they occur in the Rila manuscript", divided into four overlapping groups: I. Translations and explanations of Greek words; II. Biblical references; III. Synonyms; IV. Varia.
- The annotator "was also a competent and observant editor, who usually corrected or updated the language according to his contemporary terminology."

## Notes

1. Concrete medieval case of glossing as a religious practice over a sacred-text translation chain (Greek patristic text -> Church Slavonic), showing gloss types (translation of Greek words, synonyms, biblical references) that prefigure modern interlinear conventions.
2. Shows glosses functioning as language-standardization tools ("corrected or updated the language") - connects gloss tradition to vernacular textualization processes described by Pollock's framework.
