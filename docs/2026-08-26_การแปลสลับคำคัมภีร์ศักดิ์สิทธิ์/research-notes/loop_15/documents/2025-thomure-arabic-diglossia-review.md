- Title: "A systematic review of teaching and learning in the context of Arabic diglossia"
- Journal: Social Sciences and Humanities Open, Vol. 12 (2025), article 102281. Publisher: Elsevier. Open access.
- DOI: 10.1016/j.ssaho.2025.102281
- Type: systematic review article (PRISMA)

- Full text link from record: http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590291125010125

## Abstract facts captured

- Framing: "Arabic is a diglossic language in which standard Arabic coexists with diverse spoken varieties".
- Corpus: searched eight databases plus grey literature 1970-2021, screened 927 records (Arabic and English), included 101 studies.
- Five recurring themes identified: "Diglossic distance, achievement gap, prestige and attitudes, policy, and exposure to Standard Arabic".
- Recommendations include integrating simplified standard Arabic in early childhood education and expanding home-based exposure to Standard Arabic.

## Notes

1. Demonstrates the current state-of-the-art operationalization of Ferguson's H/L model for Arabic ("diglossic distance" as measurable construct) - useful as the modern empirical endpoint for section (E) extensions.
2. The prestige/attitudes theme empirically documents the sacral prestige of the standard variety (Qur'anic association) without itself being a theology paper - a bridge between sociolinguistics and hieroglossia.
