# Source: Leipzig Glossing Rules (MPI Evolutionary Anthropology / University of Leipzig)

- Authors of rules: Bernard Comrie and Martin Haspelmath (Dept. of Linguistics, Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology) jointly with Balthasar Bickel (Dept. of Linguistics, University of Leipzig)
- Title: The Leipzig Glossing Rules: Conventions for interlinear morpheme-by-morpheme glosses
- Version status observed on page: "The rules ... (revised version of February 2008)"; page footer: "Leipzig, last change: May 31, 2015"; future updates to be managed by "the Committee of Editors of Linguistics Journals"
- Type: disciplinary standard / conventions document
- Status: FULL-FETCHED (entire rules page retrieved 2026-08-25)
- URL: https://www.eva.mpg.de/lingua/resources/glossing-rules.php

## Concrete contents observed

- Structure: ten rules for the "syntax" and "semantics" of interlinear glosses plus an appendix "lexicon" of standard abbreviation labels (from 1/2/3 person labels through ABL, ACC, DAT, ERG, IPFV, PRS, PST, SBJV, etc.).
- Rule highlights seen on page:
  - Rule 1: word-by-word vertical left alignment.
  - Rule 2: hyphens separate segmentable morphemes in both object language and gloss, same number each; equals sign marks clitic boundaries (example languages: Indonesian, Lezgian, West Greenlandic).
  - Rule 3: grammatical morphemes rendered by upper-case category labels.
  - Rule 4 (+4A-4E): one-to-many correspondences use periods; optional underscore, semi-colon, colon, backslash (for ablaut/mutation: Irish PST\break-2SG), and ">" for transitive agent>patient (Jaminjung 2DU>3SG).
  - Rule 6: non-overt elements in square brackets (Latin puer boy[NOM.SG]).
  - Rule 9: infixes in angle brackets (Tagalog b<um>ili <ACTFOC>buy); Rule 10: reduplication with tilde (Tagalog bi~bili IPFV~buy).
- Key programmatic statements quoted verbatim from the preamble:
  - "Interlinear morpheme-by-morpheme glosses give information about the meanings and grammatical properties of individual words and parts of words."
  - "Glosses are part of the analysis, not part of the data."
  - Glossing's "purpose is not to state an analysis, but to give some further possibly relevant information on the structure of a text or an example, beyond the idiomatic translation."
- "Important references" listed by the page itself: Lehmann, Christian. 1982. "Directions for interlinear morphemic translations". Folia Linguistica 16: 199-224; and Croft 2003, Typology and universals, 2nd ed., pp. xix-xxv.
- Internal inconsistency worth noting: the page body cites "Lehmann 1982:211" for Hittite examples, while its own References list gives "Lehmann, Christian. 1983. 'Directions for interlinear morphemic translations'. Folia Linguistica 16: 193-224." (Lehmann's own publication list this session confirms Folia Linguistica 16, 1982:193-224.)

## Notes

1. This document is the current de facto international standard for interlinear morphemic glossing, co-produced by MPI EVA and University of Leipzig - directly usable as the "modern linguistic gloss" endpoint in any genealogy from the medieval gloss tradition.
2. Its explicit claim that glosses are analysis rather than data provides a theoretical bridge to philological glossing practice (glosses as interpretation layered over a fixed sacred text).
