## Contents observed on publisher page

- Abstract: Pollock traces two great transformations of Sanskrit: "around the beginning of the Common Era, when Sanskrit, long a sacred language, was reinvented as a code for literary and political expression", spreading "from Afghanistan to Java"; and "around the beginning of the second millennium, when local speech forms challenged and eventually replaced Sanskrit in both the literary and political arenas." Parallels drawn with Latin literature/Roman empire and late-medieval European vernaculars/nation-states.
- Structure (from TOC): Part 1 "The Sanskrit Cosmopolis" (chapters incl. ch.1 "The Language of the Gods Enters the World"; ch.7 "A European Countercosmopolis" with sections Latinitas, Imperium Romanum); Part 2 "The Vernacular Millenium" (ch.8 "Beginnings, Textualization, Superposition"; ch.9 "Creating a Regional World: The Case of Kannada" incl. section "Vernacularization and Political Inscription"; ch.10 "Vernacular Poetries and Polities in Southern Asia" incl. section "Religion and Vernacularization"; ch.11 "Europe Vernacularized"); Part 3 theory chapters; Epilogue "From Cosmopolitan-or-Vernacular to Cosmopolitan-and-Vernacular".
- Awards listed: Coomaraswamy Book Prize 2008 (Association for Asian Studies); Lionel Trilling Award 2007 (Columbia College); PSP Award for Excellence 2006.
