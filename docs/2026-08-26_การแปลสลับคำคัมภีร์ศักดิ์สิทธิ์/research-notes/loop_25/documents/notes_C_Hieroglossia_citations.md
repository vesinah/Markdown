CORE CONCEPTS CAPTURED FROM PDF:
- hieroglossia = sum of relations between a central/founding language of a culture area (the HIEROGLOSS, usually a "dead"/frozen language) and dependent vernacular(s) (LAOGLOSS); coined after Ferguson's diglossia (1959). Greek hieroglossos "speaking a divine/prophetic language" antecedent noted; term earlier used 1975 by Conrad M. B. Brann in a different sociolinguistic sense (per La hieroglossie japonaise 2012, p.61 n.1).
- KEY INVERSION OF FERGUSON: note 9 "I persist in considering diglossia a subclass of hieroglossia"; hieroglossia "falls in the main outside the scope of sociolinguistics" - religious phenomenon; no minimum speaker community needed.
- laogloss borrows massively from hierogloss; innovation in laogloss read as exegesis of hieroglossic original, not true innovation.
- ACTIVE vs PASSIVE hieroglossia (passive = purely liturgical use; forebodes disappearance).
- Cases treated: Arabic (most patent modern active case; diglossia lens called "partial and unsatisfactory"), Hebrew/Aramaic (+Zohar, Benamozegh 72 languages), Latin Christianity (Hebrew/Greek/Latin triad; Vatican II decline), Sanskrit (living-after-death literary continuity), Syriac, Coptic (Saidic choice), Armenian (Grabar; Mekhitarists; now passive hierogloss), Sino-Japanese kanbun/wabun (wa-kan dyad; shakkyoka Buddhist poetry; hōmonka), Persian under Arabic (integral quotation; Arabic morphology inside Persian), Amish Luther Bible, KJV and Luther translations as hieroglossic phenomena within English/German, Finnish Bible idiolect.
- Explicitly lists OLD CHURCH SLAVONIC traits in Russian literary language grammar as hieroglossic influence example (bridge to target E).
- Concluding agenda: research areas for hieroglossia (supremacy discourse on sacred language; critical discourse in reform; affiliation of vulgar to sacred languages; exegesis same/other language; translation of religious texts; shift/transfer/adaptation of religious vocabulary; polemics via translations; access of vulgar tongue to religious status/vying for sacrality).
