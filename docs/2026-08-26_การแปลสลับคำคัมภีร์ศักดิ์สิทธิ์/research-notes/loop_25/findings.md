## 2. VERDICTS PER TARGET

### (A) Ferguson 1959 - VERDICT: ACQUIRED (definition features captured)
- Original article open as scan (UMontreal course copy, 10 pp = pp. 325-340). No OCR text layer locally; T&F official PDF blocked (403).
- Verbatim definition secured via Schiffman (UPenn): diglossia = relatively stable situation, primary dialects PLUS a "very divergent, highly codified (often grammatically more complex) SUPERPOSED variety, the vehicle of a large and respected body of written literature, either of an earlier period or in another speech community, learned largely by formal education, used for most written and formal spoken purposes but NOT for ordinary conversation."
- H/L defining FEATURES (nine criteria): function allocation (sermon in church/mosque = H; instructions to servants, folk literature = L), prestige (H = great literature, CANONICAL RELIGIOUS TEXTS, public speaking), literary heritage (literature all in H; H felt to be THE language), acquisition (L mother tongue, H schooled), standardization (H grammars/dictionaries/canonical texts), stability (centuries-millennia), grammar (H more complex), lexicon (shared but differentiated), phonology (two system types). Plus boundary condition: in strict diglossia NO ONE speaks H natively (vs standard-with-dialects).
- Four defining cases: Arabic (Classical/'aammiyya), Swiss German (Standard/Dialekt), Haitian Creole French, Greek (katharevousa/dhimotiki). Ferguson also flags Tamil (literary H / colloquial L) and Latin-Romance as historical instance; Syriac/Coptic noted as liturgical languages with H-like functions in parts of Arab world.

### (B) Lehmann glossing works - VERDICT: ACQUIRED (lineage clarified; medieval-gloss question ANSWERED: NO)
- Lineage established from author's own site: 1978 Arbeitspapier "Guidelines for interlinear morphemic translations" -> 1982 Folia Linguistica 16 "Directions for interlinear morphemic translations" (author lists 193-224; LGR and Language editorial note cite 199-224 - discrepancy recorded) -> 2004 HSK 17.2 chapter "Interlinear morphemic glossing" (1834-1857) -> current website version "essentially incorporates and supersedes" it alongside Leipzig Glossing Rules.
- IMG defined as sequence-preserving L2 representation rendering each L1 morph; hybrid genre: simultaneously "a kind of translation that accompanies the original" (synoptic-edition-like) AND "a kind of linguistic analysis"; IPA-analogy standardization program.
- HISTORY SUB-QUESTION: Neither the 1982-lineage reworking nor the 2004 chapter traces glossing to MEDIEVAL manuscript glosses (zero hits for medieval/Middle Ages/biblical/Vulgate in both texts). Section 1.2 "Precursors" starts with 19th-century linguistics: Humboldt 1836 numbering device, H.C. von der Gabelentz 1861, G. Gabelentz 1901 (literal translations; early interlinear glosses; Latin-as-L2), Finck 1909 (first sizable glossed text with IMG), Bloomfield 1933. => The bridge from medieval scholia/glossae to modern IMG is an OPEN GAP our thesis can fill explicitly.

### (C) Works citing/applying Robert's Hieroglossia - VERDICT: PARTIAL (uptake real but thin/scattered)
- Core concept captured from full PDF: hieroglossia = relations between a central/founding ("dead"/frozen) language (hierogloss) and dependent vernacular(s) (laogloss); coined after Ferguson's diglossia; Robert insists DIGLOSSIA IS A SUBCLASS OF HIEROGLOSSIA and places hieroglossia largely outside sociolinguistics; active vs passive hieroglossia; vernaculars strive upward into competitive relation with the hierogloss; concluding research agenda (supremacy discourse, exegesis, religious translation, vying for sacrality).
- Six indexed citations (S2): Whitman 2011 (Ubiquity of the Gloss - most relevant to us), Wells 2011 (Literary Sinitic demotion in Korea), Severino 2015 (Pettazzoni/history of religions), Saul 2025 + Stulic 2025 (both Judeo-Spanish), Markowska 2025 (women's altars as hieroglossia - art history).
- Web-found applications beyond S2: Intralingual Translation East Asia project (hierogloss/laogloss against received H/L doctrine in Sinographosphere); Robert's own extensions: La hieroglossie japonaise (College de France/Fayard 2012), Bulletin 31 follow-up on kokoro/shakkyoka, Dogen lecture (Nichibunken/HAL). Term previously used by Conrad Brann (1975) in a different sense (per Robert 2012, p.61 n.1).
- NO monograph synthesizing hieroglossia with Fergusonian diglossia or Pollockian cosmopolis found => gap.

### (D) Appiah thick translation applied to scripture - VERDICT: PARTIAL (documented applications exist but scattered)
- Primary source confirmed: Callaloo 16(4) 1993: 801-819, JSTOR 2932211; definition "placement of a text in a rich cultural and linguistic context through annotation and accompanying notes" (p.817); Twi-proverb case; Geertz-derived; pedagogical-political aim ("genuinely informed respect for others"). Hermans 2003 extends to cross-cultural TS.
- Documented scriptural applications: (1) Cheung 2006 anthology on Chinese Buddhist sutra-translation discourse explicitly adopts/develops thick translation (review-documented) - strongest case; (2) Platform Sutra conference paper (three English translations from Dunhuang mss.) - direct Buddhist-scripture application, full citation pending; (3) Song of Songs thick-translation/performance chapter in Expanding Approaches to Bible Translation (PubPub 2025); (4) Jiang 2019 on Unschuld's Huangdi Neijing (canonical-medical adjacent).
- CAUTION: Study Quran and Steinsaltz Talmud are structural analogues but were NOT found analyzed under Appiah's label - do not cite them as documented applications.

### (E) Pollock's vernacularization beyond Sanskrit - VERDICT: ACQUIRED (rich)
- Pollock himself: LGG ch.7 (Latinitas as European countercosmopolis) and ch.11 (Europe Vernacularized) internalize Latin Christendom comparison; 1996 piece explicitly states eastern-European vernacularization "follows an analogous pattern... within... Byzantium and Eastern Christianity," with Hungarian-Latin case and Greek-Slavic interactions footnote (Sevcenko 1991) - i.e., Church Slavonic is GESTURED AT, not developed, by Pollock.
- Islamic cosmopolis: Ricci, Islam Translated (Chicago 2011; DOI 10.7208/chicago/9780226710907.001.0001) - "Arabic cosmopolis" modeled explicitly on Pollock, via Book of One Thousand Questions in Arabic->Javanese/Malay/Tamil; reviews confirm the modeling (JAS 2013; Indonesia 2012). Ricci's related work on INTERLINEAR translations of the Maulid Sharaf al-Anam links directly to our gloss theme. Persian cosmopolis extensions: Petru 2016 (Moussons 27); Leezenberg 2023 (Ottoman/Turkish, Kurdish, Georgian, Armenian within Persianate order; Bourdieu-based critique); Sufi cosmopolis (JRAS 2025); Kooria 2022 (Islamic law circulation) as adjacent.
- Latin Christendom: uptake weaker than hoped - King 2023 records that "hopes... would stimulate a response from students of Latinity... mostly disappointed"; King himself compares regional Sanskrits with "stuffed Latin" (latin farci) charter hybridity (post-Carolingian Medieval Latin as fully acquired code). Wen volume (Brill 2023) tests the model for the Sinographic cosmopolis (Lurie skeptical of "full" vernacularization in East Asia; Korea chapters incl. "script apartheid").
- Church Slavonic specifically: no dedicated study applying Pollock's model surfaced in this loop (only Pollock's own aside + Leezenberg's mention of Old Church Slavonic among sacred-language grammatical traditions) => OPEN NICHE.

### (F) "Diglossic distance" / sacred-language sociolinguistics surveys - VERDICT: TERM NOT ESTABLISHED; FIELD MAPPED
- Exact phrase "diglossic distance" is NOT a canonical term in surveyed sources. Nearest families: (i) general linguistic-distance methodology (structural/functional/perceptual; Levenshtein; intelligibility testing); (ii) psycholinguistics of Arabic diglossia using "morphological/phonological distance" (Joubran-Awadie & Shalhoub-Awwad 2023 First Language 43(2):200-230; Laks & Saiegh-Haddad 2022; Ghawi-Dakwar & Saiegh-Haddad 2024 JSLHR "Linguistic Distance Hypothesis"); (iii) survey-status chapter "Diglossia as a Sociolinguistic Situation" (Wiley handbook DOI 10.1002/9781405166256.ch12).
- Sacred-language sociolinguistics recent surveys: Routledge Handbook of Language and Religion (Pihlaja & Ringrow 2023; Part One Religious & Sacred Language: Bassetti on learning sacred languages; Bennett "Sacred Languages and Power"); Routledge Handbook of Translation and Religion (2023); Priestley 2022 sociolinguistic perception study of Latin; Haeri 2003 classic (sacred Arabic in Egypt). => Our coinage/use of "diglossic distance" must be positioned against the existing distance-in-diglossia psycholinguistic cluster, not presented de novo.

## 3. SYNTHESIS NOTES FOR PHASE C (cross-framework connective tissue)

1. Ferguson supplies the STRUCTURAL skeleton (function separation, superposed codified variety, canonical religious texts as prestige criterion, elite literacy genesis condition, religion-linked stability). His own examples already include liturgical languages (Syriac, Coptic, Latin-Romance).
2. Robert INVERTS the dependency: diglossia is a subclass of hieroglossia; the sacred relation precedes/exceeds the sociolinguistic one; laogloss innovations read as exegesis of the hierogloss - precisely the semantic world of kundoku-style glossing and of scholastic commentary. Robert's own case list covers most of our traditions (Arabic, Hebrew/Aramaic, Latin Christianity, Sanskrit, Armenian/Grabar, Sino-Japanese kanbun; OCS-Russian mentioned).
3. Lehmann supplies the MICRO-MACHINERY: IMG as hybrid translation-analysis artifact whose history he starts only in the 19th c.; the missing prehistory (medieval glossae, biblical scholia, masoretic annotation, kundoku kunten, Slavic izhoglasy) is where our case traditions plug in.
4. Appiah supplies the ETHICS/POLITICS of annotation: glosses/notes as thick translation producing informed respect; connects directly to Lehmann's "gloss as service to the reader."
5. Pollock/Ricci supply the MACRO-HISTORY: cosmopolis -> vernacularization cycles; Ricci shows the model transfers to a scriptural religion (Islam); her interlinear-Maulid work ties cosmopolis-scale processes back to concrete glossing practices; Latin/Eastern-Christian and Church-Slavonic applications remain underbuilt - our thesis can claim that space.
6. Distance research (F) offers measurable operationalization (lexical/morphological/phonological distance between H and L) that could quantify "diglossic distance" per tradition and correlate with glossing intensity.
