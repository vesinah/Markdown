## Key content relevant to the nissaya project

1. **Frasch's position quoted (pp. 1–2, citing Frasch 1996: 332, "My translation"):** Pali was for Pagan monks a foreign language whose structure had to be made transparent first; "That is why commentaries were usually composed in the form of nissaya, in which short Pāli portions were interspersed with Burmese translations." Frasch calls Pali a culture language (Hochsprache) over Old Burmese; notes calques like attaññ-may for anicca, si-cap-mraṅ-nhaṁ-so for sabbaññuta, privative a- replacing Burmese negation ma.

2. **Ruiz-Falques' counter-thesis (p. 3):** "the evidence of the Pāli grammatical texts goes against this assumption" — many grammatical texts are too technical for beginners, so they were not merely language-teaching manuals; they are scholastic/philosophical literature (logic, epistemology, scriptural authority), contra Bode 1908/1909 and Deokar 2008's "guiding manual" reading.

3. **On Burmese nissayas explicitly (pp. 27–28):** Of the Kaccāyana-commentary lineage he surveys (Chapaṭa/Saddhammajotipāla's Kaccāyanasuttaniddesa, Mahāvijitāvī's Kaccāyanavaṇṇanā, Dhāṭanāga's Niruttisāramañjusā, the anonymous Galoun Pyan "Flight of the Phoenix" which "is a Pāli commentary [whose] style follows the method of Burmese nissayas"), he states: "In my assessment of the Pāli grammatical commentaries of Burma, I will not include the Burmese nissayas, even though, as Smith has proved, they are extremely useful in textual criticism.98 Their inclusion would be beyond the scope of this article." Footnote 98 = Smith 1928: vii (Helmer Smith, Saddanīti edition preface). This is a programmatic admission that Burmese nissayas remain outside his grammatical-commentary corpus — i.e., the nissaya genre itself is NOT treated in depth in this article.

4. **Minor grammars canonized at fifteen** "since the printed edition that followed the publication of the Chaṭṭhasaṅgāyana series," but extant minor Pāli grammars are far more numerous (pp. 28).

5. **Kaccāyana/Kātantra relation (p. 17):** of ~675 Kacc rules, 215 are supposed adaptations of Kātantra sūtras; Kātantra manuscripts present in old and modern Burmese monastic libraries.
