## E2. "U Tin" vs Tin Lwin RESOLVED

The nissaya-studies figure behind loop_11's "U Tin?" query is **Tin Lwin** (Burmese names have no surname; "U" is an honorific — hence citations appear variously as "Tin Lwin", "U Tin Lwin", or misread as "U Tin").

Verified identity chain (three independent sources):
1. Okell's own reference list (Lingua 15, ScienceDirect page fetched this session): "Tin Lwin, A study of Pali-Burmese nissaya, M.A. thesis (1961)".
2. Pannabhoga Herngseng 2023 reference list (Brill full text): "Tin Lwin. 1961. 'A Study of Pali-Burmese Nissaya with Special References to Mahāparinibbāna Sutta.' M.A. Thesis., School of Oriental and African Studies." Plus "Tin Lwin. 1973." [book, Mandalay] and Tin Lwin 1991 "The Saddanīti," in Studies in Honour of Mingun Sayadaw, Yangon: Tipitaka Nikaya Ministrative Organization, 117–126 (also in Ruiz-Falques HKU handout).
3. Walker diss. fn. 17: "Tin Lwin, 'A Study of Pali-Burmese Nissaya' (School of Oriental and African Studies, University of London, 1961), 6–11" — presenting her FOUR TYPES of nissaya: verbatim, free translation, ornate, translation with short notes (taxonomy transmitted via Lammerts Cornell diss. 2010: 229–230).

Degree-level conflict from loop_11 RESOLVED: M.A. thesis (both Okell and Pannabhoga say M.A.; Walker's "(School of Oriental and African Studies, University of London, 1961)" does not assert PhD). VERDICT: M.A. thesis, SOAS, 1961, full title "A Study of Pali-Burmese Nissaya with Special References to Mahāparinibbāna Sutta".

No separate nissaya authority named plain "U Tin" surfaced anywhere this session (searches + Crossref + reference lists). Any "U Tin" attribution should be treated as a citation artifact of Tin Lwin unless a specific Burmese-language publication by another U Tin is produced.
