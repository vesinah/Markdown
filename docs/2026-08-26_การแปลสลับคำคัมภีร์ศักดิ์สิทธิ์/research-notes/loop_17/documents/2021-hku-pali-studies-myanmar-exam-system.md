# Document extracts — Burmese nissaya print tradition & monastic exam system (Foraging Loop 17, item F)

## F1. HKU "Buddhism in Myanmar" lecture series — Lecture 2: Pali Studies in Myanmar (FULL-FETCHED cluster)

**Source:** Centre of Buddhist Studies, University of Hong Kong, online lecture series, 15 Jan–5 Feb 2021. Lecture 2 (22 Jan 2021): "Pali Studies in Myanmar," Dr. Aleix Ruiz-Falques (Head, Dept. of Pali and Languages, Shan State Buddhist University) & Ven. Nyanasami (Lecturer in Pali, SSBU).
**Page:** https://event.buddhism.hku.hk/2021011501
**Local copies:** `_hku2021_pali-studies-slides.pdf` (64 slides, 15.7 MB), `_hku2021_pali-studies-handout.pdf` (2 pp. bibliography). Texts extracted.

### Exam-system facts (from lecture abstract + speaker bio)
- Abstract: nissaya commentaries developed as study aids from the Pali grammatical scholarship of 12th–13th-c. Pagan; "the technique and methodology of these nissayas is mainly based on Pali grammatical and exegetical terminology."
- Two prestigious MODERN monastic examinations described as living scholastic tradition: the **Thamane Kyaw** (private examination specially designed for young novices) and the **Alankara degree** awarded at its completion.
- Ven. Nyanasamilankara's bio: completed final level of Thamane Kyaw private exam → Alankāra Degree; also completed the **Dhammacariya degree examination**, "organized and recognized as equivalent to a First Degree by the government of the Union of Myanmar."
- Lecture recordings on YouTube (HKU CBS playlist linked from page).

### Slide-deck content bearing on nissaya history
- Śrī Kṣetra gold plates ca. 5th c. CE (Falk 1997); King Saw Lu Myittha Inscription 11th c. with original Pali record, irregular orthography (Handlin 2018: 6); Rājakumāra inscription early 12th c., four languages (Mon, Burmese, Pyu, Pali), grammatically regular Pali.
- Dhammasenāpati's Kārikā (Pagan, ca. 12th c.?) = earliest Pali treatise of Myanmar; lineage Ratnamati's Śabdārthacintā → Kārikā; Nyāsappadīpa/Saṃpyaṅ-ṭīkā (12th c.) later criticized by Dāṭhanāga's Niruttisāramañjūsā but "used, together with that work, in the Nyāsa-nissaya"; manuscripts containing grammatical texts donated to Siṅghavīr Sujjabuil Monastery in 1227 CE (Lammerts 2010: 97–98).
- Aggavaṃsa's Saddanīti colophon and Sāsanavaṃsa (ed. Bode p. 74) story of Laṅkā grammarians praising it.
- Development-of-literature slide: epigraphy (?12th c.) → **nissaya (?14th c. onwards)** → embellished poetic translations (?14th c.) → chronicles (?14th c.).
- Concluding remark: Burmese Pali grammar is the "Framework for the development of nissaya literature"; correction note re Kaccāyanasāra vs Saddavutti shows live editorial care.
- Ongoing research named on slides: Linda Cha (Mahidol PhD cand.) on Kaccāyana-commentary cycle; Ven. Kondaññakitti (SSBU PhD cand.) editing Mahā Ariyavaṃsa's Jātakavisodhana philological notes; Ven. Nyanasamilankara critical edition of Lokuppatti.

## F2. Corroborating exam-system sources (metadata-level)

1. **IATBU (International Association of Buddhist Universities), atbu.org/node/26:** "A Study of Tipiṭakadhara Selection Examination in Myanmar (1948–2007)" — notes that after the Fifth Council printing presses came into common use and many texts appeared in print; documents official oral examination history. SEARCH-VERIFIED snippet only.
2. **Dhammasami, Khammai, Buddhism, Education and Politics in Burma and Thailand: The Buddhānussati... (Bloomsbury Academic, 2018)** — Open Library confirms publisher/year; ISBNs incl. 1350054240 / 1350054259 / 1350054267 (ISBN-10). Book version of his Oxford DPhil; covers Mandalay/Pakokku training methods and exam system (per Pannabhoga 2023 citing diss. pp. 134–147).
3. **Kāruṇika, Ashin. 2008. Dhammacariya Myatshu (Dhammacariya Method), 3rd ed. Yangon: Siritipitaka Publication** — modern Burmese-language manual of nissaya interpretive methods for exam preparation (reference list of Pannabhoga 2023).
4. **Kumāravaṃsa Mahathera. 1920. Saccasaṅkhepa Nissaya. Yangon: Kawi Myet Hman Publication** — example of early PRINTED nissaya textbook (same ref list).
5. Pannabhoga 2023 (loop_11 FULL-FETCHED) remains the anchor source for the living classroom mechanics: printed textbooks with students interlining nissaya interpretation in margins (Veluvan Monastery, Taunggyi photo), zāso repeater system, Ministry of Religious Affairs Dhammacariya syllabus equivalence, >22 printed/manuscript nissayas on Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha in the 19th c. per History of the Abhidhamma (Myanmar Buddhist Propagation 1965).
6. Thai contrast (same article): no nissaya in current Thai Naktham/Pariyatti exams (Pathompong 2016: 30–32).
