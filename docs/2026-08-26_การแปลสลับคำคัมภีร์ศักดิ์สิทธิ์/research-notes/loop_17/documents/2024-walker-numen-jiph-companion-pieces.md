## D1. "Living Phonologies: Khmer Pronunciations of Pali at the Nexus of Writing and Orality" (VERIFIED)

- Author: Trent Walker. *Numen* 71(2–3):194–226, April 2024. Brill. DOI: 10.1163/15685276-20240004.
- Crossref confirms: journal Numen, year 2024, pages 194–226, vol 71, issue 2-3, publisher Walter de Gruyter GmbH.
- ORCID profile (orcid.org/0000-0002-2285-1979) lists it with date 2024-04-01.
- Brill abstract page (brill.com/abstract/journals/nu/71/2-3/article-p194_4.xml): "By offering the first detailed portrait of how a particular local system for Pali pronunciation functions, this article aims to renew our appreciation of the complex interaction between chanted sounds and written words in Buddhist cultures..."
- Abstract body (via R Discovery / ResearchGate): based on phonetic transcription and analysis of **fifteen multimedia recordings** of Pali liturgical chants in twentieth-/twenty-first-century Cambodia; ResearchGate snippet adds: "These formats draw on the pan-Theravada technology of the bitext to create a dynamic oscillation between Pali and Khmer passages."
- Open access PDF mirrored via Google Drive from The Open Buddhist University entry (buddhistuniversity.net), which also gives BibTeX with openalex_id W4393857359 and pages 194–226.
- VERDICT on task hint "a Numen 2024 article on Khmer nissaya?": EXISTS, but its subject is Khmer PRONUNCIATION OF PALI in chant (writing-orality nexus); it treats bitext formats as context, not as a study of Khmer nissaya manuscripts per se.
