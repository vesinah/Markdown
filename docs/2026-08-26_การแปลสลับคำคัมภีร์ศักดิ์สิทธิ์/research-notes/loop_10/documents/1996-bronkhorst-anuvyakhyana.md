## Key extracts (from fetched excerpt)
- "The word anuvyakhyana occurs four times in Vedic literature, three times in the Brihadaranyaka Upanisad, once in the Maitrayaniya Upanisad, and nowhere else."
- It always appears in the enumeration: "rigvedo yajurveda samavedo 'tharvangirasa itihasa purana vidya upanisadah slokah sutrany anuvyakhyanani vyakhyanani".
- Horsch argued the terms "cannot but refer to texts that explain (vyakhya-) ... predecessors of the later commentatorial literature"; Bronkhorst notes the position of anuvyakhyana between sutra and vyakhyana suggests vyakhyana might be secondary, i.e., a hierarchical structure sutra > anuvyakhyana > vyakhyana.
- Sankara's comment on BAU 2.4.10 glosses: "sutranI vastusangrahavAkyAni vede yathA ... anuvyAkhyAnAni mantravivaraNAni / vyAkhyAnAny arthavAdAH" (sutras = meaning-summarizing sentences; anuvyakhyanas = explanations of mantras; vyakhyanas = arthavada).
- Bhandarkar (1876) observed Patañjali uses anvacakSate characteristically of Katyayana's work while calling his own activity vyakhyana; since khyA replaces cakS before ardhadhatuka suffixes (P. 2.4.54), the noun corresponding to anvAcakaSte is anvAkhyAna. Hence Katyayana's varttikas would form the anuvyakhyana and Patanjali's Mahabhasya the vyakhyana.
- Bronkhorst concludes: "It is therefore at least conceivable that the terms anuvyakhyana and vyakhyana in our Upanisadic passage ... refer to two-layered commentaries on Sutra works like what we find in the case of Panini's Astadhyayi."

## Relevance to loop topic
Shows the antiquity and hierarchy of intra-language exegetical layers attached to authoritative Sanskrit corpora: sutra, then word/mantra-level explanation (anuvyakhyana), then discursive interpretation (vyakhyana) - a structural template later mirrored across Sanskrit, Pali and Tamil traditions.
