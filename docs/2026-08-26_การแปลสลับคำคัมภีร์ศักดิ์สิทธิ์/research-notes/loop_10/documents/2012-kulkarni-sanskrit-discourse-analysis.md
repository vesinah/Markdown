## Key extracts
- Three schools developed theories of verbal cognition (shabdabodha) aiming to understand the Vedas: Vyakarana (Grammar), Nyaya (Logic), Mimamsa (Exegesis).
- Traditional learning sequence: "the sentence level analysis is introduced at a tender age of 9 or 10 immediately after the students have memorized Sabdarupa (noun-word forms), dhatupatha (verbal forms) and Amarakosa"; students then analyse a chapter of Kalidasa's Raghuvamsa.
- Two prominent approaches to sentence analysis: "Dand anvaya (also known as anvayamukhi)" in which "the teacher arranges all the words in prose order," and "Khand anvaya (also known as kathambhutini)" where "the teacher gives the basic skeleton of a sentence and fills in other details by asking questions" centered on heads seeking modifiers; the latter "is more close to the modern dependency parsing credited to Tesniere (1959)."
- Mimamsakas discuss discourse relations called Sangatis for textual coherence at levels: shastra-sangati (subject), adhyaya-sangati (chapter), pada-sangati (section), adhikarana-sangati (topic); six inter-paragraph relations listed: Akshepa (objection), Drishtanta (example), Pratyudaharana (counter-example), Prasangika (corollary), Upodghata (pre-requisite), Apavada (exception).
- On the commentarial chain: "Among the scientific literature in Sanskrit we find three distinct trends. One is sutra-bhasya-tika-tippani popularly known as Bhasya parampara... The commentaries may be nested, i.e., there is a commentary on the original sutras and then commentary on this commentary, and further commentary on the sub-commentaries and so on."
- Other trends: khandana-mandana parampara (criticism chains forming tree structures) and prakarana granthas (monographs on specific topics).

## Relevance to loop topic
Documents how anvaya-based pedagogy (rearrangement into prose order) is the standard classroom technology for accessing Sanskrit verse, i.e., intra-language translation institutionalized in traditional education, plus the nested sutra-bhasya-tika architecture preserving root-text authority.
