## Key extracts
- Definition: "Manipravalam (literally, 'gems and corals') is the emic term for a dominant part of Kerala's premodern vernacular literature, binding together Kerala's local language and Sanskrit."
- Main thesis: the paper "highlights a rarely discussed aspect: the role of Prakrit in shaping both Manipravalam literature and theory", arguing Prakrit was "a precedent for Manipravalam, a language that is not Sanskrit and yet is also not some unsystematic, unsystematisable flow of words ... a linguistic mode with its own distinct set of conventions and its own [unwritten] grammar".
- On the Lilatilakam (LT): "presumably late-fourteenth-century textbook on grammar and poetics from Kerala consists of short rules (sutra) and prose explanations (vrtti) in Sanskrit as well as illustrative stanzas in Manipravalam. Divided into eight chapters"; chapters on kavyadosa, kavyaguna, arthalamkara, sabdalamkara, rasa.
- Word typology in LT ch.2: words of Kerala's local language divided into desi, samskrutarupa, samskrtabhava (same triad used for Prakrit), with desi subdivided into suddha, bhasantarasama, bhasantarabhava referencing neighbouring languages (Cola-bhasa and Pandya-bhasa = Tamil, Andhra-bhasa = Telugu, Karnataka-bhasa = Kannada).
- Aesthetic kinship: Prakrit praised for 'sweet syllables' (mahurakkharae); shared values of sweetness (madhuryam) and delicacy (sukumaratvam).
- Framing via Pollock's "cosmopolitan vernacular": Manipravalam has "a particularly close affinity with Sanskrit, even when compared to ... premodern Telugu or Kannada".
- LT explicitly "declares that Sanskrit treatises on poetics (alankara-sastra) are applicable to Manipravalam materials, since no Manipravalam treatises on poetics existed at the time of the LT's composition."

## Related items seen this session
- Freeman, Rich, "Caught in Translation: Ideologies of literary language in Kerala's Manipravalam" (2013, record at isidore.science): describes Lilatilakam as "a normative text of Kerala which tried to sanction and partly describe a regionally based literary language and literature poised between" Tamil and Sanskrit learned traditions.
- UT Austin thesis abstract "Red rubies colored gold: aureation in the Lilatilakam": manipi = red ruby of Kerala-bhasa, pravalam = red coral of Sanskrit; "Ideally words of the two language traditions blend together in a seamless and unnoticeable mixture"; motivation includes distinguishing Kerala-bhasa "from the socio-linguistic dominance of Tamil".
- Manipravala.com (project site): earliest extant Tamil-Manipravala text = Pārataveṇpā by Peruntevanar (8th c.); Srivaisnavas adopted the hybrid "since roughly the beginning of the second millennium" because they held both Sanskrit Vedas and Tamil Vedas (Nalayira Divyaprabandham) authoritative; hypotheses for the hybrid include accessibility to lay audiences, protecting subtle meanings in Sanskrit, preserving theological terms unaltered.

## Relevance to loop topic
Shows Manipravalam theorized not as degraded mixing but as a sanctioned third register between sacred Sanskrit and vernacular - a deliberate strategy letting Sanskrit liturgical/poetic authority govern vernacular religious literature in Kerala, with explicit emic theory (Lilatilakam).
