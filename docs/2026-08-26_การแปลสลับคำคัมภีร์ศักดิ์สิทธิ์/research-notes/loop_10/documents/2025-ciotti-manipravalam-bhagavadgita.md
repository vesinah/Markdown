## Key extracts
- Ms GOML R3507 contains "the hitherto only known copy of a prose version (vacanam) of the Bhagavadgita composed by Kariccankala Srinivasan, a Srivaisnava author who translated into Manipravalam a number of Sanskrit texts towards the end of the eighteenth century." Manuscript: 141 Talipot leaves, scribe Perunkaranai Ramanujan, written alternating Tamil Grantha and Tamil scripts - "ostensibly both being required to write in Manipravalam".
- Typology given: "The label Manipravalam has been used ... to refer to various mixtures of Sanskrit and south Indian languages, notably Tamil and Malayalam, that can be subsumed under two main categories: a hypersanskritised register of the local language or a real mixed/hybrid language in which in the same sentence fully inflected words of both Sanskrit and the local language of choice can be found." The BhGV is "an example of the hypersanskritised register of Tamil": Sanskrit lexemes preponderant, Tamil grammatical endings and syntax, but colloquial/Brahmin Tamil rather than Cen-Tamil; labelled "Late Tamil Manipravalam".
- Naming rule observed: "when the language used by Kariccankala Srinivasan to compose his works is talked about in Sanskrit, it is called dramidabhasa. On the other hand, when the same works are talked about in Tamil, the choice is between two different labels, namely either manipravalam or tamiz, according to the presence or absence of Grantha script when written."
- Pedagogical contrast: an anonymous Manipravalam rendition of the Gita in IFP ms RE04169 uses "an avataranika followed by simple word-by-word glosses, on the line of the pancalaksana ('five explanations') system ... a commentarial pattern used to explain the meaning of verses and which contains a word-by-word division of the text, its syntactic rearrangement unencumbered by metrical requirements, a word-by-word set of glosses, etc. This system has a clear pedagogical purpose ... most probably mirroring what teachers orally did for their pupils."
- Ritual frame: prefatory Risyadinyasa presents the whole Gita as Bhagavadgitamahamantra for recitation, with unique Srivaisnava mark kaimkaryartthe ("for the service [to Krsna-gopala]") among 16 compared manuscripts; bija = BG 2.11, sakti = BG 18.66, kilaka = BG 15.1.
- Style analysis: Kariccankala's hypotactic Manipravalam mirrors the Sanskrit mula's single finite sentence; "the occurrence of one or the other syntactic mode is done with a clear aesthetic intent"; possibly "to sound difficult, hence authoritative".

## Relevance to loop topic
Demonstrates late Manipravalam translation practice where Sanskrit scriptural authority is preserved lexically (Sanskrit compounds kept intact), graphically (Grantha script requirement), and ritually (Gita-as-mantra recitation), while Tamil syntax carries comprehension; also documents the pancalaksana word-by-word gloss system paralleling pada-patha/anvaya.
