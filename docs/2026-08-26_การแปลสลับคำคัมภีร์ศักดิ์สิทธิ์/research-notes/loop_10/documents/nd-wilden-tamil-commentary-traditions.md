## Key extracts (from publisher descriptions)
- Origin: workshop "Between Preservation and Recreation: Tamil Traditions of Commentary in Pursuit of the Cankam Era", Pondicherry Centre of the EFEO, July 2006, in honour of Pandit T.V. Gopal Iyer.
- Contents per description: Thomas Lehmann "gives a survey of the types of commentary found in Tamil"; Jean-Luc Chevillard "addresses the interaction between scholastic Sanskrit and Tamil"; G. Vijayavenugopal, Eva Wilden and A. Dhamodharan treat "the genre of grammatical and poetological commentaries"; Martine Gestin retrieves social/anthropological information from a poetological commentary; T.V. Gopal Iyer, T.S. Gangadharan, T. Rajeswari write about literary commentaries; R. Varadadesikan introduces Vaishnava theological exegesis; Sascha Ebeling characterizes the "neo-commentaries" of the 19th century.

## Companion volume also seen this session
- Title: The Commentary Idioms of the Tamil Learned Traditions (eds. Suganya Anandakichenin and Victor D'Avella), EFEO / French Institute Pondicherry, https://publications.efeo.fr/en/livres/935... and https://www.ifpindia.org/bookstore/ci141/
- Description: "detailed studies of the commentary traditions of South India with a particular emphasis on Tamil, but extended to Sanskrit and Telugu as well"; chapters include "The Beginnings of the Tamil Commentarial Idiom", "Salient Features of a Grammatical Commentary in Tamil", "Akananuru pazaiyavurai: The Subtle Growth of a Commentary", "The Old, Anonymous Commentary of the Ainkurunuru", "Showing the Way: The Metatextual Field of the Tirumurukarruppatai".
- Notably: "in one case, this has led to an extension of the very notion of a commentary to include translation." Editors specialize in Alvar bhakti poetry and Srivaishnava Manipravalam literature (Anandakichenin).

## Relevance to loop topic
State-of-the-art scholarly reference confirming that Tamil commentary (urai) is a self-conscious tradition whose typology and whose interface with Sanskrit scholasticism are actively studied; also confirms Manipravalam as the recognized vehicle where commentary and translation merge in Srivaishnava practice.
