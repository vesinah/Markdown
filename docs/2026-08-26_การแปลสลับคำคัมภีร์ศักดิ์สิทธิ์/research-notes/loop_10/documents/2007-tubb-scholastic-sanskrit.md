## Key extracts (verbatim or near-verbatim from fetched text)
- Introduction "The Sanskrit Commentary" enumerates "the five services of a commentary": 0.2.1 Padaccheda; 0.2.2 Padarthokti; 0.2.3 Vigraha; 0.2.4 Vakyayojana; 0.2.5 Aksepasamadhana.
- "Of the five services that a full commentary is expected to provide, the first four are services of glossing and deal directly with the words of a text, while the fifth is a service of discussion and is concerned more with the ideas expressed in that text."
- Service definitions given: "padaccheda - division of words, i.e., the resolution of external sandhi"; "padarthokti - statement of the meaning of the words, i.e., the giving of synonyms, paraphrases, definitions, and examples"; "vigraha - analysis of complex formations, i.e., breaking them down into their constituent elements"; "vakyayojana - construing the sentences, i.e., indicating how the words fit together syntactically".
- On Vedic pada-patha: "to each of the collections of Vedic hymns there was added in ancient times a padapatha ('word-text') version, in which each word-isolate is exhibited separately, without terminal sandhi."
- Two approaches to construing verses: section headings 1.77 "The kathambhutin approach", 1.78 "The anvayamukhi approach"; also 1.79 insertion of explanatory material, 1.80 variations on the two approaches.
- Part One draws examples "chiefly from well-known commentaries on the great mahakavya poems, especially from the commentaries of Mallinatha"; Part Two uses Sarikara-school Vedanta texts, especially Sarikara's Brahmasutrabhasya.
- Glossing mechanics described: "placing an explanation of that meaning ... at the simplest level often merely a synonym for it ... alongside the word in question"; special devices for proper names, species names, particles, indeclinables, and pronouns; compound analysis using formulas for derived stems; technical Paninian elements may also be used.

## Relevance to loop topic
Provides the canonical taxonomy (five services) showing Sanskrit commentaries function as systematic intra-language translation apparatus: word division, glossing, morphological decomposition, syntactic rearrangement, and objection-resolution keep the sacred mula authoritative while making it intelligible.
