## Extracts

From "Judeo-Persian Bible Translations: Some Sample Texts" (JSTOR 27943401):
- "But at the beginning of European interest in Persian, one of the first texts known, studied, and excerpted for the earliest dictionaries was the Judeo-Persian Pentateuch translation by Jacob ben Joseph Tavus that was published in the Constantinople polyglot Bible in 1546."

From "Another Judeo-Persian Pentateuch Translation: MS HUC 2193" (JSTOR 23506686):
- "It is arranged as follows: each verse is headed by the initial Hebrew word followed by full Judeo-Persian translation. Each weekly portion of the Pentateuch is followed by a translation of the particular prophetic portion that is traditionally assigned to it, the haftara."

## Corroborating items seen this session (non-JSTOR)
- Stanford SearchWorks catalog record: "A Judeo-Persian Pentateuch: the text of the oldest Judeo-Persian Pentateuch translation, British Museum Ms. OR.5446" (edition of the 1319 manuscript).
- Blog essay (riowang.studiolum.com, 2015): "The most important medieval Judeo-Persian manuscripts include the 1319 handwritten copy of Torat Mosheh, the earliest known Judeo-Persian text of the Pentateuch. This Judeo-Persian translation of the Torah was also one of the earliest printed texts in this language, appearing as a polyglot Pentateuch published in Constantinople in 1546."
- Oxford blog post (thebookshelf.hypotheses.org, 2023, Saloumeh Gholami): Torat Mosheh dated 1319 CE, British Library Or.5446, fol. 124v colophon; "represents the most ancient extant Judeo-Persian translation of the Pentateuch originating from Iran."
- Brill JJL article (2025) on Vat. Pers. 61: "presents the Hebrew vocalized text of the Pentateuch followed by the Judeo-Persian translation," compared with Tawus' translation per Guidi (1885).

## Notes (facts seen this session)
- MS HUC 2193's layout — Hebrew lemma heading each verse, Judeo-Persian rendering beneath, haftarah appended per weekly portion — is a manuscript instance of the same interleave architecture as the printed Constantinople polyglot page.
- The 1546 Constantinople polyglot Pentateuch (Hebrew + Aramaic + Persian, i.e., including Tawus/Tavus' Judaeo-Persian) predates by one year the 1547 Hebrew-Ladino-Greek Pentateuch from the same city; both compress multiple comprehensibility traditions into single printed spreads.
