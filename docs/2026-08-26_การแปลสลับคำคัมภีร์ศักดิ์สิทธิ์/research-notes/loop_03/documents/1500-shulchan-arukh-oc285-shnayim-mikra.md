## Extracts

Berakhot 8a (Sefaria, Steinsaltz):
- "Rav Huna bar Yehuda said that Rabbi Ami said: A person should always complete his Torah portions with the congregation. The congregation reads a particular Torah portion every Shabbat, and during the week prior to each Shabbat, one is required to read the Bible text of the weekly portion twice and the translation once."

Peninei Halakha:
- "'twice Scripture, once translation', that is, the parsha twice and the Aramaic translation once. He who does so is granted a longer life (Berakhot 8a)."

Substitution rulings (as reported in search results this session):
- koltorah.org (Jachter): "The Rosh (Berachot 1:8) and the Tur (O.C. 285) assert that Rashi's commentary to Chumash constitutes a viable alternative to Targum Onkelos" for shnayim mikra.
- Faierstein's In geveb article (fully fetched, separate document): Rabbi David ha-Levi (Taz) rules a man not fluent in Aramaic may substitute a Yiddish Humash work "like the Ze'enah U-Re'enah."

## Notes (facts seen this session)
- Shnayim mikra ve-echad targum is the private-study twin of the synagogue alternating recitation: the same verse-plus-rendering unit migrates from public performance (m. Megillah 4:4) into weekly personal practice with a promised reward (long life).
- Medieval and early modern authorities progressively redefined what counts as "targum": Onkelos, then Rashi's commentary (Rosh/Tur/Shulchan Arukh OC 285:2), then Yiddish works like the Tsenerene (Taz) — the slot of "comprehension companion to Hebrew" persists even when Aramaic died out as a Jewish vernacular.
