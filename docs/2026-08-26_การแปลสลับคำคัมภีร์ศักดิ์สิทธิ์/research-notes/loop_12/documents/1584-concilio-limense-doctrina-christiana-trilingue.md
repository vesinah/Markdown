## Extracted facts (from OCR text, doctrinachristia00cath_0_djvu.txt)

### Printing license (licencia, 13 February 1584)
- The Real Audiencia of Lima granted license on 13 Feb 1584 for Antonio Ricardo ("Antonio Tricardo Piarnontes" in garbled OCR) to print the catechism in his house in Lima and nowhere else, under stated penalties.
- Oversight during printing assigned to padre Juan de Atienza (Society of Jesus) for the Confessionario and Preparacion para morir, and padre Joseph de Acosta, "con dos de los que se hallaron en la traduction della de nuestra lengua castellana en las lenguas de los Indios" -- i.e., Acosta assisted together with two participants of the translation itself, plus secretaries of the Audiencia to verify against the original.
- Rationale recorded: it had not been possible to send the texts to be printed in Castile because printers there could not handle the Quichua and Aymara languages, and printing errors "podrian mover a los dichos naturales" (could mislead the natives), so local printing under supervision was licensed.

### "Epistola sobre la traduccion" (letter on the translation)
- The Council ordered a Cartilla, Catecismos, Confessionario, and Preparacion para el articulo de la muerte be composed and then "se mando tradujer en las dos lenguas generales de los dichos Reynos del Peru, QUICHUA Y AYMARA".
- Decree with teeth: "parecio a este santo Concilio Provincial proveer y mandar con rigor que ninguno use otra traduccion, ni aun la mandada en esta" -- no one may use any other translation, not even alongside this one; uniformity was judged preferable even at the cost of some imperfection, explicitly analogized to how "la Iglesia catolica" has providently handled "las traducciones de la Santa Scriptura" (the Church's regulation of Scripture translations).
- For the many other diverse languages of the viceroyalty, bishops were instructed to convene learned religious and secular experts in diocesan synods to produce approved translations of this same catechism for each language.

### "Decreto sobre la traduccion" (Latin, Ex Act. i. cap. 3)
- Synodus prohibits, besides the version made and published by its authority (both in the rudiments of Christian doctrine and in the catechism) "in linguam Cazquensem sive Aymaraicam", any other translation being written or used; bishops must have the catechism translated into the remaining languages of their dioceses "per idoneos et pios interpretes", approved by the bishop and received without controversy notwithstanding any contrary custom.

### "Annotaciones o scolios sobre la traduccion de la Doctrina christiana, y Catecismo en las lenguas Quichua y Aymara"
- Quechua section: translators avoided two extremes -- (1) the "modo tosco y corrupto de hablar" of some provinces, and (2) the excessive curiosity of some in Cuzco who used exquisite and obscure words outside proper Quichua limits (archaic Inga vocabulary, loans). They took the middle way: "lenguaje comun, facil, y proprio", and adopted the rule "de interpretar sentido por sentido, mas que palabra por palabra" (sense-for-sense rather than word-for-word). Attention was directed especially at provinces outside Cuzco (Guamanga to Quito, and lowland populations).
- Aymara section: aim was language neither "exquisito y obscuro al comun de los Indios" nor "tosco y grosero en demasia"; vocabulary general enough to be understood across the many Aymara-speaking nations listed (Aymaraes, Carangas, Collas, Lupacas, Pacajes, Charcas, etc.). Notes Aymara is "copiosa y de mucho artificio, y suave de pronunciar", with more synonyms and circumlocutions than Quichua.
- An Erratas page lists folio-specific corrections alternating Romance / Quichua / Aymara columns (e.g., fol. 20b Quichua "huaoacpachaman -> hanacpachaman").
