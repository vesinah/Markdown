## Publisher abstract (as deposited, Crossref)
First monograph covering the whole Portuguese grammatical tradition outside Portugal. Missionaries needed local languages to speak to potential converts; a great wealth of missionary grammars survives from the 16th century on -- some are precious records of the languages they document, all witness attempts to adapt familiar methods of grammatical description to dramatically new linguistic features. Languages covered: in Asia, Tamil, four Indo-Aryan languages, Japanese; in Brazil, Kipea and Tupinamba; in Africa and the African diaspora, Kimbundu and Sena (Angola and Mozambique). Each text is placed in historical and linguistic context; analysis of orthography, parts-of-speech system, morphology, syntax, pedagogical features, treatment of language variation and pragmatics, and missionaries' attitudes toward the languages studied.
