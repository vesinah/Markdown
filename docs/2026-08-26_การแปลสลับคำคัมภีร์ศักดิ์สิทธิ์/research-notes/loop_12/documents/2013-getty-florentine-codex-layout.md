## Facts stated on the page
- In August 1576, amid a plague outbreak, the Franciscan Bernardino de Sahagun and 22 indigenous artists worked at the school of Santa Cruz de Tlaltelolco, Mexico City, to create "the first illustrated encyclopedia of the New World".
- The manuscript is divided into 12 volumes ("12 books") and is now known as the Florentine Codex.
- "The completed manuscript reflects both indigenous and Spanish traditions of writing and painting, including parallel columns of text in Spanish and Nahuatl and over 2,000 watercolor illustrations prepared in both European and Aztec pictorial styles."
- Accompanying publication by Magaloni Kerpel announced for 2014 (Getty Research Institute Council sponsorship).
