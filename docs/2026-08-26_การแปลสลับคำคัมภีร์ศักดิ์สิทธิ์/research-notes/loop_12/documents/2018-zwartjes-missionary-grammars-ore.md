## Abstract key points (as deposited)
- Definition: "Missionary grammars are printed books or manuscripts compiled by missionaries in which a particular language is described", mainly pedagogical tools for language teaching/learning in a missionary-colonial setting; some have normative character.
- Canonical structure: opening prologue stating objectives; short introduction to phonology/orthography; largest section morphology by traditional parts of speech; sometimes syntax; sometimes appended word lists (body parts, measures, counting, manners of speaking, rhetorical figures).
- Data base: mainly oral corpus, but sometimes high registers from prestigious texts illustrating eloquence/elegance.
- Framework: modeled on Greco-Latin framework; missionaries adapted European models to new typological realities and created original works; entry concentrates on sources written in Spanish, Portuguese, and Latin.

## Reference-list evidence gathered (relevant items seen)
- Confirms Hovdhaugen 1996 chapter "Missionary grammars. An attempt at defining a field of research" (pp. 9-22, Nodus).
- Cites colonial American grammars including: Arte y Vocabulario en la lengua general del Peru (1586); Grammatica o arte de la lengua general de los indios de los reynos del Peru (1560); Gramatica y arte nueva de la lengua general de todo el Peru, llamada lengua Qquichua (1607); Arte y grammatica muy copiosa de la lengva aymara (1603 and 1612 editions); Arte de grammatica da lingoa mais usada na costa do Brasil (1595); Arte en lengva zapoteca (1578); Arte mexicana (1595); Arte de el idioma mexicano (1713); plus catechism-type works (Doctrina y ensenanza en la lengua maçahua, 1638; Confessonario en lengua mixe, 1733).
- Cites Zwartjes's own chapter on the mise en page of translated missionary texts (SIHOLS 122, 2014, DOI 10.1075/sihols.122.01zwa) and his study of teaching methods at El Colegio de Tlatelolco (2016).
