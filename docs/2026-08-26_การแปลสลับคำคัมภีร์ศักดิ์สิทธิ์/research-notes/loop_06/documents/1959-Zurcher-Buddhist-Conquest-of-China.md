# Extract: Erik Zurcher, The Buddhist Conquest of China

- Full title: The Buddhist Conquest of China: The Spread and Adaptation of Buddhism in Early Medieval China
- Publication: Leiden: E.J. Brill, 1959 (Sinica Leidensia XI); 2nd ed. 1972; 3rd ed. 2007 with foreword/introduction by Stephen F. Teiser; 2 vols (Text; Notes); DOI (Brill): 10.1163/ej.9789004156043.i-472
- Status: SEARCH-VERIFIED (Internet Archive record archive.org/details/buddhistconquest0000zrch; Google Books; Brill; WorldCat). Full text NOT fetched this session.
- Type: monograph (doctoral dissertation, Leiden)

## Key facts seen this session

- Standard work on Buddhism in China from Han to early 4th c. (vol. 1) and at Jiankang/the Southeast ca. 320-420 (vol. 2); based on dissertation begun early 1950s; Teiser's 2007 introduction calls it "the standard in the field".
- Index terms visible in Google Books preview include: Dao'an, Daode jing, geyi-adjacent themes via qingtan/xuanxue milieu, Lokaksema, Kumarajiva, Prajnaparamita, transcription practices, Zhi Qian - i.e., the book treats translators and their idioms centrally.
- Relevant to translation procedure scholarship: Zurcher mined early translations as repositories of Luoyang vernacular speech (per Boucher 1998: "Sinologists, led in the West by Erik Zurcher, have sought to mine these texts as repositories of early Chinese vernacular language... remnants of what appears to be the spoken idiom of Luoyang during the first few centuries C.E.").
- Nattier/CBC@ records show Zurcher rejected An Shigao's authorship of T105/T109 (accepted by Ui), showing his philological criteria for translator identification.
- MDPI Religions 12(8):634 (2021) notes the title can be read as a "Buddhist conquest of the Chinese language": Sanskrit concepts reshaped Chinese vocabulary analogous to Greek/Latin in European languages.
- Zurcher defined geyi (quoted via secondary sources this session) as "elucidating Buddhist terms, notably numerical categories (shu), with the help of notions extracted from traditional Chinese philosophy" - cited approvingly against the Daoist-vocabulary myth (see 2011-Mair file).
