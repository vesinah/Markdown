## Key facts seen this session

- States bluntly that "the arguments with the Five Transliterations are found in none of Xuan Zang's works"; first introduced via Fanyi mingyi ji vol. 1 by Fayun (Southern Song 1127-1279), quoting the five categories.
- Summarizes Fang (2006): three scholarly stances - wubufan as mere technical convention (Liang 1988); as core of Xuanzang's translation theory (Wang 1984); as retrospective summing-up of his experience, transliteration never being the ultimate method (Luo 1981).
- Corpus method: Sanskrit-Chinese parallel corpus of Vimalakirtinirdesa comparing Zhi Qian, Kumarajiva, Xuanzang versions on the three test terms of Fayun's list.
- Findings: Xuanzang systematically translated bhagavan as Shizun 世尊 / Fo Shizun inside sutra bodies, keeping Bojiafan 薄伽梵 only at opening/closing out of reverence; anuttara-samyak-sambodhi and prajna handled similarly; KUMARAJIVA's practice actually conforms better to the alleged Xuanzang rule. Concludes the Five Transliterations were "an inaccurate recapitulation... sorted by later generations" and probably not proposed by Xuanzang (aligning with Murata 1975 and Murata's cited Murata 1975 suspicion; also cites Murata's 1975 historical review).
- Adds phonology angle: Kukai complained Tang mandarin mantra readings strayed far from Sanskrit sounds; Lustaus (2019) replies that such criticism ignores regional/temporal sound change.
- Speculates Xuanzang balanced faithfulness vs readability: transliterations retained at fixed ritual positions (beginning/end of sutra) to show respect; meaning-rendered phrases elsewhere for comprehension.
