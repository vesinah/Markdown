## Key facts seen

- Attribution chain: Liang Qichao and others cited the five bufan from Zhou Dunyi's Fanyi mingyi xu; but Taiwan scholar CAO SHIBANG 曹仕邦 (1979, Zhongguo fojiao yijing shi yanjiu yushen zhi si 中国佛教译经史研究余渖之四) showed the true locus is Fayun's Fanyi mingyi ji juan 1, section "Ten Common Epithets", entry Pojialou 婆伽婆 - Zhou Dunyi merely excerpted (and miswrote 阎浮树 as 阎净树).
- Zanning 赞宁 (919-1001), Song gaoseng zhuan fasc. 3: "Dao'an discussed 'five losses and three not-easy'; Yanzong set out his 'eight preparations'; Mingze compiled translation liturgy; XUANZANG ESTABLISHED THE FIVE BUFAN. These are all like the general rules of the Zuozhuan, like a historian's variant usage" - names doctrine ~350 years after Xuanzang but gives no content or source; Fayun (~500 years later) gives content without source. Fang asks what lost work both may have seen and when it perished.
- Precedent debate: Yang Tingfu credited "Guangliang fashi 五不翻"; Qu Junfeng named Daliang 大亮 of Guangzhou; Fang checked Guanding's T553?/Daban niepan jing xuanyi juan 1: in explaining the sutra name under fan 翻 (four positions: none/existent/both/neither), Guanding quotes five masters incl. Daliang: "a single name contains many names; this is precisely why translators do not translate it" - i.e., only an ancestor of category 2 (polysemy). Fang concludes Daliang did NOT anticipate all five; but pre-Xuanzang discussions (e.g., Hongming ji materials) on Sanskrit-name rendering likely inspired Xuanzang; further excavation needed.
- Lists mainland scholars who kept citing Zhou Dunyi's preface as source (Ma Zuyi 1984; Yang Tingfu 1986; Chen Yangjiong 1995; Ma Pei 1997; Cheng Mei 2002) vs Taiwan adoption of Cao Shibang's correction (Wang Wenyan, Fodian hanyi zhi yanjiu).
