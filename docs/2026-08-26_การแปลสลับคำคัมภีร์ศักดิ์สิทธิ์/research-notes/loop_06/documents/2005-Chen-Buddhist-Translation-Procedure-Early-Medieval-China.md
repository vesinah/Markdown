# Extract: Jinhua Chen, "Some Aspects of the Buddhist Translation Procedure in Early Medieval China..."

- Full title: Some Aspects of the Buddhist Translation Procedure in Early Medieval China: With Special References to a Longstanding Misreading of a Keyword in the Earliest Extant Buddhist Catalogue in East Asia
- Journal: Journal Asiatique 293.2 (2005), pp. 603-661, Societe Asiatique, Paris
- Status: SEARCH-VERIFIED via NTU DDBC library record with bilingual abstract. Full text NOT fetched.
- Type: peer-reviewed article

## Key facts seen

- Re-reads the keyword chu/yichu 出/一出 in the 446-entry list of fascicle 2 of Chu sanzang jiji (CSZJJ): traditionally read as "(translation) completed", it actually denotes, depending on note-type: start of a translation; whole process for short texts; enunciation of the foreign text; oral translation; production of a draft subject to editorial/doctrinal revision; completion.
- Implication: earliest East Asian catalogue data encode a multi-stage translation WORKSHOP procedure (oral recitation -> transcription -> drafting -> polishing -> collation), essential for correctly dating early translations - complements Boucher's oral/aural model and Nattier's attribution criteria.
- Also studies obscure aspects of several early translation centers in China.
