# Extract: Jan Nattier, A Guide to the Earliest Chinese Buddhist Translations

- Full title: A Guide to the Earliest Chinese Buddhist Translations: Texts from the Eastern Han and Three Kingdoms Periods
- Series: Bibliotheca Philologica et Philosophica Buddhica X. Tokyo: International Research Institute for Advanced Buddhology (IRIAB), Soka University, 2008. 194 pp. ISBN 9784904234006 (pbk)
- Status: SEARCH-VERIFIED (NTU library record; CiNii BA85759608; Google Books; CBC@ dazangthings.nz source page summarizing its attribution judgments; author's own academia.edu OCR + Addenda & Corrigenda). Full text not fetched this session.
- Type: reference monograph

## Key facts seen this session

- Covers translations of Eastern Han and Three Kingdoms periods; early translations "offer a window into the Buddhist heritage of both India and China at a pivotal period in history" (p. 3, quoted in Brill chapter).
- Methodology: attribution criteria combining external evidence (Dao'an's Zongli zhongjing mulu 374, Sengyou's Chu sanzang jiji, later catalogues such as Fei Changfang) with internal lexical/stylistic evidence (transcription habits, four-character prosody, verse meter); texts absent from her indices are treated as not-genuine attributions ("silent method", p. 29 n.60).
- Example judgments recorded on CBC@: T361/T362 Larger Sukhavativyuha swap between Zhi Qian and Lokaksema following Harrison; T105/T109 rejected as An Shigao's (against Ui, partially against Zurcher); T313 Akshobhya-vyuha "surely thoroughly revised"; Kang Sengkai credited with no reliable texts; T735C provisionally linked to An Shigao circle.
- Addenda & Corrigenda page maintained by the author (academia.edu), updated version in preparation.
- Companion relevance: Nattier 2009 conference paper "Audiences, Agendas, and Translation Policy: Learning from Chinese Buddhist Translations" (Council on Translation of Buddhist Sutras, Los Angeles 2009).
