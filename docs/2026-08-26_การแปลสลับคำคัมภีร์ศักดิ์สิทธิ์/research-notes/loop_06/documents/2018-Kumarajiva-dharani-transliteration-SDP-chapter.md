## Key facts seen

- Kumārajīva was "the first [Chinese] translator to undertake a transliteration of the dhāraṇīs that attempted to retain their ritual efficacy for Chinese Buddhists" ("his was the first attempt to transliterate the dhāraṇīs in order to retain their sonic efficacy").
- Kumārajīva's transcription is a "fresh effort at transcription" of Middle Indic phonemes into Early Middle Chinese (using Pulleyblank 1983 EMC reconstructions).
- Dharmaraksa did NOT transcribe but translated/paraphrased the dharanis (T263, 286 CE); Jnanagupta-Dharmagupta (T264, 601) transliterated again; three further transcriptions by Jnanagupta(?), Xuanzang, Amoghavajra exist (Karashima 2001: 380-392).
- Uses Prakritisms in Kumarajiva's arapacana syllabary in the Dazhidu lun (anuppada < anutpada; cariya < carita; etc.) plus Karashima 1992 data to argue both Kumarajiva's and Dharmaraksa's sources predate surviving Sanskritization: Kumarajiva agrees with Central Asian recension 409x vs Nepalese/Gilgit 138x; Dharmaraksa 622 vs 230.
- Transmission framed as involving "dozens of human, temporal, spatial, dialectal, scribal, psychological and phonological variables", error-free transmission impossible; dharani study opens a window on Indo-Chinese information exchange.
- Ritual stakes: in Tiantai Lotus samadhi repentance (Fahua sanmei chanyi) recitation mistakes were impermissible (bude miuwu).
