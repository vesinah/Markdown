# Extract: Fanyi mingyi ji 翻譯名義集 (Collection of Meanings and Terms in Translation)

- Author/compiler: Fayun 法雲 (1088-1158), monk of Jingde Temple 景德寺, Pingjiang/Suzhou; self-styled Wuji zi 無機子
- Date: Song dynasty (compiler lived under Northern Song into Southern Song; Zhou Dunyi preface dated Shaoxing dingchou = 1157)
- Work: Taisho Vol. 54, No. 2131, 7 juan (other editions 14 or 20 juan), 64 sections, over 2,040 transliterated entries
- Status: FULL-FETCHED (preface text via tripitaka.zensoul.org/T/T2131/1 and zh.wikisource; structure via NTI Reader t2131.html; chinaknowledge.org description)
- Type: canonical bilingual glossary / lexicon

## Key facts seen this session

- The work collects Sanskrit terms scattered through sutras and treatises, lists variant translations, sources, and explanations; draws on phonetic glossaries (yinyi), commentaries, and secular texts; more than 400 works by over 100 authors cited; doctrinal explanations mainly follow the Tiantai school (chinaknowledge.org).
- Contains dedicated sections on important translators throughout history.
- THE KEY TEXT OF THE WUBUFAN: in juan 1, section "Ten Common Epithets" (十種通號), entry "Pojialou/Bhagavan 婆伽婆", Fayun writes:
  "唐奘法師明五種不翻：一、秘密故不翻，陀羅尼是。二、多含故不翻，如薄伽梵含六義故。三、此無故不翻，如閻浮樹。四、順古故不翻，如阿耨菩提，實可翻之。但摩騰已來存梵音故。五、生善故不翻，如般若尊重，智慧輕淺。令人生敬，是故不翻。"
  Translation seen: (1) secret/esoteric terms, e.g. dharani; (2) polysemous terms, e.g. Bhagavan with six meanings (from Fodi lun: 自在 sovereign power, 熾盛 blazing, 端嚴 majesty, 名稱 renown, 吉祥 auspiciousness, 尊貴 nobility); (3) things absent from China, e.g. jambu tree 閻浮樹; (4) deference to ancient usage, e.g. anouti puti (anuttara-samyak-sambodhi), which IS translatable but has kept Sanskrit sound since Moteng (Matanga); (5) terms generating reverence, e.g. prajna 般若 (revered) vs zhihui 智慧 (light/shallow).
- The layman Zhou Dunyi 周敦義 (self-styled "layman of Jingxi", Northern Song) wrote the Fanyi mingyi xu 翻譯名義序 (dated 1157), quoting Fayun's passage in abridged form; his preface frames the glossary as protecting readers against anti-Buddhist calumnies about translated names.
- Afterword by Pu Qie dated 1311 (Dade era) noted on ctext datawiki page.
- A Bodleian Library copy examined by Soothill had Sanskrit equivalents written above entries (per Soothill-Hodous dictionary preface, 1929 Hartford preface text seen at glossaries.dila.edu.tw).

## Notes for loop

This single entry inside a lexicon entry for "Bhagavan" - not a treatise - is the earliest extant full statement of the five categories attributed to Xuanzang. Zanning's Song gaoseng zhuan (fasc. 3) names the doctrine ("Xuanzang established the five bufan") ~350 years earlier than Fayun but without content (seen in Fang Guangchang repost).
