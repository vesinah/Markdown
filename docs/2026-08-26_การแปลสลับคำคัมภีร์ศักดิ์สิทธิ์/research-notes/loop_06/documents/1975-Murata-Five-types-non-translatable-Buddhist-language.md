# Extract: Chubee Murata (村中祐文? - name given as "Chubee Murata"), "The Five Types of 'Non-translatable Buddhist Language', within Scripture Translation into Chinese"

- Journal: Indogaku Bukkyogaku Kenkyu (Journal of Indian and Buddhist Studies) vol. 24, no. 52, 1975; DOI: 10.4259/ibk.24.52
- Language: Japanese; status: SEARCH-VERIFIED via J-STAGE search-result excerpts (abstract + body text quoted in results). Full PDF not fetched.
- Type: peer-reviewed journal article

## Key facts seen this session

- Murata defines wuzhong bufan as five cases where only the sound is copied and the sense is not translated.
- He traces early attestations: Song gaoseng zhuan fasc. 3, biography of Manyue: "Xuanzang established the five bufan... comparable to the rules of the Zuozhuan and the variant usages of historians" ("玄奘は五種不翻を立つ。これ皆左伝の諸凡に類し、史家の変例に同じ").
- The five categories are given per Fanyi mingyi ji preface: secret (dharani), polysemy (Bhagavan with six meanings), no Chinese equivalent (jambu tree), deference to antiquity (anuttara-samyak-sambodhi kept from Later Han onward), reverential effect (prajna vs "wisdom").
- Murata notes Mochizuki's dictionary bases Xuanzang's authorship on Zanning's Song gaoseng zhuan; Ryukoku's dictionary asserts it outright.
- CRITICAL ARGUMENT: if the five bufan were the master-plan of Xuanzang's translation bureau, one would expect mention in near-contemporary records such as the Cien zhuan (慈恩傳) or prefaces by his disciple-scribes; but "not a single word" about the five bufan appears there (searched including Daoxuan/Daoxuan who served among the nine "compilers of the draft").
- He tests category 1 against practice: dharani non-translation holds for Kumarajiva's and Jnanagupta's Lotus dharani chapters AND Xuanzang's own Heart Sutra mantra - only the oldest Dharmaraksa Zheng fahua jing translated dharani; so "dharani non-translation" was common to old and new translation eras, not distinctively Xuanzang's innovation.
- Category 2 test: Xuanzang opens with Bojiafan but then renders Shizun/Fo throughout - not consistently untranslating; Huilin's Yiqie jing yinyi juan 1 quotes Fodi lun on the six meanings and says polysemous terms keep Sanskrit ("此為文含多義譯経者故存梵記").
- Overall verdict per excerpt: wubufan fits Xuanzang's corpus roughly but shows more continuity with gu-yi/jiu-yi (old-translation) tradition than a new-program manifesto; he also problematizes the very term bufan since sound-copying (yinsha) into Chinese characters is itself a kind of translation; ends by splitting the question into three issues (attribution to Xuanzang's bureau; fit to actual practice; aptness of the word bufan).
