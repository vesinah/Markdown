## Key facts seen

- Critiques the teleological view that dharani/spells are proto-Tantric; traces that view to Chou Yi-liang, "Tantrism in China," HJAS 8 (1945): 241-332, and Strickmann's Mantras et mandarins (1996) / Chinese Magical Medicine (2002); argues instead that dharani were mainstream, ordinary practice in medieval Chinese Buddhism.
- Surveys canonical DEFINITIONS of dharani by Chinese intellectuals: Dazhidu lun (Kumarajiva), Jingying Huiyuan's Dasheng yizhang (which treats mantra as a subtype of dharani, per Bodhisattvabhumi mantra-dharani), Daoshi's Fayuan zhulin (dhutanga section for sin-eradication with 36 spells for practical needs - stopping bleeding, toothache cure, snake protection, memory retention), and Zongshi tuoluoni yizan attributed to Amoghavajra.
- Medieval writers used dharaṇī and mantra interchangeably; efficacy located in meditative cultivation and correct ritual procedure, not merely magic of words.
