## Method and case study (from 成果報告書)

- Input system built on Excel user forms; point-diagram (点図) dataset based on the 26
  charts in 築島裕編『訓点語彙集成』第一巻; positions later switched to an
  Excel-style coordinate scheme after real materials showed points missing from charts.
  Report notes that 国立国語研究所's 点図データベース uses numeric coordinates.
- Case study: 儀軌 (giki, Esoteric Buddhist ritual manuals) held by Kyushu University,
  9 items. Mantras/dharani are Sanskrit transliterated into Chinese characters;
  tone marks (声点) and kana marks (仮名点) were databased specifically for the mantra
  portions: 19,125 characters total in the mantra sections, of which 8,960 carry kunten.

## Findings reported (target D substance)

1. Tone marks on phonetically transcribed mantras do NOT reflect the Middle Chinese
   tone categories of the characters used (only ~30% agreement); instead, within each
   Sanskrit word a fixed marking pattern is observed -> monks treated transliterated
   mantras as "Sanskrit written in kanji", not as Chinese words.
2. Kana-point readings likewise deviate from standard character readings, e.g.
   薩嚩 (sarva) read サラバ, indicating awareness of the Sanskrit source.
3. Conclusion quoted: 音訳真言は「漢字（漢語）」ではない — kunten on transliterated
   mantras is a previously understudied category with new knowledge obtainable via
   database construction.
