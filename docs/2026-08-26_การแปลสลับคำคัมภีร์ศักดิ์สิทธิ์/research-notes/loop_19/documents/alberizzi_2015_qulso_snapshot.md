## Abstract (verbatim from PDF)

"The history of the Japanese written language can be seen as a main frame of different
registers of the native language – buntai 文体 – intersecting at various levels with a
foreign one, namely classical Chinese... This paper will a) provide a review of existing
scholarship on the role played by kunten materials in defining the formation process of
Sino-Japanese hybrid writing – wakan konkōbun; b) outline, for the first time, the main
differences between the two most controversial forms of written language, Japanized
written Chinese – waka kanbun – and Sino-Japanese hybrid writing... c) survey textual
evidence to show how an embryonic form of Sino-Japanese hybrid writing existed before
the twelfth century..."

Keywords: Kanbun kundoku, Kunten Materials, Sino-Japanese Hybridization, Wakan konkobun.

Footnote: findings from KAKENHI Grants-in-Aid project "Wakan konkobun in the Insei
period: The Case of Gukansho" (2014-2016), project director Valerio L. Alberizzi.
