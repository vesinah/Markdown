## Extracts (from abstract and surfaced summaries)

- Critiques prior research on speech-writing relations in East Asia and pre-20th-century Korea; asks how to name the ecology of spoken and written language in Korea and the wider East Asian formation.
- Following Sheldon Pollock's 'Sanskrit Cosmopolis,' proposes 'Sinographic Cosmopolis' for the East Asian regions using Literary Sinitic and sinographs.
- Argues the term 'diglossia' has little or no utility for speech/writing ecologies in pre-modern Korea or the Sinographic Cosmopolis: pre-modern Korea lacked a binary high/low divide, showing diverse intermediate inscriptional practices; alternatives suggested include 'hieroglossia' and 'hyperglossia'/superposition.
- Summaries also state that kundoku-style vernacular reading traditions "conceal local vernaculars behind Literary Sinitic hierarchies and practices."

- King lecture abstract (Ohio State EASC IKS lecture, https://easc.osu.edu/events/iks/rking, SEARCH-VERIFIED): introduces edited volume "Cosmopolitan and Vernacular in the World of Wen: Reading Sheldon Pollock from the Sinographic Cosmopolis"; argues against 'Sphere-speak'/'Sino-speak' and for 'Sinographic Cosmopolis.'

## Notes

- Supplies the theoretical frame for treating gugyeol/idu/eonhae as points on an inscriptional continuum rather than a diglossic binary - directly relevant to comparing Korean and Vietnamese reading strategies for Chinese-scripture originals.
