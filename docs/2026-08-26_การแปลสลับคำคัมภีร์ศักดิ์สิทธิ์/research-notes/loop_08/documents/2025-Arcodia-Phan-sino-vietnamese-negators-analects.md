## Extracts

- Corpus of THREE bilingual Classical Chinese-Vietnamese translations of the Analects, two in Nom script and one in Quoc ngu:
  - Luan ngu uoc giai 論語約解 (17th c.), earliest extant Vietnamese translation of the Analects; full title Tu thu uoc giai Luan ngu giai quoc am nghia tang bo Dai toan bi chi 四書約解論語解國音義增補大全備旨; part of the Four Books cycle translating AND annotating into Vietnamese in Nom script accompanied by Literary Sinitic annotations; anonymous translator; held at Institute of Sino-Nom Studies Hanoi, call number AB.270/1-5.
  - Tu Duc thanh che Luan ngu thich nghia ca 嗣德聖制論語釋義歌 (19th c.), 2,970 lines of luc bat verse composed by Emperor Tu Duc (r. 1848-1883), court-authorized engraving/printing c. 1896-1907 posthumously; contains Hue dialect forms (man rang, man ri, chu, ni).
  - Khong phu tu va Luan ngu (Pham 2004), modern Quoc ngu translation, National Political Publisher, Hanoi.
- Findings on negation transfer: in the last six Analects chapters there are 153 instances of 不 bu, 38 of 無 vu, 4 of 非 phi (195 sentences). The 17th-c. Nom translation rendered ALL of them with native chang - zero Sino-Vietnamese prefixes. The 19th-c. text is transitional: chang still dominant (90x for 不, 11x for 無) but khong rising (35x 不, 14x 無) and bat appears with innovative uses. In the 21st-c. text khong dominates (127/153 of 不; 27/38 無; all 4 非).
- Contextual claims: Chinese characters entered during Nam Viet (203-111 BCE) and Chinese-rule Giao Chau periods; the Han-Viet reading system emerged in the 8th-9th centuries under Tang influence and remains in use (citing Nguyen T.C. 1979; Phan 2013, 2025); Confucian examinations (1075-1919) sustained Literary Sinitic mastery; surveys show "the nine most important Confucian classics, including the Analects, were ALL translated into Vietnamese and recorded in chu Nom during the monarchal period" (citing T.C. Nguyen 2016).
