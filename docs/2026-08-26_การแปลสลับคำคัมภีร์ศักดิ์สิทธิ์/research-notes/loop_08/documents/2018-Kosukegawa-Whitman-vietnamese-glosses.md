- Same-session adjacent evidence that bilingual Hán-Nôm reading formats existed at scale (Digitizing Viet Nam project page, https://www.digitizingvietnam.com/en/our-collections/han-nom-collection, SEARCH-VERIFIED):
  - Two Nôm translation strategies described: summarizing main ideas into Nôm verse (e.g., Luan ngu thich nghia ca, lục bát rendition of the Analects in 20 chapters) versus directly translating each Chinese sentence into Nôm (e.g., Thi kinh giai am, rendering all 300+ Odes of the Book of Songs).
  - Co Chau Phap Van Phat ban hanh ngu luc 古珠法雲佛本行語錄 = bilingual Hán-Nôm prose: each Chinese phrase or sentence immediately followed by its Nôm translation engraved at half size - called a "line-by-line translation" format characteristic of Le-Trinh period works; woodblocks carved autumn of Canh Hung yr. 13 (1752) at Dau Pagoda; Hán prototype inferred mid-late 14th c.; Nôm portion (~2,360 characters) rendered byVien Thai; archaic lexicon incl. la-da attested only before late 17th c. (de Rhodes 1651 dictionary).
  - Companion work Co Chau Phat ban hanh is entirely Nôm lục bát verse; third work Hien Co Chau Phat to nghi entirely Classical Chinese ritual prose - one shrine, three registers.
  - Also notes Sino-Vietnamese pronunciation formed from late Tang/early Song phonetics, so Chinese script ceased feeling foreign, and that Nom was meant to complement rather than replace Chinese characters.
