## Extracts

- Beophwa-gyeong eonhae 法華經諺解 was published 1463 (Sejo yr. 9), the second sutra eonhae of the Gangyeongdogam after Neungeom-gyeong eonhae; woodblock, 7 vols. 7 books. No book printed at the time actually bore the label 'eonhae' in its title; the board-heart title is simply 法華經, full-title 妙法蓮華經.
- Base text (저본): Kumarajiva's Chinese Miaofa lianhua jing (translated 406) as annotated by Song-dynasty monk Gyehwan(戒環)'s Yaojie 要解, with Ming monk Iryeo(一如)'s Jizhu 集註. King Sejo personally attached jeongeum kugyol to the sutra text and the Yaojie; Yun Sa-ro and the Gangyeongdogam produced the translation.
- Page architecture described in detail: kugyol was added to the sutra text and Yaojie to create kugyol text (구결문); the translation was set beneath, separated by a circle mark (원권 ○) and printed in small characters in paired columns (쌍행). Iryeo's jizhu commentary was NOT translated - inserted verbatim in Chinese between source text and translation, in small paired columns. The sutra text is divided according to the kebun 科文 sectioning of Iryeo's jizhu. Layout called "very ingenious" (정교) for fitting much information into limited space, aimed at readers with deep Buddhist knowledge.
- Comparative translation register: Seokbosangjeol and Wolinseokbo have NO original text or kugyol text - target-language-only translations, relatively free (의역) with sometimes excessive native-Korean paraphrase of Sino-Korean lexicon; Wolinseokbo moved toward more literal translation despite lacking kugyol text, i.e., a transitional form toward the canonical 'eonhae' style. Beophwa-gyeong eonhae by contrast shows near word-for-word translation (축자역), a daeyeok 對譯 format built on the glossed kugyol text, with many lexical items carried over untranslated.
- Companion data seen this session (same author, KCI article ART001138622, 조선시대의 간경도감 간행의 한글 경전 연구, SEARCH-VERIFIED): Gangyeongdogam operated only 1461-1472 yet issued 30+ Chinese Buddhist books and 9 vernacular ones - Neungeom 1462, Beophwa 1463, Seungjong Yeonggajip 1464, Bulseol Amita-gyeong 1464, Geumgang-gyeong 1464, Banya simgyeong 1464, Wongak-gyeong 1465, Mokja su-simgyeol 1467, Sabeop-eo 1467. Common layout: text cut into paragraphs; large-character source text carries kugyol = the kugyol passage; eonhae follows in small double columns; yaojie-type material set one line lower in medium type; circle mark separates kugyol text from eonhae; black-fish-tail marks flank interpolated commentary (hyeopju).

## Notes

- Gives concrete, citable mechanics of the line-by-line alternation format (source-with-kugyol above, ○ divider, small double-column Korean below) that defines Joseon Buddhist eonhae.
- The same issue's related AKS entry 언해 (https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0036188, SEARCH-VERIFIED) defines eonhae generally: translating hanmun or baihua originals into hangul, characterized by paragraphed ORIGINAL text juxtaposed with the translation, the original usually carrying hangul kugyol; per colophons of Neungeom and Geumgang eonhae, kugyol was fixed first and translation followed it; also notes Confucian classic eonhae came roughly a century after the Buddhist ones.
