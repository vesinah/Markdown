## Extracts

- Object of study: a Han dien Nom (Chinese-with-Nom-expansion) Buddhist text on filial piety described as quite old and precious; woodblock printed BEFORE 1730 as a reprint of a prior edition that Hoang Thi Ngo dates to around the END OF THE FIFTEENTH CENTURY - making it reliable evidence for early Nom script and Vietnamese, preserving many archaic words and characters, from the Early Le period when Confucianism was at its height.
- Structure of the monograph: Part I introduces and studies the giai-am edition; Part II transcribes and annotates the entire giai-am text; Part III gives a lexicon with frequency statistics of pure Nom words in context.
- Analytical claims per publisher summary: classification of Nom character structures in the earliest period of Nom history; focus on a type of Nom character using TWO separate character codes to write one Vietnamese word/syllable; description of phonological, lexical, grammatical features of the Vietnamese in the edition; preliminary assessment of the translator's method.
- Related open-access essay by the same author (hannom.org.vn detail page param=1081, SEARCH-VERIFIED): the two-code Nom spellings preserve traces of Old Vietnamese sesqui-syllabicity - initial consonant clusters bl-, kl-, ml-, tl- written as two separate graphs (e.g., long 'cu+lung' vs 'ca lung'), preglottalized initials /?b/, /?d/ (adding Ferlus's /?j/ and Nguyen Ngoc San's /?g/); la-da 'hail' is an old disyllabic form of modern da attested in Viet-Bode-La dictionary; sacred sutra status explains why reprinters dared not emend the archaic gloss layer across reprintings (one reprint executed by Trinh Quan of the Trinh lords' clan).

## Notes

- Establishes that a fifteenth-century Vietnamese sutra already circulated in a glossed bilingual format (giai am = sound-explanation) whose archaic layers survived because sacral text was copied conservatively - a direct analogue to the conservative transmission of Korean kugyeol-glossed Tripitaka prints.
- Same-session adjacent item (Tap chi Nghien cuu Phat giao, https://www.tapchinghiencuuphathoc.vn/dieu-phap-lien-hoa-kinh-huong-hai.html, SEARCH-VERIFIED): Huong Hai thien su (Minh Chau Huong Hai, 1628-1715) left ~20 Nom exegetical works incl. Giai Phap Hoa Kinh, Giai Kim Cuong Kinh, Giai Sa Di Gioi Luat, Giai Phat To Tam Kinh; his Dieu Phap Lien Hoa Kinh (Institute of Han-Nom AB.488, 2 vols, ~1000 leaves) prints Kumarajiva's Chinese text large (~1x1 cm chars) followed by his smaller-script Nom translation (~0.5x0.7 cm) plus occasional term explanations after passages; described as earliest known PROSE Nom gloss of the Lotus Sutra, faithful to the Chinese text, about a century before Phap Lien's verse Phap hoa quoc ngu kinh (composed 1848, 3,214 luc bat lines + 350 song-thuat-luc-bat lines, first printed 1856). A separate page (nigioikhatsi.net, SEARCH-VERIFIED) records a colophon-dated nom edition: "Quoc dich Phap Hoa Kinh, ban nom, 1747" (Canh Hung yr. 8), preserved at the Han-Nom library Hanoi.
