## Extracts

- Seokbosangjeol 釋譜詳節 (1447) is identified here as "the first translated prose literature after invention of HunMinJeongEum"; Wolinseokbo 月印釋譜 (1459) has Wolincheongangjigok 月印千江之曲 as main text and Seokbosangjeol as explanatory apparatus.
- Volume mapping differs between the works: content of Seokbosangjeol vol. 11 appears as Wolinseokbo vol. 21 because the Buddha's parinirvana material had to fall near the end of the 25-vollife-of-Buddha compilation.
- Quantified register comparison via retention/publication ratio (게재율) of base text content: Seokbosangjeol vol. 11 = 49 percent; Wolinseokbo vol. 21 = 97 percent; Gangyeongdogam-style eonhae treated as 100 percent baseline. Hence spectrum: Seokbosangjeol (free/liberal) > Wolinseokbo (nearly literal but freer than Gangyeongdogam) > Gangyeongdogam eonhae (most literal).
- Both texts deal with Jijang bosal bonwon-gyeong (Ksitigarbha Sutra) material in these volumes.

## Notes

- Companion items seen this session: (a) Jo Hyunjin, "A comparative study on the translations between Vol. 11 of Seokbosangjeol and Vol. 21 of Wolinseokbo: focusing on the lexical difference" (2019, Niigata University repository https://unii.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/959, SEARCH-VERIFIED) compares lexical renderings of the SAME scripture base and attributes divergences to strict vs liberal translation choices. (b) sillokwiki 월인석보 entry (https://dh.aks.ac.kr/sillokwiki/index.php/월인석보, SEARCH-VERIFIED): structure = several stanzas of Wolincheongangjigok first, then the corresponding Seokbosangjeol-derived commentary; in Wolinseokbo the hanja is enlarged with small hangul readings at right (reversing the WCJG order), silent hanja syllables get an 'ng' final ㆁ, native words of Seokbosangjeol were partly replaced by harder Sino-Korean words, and mostly continuous syllable-block writing with occasional spaced writing appears. (c) encykorea 월인석보 E0041047 (SEARCH-VERIFIED): compiled by King Sejo merging Sejong's songs with his own Seokbosangjeol, printed 1459; vol. 1 front matter contains the eonhae of Hunminjeongeum; extant copies are incomplete across first edition (10 vols.) and reprint (4 vols.); designated Treasures.
