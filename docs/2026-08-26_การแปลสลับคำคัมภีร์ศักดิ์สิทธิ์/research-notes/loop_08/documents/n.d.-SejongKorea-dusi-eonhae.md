## Extracts

- Dusiwonhae is Korea's first vernacular-rendered poetry collection: Du Fu's poems as assembled in the Sejong-era compilation Chanju Bunlyu Dusi 纂註分類杜詩 (Yuan-dynasty base), eonhae-translated on King Seongjong's orders by officials Yu Yun-gyeom, Yu Huibok, Jo Wi and the monk Uichim; commissioned autumn 1481 (Chenghua xinchou year); printed as Eulhaeja movable-type edition, 25 vols. 17 books.
- Distinctive format flagged repeatedly: UNLIKE other eonhae, Dusiwonhae has NO to (입겿/interpunct glosses) attached to the original text, and the hanja inside its mixed hangul-hanmun eonhae text carries no hangul readings - i.e., neither kugyol layer nor embedded Sino-Korean pronunciation layer.
- Content organization: 1,647 Du Fu poems plus 16 poems by others (e.g., Yan Wu, Gao Shi), classified into 71 gates 門 (or 52-65 categories depending on counting: giro, sulhoe, hoego, sisa, etc.).
- Physical description (Heritage Service): half-leaf 8 lines x 17 characters standard, small characters and hangul types set together; bangjeom tone dots present, reflecting pre-1592 Korean; board-heart title 杜詩.
- Survival: first edition lacks vols. 1,2,4,5,12; scattered holdings at Seoul National University, Yonsei, Dongguk, Boseong High School etc.; photolithographic reprints 1954-1959 Tongmun-gwan, 1976/1978 Sejongdae-wang Kinyeom Sa-eophoe, 1984 Hongmun-gak. Reprint of 1632 (Injo yr. 10), supervised by Gyeongsang governor O Suk, woodblock in Daegu region - corrected rather than facsimile, hence evidence of 17th-century language; comparison of first and reprint editions tracks Middle-to-Early Modern Korean change (side dots, letters ㅿ and ㆁ still in 1481 edition).
- Dating controversy (earticle A189623, 『두시언해』초간본의 성립·인출 년대에 대한 재론): study of civil-service examination rosters reused as printing paper (paper-back documents) in Jangseogak vol. 10 shows the printing CANNOT precede winter 1482, so the dominant belief in 1481 completion/printing may need revision; methodology proposed for refining diachronic linguistic dating.

## Notes

- The absence of interpuncts/to in Dusiwonhae versus their presence in Buddhist sutra eonhae (Neungeom, Beophwa, Geumgang) is a key comparative datum for the loop: vernacular poetic translation chose mixed-script prose paraphrase without glossing layers, while sutra eonhae kept glossed originals facing translations.
