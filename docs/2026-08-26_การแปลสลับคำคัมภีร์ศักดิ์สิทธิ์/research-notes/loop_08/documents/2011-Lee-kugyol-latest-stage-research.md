## Extracts (from abstract and landing page)

- Defines the kugyol tradition as "a convention for adding Korean grammatical markers at appropriate places to aid the reading of originally Chinese texts."
- Article explains kugyol with concrete examples in three parts: (1) eumdok kugyol (transliteration kugyol), (2) seokdok kugyol (translation kugyol), (3) jeomto seokdok-kugyol (translation kugyol with point marks).
- Frames the topic via differences between Chinese, Korean, and Japanese languages, and stresses the importance of Buddhist texts in developing written languages in Korea and Japan (parallel drawn with Japanese kunten).
- Reports that the latest developments in kugyol research came in the 21st century after the discovery of the Yugasijiron (Yogacarabhumi-sastra) material in 2000, including detailed point maps; some questions concerning jeomto seokdok-kugyol remain open.
- Reference list (captured in full) maps the field: Chang K. (2002, 2003) on correspondences between kugyol characters and kugyol points in Yugasajiron vols. 5, 8 and the "indicator line"; Chung J. (1999) "Seokdokkugyeol in the Koryo Period" (Seoul Journal of Korean Studies 12); Chung J. et al. (2003) on Korean hakpil (angle-pen) buhok kugyol texts vs Japanese kunten texts focused on Huayan jing materials; Kobayashi Y. (2002, 2003) on relations between Korean gakpil-jeomto and Japanese old kunten, incl. Pan-bi-lyang-lon (Banbiryangnon) at Kyoto Otani University; Lee S. (2003) on Korean versions of the Surangama-sutra (Neungeom-gyeong); Lee S. et al. (2005-2006) decipherment/translation volumes of cipher kugyols in Chojodaejanggyeong and Jubon Hwaeomgyeong; Nam P. (2000, 2002) on Goryeo jeomto kugyol and reconstruction of Silla kugyol; Park J. (2003-2006) on cipher/mark gugyols in Zhou-ben and Jin-ben Huayanjing.
- Keywords: interpretation of Buddhist texts; Korean; classic Chinese; eumdok kugyol; seokdok kugyol; jeomto kugyol; jeomto.

## Notes

- Useful as an English-language map of the specialist journal Gugyeol Yeongu (Kugyol Studies, ISSN 1226-6019) and of the decipherment program for point-and-line (gakpil/jeomto) glosses scratched into Tripitaka prints.
- Confirms the standard tripartite typology of kugyol (sound-reading / sense-translation / point-marked translation) that recurs across other sources gathered this session.
