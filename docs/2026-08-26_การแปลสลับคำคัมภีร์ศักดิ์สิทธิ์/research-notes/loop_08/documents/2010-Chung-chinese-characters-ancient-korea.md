## Extracts (from abstracts)

- Examines three systems for rendering vernacular Korean with Chinese-borrowed characters in the ancient period: idu, gugyeol, and hyangchal.
- Key typological claim: idu and hyangchal are systems that TRANSCRIBE vernacular Korean (rather than translate), whereas gugyeol "can be characterized as translating Korean vernacular sentences in terms of the meaning of Chinese-borrowed characters" - i.e., gugyeol is a reading aid applied to Chinese originals.
- Developmental sequence proposed for gugyol: it started from indication of reading order using "to 吐" marks, then created symbol-gugyeol using jeomto marks, then interpretative-reading gugyeol using to marks; the author notes this process parallels idu's development. Keywords include yeokdokseon 逆讀線 (reversal line), Hunminjeongeum, phonetic-reading gugyeol.
- Companion article "The Creation of Idu" (same issue; https://accesson.kr/kj/v.50/2/97/8235): examines how idu was created from epigraphs and newly found wooden tablets; criticizes hypotheses that idu originated in Goguryeo or Baekje and highlights Silla's culmination of idu evolution from the mid-sixth century; Silla materials attest efforts to use Chinese characters to transcribe Korean sounds; Silla idu achieved transition to an agglutinative written language with its own punctuation, case markers, sentence-final endings, prefinal endings.
- Same companion abstract: Silla idu presumably formed the basis both of the hyangchal transcription principle (word stem read by meaning, ending read phonetically) AND of the gugyeol principle ("morphological affixes are inserted in between Chinese sentences in the interpretation of classical Chinese texts").

## Notes

- This pair of articles supplies the cleanest found-this-session statement of the contrast between transcription systems (idu, hyangchal) and reading-gloss systems (gugyeol) that the loop topic requires.
- Bibliography visible in the mirrors points to foundational Korean-language studies: Nam Pung-hyun, Idu Yeongu (A Study of Idu), Seoul: Taehaksa (2000); Nam (2006) on development of the Chinese-borrowing writing system in ancient times (Gugyeol Yeongu 16); Lee Ki-moon (1981) on the origin of idu (Jindan Hakbo 52); Lee Yong (2008) retrospective on ancient and medieval idu studies (Gugyeol Yeongu 21).
