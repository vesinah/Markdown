## Extracts (from bilingual abstract)

- Kugyol(口訣) is defined as one of the methods of writing Korean using the phonetic or meaning values of Chinese characters, intended for understanding texts written in classical Chinese. Kugyol divides into Eumdokkugyol(音讀口訣) and Seokdokkugyol(釋讀口訣).
- Seokdokkugyol was devised to understand classical Chinese texts "by translating them into nearly perfect Korean sentences"; the resulting seokdok sentences share many features with 15th-century eonhae texts and reveal the origin of peculiar phenomena in 15th-century eonhae.
- However, seokdokkugyol did NOT fully overcome the gap between Chinese and Korean syntax: subjects/predicates of existential sentences, modifier/modificand, and predicate/object are often split into two separate clauses, unlike 15th-century eonhae.
- Seokdokkugyol materials are decisive data for pre-Hangul Korean; because their transcription method and usage closely resemble hyangchal, they enable exact decipherment of hyangga (hyangchal poetry).
- Six seokdokkugyol sources currently known (with dates given in the abstract): Seok Hwaem gyobungi 釋華嚴敎分記 (mid-10th c.); Daebanggwangbul Hwaeom-gyeong 大方廣佛華嚴經 vol. 35 (early 12th c.); same sutra vol. 14 (mid-to-late 12th c.); (Habbu) Geumgwangmyeong-gyeong 合部金光明經 vol. 3 (after mid-13th c.); Guyeo Inwang-gyeong 舊譯仁王經 vol. 1 (after mid-13th c.); Yugasajiron 瑜伽師地論 vol. 20 (late 13th c.).
- Across these six materials there is little difference in kinds of kugyol characters or their usage; they differ only in precision of notation and degree of abbreviated writing.
- Keywords: method of writing, Seokdokkugyol, Seok-hwaeomgyobungi, Daebanggwangbul-hwaeomgyeongso, Daebanggwangbul-hwaeomgyeong, (Habbu) Geumgwangmyeonggyeong, Guyeokinwanggyeong, Yugasajiron.

## Notes

- Companion fact seen this session in the AKS encyclopedia entry 화엄경소석독구결 (https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0074499, SEARCH-VERIFIED): the Hwaeom-gyeong-so vol. 35 seokdok glosses use 61 different kugyol characters totalling 4,802 occurrences; unique characters 捨[ba-ri], 與[yeo], 甲[gap], 恨[heun] occur only there; right-side vs left-side to positions encode Korean word-order reordering, with reverse-reading dots (yeokdok-jeom) almost unused; an example gloss renders 於身心不生貪愛 as (於)身心[agui] 貪愛[eul] 生[nail] 不令[andal-hae-i-ha], i.e. full native case markers and negation; the entry distinguishes character-glossed (jato) vs dot-glossed (jeomto) seokdokkugyol and two transmission lineages (Hwaeom-gyeong type vs Yugasajiron type).
- Same entry states these glosses are precious evidence for Old Korean lexicon and grammar around the 12th century.
