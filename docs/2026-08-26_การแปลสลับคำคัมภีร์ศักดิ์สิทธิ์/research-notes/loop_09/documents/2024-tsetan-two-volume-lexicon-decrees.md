## Extracted facts

- The sGra sbyor bam po gnyis pa ("Two Volume Lexicon"), also known as Madhyavyutpatti or the "Middle Vyutpatti" (bye brag tu rtogs byed 'bring po), is treated by the author as an imperial translation manual issued as royal decree (bkas bcad).
- New fragments of the Lexicon discovered at Tabo monastery in Spiti, Himachal Pradesh, suggest it was very likely composed in 783 CE during the reign of King Trisong Detsen (khri srong lde btsan, r. 755-797 CE), per this author.
- The article compares two versions of the first part of the Lexicon containing imperial decrees: a Tabo version (decree dated 783) and a version stored in the Tibet Museum (bod ljongs rten rdzas bshams mdzod khang) in Lhasa (decree dated 814).
- Keywords of the article: Translation, Two Volume Lexicon, Madhyavyutpatti, sgra sbyor bam po gnyis pa, Tibetan imperial translation. The framing term is dharma bsgyur ba'i thabs, "methods of translating the dharma."
- Purpose per abstract: to illustrate each dharma-translation method witnessed in the decrees and use historical Indo-Tibetan translation procedure as guidance for modern projects translating Buddhist texts from Tibetan into English and Chinese.
