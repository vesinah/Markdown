## Extracted facts (from the published abstract)

- The Madhyavyutpatti (Tib. Bye brag tu rtogs byed 'bring po) is included in the Bstan 'gyur, Derge edition vol. co, ff. 131b1-160a7, and can be viewed as a commentary on the Mahavyutpatti (Bye brag tu rtogs byed chen po, Derge vol. co, ff. 1-131a4).
- The Mahavyutpatti is described as a Sanskrit-Tibetan lexicon mainly of Buddhist terms, "presumably compiled by Indian panditas and Tibetan lotsavas under the auspices of pious Tibetan kings Khri lde srong btsan and Ral pa can in the early IX century," for systematic treatment of Buddhist terminology during canon translation.
- Size figures (per the Ishihama and Fukuda edition): the Mahavyutpatti comprises 9492 lexical units organized under 277 thematic headings.
- The Madhyavyutpatti (alternatively titled Sgra sbyor bam po gnyis pa) consists of two sections: (1) an introductory description of principles implemented by Indian scholars and Tibetan translators while translating, and (2) a concise commentary on Tibetan equivalents of 413 Buddhist Sanskrit lexical units organized under 50 thematic headings (170 and 243 entries per section respectively); plus a colophon.
- The introduction proclaims it necessary to follow "the authoritative system of established Sanskrit-Tibetan translation equivalents" with general procedures to handle normative equivalents; the main part comments on form and meaning of 413 equivalents using special terminology and description models.
- Prior scholarship named in abstract: Nils Simonsson translated the colophon, introduction and selections from the commentary; Pieter C. Verhagen analyzed it as the earliest Tibetan work of scholarship on Sanskrit. Complete Western translation/thorough study claimed still unaddressed as of writing.

## Note

- Dating differs between sources seen this session: Tsetan (2024) places the sGra sbyor decree composition at 783 CE under Trisong Detsen; Grokhovskiy's abstract says early 9th century under Khri lde srong btsan (Sadnalegs) and Ral pa can. Both were seen online; reconciliation is an open question.
