## Extracted facts

- First Buddhist text translated from Tibetan into Mongolian: Shantideva's Engaging in Bodhisattva Behavior (Bodhisattvacaryavatara), by the [Uighur] translator Chokyi Ozer (Chos-kyi 'od-zer) during reign of Yuan emperor Khaisan Kulug (Wuzong, 1308-1311).
- The Mongols translated most of the Tibetan Kangyur under Altan Khan (1507-1582); work finished 1628-1629 under Ligdan Khan (ruled 1603-1629).
- Kangxi (r. 1661-1722) sponsored slight editorial revision and block printing of the Mongolian Kangyur between 1718 and 1720.
- Mongolian Tengyur completed 1742-1749 under Qianlong patronage and supervision of Changkya Khutugtu Rolpe Dorje (1717-1786); Changkya then supervised compilation of the Manchu Kangyur from the Chinese Tripitaka, started 1772, completed 1790.
- As part of that project Changkya supervised a large Tibetan-Mongolian lexicon, Dag-yig mkhas-pa'i 'byung-gnas ("A Lexicon Resource for the Learned"), completed 1741-1742; structured as two separate parallel word lists (one Tibetan, one Mongolian) - contrasted on this page with the Mahavyutpatti, which places Sanskrit terms side-by-side with Tibetan equivalents "and later the Chinese equivalents as well."
- The Tibetan-Mongolian lexicon has eleven chapters: (1) prajnaparamita, (2) madhyamaka, (3) abhidharma, (4) vinaya, (5) Indian tenet systems, (6) tantra, (7) logic, (8) Sanskrit grammar, (9) architecture and artisanship, (10) medicine, (11) archaisms and their modern equivalents (brda gsar-rnying).
- Changkya's introduction to the lexicon gives guidelines for qualities of a good translator and how to translate Tibetan texts (linked primary text: Preface to "A Lexicon Resource for the Learned").
