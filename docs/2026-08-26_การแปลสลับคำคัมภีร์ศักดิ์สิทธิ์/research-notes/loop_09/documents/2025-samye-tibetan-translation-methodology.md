## Extracted facts

- Claim: under sponsorship of Tibetan emperors, bilingual lexicons were compiled by Indian pandits and Tibetan lotsawas; "Classical Tibetan grammar was a created language, designed to convey the precise meanings of Buddhist terminology."
- The page attributes the gathering at Samye Monastery to the early 9th-century king Thride Songtsen, "better known as Sadnalegs," youngest son of Tri Songdetsen, instructing scholars to compile a classical Tibetan language; systematic method relying on loan translations; creation of grammatical manuals and dictionaries. [Note: this attribution differs from other sources seen this session; flagged in findings.]
- The sGra sbyor bam po gnyis pa is described as an important classical Tibetan grammar commentary that outlines how etymological treatment of Sanskrit terms should be translated by fixed equivalents into Tibetan, with strict adherence to agreed-upon definitions; it "serves as a commentary on" the Mahavyutpatti.
- Mahavyutpatti glossed as "The Great Volume of Precise Understanding or Essential Etymology"; includes "over 9500 terms with precise Sanskrit-Tibetan translations."
- Quoted introduction to the Mahavyutpatti: the king requested preceptors from the west - Acarya Jinamitra, Surendrabodhi, Silendrabodhi, Danasila, Bodhimitra - and Tibetan preceptors Ratnarakshita, Dharmatashila, plus learned translators Jnanasena, Jayarakshita, Manjushrivarman, Ratnendrashila and others, "to translate from Indic languages into Tibetan the terminologies of the Great and the Small Vehicles, to define the terms, and make a written word list."
- The team corrected mistakes and misinterpretations of older translations, restored omissions, reduced wordy/confusing translations.
- Example sGra sbyor-style entry for "Buddha": Sanskrit etymological derivations quoted (mohanidraprabuddhatvat... "because he has awakened from the sleep of delusion he is like a man who has awakened"; buddher vikashanad buddha... "intelligence unfolds widely") yielding Tibetan sang rgyas ("Awakened and Flowered").
