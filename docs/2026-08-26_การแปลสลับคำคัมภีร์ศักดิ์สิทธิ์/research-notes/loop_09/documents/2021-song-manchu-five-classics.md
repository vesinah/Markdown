## Extracted facts (from abstract)

- From the start of the Qing dynasty, Confucianism was both a vital component of national cultural policy and a means for rulers to build spiritual authority and implement imperial governance.
- Many Confucian classics represented by the Five Classics and Four Books were translated or compiled into Manchu; their translations were used not only for imperial examinations but also as textbooks in the Eight Banners Official Schools.
- Three stated functions of translating the Five Classics for the ruling class (poetics, politics, culture): (1) an important way for the Eight Banners to learn the mainstream academic system and traditional moral standards of the Han; (2) reading the translations shortened the cultural and psychological gap between Manchu and Han, eased tensions, enhanced exchange, consolidated governance.
