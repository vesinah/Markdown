## Extracted facts

- The Manchu Canon was created when in 1772 the Qianlong emperor ordered translation of the Buddhist canon into the "language of the Qing" (qing wen, i.e., Manchu); completed ca. 1790 with an imperial foreword.
- Only twelve sets of the canon were printed and distributed. Original woodblocks survived; in 2002 the Zijincheng Publishing House of the Beijing Palace Museum reprinted twenty copies from the original woodblocks; DDBC acquired one set.
- The Manchu Canon contains ca. 732 works; each set consists of 108 boxes plus one unnumbered box containing the catalog in four languages (Manchu, Chinese, Tibetan and Mongolian).
- Project outputs: image database of digitized cases (three made available, including the Heart Sutra and Diamond Sutra); improved catalog in Chinese, Manchu and Tibetan; a Chinese-Manchu-Sanskrit(-Mongolian) glossary of Buddhist terms; research bibliography.
- Relevance: documents Qing quadrilingual/multilingual canonical apparatus parallel to the Mongolian Kanjur tradition, with multilingual catalog and glossary serving terminology control across sacred languages.
