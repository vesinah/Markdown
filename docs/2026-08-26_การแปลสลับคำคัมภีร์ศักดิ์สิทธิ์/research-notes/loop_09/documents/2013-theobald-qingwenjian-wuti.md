## Extracted facts

- The Qingwenjian ("Mirror to the national language", officially Yuzhi qingwen jian) is a series of multilingual dictionaries compiled on imperial order; "national language" = Manchu. Oldest version compiled 1673-1708.
- Qianlong-era extensions named: Man-Meng wen jian (Manchu-Mongol), Liangti/Santi qingwen jian (three languages incl. Manchu, Mongolian, Tibetan), Zengding qingwen jian (finished 1771), Manzhu-Menggu-Hanzi sanhe qieyin qingwen jian, Siti qingwen jian (Manchu-Mongolian-Chinese-Tibetan), and the Wuti qingwen jian in five languages (+ Turki/Uyghur). Manchu title of Wuti: Han-i araha sunja hacin-i hergen kamciha manju gisun-i buleku bithe.
- Original Qingwenjian: 46 juan + 4 juan supplement, zonggang 8 juan + bu zonggang 2 juan; a handbook preserving old Manchu written before the 1632 script reform (tongki fuka aku hergen vs tongki fuka hergen).
- The 1708 book had 280 themes with more than 12,000 words; lemmata arranged alphabetically. Zengding version: 35 chapters, 292 themes; each lemma written in modern Manchu script and translated into Chinese, with reciprocal phonetic transcriptions (Manchu into Chinese, Chinese explanation into Manchu).
- Sanhe qieyin example illustrated from the 1771 three-language mirror: Manchu niohon abka "blue sky/heaven" rendered via fanqie transcription into Chinese characters plus direct transcription niuhuan abuka; Mongolian koke tngri likewise fanqie-transcribed and directly transcribed kuke tenggeli; Chinese cangtian transcribed into Mongolian and Manchu. (Copy owned by Staatsbibliothek Berlin.)
- Wuti qingwen jian: finished around 1790 (no preface; exact date unknown), brought into book form not before 1805; 32 juan; vocabulary in 36 encyclopaedic fields, 292 categories and 556 sub-categories; total 17,052 words; supplement adds 26 categories and 1,619 words (total 18,671 lemmata).
- For each word 8 entries are provided: Manchu word; Tibetan counterpart enriched by a literal qieyin and a phonetic duiyin transcription into Manchu; Mongolian counterpart; Turki counterpart with phonetic transcription into Manchu; finally the Chinese counterpart.
- Value noted: multi-language administrative terminology of the Qing, and linguistic study of five languages, especially Tibetan whose modern pronunciation differs from archaic written form, and Turki dialects.
- Modern editions: Minzu Press facsimile of palace-library manuscript 1957 (3 vols); Tamura Jitsuzo 1966 Gotai shinbunkan yakukai (Tokyo University) with Latin transcription (2 vols); another specimen in the British Museum; Oliver Corff 2013 annotated latinized German version (Fuenfsprachenspiegel), Wiesbaden: Harrassowitz.
- Included in Siku quanshu (Qingwenjian and Sanhe qieyin qingwen jian as manuscripts); first also in Chizaotang Siku quanshu huiyao.
