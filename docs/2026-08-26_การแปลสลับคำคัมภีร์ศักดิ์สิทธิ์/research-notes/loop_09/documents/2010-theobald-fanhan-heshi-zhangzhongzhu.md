## Extracted facts

- Title translated "Simultaneous Tangut-Chinese pearls-in-the-hand"; a Tangut-Chinese dictionary/glossary compiled by Gule Maocai gu le mai cai, described as a Tangut historian at the court of emperor Renzong of Western Xia (r. 1140-1193); finished 1190.
- Lost with the Mongol conquest; rediscovered in the ruins of Khara-Khoto (Heishui/Heicheng, Ejin Banner, Inner Mongolia) in 1909 by Pyotr K. Kozlov (1863-1935).
- The Kozlov copy is a woodblock print of 37 pages in butterfly binding.
- Bilingual preface (once in Tangut, once in Chinese) stresses the importance of learning two languages to administer an empire governed by a Tangut dynasty and inhabited by Chinese subjects.
- Cross-transcription design: every Tangut word is transcribed with Chinese characters, and the Chinese words are transcribed with Tangut script - i.e., reciprocal phonetic glossing.
- Arrangement encyclopaedic rather than alphabetical ("rather a glossary than a real dictionary"): sections on heaven; stars and planets; nature; earth; rivers and mountains; minerals, plants and animals; humans; the human body; and human activities. The last chapter covers almost half the book and includes kinship, Buddhism, architecture, tools, clothing, agricultural tools, administration/politics/law, musical instruments, food, equestrian implements, matrimonial terms etc.
- Editions noted: first facsimile printed in the Soviet Union; first Chinese edition by Luo Fucheng luo fu cheng (1885-1960) in 1924; modern facsimile 1989 by Ningxia Renmin Press (Gule Maocai comp.; Huang Zhenhua et al. ed.).
- Cited scholarship includes Nie Hongyin 1987 on the phonetic notation symbols of the Zhangzhongzhu (Yuyan yanjiu), Jing Yongshi 2013 on reconstructing the Russian-held copy's numbering and block-print history (Ningxia shehui kexue), Shi Jinbo encyclopedia entries, and Ivanov 1909 report on Tangut manuscripts from Khara-Khoto.
