## Key facts extracted

1. Ottoman/Turkic translation history: "Turkic translations of the Qur'an have a long history, dating back to at least the 13th or 14th century, and there are dozens of extant interlinear manuscript translations from the 15th to 19th centuries."
2. Format logic of interlinear translations: "Often scribbled below the calligraphic Arabic Qur'anic text, these translations in terms of format make no suggestion of replacing the Qur'an or of being equal to it, nor do they appear to have been used for ritual recitation, as most lack any indication of vowel markings."
3. Debate ignition: after the 1908 Constitutional Revolution, a public debate on whether commentaries should be composed in Turkish erupted between Ahmed Midhat (1844-1912) and Mustafa Sabri (1869-1954); the journal Sirat-i Mustakim began publishing Turkish renderings of verses immediately after 1908 but avoided calling them "translations". Russian Muslim reformist Musa Jarullah Bigiev argued translating the Qur'an was not only permissible but obligatory.
4. First printed translations (1924): Tevfik (1865-1939), prolific popularizer; and Huseyin Kazim Kadri (under pen name Seyh Muhsin-i Fani), whose Nur ul-Beyan was described by its author as an "explanatory translation" (tercume-i tefsiri) with format: Arabic verse, then translation, then explanatory passage. Kadri called translation "the genre of hard times", substituting for a declining commentary tradition.
5. Reception: Sebilurreşad published detailed error-lists against Nur ul-Beyan while respecting the translators personally as unqualified; pro-translation arguments stressed that Arabic speakers are a minority among Muslims.
6. State response: private amateur translations prompted the Turkish Grand National Assembly to sponsor a devout member of the late-Ottoman ulema - Elmalili Muhammad Hamdi Yazir - whose Hak Dini Kur'an Dili took the form of a traditional multivolume commentary containing verse translations, not an "authorized version"; the author rejected the idea of replacing or "translating" the Qur'an outright.
7. Commonly cited earlier Turkish tefsir works of the late Ottoman period named: Mehmet Debbagzade Antebi, Tefsir-i Tıbyan (Cairo/Bulaq 1842); Ismail Ferruh's translation of al-Kashifi's Tefsir-i Mevakib (Istanbul 1865).
