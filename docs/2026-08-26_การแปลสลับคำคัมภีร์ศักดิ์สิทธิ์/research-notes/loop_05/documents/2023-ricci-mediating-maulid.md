## Key facts extracted

1. The article studies five manuscripts of the Maulid Syaraf al-Anam ("The birth of the best of mankind", a popular Arabic panegyric recited for the Prophet's birthday), all carrying interlinear translations written between the lines of the Arabic.
2. Geographic spread of the five copies (late 18th to late 19th centuries): Aceh; the 'Malay' diaspora in colonial Ceylon (Sri Lanka); Patani (southern Thailand); Mecca - these four translate into Malay; the fifth, from Java, translates into Javanese.
3. Argument: through close reading of one narrative section across the translations and comparison with a vernacular telling, Ricci engages questions of standardisation, creativity, and cultural particularity within what she calls the wide, yet little-studied realm of interlinear translation from the region.
4. Keywords assigned: Interlinear; Javanese; Malay; Maulid Syaraf al-Anam; translation. Scopus citations at retrieval: 5.
5. Relevance beyond Qur'an proper: shows that interlinear translation was a pan-genre technology of Arabic-vernacular accommodation in the Indonesian-Malay world (Qur'an, tafsir, maulid devotional poetry alike), not a one-off format.
