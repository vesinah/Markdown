## Per-source notes

**1. Gallop 2022 (RAS blog) - FULL-FETCHED.** Royal Asiatic Society Arabic 4 is "the only known single-volume Qur'an with a full interlinear translation in Malay": nine lines of Arabic per page with a fine Malay hand sloping beneath each line, red surah headings, gold verse roundels (very rare in Southeast Asia), Asian laid paper; presented to the RAS in 1830 by Admiral C. M. Pole after his 1773-1778 East Indies service; dated on evidence to the early 18th century. Its Malay text is nearly identical to that of two 18th-century Banten Qur'ans (PNRI A.51, W.277) studied by Nurtawab, who argues such interlinear translations constitute *tafsir* because they embed interpretation and variant views (e.g., Q 18:11 gloss: "some say their sleep lasted 309 years"). Banten is linked to 2 of the 4 other known interlinear-Malay Qur'ans and 3 of the 11 known Qur'an manuscripts with Javanese translations. The post frames these manuscripts as filling the ~250-year gap between 'Abd al-Ra'uf's Tarjuman al-Mustafid (2nd half 17th c., Aceh) and Tafsir Nur al-Ihsan (Kedah, 1925-1927).

**2. Columbia exhibition - FULL-FETCHED.** Smith or. 222 (colophon 1154/1741, India?) displays the canonical bilingual convention: Arabic in black naskh, Persian in smaller red nasta'liq between the lines. Curatorial text states plainly that "at a first glance a Persian translation of the Quran appears as a blatant violation of the Islamic dogma of the inimitability", yet Persian glosses survive in tenth-century Qur'an manuscripts and early Persian tafsir translations; when complete Persian interlinear mushafs became common is "a matter of debate" since most extant Arabic-Persian copies were made after 1500 CE. Ottoman Burke MS 4 (1175/1761-62) shows post-1500 illuminated Qur'ans as middlebrow trade goods, with colophons kept in Arabic even in Ottoman production.

**3. Tehran Times 2026 (Zaferani Quran) - FULL-FETCHED.** The oldest known complete and dated Persian-translated Qur'an was copied 546 AH (1151-52 CE) by Abolfazl Zaferani in Rey (Reza Abbasi Museum, Tehran); an eight-year facsimile project was unveiled at the National Library and Archives of Iran in July 2026. Its interlinear Persian is closely associated with the 11th-century commentary of Abu Bakr Atiq Nishapuri (Surabadi). Speakers framed both legitimacy (Emadi Haeri: independent scholarly tradition rooted in early Iranian exegesis; early scholars legitimised Persian Qur'an translation) and limits (linguist Ali Ashraf Sadeghi: "a perfect, hundred-percent translation ... is practically impossible"); Yahaqi called the 6th century AH a golden age of Persian Qur'anic exegesis/translation.

**4. Yahaghi 2002 - SEARCH-VERIFIED.** Periodises Persian Qur'an translation into an "early period" (informal and formal translations) and a "mature period" (comprehensive developed style). Records the traditional narration that Salman al-Farisi made the earliest attempts to render the Qur'an into Persian in the Prophet's lifetime (citing Zurqani on the people of Fars). Short programmatic piece (pp. 105-109) useful as the standard entry citation for early Persian vernacularisation.

**5. Ricci 2011 - SEARCH-VERIFIED.** Uses one Arabic work (Book of One Thousand Questions) and its 16th-20th-century Javanese, Malay, Tamil adaptations to theorise the "Arabic cosmopolis" of South/Southeast Asia; chapter 2 is titled "On 'Translation' and Its Untranslatability" - the theoretical core for how vernacular Islamic texts coexist with Arabic authority rather than replace it. Thesis: literary translation and conversion were interconnected forms of globalization. Won AAR Best First Book and Harry J. Benda Prize; reviews praise its tri-vernacular range and frame it against both "indigenous uniqueness" and "derivative Arabization" readings.

**6. Ricci 2023 (Maulid) - SEARCH-VERIFIED.** Five Maulid Syaraf al-Anam manuscripts (late 18th-late 19th c.) from Aceh, Ceylon's Malay diaspora, Patani, Mecca (Malay interlinear) and Java (Javanese interlinear) show interlinear translation operating across genres beyond the Qur'an. Ricci poses standardisation vs creativity vs cultural particularity as the open questions of this "wide, yet little-studied realm". Companion pieces sighted in citations: Ricci 2016 (Journal of World Literature 1(1): 68-80, "Reading between the lines: a world of interlinear translation") and Ricci 2010 ("On the untranslatability of 'translation'", Translation Studies 3(3): 287-301).

**7. Wilson 2009 - SEARCH-VERIFIED.** Documents dozens of extant Ottoman-era interlinear Turkish Qur'an translations (15th-19th c.), "scribbled below the calligraphic Arabic", whose very format "makes no suggestion of replacing the Qur'an or of being equal to it" and which were not recited ritually (no vowel marks). Modern printed Turkish translation emerged from the 1908 press debate (Ahmed Midhat vs Mustafa Sabri; Sirat-i Mustakim publishing renderings without calling them "translations"; Bigiev declaring translation obligatory), the amateur 1924 translations (Tevfik; Huseyin Kazim Kadri's Nur ul-Beyan, an "explanatory translation"/tercume-i tefsiri attacked by Sebilurreşad), and parliament's commissioning of Elmalili's Hak Dini Kur'an Dili - a traditional commentary with embedded translations, explicitly not an authorised replacement of the Arabic Qur'an.

**8. Leiden Or. 504 (1520) - SEARCH-VERIFIED.** Dated exemplar (5 Racab 926 = 1 July 1520, Gallipoli, copyist Mehmed b. Saruhan) of the Old Anatolian Turkish word-for-word interlinear Qur'an, with occasional commentary, bold Arabic script above smaller Turkish; philological editions cited by Leiden (Inan 1961; Topaloglu 1976 attributing a version to Muhammed bin Hamza, early 15th c.; Karabacak's Manisa MS edition) confirm an established scholarly literature on this format.

**9. Sobirin & Muhdi 2026 - SEARCH-VERIFIED.** Codicological study of kitab kuning manuscripts (1890s-1970s) in Penginyongan pesantren: Arabic base text in larger/bolder script with explanatory Pegon prose/glosses interlinear or marginal, mirroring classroom practice where teachers recite Arabic then unpack it in Javanese. Pegon (not romanised Javanese) marks a distinct pesantren literacy; citing Johns and Azra, Arabic-script adaptation "naturalised transregional canons within local linguistic ecologies". Corpus spans tafsir, fiqh, kalam, tasawuf; conclusion names pesantren manuscripts as "active educational tools" transmitting Arabic-Islamic knowledge through interlinear glosses.

**10. Van Bruinessen 1995 - SEARCH-VERIFIED.** Foundational mapping of kitab kuning ("books in Arabic letters used in pesantren"), pesantren pedagogy, and tarekat; grew out of LIPI research 1986-1990 with a preface by Abdurrahman Wahid. Chapter list includes dedicated chapters on kitab kuning as Arabic-script books, fiqh books in Indonesian/Malaysian pesantren, and gender; Google Scholar shows ~2682 citations (his most-cited item). Editions in circulation vary (Mizan 1995; 1999 printing cited by others; Gading reprint 2012).

**Supplementary:** Nurtawab 2020 (Banten Qur'ans A.51/W.277: identical interlinear Malay with exegetical additions and qira'at-related notes - interlinear = tafsir argument); Munip 2016 (history of Arabic-Javanese pegon translation books; KH Shaleh Darat 1820-1903 as pioneer of printed Javanese translations; pegon kitab as bridge for non-elite readers); IJL 2014 untranslatability survey (i'jaz al-Qur'an doctrine grounds claims that any rendering is interpretation/tafsir, citing Tibawi 1962, Abdul-Raof 2001, Kidwai 1987); SEALG/Gallop 2023 blog (discovery of a Bursa manuscript of Tarjuman al-Mustafid; Ottoman Tedkik-i Muellefat approval documents 1905 for printing the "Jawi tafsir"; the erroneous identification with Bayzavi's tafsir propagated in Istanbul editions).

## Cross-cutting synthesis observed this session

- **One shared technology, four language worlds:** interlinear (word-for-word or phrase-below-line) rendering appears independently documented in Persian (Zaferani 1151-52; 10th-c. glosses), Ottoman Turkish (Or. 504, 1520; dozens of mss), Malay (RAS Arabic 4 + Banten group), and Javanese pegon (kitab kuning glosses) - always subordinated visually and ritually to the Arabic.
- **Authority-preserving grammar of the format:** smaller vernacular script beneath authoritative Arabic; no vowel marks on translations (Wilson); no claim to ritual recitation; translations described as tafsir/interpretation rather than "Qur'an" (Tibawi/Abdul-Raof framing; Ottoman officials refusing the word "translation").
- **Doctrine and practice coexist:** i'jaz/untranslatability discourse (Columbia curatorial framing; Sadeghi's "perfect translation impossible") never prevented glossing - juristic legitimation exists from Samanid-era fatwa narratives through modern committees (Ottoman Tedkik-i Muellefat 1905).
- **Pedagogy is the driver:** every corpus is tied to teaching contexts - pesantren santri-gloss practice, Ottoman medrese-style copying, Indian/Persian market mushafs - supporting the "ngawul"-style oral-unpacking model Sobirin & Muhdi describe.

## Open questions

1. Dating threshold: when exactly did complete Persian interlinear mushafs become common? Columbia flags this as debated (most extant copies post-1500); the Zaferani ms (1151-52) shows completeness existed early but apparently without continuous manuscript survival. Needs targeted codicological literature (e.g., studies of TIEM 555, early-11th-century Iran Qur'an seen referenced this session).
2. Genealogy of the Malay interlinear text family: how many copies share the exact translation of RAS Arabic 4 / PNRI A.51 / W.277, and did they descend from a single lost archetype? Nurtawab's article (full text not retrieved) should be fetched.
3. Terminology mapping: is "ngawul/ngawulake" documented in scholarly sources this session? The term appeared in search queries but retrieved Indonesian sources use "makna gandul", "pentul", "ngapsahi" (Javanese), "ngalogat" (Sundanese) - the precise verb for oral interlinear unpacking needs direct confirmation (candidate sources: kemenag.go.id column "Pentul, Kitab Kuning, dan Pesantren" 2023).
4. Witkam-specific treatment of Malay interlinear Qur'ans: this session surfaced Witkam's Leiden catalogues/inventories and a Leiden Or. 5690 pegon-glossed Tuhfah (via eudl.eu paper), but no dedicated Witkam study of Malay interlinear Qur'an was retrieved - his relevant article (if any) remains UNVERIFIED.
5. Ottoman-to-Malay channel: SEALG 2023 documents Ottoman printing approval (1905) for the Jawi Tarjuman al-Mustafid and Mecca printing-house Jawi tafsirs - the extent of Ottoman institutional engagement with Malay Qur'anic scholarship needs deeper archival work (Digirolu 2018 article).
6. Riddell 1990 *Transferring a Tradition* (Abd al-Ra'uf's Jalalayn rendering) was sighted only as a citation; full text needed to substantiate the "translationese" characterization of early Malay Qur'anic Malay.
7. Whether the Zaferani/Surabadi connection implies interlinear mushafs routinely carried a tafsir-derived translation layer (paralleling Nurtawab's tafsir claim for Malay) - comparative question across Persian, Turkish, Malay, Javanese corpora.
