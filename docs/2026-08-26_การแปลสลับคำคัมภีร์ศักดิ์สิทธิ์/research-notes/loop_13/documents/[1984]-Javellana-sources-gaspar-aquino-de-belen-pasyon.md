## Key extracts seen this session

- Opening line visible via search extraction of the Archium PDF: "The first datable pasyon to have appeared was a 980-verse narrative written by the master printer of the Jesuits, Gaspar Aquino de Belen".
- JSTOR record confirms: Philippine Studies vol. 32, No. 3 (Third Quarter 1984), pp. 305-321; journal founded 1953, published quarterly by the Ateneo through its School of Social Sciences.
- Related Javellana works verified on Crossref this session: "Pasyon Genealogy and Annotated Bibliography" (Philippine Studies, DOI 10.13185/2244-1638.1623) and monograph Passion/Pasyon with Sebastian Dickes (2010, Brill/Gratz, DOI 10.30965/9783846749449).
- Companion facts from a conference paper citing Javellana (IAFOR ACERP2018 proceedings, seen via search): the pasyon is a verse narrative of Christ's life modelled on Juan de Padilla's Retablo de la Vida de Cristo (1529/1585 references differ by source); Aquino de Belen's Mahal na Pasion ni Jesu Christong Panginoon Natin na Tola was written 1703 and printed 1704; per Javellana it was appended to Mga Panalanging Pagtatagobilin sa Caloloua nang Taoung Naghihingalo (1703), a Tagalog translation of prayers for the dying (Villacastin's Ordo commendationis animarum tradition); quintilla form (5-line, 8-syllable stanzas) sung in alternating pabasa performance during Holy Week; the anonymous early-19th-century expansion Casaysayan nang Pasiong Mahal ("Pasyon Pilapil"/"Pasyon Genesis") added Creation to Judgment and was translated into Pangasinan, Bicol, Ilocano, Ilongo, Waray, Pampango.
- National Library of the Philippines digital collection page (seen via search): "The pasyon first appears in Tagalog in 1703 or 1704. The first published text is Gaspar Aquino de Belen's Mahal na Passion ... which is appended to a book of prayers for the dying."
- LOC blog "Negotiating Empire" (2021, search excerpt): Aquino de Belen was "a printer in the Jesuit press in Manila from 1703 to 1716", an indigenous principal whose pasyon fortified Catholicism's discourse on death.

## Notes

- The pasyon represents the next generation after the 1593 Doctrina: devotional narrative poetry composed BY a native Christian (not friars), in Latin-script Tagalog, issued from the Jesuit press - evidence that missionary bilingual print created a Filipino author-printer class within one century.
- Javellana is the key scholar for source-criticism of the pasyon text (Spanish Jesuit devotional sources vs folk material); his full argument could not be read this session (PDF 403) and remains an open item.
