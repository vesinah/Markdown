## Key extracts seen this session

- Abstract (translated points): Catholic missionaries entered the Chinese cultural environment at the end of the sixteenth century. To be accepted by Chinese elites, the Jesuits, led by Matteo Ricci (1552-1610), adapted their message to Confucianism, the dominant philosophical current.
- "En 1603, publico su obra Tianzhu Shiyi 天主實義 (Verdadero Significado del Senor del Cielo), en la que el misionero italiano elabora un puente entre la etica confuciana y la religion cristiana."
- Manila-Chinese link: "Anos antes, en 1593, se habia publicado en Manila el libro Shilu 實錄 (Autentico registro de la religion verdadera) del dominico castellano Juan Cobo (1547?-1592), en la que se introducen algunos temas de la ciencia europea y se utilizan conceptos de origen neoconfuciano." Cobo and Ricci are presented as scientific/philosophical mediators between Europe and China.
- Keywords: Matteo Ricci, Juan Cobo, siglo XVI, Dinastia Ming, neoconfucianismo, dialogo intercultural.
- Citation list on the landing page begins with Diego Aduarte's Historia de la Provincia del Santo Rosario - the same Dominican chronicle used by Wolf for the 1593 Doctrinas.

## Companion record: Cervera Jimenez 2023 in Cauriensia

- Author: same; Title: "El Tianzhu Shiyi (Verdadero Significado del Senor del Cielo) de Matteo Ricci y su refutacion del monismo ontologico"; Cauriensia 18 (December 2023), Universidad Pontificia de Salamanca / Universidad de Extremadura; DOI 10.17398/2340-4256.18.101.
- Abstract seen via DOAJ this session: Ricci's accommodation policy enabled Jesuit acceptance by Ming literati; Tianzhu Shiyi is his most important philosophical work, seeking connections with Confucian ethics while refuting Buddhist and neo-Confucian doctrines incompatible with Christian faith; sharpest criticism directed at ontological monism of the Cheng-Zhu school ("la corriente filosofica mas influyente en China en tiempos de Ricci").

## Notes

- This source ties sub-topics (A) and (C) together: Juan Cobo, the Dominican author whom Wolf identifies as writer of the lost 1593 Manila Chinese Doctrina, is the same figure Cervera analyzes as author of the Chinese Shilu introducing European science and neo-Confucian concepts - i.e., the Manila Chinese mission print and Ricci's China strategy belong to one continuum.
- Ricci's 1603 Tianzhu Shiyi is characterized across both articles as a Confucian-accommodation catechism that bridges ethics and attacks neo-Confucian monism.
