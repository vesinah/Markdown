# Source Extract: [Anonymous] 1593 - Doctrina Christiana (Manila xylographic trilingual catechism)

- Author: Anonymous (title states "corrected by the Religious of the Orders"; Dominican church of San Gabriel, Manila)
- Title: "Doctrina Christiana, en lengua espanola y tagala, corregida por los Religiosos de las ordenes. Impressa con licencia en S. Gabriel de la Orden de S. Domingo. En Manila, 1593"
- Year: 1593 (facsimile 1947; digital edition Project Gutenberg #16119)
- Type: Primary source (xylographically printed book); digitized facsimile
- Publisher/Journal: Printed at San Gabriel, Manila; unique copy in Lessing J. Rosenwald Collection, Library of Congress
- DOI/ISBN: none seen
- URL: https://archive.org/details/doctrinachristia16119gut ; https://www.loc.gov/item/48031307/ ; https://www.loc.gov/item/47031748/
- Status: FULL-FETCHED (full Gutenberg text of facsimile downloaded and read this session; LOC catalog summaries seen via search)

## Key extracts seen this session

- LOC summary: published in Manila in 1593, this catechism in Spanish and Tagalog is the first book printed in the Philippines, the first printed in a Philippine language, and "the first, and only, 16th-century source showing an explicit and distinctly Philippine abecedarium (alphabet)". Illustrated with a woodcut frontispiece of St Dominic; initial letters in both Spanish and Tagalog; the only known copy in existence (LOC item 48031307).
- LOC record for the 1947 facsimile (item 47031748, contributor Edwin Wolf): "The text of the facsimile is in Spanish, Tagalog transliterated into Roman letters and Tagalog in Tagalog characters."
- Physical description per Wolf (from this session's reading of the facsimile text): printed "in Gothic letters and Tagalog characters on paper made from the paper mulberry"; thirty-eight leaves; seventy-four pages of text "in Spanish, Tagalog transliterated into roman letters, and Tagalog in Tagalog characters"; verso of title bears manuscript valuation "Tassada en dos rreales" signed Juan de Cuellar; four gatherings (12+10+10+6 leaves); size 9 1/8 by 7 inches.
- Entirely xylographic: "The volume is printed throughout by the xylographic method, that is to say, each page of text is printed from one wood-block which was carved by hand"; uneven impression indicates printing "without the help of a conventional press".
- Contents sequence: "After a syllabary comes the Pater Noster ... then follow the Ave Maria, Credo, Salve Regina, Articles of Faith, Ten Commandments, Commandments of the Holy Church, Sacraments, Seven Mortal Sins, Fourteen Works of Charity, Confession and Catechism." The opening Tagalog syllabary (baybayin) plus parallel romanized Tagalog makes the book itself a script-teaching instrument.
- Companion volume documented in Governor Gomez Perez Dasmariñas' letter to Philip II, Manila, June 20, 1593: license granted "for the printing of the Doctrinas Christianas ... one in the Tagalog language, which is the native and best of these islands, and the other in Chinese"; an Archives of the Indies account of 1593 prices the Tagalog Doctrina at two reales, the Chinese at four. Of the Chinese Doctrina "no copy has yet come to light" (Wolf).
- Wolf's summary sentence: "we know that in the first half of the year 1593 two Doctrinas were printed xylographically ... one in Tagalog from the Talavera-Plasencia-Oliver text, and one in Chinese written by Juan Cobo, both edited and printed under the supervision of Domingo de Nieva and Juan de San Pedro Martyr."
- LOC blog (2019, seen in search excerpts): baybayin "was a writing system based on an Indic script" developed before Spanish contact; the Doctrina shows "one of the earliest examples of printed Tagalog in Romanized and Baybayin script"; "Printed by a Chinese printer in Manila under the auspices of Dominican clergy".

## Notes

- The three-column trigraphic design (Spanish Gothic type, romanized Tagalog, baybayin) is direct evidence of a bilingual AND biscriptal evangelization strategy: the same prayer text is given so that readers literate in either script can access it, and the syllabary teaches baybayin alongside Christian content.
- Provenance narrative seen in Wolf: one copy sent to Philip II in 1593; Hervas y Panduro printed Tagalog texts from an extant copy in 1785; the Rosenwald copy surfaced in Paris in 1946 via dealer William H. Schab and was presented to the Library of Congress.
