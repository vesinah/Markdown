## Key extracts seen this session

- "The first printed Chinese-English dictionary was the Dictionary of the Chinese Language in Three Parts compiled by Robert Morrison (1782-1834) published between 1815 and 1823."
- Structure verified via publisher summary and its references: Vol I Part 1, 字典 Zidian, "Chinese and English arranged according to the radicals" (1815); Vol I Part 2 onward, 五車韻府 Wuche yunfu ("Erudition Syllabic Dictionary"), "Chinese and English arranged alphabetically" (1819); Part 3, English and Chinese (1822-1823).
- Imprint facts (Wellcome Collection catalogue + archive.org records seen this session): Macao, "Printed at the Honorable East India company's press, by P.P. Thoms", 1815-1823, 6 volumes 4to; complete digitized set public domain at Wellcome and multiple scans on archive.org/HathiTrust.
- Yang's summary claims: Morrison "improved the transliteration system which he had inherited, invented a new index system matching alphabetically arranged transliterations with Chinese characters, and provided a large number of citations from Chinese classics and popular contemporary Chinese books."
- Legacy claim: "his transliteration was adopted as the basis for the Wade-Giles system and ... the macrostructure and microstructure of his dictionary became a model for Samuel Wells Williams' Syllabic Dictionary of the Chinese Language 漢英韻府 (1874 ...) and Henry Allen Giles' Chinese-English Dictionary (1892...)".
- The paper traces "the tradition of missionary bilingual lexicography in China from its origins down to Morrison" (early manuscript bilingual dictionaries solved transliteration, alphabetical arrangement, definition, grammatical information).
- Related primary work seen in references: Ruggieri-Ricci Portuguese-Chinese dictionary manuscript (Yang Fu-mien & Witek 2001 introduction); Morrison's Guangdong sheng tuhua zihui (1828).

## Notes

- Establishes Morrison's dictionary as both a Protestant interlinear tool-chain component (radical + alphabetical + English-Chinese lookup enabling Bible translation) and a carrier of classical citations - i.e., the dictionary embeds the Confucian classics inside missionary lexicography.
