## Key extracts seen this session

- From DOAJ abstract: Ricci's introduction of the Western theory of soul "began to influence the connotation of ling 靈 in Chinese biblical texts"; "Robert Morrison's version, though heavily dependent on Jean Basset's translation, was also indebted to Emmanuel Diaz and Louis A. de Poirot in its dichotomous anthropology, developing a ling-rou 靈-肉 (lit. spirit-flesh) dichotomous discourse"; the second generation of Protestant translators culminated in the Delegates' Version; "With the assistance of some Chinese scholars in completing this version, Medhurst launched a dialogue between Christian anthropology and Chinese traditional outlooks of human beings by emphasizing the concepts of shen 身 and xin 心".
- Term Question detail from extracted full text: "the British missionaries Medhurst, William Charles Milne (1815-1863) and John Stronach (1810-?) proposed to render theos as 'Shangdi 上帝' and pneuma/ruach as 'shen 神'; meanwhile, the American missionaries William Jones Boone (1811-1864), Bridgman and Culbertson insisted that 'shen' should be the equivalent of theos, and pneuma/ruach should be rendered as 'ling 靈'."
- "Medhurst and Boone even launched written polemics for this issue, publishing serial articles in the newspaper entitled 'Chinese Repository'" (Medhurst 1848a, 1848b; Boone 1848); vote by the Committee of Delegates "ended at a flat ballot"; publishers left to decide; Medhurst's articles collected as treatises incl. "A Dissertation on the Theology of the Chinese".
- Continuity with Ricci: the Medhurst-Gutzlaff Version (MGF/MGV) "readopted the name Shangdi 上帝 which originated in Chinese antiquity and had been proposed in the Tianzhu Shiyi"; MGV also used Dao 道 to render logos ("dao cheng roushen 道成肉身").
- Wang Tao: DV "at least partially, resulted from the participation of Medhurst's Chinese literary assistant Wang Tao 王韜 (1828-1897)", who inserted terms from Chinese traditional culture.
- Primary pamphlet exchange documented: Medhurst, Stronach and W.C. Milne 1852, "Reply to Dr. Boone's 'Vindication of Comments on the Translation of Ephes. I. in the Delegates' Version of the New Testament'" (Shanghae: London Mission Press) - answering the 1852 Boone pamphlet held at Cornell.

## Notes

- Demonstrates a direct Catholic-to-Protestant terminological inheritance chain (Ricci's Tianzhu shiyi -> Shangdi -> Protestant versions) - the clearest cross-topic bridge found this session between Jesuit accommodation and later interlinear Bible tools.
- Morrison's dependence on Catholic translator Jean Basset's 1707 version is stated twice (abstract and notes), quantifying how much the first Protestant translation leaned on existing Catholic renderings.
