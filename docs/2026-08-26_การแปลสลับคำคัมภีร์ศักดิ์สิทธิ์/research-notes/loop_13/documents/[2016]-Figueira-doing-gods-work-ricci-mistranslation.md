# Source Extract: Dorothy Figueira 2016 - Doing God's Work (Ricci's Tianzhu shiyi as cultural mistranslation)

- Author: Dorothy Figueira (University of Georgia)
- Title: "Doing God's Work: The Missionary's Task of Translation or Who Makes the Best Jesuits: Comparatists, World Literature Scholars, or Real Jesuits?"
- Year: 2016 (published 4 July 2016)
- Type: Journal article
- Publisher/Journal: Interlitteraria 21(1), University of Tartu Press; ISSN 1406-0701 / 2228-4729; issue theme "Belletristic Translation: a Means of Cultural-Spiritual Dialogue or a Tool of Acculturation?"
- DOI/ISBN: 10.12697/IL.2016.21.1.2
- URL: https://ojs.utlib.ee/index.php/IL/article/view/12970
- Status: FULL-FETCHED (journal landing page with abstract fetched this session; PDF galley returned an HTML stub on download attempt)

## Key extracts seen this session

- Abstract verbatim points: "This paper takes as its point of departure the sixteenth-century Jesuit construction of Confucianism and the manufacture of the figure of Confucius in the form of translations of the Chinese classics."
- On accommodation: "In examining the Jesuit policy of accommodation in Asia, I ask whether we might not view these efforts as precursors for the tasks we seek to perform as Comparatists and World Literature scholars. Like the Jesuits in China who sought to package Confucius..."
- On Ricci's catechism: "I focus in particular on the work of Matteo Ricci and his catechism, the Tianzhu shiyi, as a work of cultural mistranslation. I ask to what degree are our current critical readings of the Other not also failures. I question the purposes for which one 'misreads'."
- Keywords: Jesuits, Confucius, Comparative Literature, World Literature, Matteo Ricci, China and India missions. License CC BY-NC-ND.

## Notes

- Frames the Jesuit use of the Chinese classics (the "manufacture" of Confucius through translation) as a deliberate hermeneutic packaging strategy - directly relevant to the topic's question of how missionaries handled Confucian sacred-classical text in Christian translation.
