# Source Extract: Jesuit Mission Press 1598 - Racuyoxu / Rakuyoshu (kanji character dictionary)

- Author: Jesuits of the Japan mission (anonymous); cover form "Racvyoxyv"
- Title: Rakuyoshu (落葉集; Latinized Racuyoxu), dictionary of Chinese characters and compounds in three parts
- Year: 1598
- Type: Primary source (Kirishitan-ban monolingual kanji dictionary printed by the Jesuit Mission Press, Nagasaki)
- Publisher/Journal: Jesuit Mission Press, Nagasaki; modern facsimile: "Rakuyoshu: Kirishitan kokuji-bon" 2 vols., Sasage Shuppan Insatsu Kikakushitsu, 1984 (NDL record seen)
- DOI/ISBN: none seen
- URL (bibliographic evidence): NDL Search records https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R000000004-I031194647 and https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I000001807925 ; Orii 2015 in Journal of Jesuit Studies https://brill.com/view/journals/jjs/2/2/article-p189_2.xml?language=en
- Status: SEARCH-VERIFIED (NDL records and Brill article excerpt seen this session; no full text of the dictionary or of Chiba's article read)

## Key extracts seen this session

- Orii Yoshimi 2015 (Brill JJS 2(2):189-207, search excerpt): lists "Racuyoxu, a dictionary of Chinese and Japanese characters (1598)" among the three major Jesuit contributions to historical Japanese linguistics, with the Vocabulario da lingoa de Iapam (1603-1604) and the Dictionarium Latino-lusitanicum ac Japonicum (1595).
- NDL record for Chiba Kenshi, "Iezusu-kai senkyoshi no sakusei shita kanji jisho 'Rakuyoshu' ni tsuite" ("On the kanji character dictionary Rakuyoshu compiled by Jesuit missionaries"), series portfolio, journal Arena 23, pp. 507-512 (Chubu University International Humanistic Research Institute) - title confirms the dictionary's compiler attribution to Jesuit missionaries; CiNii CRID 1520572359457654912 linked.
- NDL record for the 1984 Sasage facsimile: "Rakuyoshu: Kirishitan kokuji-bon" (Rakuyoshu as a Kirishitan movable-type edition), 2 volumes - confirms its status within the Kirishitan-ban corpus.
- Context from same JJS excerpt: after Hideyoshi's death in 1598 the missionaries settled in Nagasaki where the press operated until the great persecution of 1614; many Kirishitan-ban books were printed without place of publication due to upheavals.

## Notes

- Rakuyoshu is significant for this topic because it shows the mission press producing an indigenous-format reference work (a kanji dictionary giving Sino-Japanese and native readings) rather than a European-format bilingual one - the reverse adaptation direction compared with the Calepino-based Dictionarium.
- Claims circulating online that the Rakuyoshu first introduced the handakuten (semi-voiced p circle) were seen only on encyclopedia/wiki-derived pages this session and are NOT cited per loop rules; treat as UNVERIFIED pending a scholarly source.
