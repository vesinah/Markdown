## Key extracts seen this session

- NDL records reproduce the long title of Bernard Petitjean's 1870 Rome reprint (Typis S.C. de Propaganda Fide, Petro Marietti admin., 749 p., 26 cm): "Lexicon latino-iaponicum, depromptum ex opere cui titulus Dictionarium latino-lusitanicum ac Iaponicum [ex Ambrosii Calepini volumine depromptum] typis primum mandatum in Amacusa in Collegio Iaponico Societatis Iesu anno Domini M.D.XCV. Nunc denuo emendatum atque auctum a Vicario Apostolico Iaponiae."
- The bracketed phrase "ex Ambrosii Calepini volumine depromptum" confirms on-title derivation from Ambrogio Calepino's European polyglot dictionary; NDL attributes the work to Calepino (1435-1511) with Petitjean (1829-1884) as reviser; DDC 495.637 / LCC PL681.L3 ("Latin language - Dictionaries - Japanese").
- Shima Shozo's study editions verified: Kirishitan-ban rapo-nichi taiyaku jisho biko (Bunka Shobo, 1962) and Rapo-nichi taiyaku jisho ken'an (Bunka Shobo Hakubunkan, editions 1971-1977), whose NDL description explicitly pairs the Petitjean "Lexcon Lationo-Iaponicum" (Rome 1870) page-by-page against the "Dictionarium Latino Lusitanicum ac Iaponicum" (Amakusa, 1595).
- Orii Yoshimi (Journal of Jesuit Studies 2(2), 2015, search excerpt seen this session) groups it as one of three major Jesuit linguistic tools: "the multi-lingual works Vocabulario da lingoa de Iapam (1603-1604), Dictionarium Latino-lusitanicum ac Japonicum (1595), and Racuyoxu, a dictionary of Chinese and Japanese characters (1598)".
- New World Encyclopedia page (search excerpt): Valignano initiated Japanese language training for foreign missionaries, "and by 1595, the Jesuits had printed a Japanese grammar and dictionary".

## Notes

- The title evidence shows the Jesuits did not invent a lexicographic form but adapted the Calepino template (European polyglot tradition) to the Japan mission, adding Japanese as the third language of an originally Latin-Portuguese dictionary - a bilingual print strategy serving Latin teaching at the Jesuit colleges.
- The 1595 Amakusa printing is thus a language-learning instrument for missionaries (Latin via Portuguese into Japanese), complementary to the college textbooks covered in loop_07 (e.g., Esopo no fabulas).
- Full digital scan of the 1595 original was not located this session (NDL digitized holdings show reprints/studies); physical copies are held in European/Japanese rare collections per secondary references - exact locations remain UNVERIFIED this session.
