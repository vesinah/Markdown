## Key extracts seen this session

- Core linguistic claim: "As far as historical Japanese linguistics is concerned, Jesuits contributed significantly through the multi-lingual works Vocabulario da lingoa de Iapam (1603-1604), Dictionarium Latino-lusitanicum ac Japonicum (1595), and Racuyoxu, a dictionary of Chinese and Japanese characters (1598). These publications are essential linguistic tools for identifying correct historical usage and for reading kanji [adapted Chinese logographic] characters."
- Field shift observed: "Rather than focus on their significance to the Japanese language, scholars now regard the Jesuit Japanese books as part of a wider enterprise that accompanied the maritime expansion of the Iberian kingdoms"; comparative linguistics across Spanish/Portuguese empires "is currently flourishing"; mentions ongoing complete database project of the Kirishitan-ban.
- Printing history framing: "Japanese typography began around the time of the introduction of the Society of Jesus's printing presses, which coincided with the arrival of printing in Japan from Korea."
- Print-run evidence cited from primary reports: Diogo de Mesquita reported 1,300 copies of Contemptus Mundi (Laures no. 32) and 1,500 copies of Confessionarios (Laures no. 19).
- Press chronology: upheavals forced moves; many Kirishitan-ban printed without place reference; after Hideyoshi's death (1598) press settled in Nagasaki until the persecution of 1614 (deportation order by Hidetada).
- Bibliographic apparatus referenced: Laures numbering system ("(Laures no. 1)" etc.), Satow 1900 "The Jesuit Mission Press in Japan" (TASJ 27), Makita Tominaga 1973 Kirishitan-ban no kenkyu (Tenri).

## Notes

- Orii provides the historiographical map for the Kirishitan-ban language tools and confirms the 1595/1598 dates and character-dictionary nature of Dictionarium/Racuyoxu in a peer-reviewed venue - used this session to verify those two primary sources.
