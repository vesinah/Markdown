## Key extracts seen this session

- Duke UP description: "In an innovative mix of history, anthropology, and post-colonial theory, Vicente L. Rafael examines the role of language in the religious conversion of the Tagalogs to Catholicism and their subsequent colonization during the early period (1580-1705) of Spanish rule in the Philippines. By tracing this history of communication between Spaniards and Tagalogs, Rafael maps the conditions that made possible both the emergence of a colonial regime and resistance to it."
- Subject headings on archive.org record include "Spanish language -- Translating into Tagalog", "Christian converts -- Philippines", "Philippines -- Colonization".
- Praise blurbs fetched from Duke page: Bruce L. Fenner (American Historical Review) - focus on translation and conversion makes the book valuable for "the role of language in history"; J. Jorge Klor de Alva (American Anthropologist) - "insightful exegeses of the texts he analyzes"; Renato Rosaldo - "significant, original, and engaging".
- Chapter title visible via Crossref: "The 'Inadequacies' of Tagalog Conversion" (chapter 10 of the DUP e-edition).
- LOC blog (2021, seen in search excerpts) cites Rafael's reading of Gaspar Aquino de Belen's pasyon (1703): the pasyon by a native principal "laid the foundation for subsequent Tagalog pasyones" and appeared appended to Aquino's Tagalog translation of Tomas de Villacastin's "La recomendacion del alma" (prayers for the dying); Rafael's interpretation ties the pasyon to the principalia's role as lay agents of Catholic discourse on death.
