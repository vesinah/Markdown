## Per-target verdicts

### (A) Avendaño Sermonario + Molina 1649 — RESOLVED
- Digitized copy located and verified: archive.org/details/sermonesdelosmis00aven (JCB copy; ark:/13960/t4dn4x433; OCLC 1084963312). Full description captured: 2 vols, folio, Spanish+Quechua+Latin, Lima: Jorge Lopez de Herrera; vol. 1 [5],129 leaves, vol. 2 110 [i.e. 101] leaves; licencia 27 May 1648 under Viceroy Marques de Mancera; aprobacion by Francisco Conde SJ; dedicated to Archbishop Villagomez; Medina suggests possible Tercera parte; Vargas Ugarte links volume to Villagomez Carta pastoral (Lima 1649). References: JCB pre-1675 II:367-68; Medina Lima 288; Palau 68866; Vargas Ugarte Impresos peruanos 410. OCR downloaded and mined (title page transcription; erratas correcting Quechua by folio; sermon markers I-XXX).
- Scholarly description secured: Dedenbach-Salazar Saenz 1999 (BAS 32) - Avendano's propia parte adopts fictitious dialogue with indigenous audience, asymmetric churi address; SEGUNDA PARTE REPRODUCES SOME SERMONS OF THE TERCERO CATHECISMO.
- Molina name correction: task's "Juan de Molina" unsupported. Crossref confirms Gerald Taylor, "Un sermón en quechua de DIEGO de Molina (Huánuco, 1649)", BIFEA 30(2):211-231, DOI 10.4000/bifea.7032. Contextual corroboration: UNHEVAL Identidad 4(4) article quoting Torero 2005 - Diego de Molina, cura of Huánuco, defended examining in Chinchaysuyu Quechua rather than Cuzco terms (1649), parallel to Avendaño's stated choice of Lima-area Quechua.

### (B) Authorship of Doctrina Christiana 1583/84 — RESOLVED (with name-form corrections)
- Task names garbled: NO source uses "Jeronimo de Acosta" or "Antonio de Atienza". All authorities use JOSE de Acosta (1540-1600) and/or JUAN de Atienza (1542-1592).
- The attribution debate is now documented from the JCB catalog description of a second digitized copy (archive.org/details/doctrinachristia00cath): Backer-Sommervogel attributes to Jose de Acosta; Medina argues Acosta's role was primarily proofreading; Medina/Vargas Ugarte record wider participation; Vargas Ugarte specifically assigns Spanish text drafting to Acosta and principal Quechua/Aymara authorship to Bartolome de Santiago and Blas Valera.
- Corroboration: HathiTrust Record 103029146 ("variously ascribed"); BNP description (majority view: Acosta directed publication; participants Blas Valera, Alonso de Barzana, Bartolome Santiago, Juan de Atienza); Durston 2007 ("the council presented the catechisms as the work of a team", via indexed snippet); EBSCO research starter (Acosta authored on behalf of council).
- Resolution: conciliar team production under Acosta's direction; Atienza = printing supervisor (13 Feb 1584 licencia, per Loop 12 primary evidence) and legacy co-attribution. Not a genuine two-man either-or.

### (C) Tercero Cathecismo 1585 — RESOLVED
- Digitized at IA (tercerocathecism01cath) and BNP. ### (E) Protestant missionary linguistics beyond China — MOSTLY RESOLVED
- Carey grammars verified by title-page/reissue records: Punjabee (Serampore Mission Press, 1812; FT says first written 1811), Sungskrit (1806), Mahratta grammar (1805) + Mahratta dictionary (1810).
- Serampore trio (Carey/Ward/Marshman) translation program documented via careycenter.wmcarey.edu annotated bibliography (Crockett/Noonkester): Bengali NT off press 7 Feb 1801; Marathi Matthew 1805; Punjabee Bible vols 1811-1818; Hindee/Kunouji/Marwari/Kaithi NTs; Felix Carey Burman grammar 1835; programmatic Proposals of 1806 listing Shanscrit, Bengalee, Hindoostanee, Persian, Mahratta... AND Chinese. Scale claims range "more than 30 languages" (CUP) to "nearly three dozen" (Richard 2017).
- Critical citable view: Richard 2017 IBMR abstract - Carey's actual translations "of an inadequately low quality", five reasons they were never widely used.
- Martyn verified: EIC chaplain; Hindustani NT completed March 1808 (printed Serampore 1814); Persian NT made at Shiraz 1811-12 with Mirza Sayyid Ali's assistance (Calcutta printings 1816, later 1841 Baptist Mission Press for American and Foreign Bible Society); Persian Psalter, Hindi Prayer Book, Arabic NT revision; died Tokat 16 Oct 1812; papers at CCCW Cambridge.
- Eliot verified via MHS/LOC/Brown/NYHS/JCB: Mamusse Wunneetupanatamwe Up-Biblum God; NT 1661, complete Bible 1663, Cambridge Mass., Samuel Green + Marmaduke Johnson; FIRST Bible printed in any language in North America and largest single printing venture of early colonial period; funded by (Corporation for) the SPG in New England; base text = Geneva Bible translated into Massachuset/Natick/Wopanaot8aok; approach characterized by MHS: imperfect knowledge compensated by resorting to English words where no equivalents existed; native collaborators John Nesutan (Harvard-educated) and James Printer (Nipmuck); second edition 1685 revised with John Cotton (JCB scan digitized).

## Key findings

1. Both flagship Lima sermon books now have verified digital surrogates with downloadable OCR (Avendaño 1649 JCB; Tercero Cathecismo 1585 JCB), enabling direct layout comparison with the 1584 Doctrina already mined in Loop 12: all three use language-labeled blocks/columnar vernacular sections rather than interlinear glossing.
2. The 1649 pair (Avendaño Lima; Diego de Molina Huánuco) belongs to one moment: public defense of the Chinchaysuyu/Lima Quechua norm against Cuzco prestige norms, documented through Torero 2005 quotes and Taylor's BIFEA edition.
3. Authorship question (B) dissolves under evidence: the "Acosta vs Atienza" pairing in older catalogs reflects printing-supervision roles, while modern scholarship distributes composition across the conciliar commission (Spanish: Acosta; Quechua/Aymara principally Santiago and Valera; Barzana among translators; Medina even demotes Acosta to proofreader).
4. Hovdhaugen 1996 is a slim North-America-heavy manifesto volume (Huron, Nez Perce chapters), not a global survey - relevant when citing it as the field's founding document.
5. The Protestant corpus shows society-funded, printer-centered production (SPG/New England Company; BMS/Serampore Press) with named native collaborators but without the conciliar authorization/translation-monopoly apparatus of the Lima corpus; translation-method self-descriptions differ accordingly (Geneva-Bible literalism + English loans for Eliot; elegance-seeking with munshis for Martyn; pandit-relay mass production for Carey, critically reassessed by Richard 2017).
6. Field historiography has pivoted since 2015 toward "colonial linguistics" reframing (Stolz/Warnke; HL special issue), while 2024-2026 brings a new encyclopedic wave regionalizing the subfield (Asia/Americas/Africa/Taiwan/Mesoamerica).
