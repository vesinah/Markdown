# [1583/1584] Doctrina Christiana y catecismo (Lima) - AUTHORSHIP QUESTION RESOLVED

- Target: Loop 23 (B)
- Status: RESOLVED - authorship debate documented across independent bibliographic authorities; task's name forms corrected

## Correction of name forms

- Task's candidate names "Jeronimo de Acosta" and "Antonio de Atienza" were NOT found in any source this session. All located authorities use JOSE DE ACOSTA (Jesuit, 1540-1600) and/or JUAN DE ATIENZA (Jesuit, 1542-1592). The task's forms appear to be garbles of these.
- No bibliography consulted attributes the work to a "Gerónimo de Acosta" or an "Antonio de Atienza".

## The documented attribution debate (from John Carter Brown Library scan description, archive.org/details/doctrinachristia00cath, fetched 2026-08-25)

Verbatim points from the JCB catalog description:
1. "Backer-Sommervogel attributes this work to José de Acosta."
2. "Medina, however, argues that Acosta's role was primarily that of proof reader."
3. "Medina and Vargas Ugarte also note the participation of other Jesuits, members of other orders, and secular clergy in the preparation of this work."
4. "More specifically Vargas Ugarte maintains that Acosta drafted the Spanish text and that Fathers Bartolomé de Santiago and Blas Valera were the principal authors of the Quechua and Aymara version."

## Corroborating authorities

- HathiTrust Record 103029146 (search-result snippet): "First book printed in Lima. Variously ascribed to José de Acosta and Juan de Atienza." https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/103029146
- Biblioteca Nacional del Peru description (reproduced at fuenteshistoricasdelperu.com/2020/06/14/doctrina-christiana/, fetched 2026-08-25): "La mayoría de académicos atribuyen la autoría de este escrito a José de Acosta, puesto que este estudioso jesuita dirigió la publicación de los textos mandados por el Tercer Concilio Limense (1582-1583). Vargas Ugarte, señala también la participación de Blas Valera, Alonso de Barzana, Bartolomé Santiago y Juan de Atienza, en la publicación de este ejemplar."
- Durston, Pastoral Quechua (Notre Dame UP 2007), search snippet from indexed full text: the council "presented the catechisms as the work of a team" (with reference to Third Lima Council 1985 facsimile edition [1584]: 13-14).
- EBSCO Research Starters entry "José de Acosta": "On behalf of the council, Acosta authored a trilingual book of catechisms and sermons for the indigenous of Peru in Spanish and the local languages Aymara and Quechua"; Doctrina Christiana was "the first full-length book printed in Peru". (tertiary; corroborates Acosta-as-council-author tradition.)
- Loop 12 primary evidence (licencia 13 Feb 1584, from archive.org/details/doctrinachristia00cath_0): printing oversight assigned to padre Juan de Atienza (for Confessionario and Preparacion para morir) and padre Joseph de Acosta "con dos de los que se hallaron en la traduction della" plus Audiencia secretaries.

## Resolution statement

The either-or framing (Acosta vs Atienza) does not match the bibliographic tradition. The sources converge on:
- The 1583 council approved the texts; the volume is officially conciliar ("Compuesto por auctoridad del Concilio Prouincial... el año de 1583").
- Backer-Sommervogel tradition credits Jose de Acosta (with Juan de Atienza associated); Medina demotes Acosta to proofreader; Vargas Ugarte assigns the Spanish text to Acosta and the Quechua/Aymara versions principally to Bartolome de Santiago and Blas Valera, with Alonso de Barzana and Juan de Atienza among the participants; BNP follows the majority view crediting Acosta with direction of publication.
- Juan de Atienza's documented role: printing supervision (licencia of 13 Feb 1584) and association in older catalog attributions.
- Verdict: TEAM/COUNCIL PRODUCTION under Acosta's direction; no single-author attribution is sustained; the specific pair "Jerónimo de Acosta vs Antonio de Atienza" is a corruption of "José de Acosta / Juan de Atienza".
