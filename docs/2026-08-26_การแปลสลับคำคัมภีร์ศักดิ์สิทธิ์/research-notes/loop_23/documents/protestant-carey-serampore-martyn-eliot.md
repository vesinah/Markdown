## William Carey and the Serampore enterprise

### Grammars and dictionaries (title-page evidence)
- A Grammar of the Punjabee Language, William Carey. Serampore, Printed at the Mission-press, 1812 (Wikisource scan index en.wikisource.org/wiki/Index:A_Grammar_of_the_Punjabi_Language_-_William_Carey.pdf; title-page transcription verbatim). The Friday Times feature states Carey was the first to write a Punjabi grammar "back in 1811" - year discrepancy 1811 vs 1812 noted.
- A Grammar of the Mahratta Language to Which are Added Dialogues on Familiar Subjects - Cambridge University Press reissue listing (barnesandnoble/ernster dealer pages); bibleportal CUP description says "Carey's 1805 grammar of Marathi is also reissued in this series"
- A Grammar of the Sungskrit Language - "First published in 1806" per CUP reissue description (Barnes & Noble page)
- A Dictionary of the Mahratta Language, first published 1810, over 9,500 entries in Devanagari with English translations (CUP description via bibleportal.com)

### Serampore trio and translation project
- The trio = Carey, William Ward, Joshua Marshman; settled in Danish colony of Serampore from 1793 arrival; goal of translating Scripture into all possible languages; "translating the Bible into more than 30 languages" (bibleportal/CUP author note); Fort William College professorships (first professor of Sanskrit, Bengali and Mahratta); Bengali NT last sheet off press February 7, 1801; 2,000 copies Bengali Bible printed by Feb 1801 costing GBP 612 (scripturesource.com narrative).
- Serampore Mission Press production documented item-by-item at the Carey Center (William Carey University) online bibliography careycenter.wmcarey.edu/bib/works_bible.htm (annotated by Bennie R. Crockett Jr. and Myron C. Noonkester): Marathi Matthew 1805 and 1807; Mahratta Bible volumes 1811-1821; Punjabee Bible NT vol. 1811, Pentateuch 1814, complete 1818; Hindee NT [Serampore Press] 1837; Kunouji NT vol.; Marwari NT 1821; Kaithi Gospels 1821; Felix Carey, A Grammar of the Burman Language (Calcutta, Baptist Mission Press, 1835); Psalms of David in Bengalee for Calcutta Auxiliary Bible Society 1826.
- Programmatic document: Proposals for a Subscription for Translating the Holy Scriptures into the Following Oriental Languages: Shanscrit, Bengalee, Hindoostanee, Persian, Mahratta ... [etc.] and Chinese. Serampore, Bengal: Printed at the Mission Press, 1806 (same bibliography) - shows Chinese already targeted from Serampore in 1806.
- Also: Brief View of the Baptist Missions and Translations with Specimens of Various Languages... Accompanied with a Map (Serampore).

### Critical assessment (citable)
- H. L. Richard, "Some Observations on William Carey's Bible Translations", International Bulletin of Mission Research 42:3 (2017 [per Crossref]), pp. 241-250, DOI 10.1177/2396939317747514. Abstract (Crossref, fetched): "William Carey's historic role in Bible translation is widely recognized. That Carey's actual translations were of an inadequately low quality is not so widely known." Article gives five reasons Carey's translations were never widely used; modern translation theory informs critique; calls for new translations in major India languages.

## Henry Martyn (1781-1812)

- Cambridge Centre for Christianity Worldwide archive page (cccw.cam.ac.uk/?archives=martyn-henry): Cambridge student, acolyte of Charles Simeon, chaplain to the British East India Company, "in Bengal and Persia a translator of the Bible"; his papers held at CCCW.
- Schaff-Herzog Encyclopedia (ccel.org): by 24 Feb 1807 completed part of Book of Common Prayer into vernacular (Calcutta 1814); March 1808 completed Hindustani NT (printed Serampore 1814); undertook supervision of a Persian NT.
- Justus Anglican resources / satucket hagiography: translated NT into Hindi and Persian, revised an Arabic NT, translated Persian Psalter and Hindi Prayer Book; left India 1811 for Persia; died Tokat 16 October 1812.
- Shiraz phase: SBTS repository dissertation abstract - while translating the NT in Shiraz, Persia, in 1811-12 he composed three Persian tracts ("the Shiraz Controversy"); rare-book dealer record quotes the title page of the Calcutta 1816 printing: "The New Testament ... translated from the original Greek into Persian at Sheeraz by the Rev. Henry Martyn ... with the assistance of Meerza Sueyid Ulee [Mirza Sayyid Ali] of Sheeraz"; a later Calcutta printing (Baptist Mission Press, for American and Foreign Bible Society, 1841) is catalogued at careycenter.wmcarey.edu.
- Note: "Urdu" vs "Hindustani/Hindi" naming varies by source; primary imprints use Hindustani/Hindee.

## John Eliot's Massachuset (Algonquian) Bible

### Primary facts (Massachusetts Historical Society Collections Online, masshist.org/database/viewer.php?item_id=54&pid=15)
- Title: Mamusse Wunneetupanatamwe Up-Biblum God ("Eliot Indian Bible"); Cambridge, Mass.: printed by Samuel Green and Marmaduke Johnson, 1663; 18.1 x 14.5 cm
- New Testament printed separately 1661; combined Old+New Testament 1663
- "It is the first Bible printed in any language in North America and the largest single printing venture of the early colonial period"
- Funding: Society for the Propagation of the Gospel in New-England and Adjacent Parts (English missionary society)
- Translation problems per MHS: "Eliot's knowledge of Massachuset was imperfect, and there were grammatical problems that could only be resolved by resorting to English words when there were no equivalent Massachuset terms" (i.e., English loanwords as stopgap)
- Native collaborators: John Nesutan (preacher who had studied at Harvard) assisted translation; James Printer (young Nipmuck apprenticed to Green) assisted both translation and printing
- Recruiting English printer Marmaduke Johnson + extra equipment was required for scale

### Base text and language naming
- Library of Congress blog "Eliot's Bible" (blogs.loc.gov/loc/2024/08/eliots-bible, 2024-08-06): Eliot learned the previously unwritten regional Algonquian language ("also known as Natick", Wampanoag per LOC wording) and spent years translating the Geneva Bible into it; help from James Printer and John Nesutan per MHS.
- Brown University Library exhibit (library.brown.edu/create/libnews/exhibit-eliot-bible/): full English title "The Holy Bible: containing The Old Testament and the New. Translated into the Indian Language, and Ordered to be Printed by the Commissioners of the United Colonies in New-England, At the Charge, and with the Consent of the Corporation in England for the Propagation of the Gospel amongst the Indians in New-England"; printed on the press at Harvard University; language Wopanaot8aok, one of ~40 Algonquian languages.
- Second edition 1685 revised by Eliot and John Cotton; digitized JCB copy: archive.org/details/mamussewunneetup02elio (includes single-leaf dedication to Robert Boyle in presentation copies; separate dated title page for Wusku Wuttestamentum, 1680, within the 1685 volume); 1661 NT scan included in Digital Bible Society item ALQELIO_DBS_HS.
- New-York Historical exhibition (2019) lists it among "the first Bible printed in America".

## Cross-cutting observations for the loop topic

- Protestant pattern differs from Lima's council-controlled trilingual corpus: production is society-funded and printer-centered (SPG/New England Company; Baptist Missionary Society/Serampore Press), with native assistants named individually but without conciliar authorization apparatus or translation monopolies like the 1584 Epistola's.
- Translation approach contrast: Lima Doctrina's Annotaciones prescribe sense-for-sense middle register (Loop 12); Eliot works from the Geneva Bible with literal word-seeking and English loans where terms are missing (MHS); Carey relies on pandit/munshi collaboration and multilingual relay via Sanskrit plan (scripturesource), later judged inadequate by modern standards (Richard 2017); Martyn seeks Persian literary elegance with native scholar assistance (Mirza Sayyid Ali) - "elegance as well as doctrinal exactness".
