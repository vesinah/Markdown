## Notes by target

### (A) Flesher & Chilton, The Targums: A Critical Introduction (2011)
- Full Brill e-book TOC obtained: 21 chapters from "Introduction" through "Targums in the Rabbinic World and Beyond," plus Appendix A (Parallel Expansions of Genesis 28-50), Appendix B (Guide to Babylonian Pointing), glossary, bibliography, index.
- Relevant chapters for alternation/layout comparison: ch. 2 "Defining Targum"; ch. 3 "The Seven Rules of Targum"; ch. 14 "Targums and Translation in the History of Rabbinic Literature"; ch. 15 "Targum as Scripture and Hidden Interpretation"; ch. 21 "Targums in the Rabbinic World and Beyond."
- Print pagination differs from e-book chapter pagination (Google Books shows Section II starts p.69, Section III p.167; Polliack review says 557 pp.; Baylor site says 575 pp.).

### (B) Alexander in Mikra (1988)
- Verified pp. 217-253 in original Van Gorcum/Fortress printing via two independent published bibliographies (Kusmirek 2021; also bibliotekanauki record). Brill e-book of the reprint gives 217-254. Cambridge-published reference list confirms a Peabody reprint (2004) retaining 217-53.
- Volume context chapters adjacent to Alexander's: "The Reading of the Bible in the Ancient Synagogue" and "The Samaritan Targum of the Pentateuch."

### (C) Shnayim mikra ve-echad targum
- Chain: Berakhot 8a-b (Rav Huna bar Yehuda citing R. Ammi) -> Rambam Tefillah 13:25 -> Tur OC 285 -> SA OC 285:1-7 with Rema glosses -> MB/Aruch HaShulchan.
- Structural parallels to synagogue practice documented by Ra'avan (siman 88): two mikra readings correspond to sirsur (paired readers at the bima); targum reading corresponds to the meturgeman reciting from memory while the reader reads from the scroll (cf. Mishnah Megillah rule discussed under target B).
- Substitution debate: Tosafot reject foreign-language translations as substitutes; Rosh permits Rashi; Semag (pos. 19) insists on Onkelos as Sinaitic; Rema: God-fearing read both.
- Printed-layout embodiment exists: special chumashim print Hebrew twice then targum once to enforce the ritual sequence.

### (D) Polyglot Bibles
- Complutensian (LOC): Pentateuch pages = upper three-quarters three columns (Greek left, Vulgate middle, Hebrew right); lower band two columns (Aramaic Onkelos left, Latin translation right); epigraphs/apostilles in right margin. 6 vols., ~1500 pp., 600 paper + 6 vellum copies; printed 1514-17, released 1520.
- Antwerp/Plantin (Oxford Cabinet + Chetham's): OT spread = left page Hebrew+Latin parallel; right page Septuagint+Latin parallel; Aramaic Targum Onkelos beneath left page, its Latin beneath right page. Vol. 5 NT Greek+Syriac each with Latin plus Syriac in Hebrew characters. Vols. 6-8 apparatus incl. interlinear Hebrew/Greek-Latin complete Bible. Copy counts conflict across sources (1100 / 1200 paper +12 parchment / 1212).
- London/Walton (Kitto + COJS after British Library): Pentateuch single page carries up to ~9 text streams (Hebrew w/ interlinear Latin, Clementine Vulgate, Roman Septuagint w/ variants + Nobilius Latin, Syriac w/ Latin, Onkelos w/ Latin, Samaritan + Samaritan version sharing one Latin, Arabic w/ Latin). Samuel example: red-outlined columns Hebrew(interlinear Latin)-Vulgate-Greek(Latin), Aramaic Targum Jonathan beneath with Latin; facing page Syriac and Arabic each with Latin. Nine languages total but no single book in all nine; second subscription book in England; Cromwell->Charles II dedication swap survives in copies.
- English Hexapla (Internet Bible Catalog + HathiTrust): Greek text at head of each double page; six English versions in parallel columns across two pages; original spelling retained, not facsimiles; Scholz Greek base; first edition with 160-page Tregelles historical account.
- Bonus cases surfaced: Genoa Psalter (Giustiniani, 1516) eight columns incl. Aramaic paraphrase in Hebrew characters + its Latin translation + scholia; Paris Polyglot (Le Jay, 1629-1645) first printed Samaritan Pentateuch and Syriac OT.

### (E) Yiddish/Ladino interleaved formats
- Amsterdam Yiddish Bibles (Blitz 1678 Uri Fayvesh; Witzenhausen 1679 Athias): first complete Yiddish OTs; >6,000 copies each; fierce copyright war; Athias physically inserted Blitz sheets (fols. 21-36) into his own edition. Layout strategy is NOT a parallel interleave: vernacular-only pages, commentary removed, Christian models (Luther, Statenvertaling) emulated, Hebrew retained inline for key terms/divine names. Earlier Yiddish multi-column format: Mirkevet HaMishneh (Cracow 1534) three-column glossary (Hebrew word | source ref | Yiddish in vaybertaytsh).
- Constantinople Pentateuch 1547 (Eliezer b. Gershom Sonsino): true Jewish polyglot page - Onkelos on top; Hebrew center flanked by Ladino and Judeo-Greek columns (both vocalized Hebrew script); Rashi at bottom in smaller square letters. Aslanov 1999 confirms two vernacular translations flank the Hebrew.
- Ferrara Bible 1553 (Atias & Usque): contrast case - all-Latin-script, two-column, no Hebrew on page; Jewish and Christian issues.
- Mendelssohn Be'ur (1780s) later re-traditionalized layout toward Mikraot Gedolot model (Gillman).
