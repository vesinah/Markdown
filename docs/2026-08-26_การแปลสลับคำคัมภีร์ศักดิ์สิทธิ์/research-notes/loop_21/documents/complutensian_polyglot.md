# Document: Complutensian Polyglot Bible (1514-1517) - layout details

## Primary library source (fetched search content)

Library of Congress record: https://www.loc.gov/item/2021666855/ (also WDL resource https://www.loc.gov/resource/gdcwdl.wdl_10636_001/)
Citation: Brocar, Arnao Guillen de, contributor; Jimenez de Cisneros, Francisco, editor. *Complutensian Polyglot Bible*. Alcala de Henares: Arnao Guillen de Brocar, 1514.

### Verified layout facts (per LOC description)

- Printing done 1514-1517; distribution delayed until 1520 after papal authorization from Rome.
- Six separately-bound volumes totaling about 1,500 pages; 600 copies on paper, six on vellum.
- Volume 1 (Pentateuch): upper three-quarters of each page divided into three columns - Greek text LEFT column; Latin Vulgate MIDDLE column; Hebrew text RIGHT column.
- Lower section of page divided into two columns: LEFT = Aramaic Targum Onkelos; RIGHT = Latin translation of that Aramaic text.
- Each page includes an epigraph and apostilles (marginal notes) on the right margin.
- Volumes 2-3: remainder of Old Testament in Greek, Latin, Hebrew.
- Volume 4: New Testament in Greek and Latin; last part of vol. 4 and vol. 5: Hebrew-Aramaic dictionary, Hebrew grammar, Greek dictionary.
- Colophon (Latin), last volume: printing at Complutense University by Arnold William of Brocario (note: LOC transcription gives 1565 date in the quoted colophon excerpt as extracted from WDL notes).

## Significance for project (layout strategy preserving originals)

The Pentateuch pages place three ancient versions side-by-side above, with the vernacular-of-scholarship Aramaic targum plus its Latin gloss below: a five-text stack per page whose hierarchy privileges original languages at top and auxiliary translations beneath. The editors' own metaphor (preface): the Latin Vulgate placed between Hebrew and Greek like Christ crucified between two thieves (also reported by storyofbible.com page). First printed edition ever of: Greek NT, complete Septuagint, Targum Onkelos (multiple sources concur).
