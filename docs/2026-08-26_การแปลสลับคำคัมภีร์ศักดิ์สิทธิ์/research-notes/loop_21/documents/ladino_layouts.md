## Verified layout of Constantinople Pentateuch (1547)

Title page (transliterated from Hebrew characters): "Hamisha Humshey Tora ketubim biktab Ashuri, 'im Haftarot vehamesh Megillot ... Targum hamiqra belashon Yevani ulshon La'az ... Kostandina, Defus Eliezer ben Gershom Sonsino" - i.e., Five books of Torah in Assyrian (square Hebrew) script, with translation of Scripture in Greek language and in La'az (Romance), Constantinople, press of Eliezer ben Gershom Sonsino, year 307 (=1547).

Page architecture (per Brill chapter):
- CENTER of page: Hebrew text.
- SIDES of the Hebrew: Ladino (Judeo-Spanish) translation AND Judeo-Greek translation, both in vocalized Hebrew letters.
- TOP of page: Targum Onkelos (Aramaic) in vocalized Hebrew letters.
- BOTTOM of page: Rashi's commentary in smaller non-vocalized square letters.

Aslanov (Persée abstract): "The presence of two translations of the Torah text on both sides of the Hebrew original in the Constantinople edition of the Pentateuch is a rare opportunity for comparing two streams of translation in the Jewish tradition." Confirms column arrangement.

Note per same Brill chapter: the promised Haftarot and five scrolls were absent from the actual text (apparently never printed despite title-page claim).

## Ferrara Bible (1553) - contrasting strategy

- Published exclusively in LATIN letters; NO Hebrew text included; text set in two columns (per Brill chapter figure description).
- Title: "BIBLIA En lengua Espanola traduzida palabra por palabra de la verdad Hebrayca por muy excelente letrados, vista y examinada por el oficio de la Inquisition Con privilegio del Illustrissimo Senor Duque de Ferrara"; publishers Yom Tov Atias and Abraham Usque named on verso of title page.
- Appeared in both Jewish and Christian versions (different title/imprint configurations).
- Word-for-word fidelity to Hebrew claimed on title; closely aligns textually with the Constantinople Pentateuch Ladino despite different scripts.

## Contextual corroboration

- An earlier related Constantinople Pentateuch polyglot of 1546 reportedly added Persian and Arabic translations beside text and Targum (noted in secondary literature; primary verification still needed - flagged as open question).
- The multilingual stack (Onkelos top, Hebrew center flanked by two vernaculars, Rashi below) is a Jewish internal analogue to the Christian Complutensian bottom-band solution: originals central/top, translations peripheral, commentary lowest.
