## Verified facts

- Editor: Brian Walton (1600-1661, later Bishop of Chester); printed London by Thomas Roycroft; assistants included Edmund Castell, Edward Pococke, advice from James Ussher (Marsh's Library).
- Dating across sources: proposals 1652; Bridwell gives imprint "London: Thomas Roycroft, 1653-1658"; COJS "issued in six volumes between 1654 and 1657"; Reformed Books Online: vol.1 September 1654, vol.2 July 1655, vol.3 July 1656, vols.4-6 1657. Commonly dated 1655-57 or 1657.
- Six folio volumes; nine languages total: Hebrew, Greek, Latin, Aramaic (Chaldee/Targums), Samaritan, Syriac, Ethiopic, Arabic, Persian. No single biblical book printed in all nine (COJS).
- Subscription financing: COJS (after British Library/Reeve 2007): second book published by subscription in England; ~9,000 pounds raised at 10 pounds per set. Originally dedicated to Oliver Cromwell, who died during printing; re-dedicated to Charles II; copies with both prefaces survive. [Remediation note: a Britannica "one of the first English books assembled by public subscription" corroboration was removed as a prohibited source; the subscription claim now rests on COJS/Reeve only and its exact rank ("first" vs "second" subscription book) remains UNVERIFIED independently.]
- Title (Bridwell): *Biblia sacra polyglotta complectentia textus originales [...] cum textuum, & versionum orientalium translationibus latinis* ("...with the original texts [...] together with the Latin translations of the texts and the Oriental versions").
- Illustrations: full-page engravings by Wenceslaus Hollar (Bridwell).

### Page layout (Pentateuch, per Kitto/Biblicalcyclopedia)

One open page exhibits: Hebrew text with Arias Montanus' interlinear literal Latin; Clementine Vulgate; Roman edition Septuagint with variants of Codex Alexandrinus and Flaminius Nobilius' Latin; Syriac with its Latin version; Targum Onkelos with a Latin version; Samaritan Pentateuch plus Samaritan version sharing a single Latin translation; Arabic with Latin version.

### Page layout (Samuel spread, per COJS after British Library)

Parallel columns outlined in red, left to right: Hebrew with linear Latin translation; Latin Vulgate; Greek with Latin translation. Directly beneath: Aramaic Targum Jonathan in Hebrew characters with its Latin rendition. Facing page: Syriac and Arabic versions with their respective Latin translations.

### Volume contents (per Reformed Books Online)

- OT: Hebrew; Vulgate; Septuagint (with Latin); Syriac (with Latin); Targum Onkelos (with Latin); Samaritan (with Latin); Arabic (with Latin). Targum Pseudo-Jonathan to Numbers and Deuteronomy (with Latin) prefixed to the NT volume.
- NT: Greek (Stephanus text); Vulgate; Syriac (with Latin); Ethiopic (with Latin); Arabic (with Latin); Persian (with Latin).
- Vol. 6: variant readings and critical remarks (also Kitto).
- Special contents: Psalms in Ethiopic with Latin (vol. 3 area); Deuterocanonicals in Greek, Latin, Arabic, Syriac; two Hebrew texts of Tobit; two Chaldee and a Persian Targum on the Pentateuch with Latin versions (Kitto, vol. 4).

## Significance for project

Every non-Latin ancient version carries its own dedicated Latin translation column beneath/beside it: the polyglot page functions as an apparatus in which the original-language texts are never presented without a gloss into the scholarly lingua franca. Continues Complutensian strategy (originals above, translations below) at larger scale; adds subscription financing model.
