## Verified facts

- First two complete Yiddish Old Testaments appeared nearly simultaneously in Amsterdam 1678-1679:
  - Blitz translation: commissioned and printed by Uri Fayvesh (Feivush/Phoebus) ha-Levi; finished late 1678 (some title pages carry dates 1676, 1678, 1679). Title: Torah Nevi'im u-Khtuvim bilshon Ashkenaz.
  - Witzenhausen translation: typesetter Joseph ben Alexander Witzenhausen for publisher Joseph Athias; appeared early 1679.
- Both print runs over 6,000 copies (Rosenthaliana); neither a commercial success.
- Rivalry: Athias withdrew as financier during printing, took pages of Blitz's printing he held in safekeeping, obtained a Dutch "privilegie" using them, and inserted those sheets (fols. 21-36) into his own Witzenhausen edition rather than reset type. Conflicting copyrights in Holland and Poland; approbations on both sides (Blitz: Portuguese and Ashkenazic Amsterdam authorities + Polish royal copyright; Witzenhausen: Council of the Four Lands + States of Holland and West-Friesland copyrights). Athias' edition contains a Latin preface dedicated to the Great Elector of Prussia.
- Physical collations (Christie's note): Athias edition 234 leaves; Phoebus edition 282 leaves; each with two title pages (engraved + letterpress).
- Translation character (Aptroot/Gillman): Blitz relied heavily on Luther's German Bible and Dutch Statenvertaling (1637); broke abruptly with older homiletic Yiddish tradition; inserted anti-Christian polemic; many Dutch/Low German words. Witzenhausen closer to traditional style, consulted Statenvertaling only to solve problems.
- Layout facts (Gillman ch. 1): unlike Mendelssohn's later Be'ur (1780s, modeled on Rabbinic Bible/Mikraot Gedolot layout encircling Scripture with commentaries), the Amsterdam Yiddish Bibles REMOVED commentary from the page and were closer to Christian models of translation; they retain Hebrew for key terms/divine names (Witzenhausen sets "Ehyeh asher Ehyeh" in bold large font; Blitz sets off Hebrew words with parentheses). I.e., these are vernacular-only Bibles, NOT parallel-column Hebrew-Yiddish editions.
- Marsh's Library holds what it calls a 1687 Amsterdam second edition of Witzenhausen; Gillman (fn citing Aptroot) reports evidence that 1687-dated title pages were doctored, creating an illusion of a second printing that never occurred.

## Interleaved-format precedents in Yiddish printing (from Gillman excerpts)

- Sefer Mirkevet HaMishneh (Cracow 1534), first known printed Yiddish book: a biblical concordance/glossary whose pages have THREE columns: biblical word in Hebrew square font; reference to sources; Yiddish equivalents in semicursive vaybertaytsh script - an early multi-column Hebrew/Yiddish glossary layout.
- Older Yiddish tradition translated only liturgical portions: Torah, haftarot, five megillot, Psalms (Rosenthaliana).
