## Verified facts

- Published Antwerp 1568-1573 by Christopher Plantin; eight folio volumes; also called Plantin Polyglot, Antwerp Polyglot, Biblia Regia ("King's Bible") for financing by Philip II of Spain; editor Benito Arias Montano sent by Philip II to supervise.
- Print run: Oxford Cabinet: 1,200 copies on paper + 12 on parchment. Chetham's: 1100 copies. biblequestions.info: 1,212 copies. (Record all three figures; primary archival figure needs confirmation at Plantin-Moretus Museum.)

### Layout (per Oxford Cabinet)

- Volumes 1-4 = Old Testament. Left-hand page: Hebrew original and its new literal Latin translation in parallel columns. Right-hand page: Septuagint with facing Latin translation. Below these: parallel text of Targum Onkelos (Jewish Babylonian Aramaic translation of the Torah) and a Latin translation of it.
- Chetham's phrasing (double-spread view): left page two columns Hebrew + Latin; right page Greek + Latin; underneath, Aramaic version on the left-hand page and its Latin translation on the right-hand side.
- Volume 5 = New Testament in Greek AND Syriac, each with its own Latin translation, plus the Syriac transliterated in Hebrew characters (Chetham's: "a translation of the Syriac into Hebrew"). This is the first printed European access to the Syriac Peshitta NT (per Grokipedia synthesis citing scholarly sources; corroborated by PIMS description mentioning Syriac versions).
- Volumes 6-8 = apparatus: grammars, dictionaries, "treasures" of the languages, eighteen treatises on linguistics and archaeology, collections of variants and critical notes. Chetham's adds: Volume Six has the complete Bible in Hebrew and Greek with an interlinear Latin translation printed between the lines.
- Volume-by-volume contents (from Puritan Board quotation): v.1-4 OT 1569-1570; v.5 NT 1571; v.6 includes Thesauri Hebraicae linguae epitome (Pagnini), Dictionarium Syro-chaldaicum (Guy Le Fevre de La Boderie), Grammatica linguae Syriacae and Syrorum peculium (Andreas Masius), Lexicon Graecum; v.7 OT Latin & Hebrew + NT Latin & Greek + Communes et familiares Hebraicae linguae idiotismi (Montano); v.8 Montano monographs (Ioseph, Ieremias, Tubal-Cain, Phalegh, Canaan, Caleb, Noah, Aaron, Neemias, Daniel) + Index Biblicus.

## Historical significance (PIMS/Dunkelgrun)

- "The most elaborate Bible ever printed, a library of biblical erudition with editions of the ancient Hebrew, Aramaic, Syriac, Greek, and Latin versions together with new scholarly instruments necessary to study and compare them."
- Doctrinal tension: including the Hebrew Bible and Aramaic Targums of Jewish tradition ran counter to the Council of Trent's decree that the Vulgate was the only authentic version of Christian Scripture; Arias Montano was denounced to the Inquisition over the targum-with-Latin material but acquitted (also reported by bible-download.jimdofree.com page).
- Hebrew base text: rabbinic Bible text of Jacob ben Chayyim (1524-25) with full Masoretic pointing (per Grokipedia synthesis); expanded Aramaic coverage vs Complutensian (full Targum Onkelos + Targum Jonathan).
- Key further scholarship: Alastair Hamilton, "In Search of the Most Perfect Text: The Early Modern Printed Polyglot Bibles from Alcala (1510-1520) to Brian Walton (1654-1658)," in *The New Cambridge History of the Bible*, vol. [noted by Oxford Cabinet as standard context]; Dunkelgrun thesis (Oxford, unpublished) now published by PIMS/Brepols.
- Comparative resource: Newberry Library, "Polyglots: The Bible in Multiple Tongues, 1502-1657" - https://publications.newberry.org/polyglot/
