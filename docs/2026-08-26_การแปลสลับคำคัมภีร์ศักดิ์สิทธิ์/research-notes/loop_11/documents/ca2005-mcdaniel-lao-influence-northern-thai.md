## Key extracts
- CRITICAL BIBLIOGRAPHIC FACT: in the Works Cited, McDaniel's own Harvard dissertation is given: "McDaniel, Justin. 2003. Invoking the Source: Nissaya Manuscripts, Pedagogy and Sermon-making in Northern Thailand and Laos. PhD Dissertation Harvard University." Also cited: McDaniel 2002 "The Curricular Canon in Northern Thailand and Laos," Manusya Special Issue: 20-59.
- Nissaya and vernacularization under Burmese/Lao rule: "the rise in vernacular literature and the decline in Pali composition (although nissaya, namasadda, and vohara manuscripts show a dynamic bi-lingual Pali-vernacular literary atmosphere at this time and under the Burmese)... must have been influenced by the Burmese and Lao penchant for composing nissaya manuscripts in the vernacular"; "The vernacular which had been relatively undeveloped in Northern Thailand before the eighteenth century (outside of chronicles) grew with the influence of the Burmese nissaya genre and work of Guru Pa Kancana."
- Kancana collection at Wat Sung Men (Phrae), quoting Hundius 1990: 34-35: the 1835-36 copying campaign in Luang Prabang produced "scholarly texts like (sub-)commentaries on canonical and post-canonical Pali literature, Pali Grammar, a great many of which are bilingual (Pali/NT or Lao) versions, including numerous works of the indigenous learned tradition, for instance a complete Nissaya version of the Pannasa-Jataka"; collection exceeds 15,000 phuk, "the largest one known to exist in a single place in Northern Thailand."
- Genre profile of the collection: "mostly comprising vernacular and bi-lingual genres (nissayas, namasaddas, voharas, anisong, xalong, pithi, horasat, parit, chadok nok nibat) that are also common in Laos"; many manuscripts drawn directly from Laos; most Lao manuscripts of the 17th-18th c. "were not Pali texts, but vernacular commentaries, glosses, and translations."
- Regional episteme argument: local canons of Laos and Northern Thailand were nearly identical and distinct from Siam, Cambodia, Burma; pedagogical methods and rhetorical styles reveal a shared regional epistemology of Buddhist learning largely untouched by colonial/modernist reform.
- Cross-border manuscript movement documented via Akiko Iijima's Nan/Xayabury study and monks Khuba Wong, Tu Nantha, Phra Suphan (1920s-30s exchanges).

## Relevance to loop topic
Directly substantiates sub-topics (B) and (C): names Lao and Lan Na nissaya/namasadda/vohara manuscript genres, gives the Wat Sung Men bilingual corpus (incl. a complete Pancasa-Jataka nissaya), and attributes part of Lan Na's vernacular rise to the Burmese nissaya genre carried by monk-scholars across the Mekong world.
