## Key extracts (from sources fully read this session)
- Walker 2020 (JAOS 140.3:676, fn. 2): "William Pruitt, building on earlier work by Tin Lwin and John Okell, meticulously demonstrates the extraordinary range of grammatical particles and abbreviation systems employed in a single type of Pali-Burmese bitext" - cited there as "William Pruitt, Etude linguistique de nissaya birmans (Paris: Ecole francaise d'Extreme-Orient, 1994)".
- McDaniel 2002 reference list additionally records three precursor articles by Pruitt on a single Burmese nissaya manuscript in the Bibliotheque Nationale: "Un Nissaya Birman de la Bibliotheque Nationale, le Patimikkha. Etude linguistique: premiere partie", Cahiers de l'Asie du Sud-Est 19 (1986): 84-119; deuxieme partie, 21 (1987): 7-45; troisieme partie, 22 (1987): 35-57.
- Pannabhoga 2023 quotes Pruitt 1992 (a related English-language study, Int. J. Lexicography 5(4):278-304): "[the] texts are given word-by-word translation into Burmese... in the texts called nissayas", and "Sinhalese Sanne and Burmese Nissayas have been important in Pali studies in the West"; Pruitt notes Shwe Zan Aung and Pe Maung Tin used the nissaya method when translating the Abhidhammattha-sangaha (1910) and Atthasalini (1920) into English for the Pali Text Society.

## Relevance to loop topic
The most detailed technical monograph on the grammar of Burmese nissaya particles/abbreviations; anchors the Burmese philological strand of the mainland-SEA glossing complex and its link to Western Pali scholarship (PTS translations mediated through nissayas).
