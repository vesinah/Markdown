## Key extracts
- Genre claim: "The nissaya genre is unique to Laos, Burma and Northern Thailand. Beginning as early as the 15th century, but becoming much more prevalent between 1720 and 1880, monks began to translate Pali texts in local vernacular languages using a variety of scripts." Nissaya ("support") texts "consist of Pali words or phrases followed by vernacular translations, usually with numerous commentarial additions by the translator"; many are based on canonical texts but often the cited Pali phrase cannot be located in the classical text, and Lao/Lanna authors also composed nissayas on non-canonical local Pali narratives.
- Terminology: in Thailand/Laos several designations coexist: nissaya, nissai, nisai sab, khan, nisrai, wohiin; e.g., at Wat Sung Men the "Hii Sip Jiit Nissaya" is colophon-titled "Wohiin Hii Sip Chiit Nisrai Hii Sip Chat". He reports the Chiang Mai Critical Study of the Northern Thai Version of the Panyasa Jataka stating no Pali-language Pancasa-Jataka manuscript has been found in Northern Thailand, and that the only difference between nissayas and voharas (wohan) may be the amount of Pali used - which he calls oversimplified.
- Manuscript corpus named: Channaburi Dhammapada Nissaya; Chiang Mai Siitmon Nissaya; Chiang Mai Kit Nissaya; Chiang Mai Atthasalini Nissaya; Lampang Kammavica Nissaya; Liang Pabiing Kammavaca/Uposatha Khan/Xai Noi/Saman Nissayas; Phrae Madhurasachambu Nissaya; (Vientiane) Panchakanipit Nissaya; Wiangchan Malai Bodhisat Nissaya; Wiangchan Phrakaeo Nissaya, etc.
- Commentarial services identified: first service is repetitive glossing of individual words (Madhurasa chambii Nissaya glosses even aham and dhamma); some nissayas operate "more like a bi-lingual dictionary than a textual translation" (Dibbamon Nissaya from Wat Mai SuvaNNabhumaram, Liang Pahang, Laos), the function usually associated with wohara/sab texts, and indeed some are titled nisrai wohan or nisai sab in colophons.
- Emic meaning: "Nissaya traditionally means 'support'... the relationship between a young monk or novice and his teacher (acariya or upadhaya)... the period of training, support or dependence of a newly ordained monk, usually lasting between five and ten years (Mahavagga 1.32,1; 1.54,4; 1.1,36; V. 1,5; V.4,2)." Oskar von Hinuber (personal communication recorded in fn. 52) suggested nissaya texts were so named because they "lean on" Pali texts.
- Function thesis: nissayas are disjointed reading/teaching supports, not sermons read aloud - "supports for those who have to read and explain Pali texts to an audience"; a student's guide and teacher's handbook sitting between the Pali object text and straight vernacular translation.
- Reference list includes: William Pruitt's EFEO 1994 monograph and Cahiers de l'Asie du Sud-Est articles (1986/1987) on a BnF Patimokkha nissaya.

## Relevance to loop topic
Core statement of McDaniel's pre-book position that Lao-Lanna nissaya/namasadda/vohara manuscripts form a pedagogical genre with distinctive rhetorical style and canon-shaping effects; supplies the terminological map (nissai/nisrai/wohan/khan) and dated prevalence window (15th c.; peak 1720-1880).
