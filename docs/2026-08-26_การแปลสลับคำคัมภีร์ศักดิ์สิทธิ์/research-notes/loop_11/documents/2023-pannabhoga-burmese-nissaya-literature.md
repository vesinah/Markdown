## Key extracts
- Definition and etymology: nissaya denotes "guidance/reliance/support" from the Mahavagga of the Vinaya-pitaka (novice must live under a preceptor's guidance for at least five years); the literary genre is word-to-word translation of canonical Pali or atthakatha texts, "not simply renderings of the Pali" but "meant to provide various interpretive resources in a compact encyclopaedic style."
- Deep history claims reported: earliest forms include a 7th-c. Sanskrit-Pyu "nissaya" found at Sriksetra and Kyanzittha-era inscriptions at Pagan; Tin Lwin (1961) holds the oldest extant Burmese nissaya is the Khuddasikkha nissaya by Mahakassapa under Narapatisithu (1174-1211), and classifies nissayas into canonical/non-canonical/secular types; Ruiz-Falques (2015) cautions that no grammatical nissayas are recorded from the Pagan period; Walker (2020, 678) supports Pali-Burmese nissaya extant from the late 13th c. plus the 1442 Thaungdwing governor inscription listing Pali and Sanskrit works "together with their nissayas."
- Relationship to official translation: the Chatthasangiti Pitaka Burmese translation "so frequently reproduces the interpretation of this nissaya with identical, or near identical, wording" (quoting Chris Clark 2015, Sydney PhD on the Apadana) - official translations are essentially old nissayas with the Pali removed; pathasesa ("extra text") additions appear in parentheses.
- Seven nissaya methods per Karunika (2008): pathanusari, pathasesa, saddattha, sanketattha, pannatyattha, voharattha, adhippayattha - each illustrated with worked examples (khinasavo, dvako, buddho, karopeti).
- Classroom practice: teacher + zaso ("repeater") recite; students perform ane'-de ("marking annotation in the margin") interlining printed textbooks; sub-techniques ane' kau'/pjan'/pwa/hpwin/hpo; canonical maxim quoted from the Samyankitika (Thanbyin Tikā, minister Thanbyin under Narapatisithu): "first break up the compound words... then the meaning should be brought out"; Janakabhivamsa's eight-step progression from word-to-word nissaya toward independent Pali composition.
- Named authors/titles: U Budh (Bhikkhu-patimokkha-pali nissaya), Neyin (Silakkhandha), Nan Kyaung (Kaccayana, Padarupasiddhi), Bagara (Abhidhammattha-sangaha), Payagyi (Parajikan), Pyay (Atthasalini), Pakokku (whole Tipitaka set), Janakabhivamsa (Bhasatika series, graded basic/advanced), Ma Oo (Prajikan), Moe Htee (whole Tipitaka); over twenty-two nissayas on the Abhidhammattha-sangaha alone by the 19th c.; Mandalay vs Pakokku training methods (per Dhammasami 2004, 134-147); Thai gambhi-nisai (gambhira-nissaya) noted via Gandhasarabhivamsa interview; Hinuber (1997, 100-01) on Thai vohara/namasap; Pathompong (2016): nissaya absent from current Thai Naktham/Pariyatti curricula.
- Regional survey section covers Sri Lanka (sannaya; Sutanipata-sannaya of 12th c.), Cambodia, Thailand and Laos, leaning on McDaniel 2008.

## Relevance to loop topic
The single most comprehensive open-access study for sub-topic (A): documents the living pedagogy, textual taxonomy, method typology, and the nissaya-to-official-translation pipeline in Burma, while connecting outward to sannaya/nissai parallels.
