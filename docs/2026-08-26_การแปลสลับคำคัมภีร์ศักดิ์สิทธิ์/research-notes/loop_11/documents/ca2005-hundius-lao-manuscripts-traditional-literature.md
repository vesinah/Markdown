## Key extracts
- Historical frame: between late 14th and mid-16th c., Chiang Mai's "Pali School" radiated learning across Tai kingdoms (Kengtung, Sipsongpanna, Luang Prabang); the shared "Tham script" (derived from ancient Mon alphabet) bound what he calls the "Tham Script Domain" / "Greater Lao Cultural Domain," versus hinduized Siam where a Khmer-derived (khom) script served religious writing until the late 19th c. (Siamese letters first used for the printed Tipitaka under Chulalongkorn).
- NISSAYA EVIDENCE 1 (fn. 3): in 1835-36 Ven. Kancana Mahathera of Wat Sung Men (Phrae) traveled to Luang Prabang and had copies made of over 2,800 phuk of Buddhist texts; "Along with various rare works written in Pali and nissaya texts, a complete version of the famous Pannasa-Jataka was brought to Phrae where it is still kept in perfect condition at Wat Sung Men until today." The Pannasa-Jataka edition in Siamese transliteration (Toyota Foundation-supported, Chiang Mai University team incl. Udom Roongruangsri, Bampen Rawin, Harald Hundius) appeared in 1999.
- NISSAYA EVIDENCE 2 (research agenda): among prospective studies he lists "Studies on the relationship between monolingual Pali versions of non-canonical Buddhist Jatakas, their nissaya or wohan versions and their secular literary adaptations, as well as orally transmitted versions across the whole Lao 'cultural domain' and Central Thailand." (Wohan/vohan = Lao cognate term for the vernacular-rendering genre.)
- Preservation data: Toyota-funded inventory project (1988-1994) inventoried about 128,000 phuk in 250 monasteries; German-funded PLMP (1992-2002) surveyed about 830 monasteries in all 17 provinces, preserved/inventoried about 368,000 phuk, and microfilmed about 1,000 rolls of 30 m each; roughly seventy percent of monasteries still uninventoried.
- Earlier scholarship frame: French pioneers (Finot's Recherches sur la litterature laotienne 1917 with its Liste generale des manuscrits laotiens; Lafont's 1959 inventory covering 83 monasteries, finding only 32 titles common to Luang Prabang/Vientiane/Champasak out of 1,616 - "trois litteratures essentiellement regionales").
- His own prior work cited: Hundius, "The Colophons of thirty Pali manuscripts from Northern Thailand," Journal of the Pali Text Society XIV (1990), pp. 1-173.

## Relevance to loop topic
Primary Laotian-side documentation: confirms nissaya (and wohan) as living Lao-Lanna manuscript categories tied to the Pannasa-Jataka transmission, quantifies the manuscript base, and defines the trans-regional (Lao/Lan Na/Khun/Lu) field within which Lao nissaya circulated.
