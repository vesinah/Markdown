## Per-source notes

### Alberizzi 2014
Kundoku is presented as a reading practice distinct from translation because it produces no parallel target text and keeps the Chinese original present as a visual outline; kana glosses supply inflectional endings while kunten marks signal SOV reordering of SVO Chinese. The oldest extant glossed manuscript is identified as the Zokukegonkyo ryakuso kanjoki, collated at Todaiji in 783 and 788, with white marks for punctuation and vermilion marks for syntactic reordering. The paper documents systematically different glossing conventions for Buddhist versus secular texts, varying across schools and scholar families. It also surveys the spread of vernacular reading across East Asia: official method in Japan, narrower in Uighur usage, abandoned in Korea, possible traces in Vietnam.

### Morley 2022
Argues that kundoku registers differ from any vernacular Japanese register, excluding ordinary vocabulary and admitting phraseologies found nowhere else in the language. Current taught kundoku conventions largely reflect late 19th-century codification; many premodern school techniques are lost. Any complete kundoku system allowing both decoding and encoding must depart from vernacular Japanese because Sinographs cannot fully inscribe Japanese. Advocates "Literary Sinitic"/"Sinograph" terminology to avoid conflating script with language, citing Ross King's "Sinographic cosmopolis."

### Takada / NINJAL kunten-shiryo project
A 2009-2012 NINJAL incubation project explicitly framed kanbun-kundoku, kunten-shiryo, and digital archive as keywords, acknowledging the field lagged in digital technologies. Pilot object was an esoteric sutra volume owned by NINJAL (Kongocho Issai nyorai Shinjitsu sho Dai jogensho Daikyo Okyo Vol. 1). Goal was structured description of interpretations verifiable against digital images of originals - an early model for digital critical editions of glossed texts.

### NINJAL Shosho kunten database
The live database covers volumes 1-9 of the NINJAL-held Shangshu old movable-type edition, letting users search wokototen (morphosyntactic, vernacular-reading, Sinoxenic-reading, and tone glosses) and order marks (re-ten, one-two marks) by position, color, shape, with IIIF-based character cutouts. Test release 2020; the EAJRS 2023 note documents improved search accepting kanji+kana strings like 明ニシ, co-occurrence indexes, and automatic glyph-position detection from images using reprint text. Development is tied to JSPS grant 17K18506 aiming at international document-structure description of kunten materials and automatic generation of kakikudashi-bun. NINJAL also distributes the Saidaiji-bon Konkomyo saisho okyo Heian early-gloss dataset (vol. 1) through its Text Data Sets site.

### Kyushu Ishiyamadera Konkomyo saisho okyo database
Experimental per-character kunten records for the former-Ishiyamadera Golden Light Sutra held at Kyushu University, including coordinate positions of each wokototen dot, ink color, shape codes, tone-mark values, and return-mark symbols outside standard charts. Notably records whether a glossed character is Sino-Japanese lexical or transliterated Sanskrit (梵漢 field) - direct evidence that Sanskrit transliteration strings received special treatment within sutra glossing. White (chalk) marks were prioritized over faded vermilion. Companion AiRIMaQ page argues kunten materials are unusually reliable linguistic evidence because the very copies that were glossed survive.

### Bundschuh (Routledge chapter + PAJLS paper)
Locates the earliest examples of translation in Japan in glossed Sinitic Buddhist texts read via kanbun kundoku, analyzing a 9th-century Golden Light Sutra parable for tense/aspect/modality/mirativity added by glossators. His PAJLS paper shows evidential asymmetry: secular Heian narrative frames with keri while Buddhist kundokugo frames with ki because religious authority is taken for granted ("Thus I have heard" needs no hearsay marking). Quotes Guest (2013) on semi-standardized calques and Steininger (2017) on kundoku cleaving to source structure in a formalized register. An Ohio State dissertation by the same author analyzes six auxiliaries (ki, keri, tsu, nu, ari, tari) across early Heian kundokubun.

### Payne 2017
Documents ajikan visualization of the Siddham syllable A as core Shingon preliminary practice, with bija semantics of origin/presence/destruction of being. States written mantras/dharani in Japan are usually in Siddham script and lists material culture: gorin sotoba funerary stupas with five Siddham seed mantras used even in Zen, Nichiren gohonzon combining kanji and Siddham mantra. Frames the phenomenon as transmission problems of transliteration from Sanskrit via Chinese to Japanese and calls for linguistic (rather than philosophy-of-language) approaches.

### Green (n.d.)
Explains why Siddham rather than Chinese graphs became the prestige medium for mantra in East Asia: Indian tantric masters in China wrote in Siddham, considered more powerful than Chinese representation; quotes van Gulik on Siddham's Gupta-era origin and use for dharani and bija letters. Documents physical adaptation: Siddham written with Chinese brush, vertically, and with the wooden stylus still used in Japan today. Standard ajikan iconography (Siddham A in moon disk on lotus) first standardized in Jitsue's oral-instruction record.

### Carnes (Reinardus)
Establishes the Amakusa Esopo no fabulas (printed 1592/93, unicum in the British Library) links to European fable collections: Steinhöwel, late Dorp, Camerarius, or Romulus Roberti. Argues fables were originally intended for teaching Latin to Japanese students before becoming literary Japanese reading matter that seeded a domestic fable tradition. Corroborated this session by NINJAL's image site (BL Or.59.aa.1, 17 x 11 cm, Portuguese-style romanization, bound with Heike monogatari and Qincuxu plus glossary/vocabulary sections) and Sophia's Laures Kirishitan Bunko title entry stating the book renders Latin "into the Japanese tongue" under Jesuit college authority at Amakusa, dated M.D.L.XXXXIII (1593).

### Wang et al. 2023 (+ EACL 2026 companion)
Computational formalization of the kundoku apparatus: kaeriten resolution equals stack-sortable permutation handling (re-ten push/pop; nested ordinal marks 一二/上下/甲乙 obeying Knuth's theorem), with okurigana as inflectional tagging. First Classical-Chinese-to-Kanbun parallel dataset released. The EACL 2026 follow-up validates annotations with a pushdown automaton and finds LLMs translate well but annotate kaeriten imperfectly - quantitative confirmation that return-mark syntax encodes genuine structural information.

## Supplementary observations seen this session (not given separate files)

- University of Tokyo Historiographical Institute: its English database list shows the Nara Period Komonjo full-text database (documents 702-780) and Dai Nihon komonjo cross-references, but no dedicated Nara-period kunten database surfaced there in this session's searches. The active kunten databases found are at NINJAL and Kyushu University. Premise of a UTokyo-HI Nara kunten database remains UNVERIFIED.
- Nick Williams (JPACT vol. 4, 2021, Hawaii) SEARCH-VERIFIED excerpts: Kukai assigns scripts a hierarchy - Siddham privileged above Chinese as direct written representation of the Dharma, Japanese absent from the hierarchy; cites Shoji jisso gi and Kukai's memorial to Emperor Saga (814) celebrating Siddham writings.
- UW thesis by Dine (handle 1773/20584) SEARCH-VERIFIED abstract: bonji spreads from esoteric deity representation (Heian-Kamakura) into non-religious objects and Shinjo-Buddhist syncretic symbolism - art-historical complement to textual studies.
- JLAK journal study (2022, DOI 10.14817/jlak.2022.71.85): 730 auxiliary-verb tokens in the Mubatsu-bon Lunyu jijie medieval glossed Analects; past-tense ki rare (11 examples) because kundoku style favors historical present to aid memorization of the original.
- KAKENHI 20K13050 (Kyushu, 2020-2023) "Construction of a database of the text of kunten materials": notes kunten corpus reliability (glossed originals survive) and includes outputs on kunten attached to phonetically transcribed mantras (音訳真言の訓点) - bridging kunten studies and Siddham transliteration strings.
- Wuglein (JK30, Stanford CSLI PDF): -aku nominalization in 9th-century kundokubun Buddhist narratives (Saidaiji Konkomyo; Todaiji Jizo jurin kyo) functions modally in quoted discourse - evidence glossed translations carried rich narrative morphology despite reputation as mechanical translationese.
