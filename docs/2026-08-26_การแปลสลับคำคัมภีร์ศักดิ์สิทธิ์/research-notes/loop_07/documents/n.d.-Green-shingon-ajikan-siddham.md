## Key extracts seen this session

- Ajikan described as "the most basic and essential element of Kukai's (774-835) religio-philosophical system" (citing Yamasaki 1988:190); attributed by tradition to Kukai transmitting Shingon from China early 9th century; over one hundred instructional texts on ajikan have been written since Kukai's time.
- Standardized components: Sanskrit syllable 'A' drawn in Siddham script inside a moon disk upon a lotus base; formula first found standardized in Record of Oral Instruction on the Ajikan by Kukai's direct disciple Jitsue.
- On Siddham script, quotes van Gulik (p. 7): Siddham, "a variety of the Brahmi script", popular in 4th-5th century Gupta India; "owed its popularity in China and Japan especially to the rise of Mantrayana... used in particular for writing dharani and mantra, and for the magic syllables known as bijakara or 'germ-letters'"; believed still in use in India around year 1000.
- Explains why Siddham was favored over Chinese transliteration: Indian tantric masters in China (Shubhakarasimha, Vajrabodhi, Amoghavajra) wrote using Siddham and the script "was thought to render the syllables more powerful than if represented in Chinese."
- Documents adaptation to Chinese writing practice: after arrival in China tantric masters wrote Siddham with ordinary Chinese brush rather than Indian reed pen; vertical columns top-to-bottom right-to-left; wooden stylus (mubi 木筆) used to the present day in Japan for writing Siddham letters (quoting van Gulik 56-62).
- Notes term origin: siddham from teacher's salutation on student writing boards ("siddhir-astu may there be success!"); Chinese xitan 悉曇 / Japanese shittan are transliterations.
