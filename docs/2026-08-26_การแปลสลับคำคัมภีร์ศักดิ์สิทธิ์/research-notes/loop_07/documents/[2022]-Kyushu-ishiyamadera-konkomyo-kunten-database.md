## Key extracts seen this session

- Experimental release of digitized kunten information attached to the Kyushu University-held former-Ishiyamadera manuscript of the Konkōmyō saishō ō kyō (Golden Light Sutra); page states material images are published separately by the Kyushu University Library.
- Unit of entry is one kanji character of the sutra; each wokototen dot gets its own record with fields: point number, ink color (墨), shape number/shape, position in a coordinate "tsubo" grid, reading per Tsukishima's (2009) wokototen chart, composite reading; kana-gloss fields include reading info and phonetic notes.
- Site records two ink types confirmed on this copy, vermilion (朱) and white (白); vermilion has faded so data covers white marks only. Also records tone marks (声点, single only here) with register value, and a field distinguishing whether the glossed character is Sino-Japanese lexical or transliterated Sanskrit (梵漢 distinction 漢語か音訳梵語か).
- Return marks (返点) outside the standard dot-chart shapes (e.g., one-two marks 一二点) recorded as separate symbol entries; interlinear note rows (割注) flagged.
- References cited include Kasuga Seiji (1958) "Ishiyamaji-bon saishōōkyō kototen yori", Katsuyama Yukito (1984) on old kunten methods of the sutra, Tsukishima Hiroshi (2009) Kunten goi shūsei sōron, Hirunuma Mei (2015) on the frontispiece and explanation.
- Related Kyushu/AiRIMaQ topic page (fetched via search): kunten materials are highly reliable language sources because "the original at the time glosses were added survives," unlike vernacular texts known only through later copies; they carry vocabulary/usage, tone/accent from tone marks, syntax from return marks, and kana-script evolution from kana-gloss letterforms.
