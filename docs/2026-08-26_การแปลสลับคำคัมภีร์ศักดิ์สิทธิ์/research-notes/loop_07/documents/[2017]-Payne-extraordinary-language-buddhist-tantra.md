## Key extracts seen this session

- Describes ajikan 阿字観 meditation: gazing at a white circle on black background inscribed with the syllable A in Siddham script; second of two preliminary practices before the fourfold training (shido kegyo 四度加行) of a Shingon priest (ajari 阿闍梨); also taught standalone for lay adherents.
- The syllable A is a seed syllable (bija) mantra with threefold significance: origin of all being, present in all being, destruction of all being; linked to phonematic emanation theory (citing Padoux 1990) where A is first syllable of the cosmic phonemic sequence and the negative prefix ending all being.
- States Shingon and Tendai employ mantra (shingon 真言), dharani (darani 陀羅尼), vidya (myoshu 明呪) in ritual and written forms, "the latter usually in Siddham script"; these uses spread beyond tantric traditions into a wider "tantric penumbra" in Japan.
- Examples given: komyō shingon 光明真言 promoted by Myoe Koben (1173-1232); gorin sotoba 五輪率都婆 funerary stupas inscribed in Siddham with five seed mantras for the five elements, used across sects including Zen; Nichiren gohonzon described as mandala-like written arrangement combining Chinese characters and Siddham script mantra.
- Frames transmission problems as transliteration/translation issues from Sanskrit through Chinese to Japanese (citing Abe 2005 p. 292); argues philosophy of language has contributed little to studying such usage, suggesting linguistics tools (pragmatics per Davidson, cognitive/socio/historical linguistics, semiotics; Rambelli 2013, Yelle 2013).
