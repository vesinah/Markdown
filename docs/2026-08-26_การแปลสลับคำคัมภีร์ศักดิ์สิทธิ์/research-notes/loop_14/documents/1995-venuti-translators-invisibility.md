## Secondary definitions encountered this session

- A 2025 preprint abstract (DOI 10.20944/preprints202505.0392.v1) states: "Domestication is a translation theory in which the source text (to be translated) is matched to the foreign reader by erasing its original linguistic and cultural difference. This match aims at making the target text (translated text) more fluent. On the contrary, foreignization is a translation theory in which the foreign reader is matched to the source text." The same abstract reports a "domestication index" computed over translations of the Greek New Testament into Latin and 35 modern languages.
- Handbook of Translation Studies vol. 2 (John Benjamins, 2011) contains the entry "Domestication and foreignization" by Outi Paloposki (University of Turku), pp. 40-42, DOI 10.1075/hts.2.dom1; its reference apparatus cites Schleiermacher's "On the different methods of translating" and links to Venuti's Translator's Invisibility DOI - documenting the standard Schleiermacher-to-Venuti lineage for the pair of terms.

## Relevance to sacred-text literalism vs comprehension

Venuti's domestication/foreignization pair supplies the modern vocabulary for the literalism-vs-comprehension axis: word-for-word or archaizing sacred-text translation aligns with foreignizing retention of source-culture strangeness, while dynamic-equivalence-style fluency aligns with domestication. The 2025 GNT-focused domestication-index study found in the same session applies exactly this framework to Bible versions.
