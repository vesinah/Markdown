## Publisher description (verbatim)

"This volume addresses the methods and motives for translating the central texts of the world's religions and investigates a wide range of translation challenges specific to the unique nature of these writings. Translation theory underpins the methodology for the analysis of a variety of scriptures and brings important and sensitive issues of translation to the fore."

## About the editor (publisher bio)

Lynne Long teaches Translation Studies at the University of Warwick; has published on Bible translation and translation history; involved with American Bible Society projects and the AHRC programme "Translation and Translation Theories East and West" (Centre for Asian and African Literatures); member of ACUME European research project in Cultural Memory.

## Relevance to sacred-text literalism vs comprehension

The collection frames sacred-text translation as a distinct problem area spanning multiple religions (Jewish, Christian, Buddhist, Hindu, Muslim, Sikh-tradition, Orthodox liturgical). Chapter titles themselves map the literalism/comprehension axis: Johnson's "Making Sanskritic or Making Strange?" (foreignizing vs domesticating for Hindu texts), Serban's "Archaising versus Modernising" for Orthodox liturgy, Kirk's survey of "current approaches to Bible translation worldwide" (the Nida-line dynamic equivalence camp), Abdul-Raof on Qur'an translation where cultural considerations interact with strong resistance to rendering. Burke's chapter is from the Nida Institute of Biblical Scholarship.
