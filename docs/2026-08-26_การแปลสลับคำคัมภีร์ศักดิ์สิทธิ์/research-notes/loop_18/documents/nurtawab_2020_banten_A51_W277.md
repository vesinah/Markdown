## Content summary from citable secondary pages

From Nurtawab, Ervan. "The Abolished Sultanate and its Royal Library: Arabic Qur'an Commentaries from Eighteenth-Century Banten", Archipel (openedition.org/archipel/9536):
- A.51, W.277 and RAS Arabic 4 contain IDENTICAL interlinear Malay translations; A.54 has Javanese interlinear translation.
- A.51 uses a non-Hafs reading system (Warsh/Qalun); copyist of W.277 attempted to convert the Warsh/Qalun qira'a to Hafs; margins of A.51 carry qira'at options and verse-numbering information.
- RAS Arabic 4 appears to be the most recent copy, referring to both A.51 and W.277 (Nurtawab 2023: 11-19).
- The Jalalayn served as the main reference/bridge for these interlinear translations (see Nurtawab 2020: 181 ff.); the royal collection held many Jalalayn copies plus Tafsir al-Baghawi with interlinear Javanese (Friederich & van den Berg 1873: 69).
- W.277 and W.278 passed through the collection of Hermann von de Wall (1807-1873), handed to the Bataviaasch Genootschap in 1873; collection later merged into PNRI.
- Also: Nurtawab & Syukroni, "Qur'anic Arabic, Tafsir al-Jalalayn and Javanese: Javanese Translations in 18th-Century Bantu/Banten Qur'an A.54" (in Johanna Pink ed., Translating the Qur'an in Indonesia, Routledge).

From Annabel Teh Gallop, Royal Asiatic Society blog, 25 Nov 2022 ("A Quran manuscript with full Malay translation"):
- RAS Arabic 4 is "the only known single-volume Qur'an with a full interlinear translation in Malay"; presented by Admiral C.M. Pole Bart, June 19, 1830; nine lines of Arabic per page with "Malay text sloping beneath in a much smaller, very fine, hand"; gold verse roundels; Asian paper; likely 18th century, likely from Banten.
- Gallop reports Nurtawab's (2020) proposal that Qur'an manuscripts with interlinear translations should be regarded as tafsir because they explicate meaning beyond pure translation (interpretation, variant views), composed independently of Abd al-Ra'uf's Tarjuman al-Mustafid.
- Example given: Surat al-Kahf v.11 Malay wording nearly identical across RAS Arabic 4 and PNRI A.51 (309 years sleep detail, annual turning of the Sleepers).
- Further reading: Gallop, 'Qur'an manuscripts from Southeast Asia in British collections', in Daneshgar & Nurtawab (eds.), Malay-Indonesian Islamic studies: a Festschrift in honor of Peter G. Riddell, Brill.
