# Document: Swahili ajami Qur'an translation tradition (item E)

## Manuscript evidence of interlinear Swahili

- Centre for the Study of Manuscript Cultures (CSMC Hamburg), Manuscript Cultures journal vol. 17 (complete PDF, csmc.uni-hamburg.de/publications/mc/files/articles/mc17-complete.pdf): special issue on Swahili manuscripts. Key statements: "much of the variability one can find is due to the differing arrangement of lines, variable marking of pauses and the insertion of glosses and interlinear translations" (on Ahmed Parkar's findings for Hamziyya mss); early non-European-commissioned translations from Arabic into Swahili include Qissat al-Yusuf, Tabaraka, Hamziyya, Maulidi Barzanji; Annachiara Raia contributes on Qissat al-Yusuf/Chuo cha Herkal; Ann Biersteker contributes an essay ("Writing in Swahili") linking stone carving practices to manuscript conventions. Bilingual Hamziyya manuscripts analyzed with matn/tarjama line layout: manuscript L has tarjama lines; manuscript M has paratexts but no tarjama.
- Ahmed Parkar / Burhan (1976), 'Hamziyyah - The Oldest Swahili Translation', Kiswahili 46/1: 71-75.
- Annachiara Raia (2020), "Between the Lines: Re-citing Qur'anic Verses in Swahili Manuscripts", in Copying Manuscripts: Textual and Material Craftsmanship (CSMC Series Minor XCIII) - Academia.edu 47330904.

## Assessment

Interlinear Swahili under Arabic is well attested for pedagogical religious poems (Hamziyya tarjama lines; glosses/interlinear translations per Parkar and MC17), and a full ajami-script Swahili Qur'an commentary by al-Buhriy exists (Chande 2021). However, a specific study devoted to SWAHILI INTERLINEAR TRANSLATION INSIDE QUR'AN MANUSCRIPTS (parallel to the Malay/Javanese Banten cases) was not located - candidate open question.
