## Key findings

1. Item B resolved decisively. The correct citation is ERVAN (not Evan) Nurtawab 2020 in Indonesia and the Malay World 48(141):169-189 (DOI 10.1080/13639811.2020.1724469) - not the Journal of Qur'anic Studies. Core thesis per Gallop's RAS blog: Banten Qur'ans A.51/W.277 (PNRI) share an identical interlinear Malay translation derived from Tafsir al-Jalalayn; Nurtawab argues such interlinear translations constitute tafsir (exegetical intent, variant views, qira'a notes). Sequel: IMW 51(150) 2023, adding RAS Arabic 4.
2. Item F resolved beyond expectation. The exact 1905 Ottoman print-approval chain for a Jawi tafsir is documented with shelfmarks: Meşihat Archive IST_MFT_MSH_DFT02_01785 S.133, NR.31 (6 Aug 1905) and BOA MF.MKT. 865/52 (20 Aug 1905). The Jawi work is Tarjuman al-Mustafid, erroneously registered as Beyzavi's tafsir; Meccan scholars approved it because Istanbul staff could not read Jawi. Committee historiography anchored in Digiroglu (2018, Marmara Ilahiyat 54; plus her mushaf-library study) and the 1892-origin Tedkik-i Muellefat Commission.
3. Item D fully verified: Riddell, Transferring a Tradition (Berkeley: Centers for South and Southeast Asia Studies, University of California, 1990, 297pp.) on al-Singkili's Malay rendering of the Jalalayn; complemented by his 2001 survey and Brill 2017 monograph.
4. Item A clarified but not closed. Witkam has no dedicated article on Malay interlinear Qur'an translation. His relevant corpus = Qur'an-manuscript articles (1993; 2002 Ottoman copying rules; 2012), the Leiden inventories (Ter Lugt Press, electronic 2006-2007, which do describe interlinear mss incl. Malay, e.g. Or. 4976), and Malay catalogue editorship. The title fragment "The Islamic Manuscript Tradition" matched nothing by Witkam.
5. Item E mapped. Interlinear Swahili is best attested in pedagogical poem manuscripts (Hamziyya tarjama lines; MC17 special issue; Parkar; Raia 2020; Biersteker & Plane 1989), while full Qur'an translation in ajami script is documented for al-Buhriy (Chande 2021, AJIS 6(4)) and printed traditions (al-Farsy 1969; Mayunga 2003). A dedicated study of interlinear Swahili INSIDE Qur'an manuscripts was not found.
6. Item G partially resolved. The practice (kiai reads Arabic, santri write pegon interlinear meanings with i'rab codes) is abundantly documented as bandongan/weton/sorogan/gandul/ngabsahi/maknani (Nurtawab 2019; Pink 2020; Munip 2016; pesantren sources). The specific lexeme "ngawul" produced zero scholarly hits across ID/EN queries; treat as unattested until located in a dictionary or field source.
7. Item C remains open. Daneshpajuh's biography is solidly verified; the claimed ca.-1348 SH (1969) work on oldest Persian Qur'an translations could not be confirmed on any reachable citable page; Persian reference lists (e.g., Wikishia's old-translations list) route instead through Azarnoosh (Encyclopedia Islamica Foundation, vol. 15).
