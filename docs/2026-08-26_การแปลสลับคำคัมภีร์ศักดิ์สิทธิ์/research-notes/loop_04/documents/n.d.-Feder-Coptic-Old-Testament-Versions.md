## Key facts (session-verified)

- Oldest witnesses of Coptic biblical translations are Coptic glosses to Greek biblical texts. Example: fragmentary papyrus British Museum EA 10825 with a Greek-Coptic glossary to Hosea and Amos (TM 61982); verso of a Greek land register dated around 200 CE; glossary dated third-fourth century; presumably found near Oxyrhynchos; organized in columns of Greek words/phrases from Hosea and Amos "to which a Coptic translation was then added"; idiom clearly Mesokemic.
- Fourth-century miscellany manuscripts plus these glosses likely form "the oldest stratum of Coptic biblical translations."
- The Sahidic dialect stands out as a codified norm; it became in the later fourth century "the standard language of Egyptian Christian communication and literature," remaining so from the classical period (fourth-eighth century) to the twelfth century.
- "It is very likely... that the Sahidic Bible in the form of the Sahidic Standard Translation became the canonical Bible of native Egyptian Christians in the course of the fifth century."
- "Full or pandect bible manuscripts never existed in the Coptic tradition."
- Dialect versions divide into short-lived (Akhmimic, Lycopolitan, Mesokemic, Old/Paleo-Bohairic, early Fayyumic) and long-lived (Fayyumic, Bohairic).
- Bohairic: current Coptic Bible of the Coptic Church alongside Arabic; the only Coptic dialect still used in the liturgy "in addition to Greek and, above all, Arabic." Classical Bohairic version emerged before the ninth century, probably created early seventh century; proven by Kellia inscriptions and documentary witnesses bridging fourth-century Old Bohairic to ninth-century classical transmission.
- From the twelfth century onward Bohairic was the only Coptic Bible as knowledge of Coptic declined with loss of Upper Egyptian monasteries; Bohairic OT canon is reduced: Ruth, Ezra-Nehemiah, 3 Ezra, Ecclesiastes, Canticles, Esther, Judith, Tobit have no Bohairic witness at all; Wisdom and Sirach survive only as lectionary pericopes, which are "merely transpositions from a Sahidic Vorlage."

## Corroborating session evidence

- CoptOT project blog (Göttingen): "the Coptic Old Testament is a daughter translation of the Greek Septuagint"; Bohairic OT partially translated (Pentateuch, Job, Psalms, Prophets complete); some Bohairic Holy Week lectionary pericopes show undeniable Sahidic Vorlage influence; scribes "seem most probably to have translated them directly from a Sahidic Vorlage"; raises question whether Sahidic and Bohairic were fully mutually intelligible at the White Monastery while passages were still read aloud in Coptic.
- Zanetti (Bohairic Liturgical Manuscripts, Orientalia Christiana Periodica 61, 1995, pp. 65-94) per Academia.edu abstract: bilingual Coptic-Arabic liturgical manuscripts reflect a tradition of liturgical bilingualism since the 7th century; all Coptic liturgical manuscripts after the 11th century are primarily Bohairic; significant retention of Greek terms and phrases in Bohairic liturgy into the 7th century.

## Relevance to loop topic

Coptic shows the full arc: Greek sacred original retained, vernacular Egyptian versions created for access, then the vernacular version itself becoming liturgically sacralized (Bohairic), with Arabic later layered as further access language — hieroglossia stacking.
