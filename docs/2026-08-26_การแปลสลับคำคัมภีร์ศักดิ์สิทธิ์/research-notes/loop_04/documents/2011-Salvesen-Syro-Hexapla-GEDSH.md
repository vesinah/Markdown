## Extracts and notes (session-verified)

- The Syro-Hexapla is "a rendering into Syriac of the Septuagint column of Origen's Hexapla."
- Origen (ca. 185 - ca. 253) created the Hexapla as a six-columned synopsis of Scripture: Hebrew text word by word, a Greek transcription, the Septuagint, and three later Jewish Greek versions (Aquila, Symmachus, Theodotion).
- Origen marked up the fifth (Septuagint) column with text-critical signs indicating material added or omitted compared with the current rabbinic Hebrew text.
- The Hexapla original was created and kept in Caesarea and may have perished during the Persian or Arab invasions of the mid-7th century; only a few folios of Psalms remain, dating from the 9th-10th centuries.
- Because most Greek manuscripts dropped Origen's critical signs, the Hexaplaric Septuagint column was copied separately with its critical signs; this column was translated literally into Syriac by Pawlos (Paul) of Tella in 616-17, supplemented with short readings from the other Greek versions.
- Value for scholarship: the Syro-Hexapla preserved the text-critical signs and fragments from the later Jewish Greek versions.
- Reception: much more influential among West Syrians than in the Church of the East, though 'rediscovered' by Timotheos I and used by Isho'dad of Merv (9th cent.) in his biblical commentaries; influenced Ya'qub of Edessa's revision of the Old Testament; individual readings sometimes crept into later Peshitta manuscripts.
- Listed sources include: Ceriani's Pentateuchi Syrohexaplaris quae supersunt (Monumenta sacra et profana 2, 1864); de Lagarde, Bibliothecae Syriacae (1892); Field, Origenis Hexaplorum quae supersunt (1875); Vööbus, The Pentateuch in the version of the Syro-Hexapla, facsimile edition of a Midyat MS discovered 1964 (CSCO 369, 1975).

## Relevance to loop topic

Canonical case of preserving a sacred-language original (Origen's recensional apparatus) by embedding it in a vernacular-adjacent translation language (Syriac), including columnar/marginal variant access to multiple Greek versions.
