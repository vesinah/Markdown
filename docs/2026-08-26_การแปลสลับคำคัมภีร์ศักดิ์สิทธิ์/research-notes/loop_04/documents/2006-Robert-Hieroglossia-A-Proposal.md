## Key definitions and claims (session-verified from search excerpts)

- Definition of hieroglossia: "the sum of relations that develop between a language perceived as a central or founding element in a given culture area (this language being the hierogloss) and a language or languages that are perceived as being dependent, not historically or linguistically, but ontologically or theologically, on that hierogloss."
- The dependent language ("vulgar tongue" or "vernacular"; Robert's coinage "laogloss") "is clearly considered not to be self-sufficient."
- Two strategies of the laogloss: (1) massive borrowing from the hierogloss, adapting foreign terms to its phonetics; or (2) "the much more subtle and deep task of reconstructing its own vocabulary, reorientating its conceptual links on the basis of the hierogloss."
- Innovations in the laogloss are "understood as enriching and concretizing the potentialities of the hierogloss... an exegesis of the hieroglossic original, but not a true innovation."
- Robert states hieroglossia is found across "all the religious and cultural areas of Eurasia," explicitly including Ethiopia, and lists Christian traditions: "Syriac, Coptic, Armenian, Ethiopian, and even Slavonic. The translation of the Bible into those languages... conferred a dignity close to the original Hebrew and Greek."
- He developed the concept from his study of Japanese Buddhism (Sanskrit - Chinese - Japanese relations).
- Ten proposed research categories include: discourse on supremacy/truth/efficacy of a sacred language; critical discourse aimed at a sacred language in time of reform; discourse on affiliation of vulgar languages with sacred languages; exegesis performed in the same or another language; translations of religious texts; shift/transfer/adaptation of religious vocabulary; access of a vulgar language to religious status ("the vying for sacrality"); liturgical and magic use of sacred languages; language purism and religion.
- Robert distinguishes hieroglossia from diglossia: it is "essentially a religious phenomenon" and "falls in the main outside the scope of sociolinguistics."

## Attribution note (important for this loop)

Per the NIHU Magazine interview page and a 2025 Balcania et Slavia article both seen this session, the term "hieroglossia" was coined by Jean-Noël Robert. The loop topic attributed the concept to Boris Uspensky; what this session verified is Uspenskij's associated framework of diglossia for Church Slavonic vs Russian (see Bunčić document). The pairing "Church Slavonic as hieroglossia" appears in Robert's own list of Christian hieroglossic traditions.

## Relevance to loop topic

Provides the umbrella theoretical category for the entire loop: sacred-language originals (Greek, Ge'ez, Church Slavonic) retained while vernaculars (Syriac, Armenian, Coptic, Amharic, Romanian) gain access through translation understood as exegesis.
