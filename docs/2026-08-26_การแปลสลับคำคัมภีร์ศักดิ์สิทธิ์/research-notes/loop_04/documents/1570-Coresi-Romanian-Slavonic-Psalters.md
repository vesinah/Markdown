# Coresi (deacon, Brașov) — Romanian Psalter 1570 and Slavonic-Romanian bilingual Psalters 1577/1589

## Key facts (session-verified)

- The 1570 title page/epilogue reads: "Cu mila lu Dumnezeu eu diaconu Coresi, déca văzuiu că mai toate limbile au cuvântulu lu Dumnezeu în limba lor, numai noi Rumânii n'avămu" ("By the mercy of God I, deacon Coresi, having seen that almost all languages have God's word in their language, only we Romanians do not have it"), citing Matthew ("cine céteşte să înţelégâ") and Paul to the Corinthians: "întru besérecâ mai vrătosu cinci cuvinte cu înţelesulu mieu să grâescu ca şi alalţi să învăţu, de cătu untunérecu de cuvinte neînţelése într'alte limbi" (five intelligible words in church better than ten thousand unintelligible in other languages).
- The 1570 Romanian Psalter was printed at Brașov between 6 February and 27 May 1570; the initiative belonged to Pavel Tordași, the Romanian Reformed bishop, who came to Brașov to supervise the printing. Three copies survive.
- An earlier Coresi Psalter of 1568 is known only through a glossary published by G. Săulescu in 1840 (journal Albina românească); no exemplar survives, and its existence was doubted, but researchers hold that the 1570 and 1577 psalters used its text. A further psalter print ca. 1576-1578 is known from a single leaf discovered by Iorga, also lost; it was bilingual Slavo-Romanian intercalated.
- The Slavo-Romanian Psalter of 1577 repeats the 1570 epilogue with an addition stating purpose: "...scrisu-v-amu aceaste psaltiri cu otveat [with response/translation], de-am scos de in psaltirea srabeasca pre limba rumaneasca, sa va fie de intelegatura si gramaticilor" — i.e., extracted from the Serbian (Church) Slavonic psalter into Romanian "so that it may serve for understanding and for grammarians."
- The crestinortodox.ro study states the 1577 bilingual psalter filled a gap "datorită tot mai puținei cunoașterii a slavonei bisericesti din partea preoților, ale căror cărți de cult circulau mai ales în această limba" (due to priests' declining knowledge of Church Slavonic, whose liturgical books mostly circulated in that language).
- Șerban, son of Coresi, printed a second bilingual Slavo-Romanian psalter around 1589 (paper watermarks from Brașov and Sibiu), textually dependent on the 1577 edition; per the uadventus article these bilingual prints were made "pentru a învăța limba slavonă prin intermediul românei" (to teach the Slavonic language through Romanian).
- Coresi's activity ran over twenty years (1559-1583), begun at the invitation of Brașov judge Johannes Benckner, owner of the first paper mill in Ardeal; earlier scripture prints: Tetraevangheliarul 1561, Apostolul (Praxiu) 1566. His epilogues are described as raising, "for the first time, with determination and clarity, the problem of introducing the Romanian language into religious worship."

## Relevance to loop topic

Coresi's bilingual books are printed instruments of hieroglossic access: Church Slavonic sacred text preserved alongside a Romanian rendering explicitly framed as translation-and-teaching apparatus ("otveat," "de intelegatura si gramaticilor").
