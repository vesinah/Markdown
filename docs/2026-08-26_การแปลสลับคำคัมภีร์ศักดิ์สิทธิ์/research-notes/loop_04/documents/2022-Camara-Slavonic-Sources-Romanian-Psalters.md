## Key facts (session-verified)

- "Eight versions of the Psalter - four manuscripts and four prints - date back to the 16th century. The oldest version is the Hurmuzaki Psalter (PH), rather uncertainly dated 1491-1516, because of the watermark... The second manuscript... is the Psalter from Voroneț (PV), dated 1551-1558. The other two manuscripts are the Scheian Psalter (PS), dated 1573-1578, and the Moldavian Psalter (PM), dated 1573-1585." Printed: Săulescu Psalter (ca. 1568, now lost); Romanian Psalter (CP, 1570); Slavo-Romanian Psalter (CP1, 1577); and a fourth print.
- The oldest Romanian Psalters "are actually the oldest Romanian language monuments."
- Texts divide into two groups by redactional features, A and B.
- Al. Mareș demonstrated the translation source was Slavonic; the Slavonic Psalters do not usually match the parallel Romanian versions; he found congruencies between PH and the Mladenović Psalter (1346), Prague Psalter (1359), Paris Psalter (1380), Vienna Psalter (1463), Bologna Psalter (13th c.), Sinai Psalter (11th c.).
- Proof of a commentated Slavonic Psalter behind the tradition: the title of Psalm 110 in the Hurmuzaki Psalter ("Lăudăm Domnulu cel viu" 'Praise the Living Lord') interprets the interjection Hallelujah exactly as in the Vienna Psalter gloss: "Alliluiě se tl'kuet se ubo hv(a)la živomu B(og)u."
- Directly relevant to interlinear practice: "It is known that this manuscript contains a large number of Slavonic elements. According to Mareş..., these can be attributed to the carelessness of the copyist, who took sequences from the Slavonic interlinear text, and then crossed them with a line."
- Camară's new conclusions: version A (source of PH and PV) is based on a version related to the Belgrade Psalter (13th century, from Rascia); version A was later revised on the basis of a text closely related to the Oxford Serbian Psalter (14th century), producing version B (source of PS, PM, and the four printed psalters). Version A is plausibly the original Romanian translation. The archaic elements in PH "seem to be due to the Slavonic version of the antigraph, which was bilingual."
- The Slavonic sequences present in PH are of Bulgarian recension, not Serbian.

## Relevance to loop topic

Documents a bilingual Slavonic-Romanian antigraph with an actual interlinear Slavonic layer visible through crossed-out Slavonic sequences in the Hurmuzaki Psalter: Church Slavonic retained as sacred source while Romanian provides line-by-line vernacular access.
