## Key facts (session-verified)

- "The sixteenth century Romanian Psalters are among the oldest texts written in Romanian. There is no consensus in the academic literature on their translation initiative, which was attributed to Orthodox, Hussites, Catholics or Reformers."
- "It is generally acknowledged that the prototype translation, from a Church Slavonic source yet unidentified, underwent multiple revisions following different sources, resulting in related versions, of which four manuscripts and three printed texts from the sixteenth century survived; some of them are bilingual (Slavonic-Romanian)."
- Latin or Hungarian source hypotheses have been advanced but "need further investigation."
- Manuscript shelf marks: PH Hurmuzaki Psalter, beginning of 16th c., ms. Rom. 3077 Romanian Academy Library; PV Voroneț Psalter, c. 1551-1558, ms. Rom. 693; PS Scheian Psalter, c. 1573-1578, ms. Rom. 449; PC "Ciobanu" Slavonic-Romanian Psalter, c. 1573-1585, ms. Rom. 3465.
- Printed: CP Coresi, Brașov 1570 (CRV-16); CP1 Coresi, Brașov 1577 (CRV-19); CP1a Coresi, Brașov 1577 (CRV-78); CP2 Șerban Coresi, Brașov 1589 (CRV-19A).
- Project provides verse-level alignment of the 16th-century Romanian psalters with an XML annotation interface.
- Related items on the page include Camară's studies on the bilingual source ("Izvodul bilingv al Psaltirii Hurmuzaki," Limba română 71/1-2, 2022, p. 39-54) and "Straturi redacţionale în Psaltirea 'Hurmuzaki'."

## Supporting detail from the Romanian Wikipedia page for the Voroneț Psalter surfaced in search results (used only as a locator; not cited as a scholarly source)

- The Voroneț manuscript presents first the Slavonic phrases, followed by the Romanian translation; discovered by Simion Florea Marian in 1882 at Voroneț monastery; donated to the Romanian Academy as ms. rom. 693; contains 73 leaves, missing the beginning (up to Psalm 77); bilingual text edited by George Giuglea (1910-1911).

## Relevance to loop topic

Confirms the corpus and its bilingual (interlinear-style, alternating-language) layout, with Church Slavonic as authoritative source language and Romanian as access vernacular across both manuscripts and prints.
