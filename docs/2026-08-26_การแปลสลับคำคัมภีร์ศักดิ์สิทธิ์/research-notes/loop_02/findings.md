## Per-source notes

1. **Henkel 1996** - Programmatic essay defining the OHG interlinear versions as a "Quellengruppe" and assessing their language- and literary-historical value; delivered at the Wolfram von Eschenbach-Gesellschaft conference in Cambridge (Sept. 1994), printed in Wolfram-Studien 14, pp. 46-72. Open-access scan hosted at Freiburg (Freidok publicationId 10535, DOI 10.6094/UNIFR/10535), but the PDF is image-only (JBIG2 scans), so its argument could not be quoted this session. Cited by Handschriftencensus in the Clm 19410 record.
2. **Krotz 2002** - Reconstructs the codex structure of the Monsee fragments and the Latin paratexts of the Paris Isidor manuscript; includes a re-edition of glossary Jc from Junius 25. Krotz is preparing a new edition of the Monseer Fragmente (De Gruyter, announced on her Vienna page); with Katharina Kaska she published "Ein neues Monseer Fragment" (ZfdA 145, 2016, pp. 82-88).
3. **Handschriftencensus Isidor records** - Transmission: Paris BN lat. 2326 (codex, dated c. 800, western Rhenish Franconian/Lothringian script-language, written in Austrasian territory) plus the Monsee fragments scattered over Vienna OeNB Cod. 3093*, Hannover Ms I 20b, and Vienna Cod. 2997 pastedown. Layout of the Paris bilingual: Latin left column, OHG right column; the OHG translation breaks off at fol. 22r; contents include Isidore's De fide catholica (fols 1-79r), the Affatim glossary, and Sententiae sanctorum patrum. Standard editions: Hench 1893; Eggers 1964 (ATB 63); excerpts in Hellgardt 2022.
4. **Masser 1997 / Benediktinerregel** - Single witness St Gall Cod. 916; early 9th century, probably St Gall per Greifswald teaching material describing it as "die unvollstaendige Interlinearversion der Moenchsregel". Masser's companion volumes: edition of Cod. 915 as correction copy (2000) and a full commentary volume (2002). TITUS offers a digital parallel text based on Daab 1959 (ATB 50); e-codices hosts the facsimile.
5. **Tax editions / Cod. Sang. 21** - Handschriftencensus 15459 (fetched in full): mid-12th-century Einsiedeln copy of Notker's psalm translation with commentary, classified "Lateinisch-deutscher Mischtext", with rich marginal and interlinear glosses and accentuation that often deviates from Notker's system. Tax's three ATB volumes pair with three "Notker latinus" volumes documenting Latin sources verse by verse. NCE describes the work as an OHG-Latin interlinear psalter (dating it c. 1100, vs HSC's 1125-1150 - discrepancy flagged).
6. **Ochsenbein (HLS) 2011** - Notker (c. 950-1022), rector of the St Gall monastic school, translated Latin schoolbooks with commentary across all seven arts; his OHG Psalms and Gregory's Moralia in Iob aimed at a wider circle; first medieval commentator on Aristotle; several translations lost (Bucolica, Cato, Andria, probably Boethius De sancta trinitate).
7. **Morawetz 2023** - Oxford DPhil analyzing Murbach Hymns glosses (Junius 25, ninth-century interlinear version) via a three-level "functional net": vertical lemma relation, horizontal syntactic linkage, visual/graphetic expression. Finds glosses consistently vernacular-based within the limits imposed by the Latin main text and interprets the whole as "an elaborate teaching aid designed to illustrate how the linguistic systems of Latin and Old High German function."
8. **Junius 25 context** - Bodleian HAB project pages: booklet collection of 8th-9th-century fascicles with three OHG glossaries plus the Latin hymnal with interlinear German glosses; transmission Reichenau -> Murbach (Alsace) -> 17th-c. Netherlands (Boxhorn -> Vossius -> Junius). Feb. 2022 Oxford workshop gathered linguists and manuscript specialists (Glaser, Krotz, Blom, Gittos et al.); Sievers' old edition is on archive.org.
9. **Eadwine Psalter (ASMMF 16 + EM1060)** - Trinity R.17.1, Christ Church Canterbury, script-dated 1150s (Webber; EM1060 adds "perhaps c. 1160"): psalterium triplex with Gallicanum (Glossa Ordinaria marginal+interlinear), Romanum (continuous OE interlinear gloss), Hebraicum (continuous Anglo-Norman interlinear gloss); >=13 scribes; vernacular gloss sources unresolved, multiple erasures/overwritings show active revision; sister manuscript Paris BN lat. 8846 carries the same AN gloss tradition (Markey 1989 Ghent PhD critical edition).
10. **Lendinara 2014** - DOI resolves to ABaG 73/1, pp. 301-327 (Brill). Subject per IRIS UNIPA snippet: Groningen UB 404, oldest surviving medieval Frisian fragmentary codex, with verses of Psalms XVII, XXVII(?), XVIII(?) accompanied by vernacular glosses; title signals a pragmatics-and-competence approach. Full argument not accessed (paywalled; repository returned 403).
11. **Ormulum (Junius 1)** - Autograph homiliary, (? )Bourne Lincolnshire, 1170s x 1180s; plan of at least 242 lection+homily units, ca. 32 survive; each unit = metrical English paraphrase/adaptation of the Latin Gospel pericope in septenary lines followed by exposition; Hand C entered the Latin pericope headings with full Latin abbreviation practice; Middle English and Latin mixed throughout ("The table of contents enumerates the Latin pericopes"). New Stockholm edition freshly transcribed from the autograph.
12. **Notker method context** - St Gall exhibition coverage (SPG Mitteilungen 67, App 2022): Notker translates Latin passages into OHG and comments; a newly presented bifolium shows Latin text followed by OHG for De quatuor quaestionibus compoti; Vienna Codex 2681 (Wessobrunn c. 1100) transmits a second branch of the Notker Psalter (Firchow edition 2009).

## Cross-cutting observations

- Three layout types recur across the corpus: (a) true between-the-lines interlinear version (Benedictine Rule Cod. 916; Murbach Hymns), (b) column/column or facing-text bilingual (Paris Isidor; Eadwine Gallicanum parallel columns), (c) Latin text retained as heading/lemma with running vernacular translation+commentary (Notker Psalter; Ormulum pericopes).
- Current scholarship (Morawetz 2023; Lendinara 2014 title) converges on treating close word-for-word following of Latin not as failure of translation skill but as pragmatically motivated pedagogical design.
- The Junius 25 booklet collection physically links the Murbach Hymns with the Isidor-group glossaries (Jc edited by Krotz 2002), making one codex a hub for Loop 02 topics.
