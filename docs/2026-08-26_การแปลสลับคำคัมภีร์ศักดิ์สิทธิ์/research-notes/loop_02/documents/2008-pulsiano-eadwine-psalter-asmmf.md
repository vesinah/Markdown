## Excerpts / observed facts

- "A lavishly illustrated, large-format triple Psalter containing the Gallicanum, Romanum, and Hebraicum versions of the Psalms, with marginal and interlinear glosses to the Gallicanum from the 'Glossa Ordinaria' ('parva glosatura'), a continuous interlinear OE gloss to the Romanum, and a continuous interlinear Anglo-Norman (French) gloss to the Hebraicum."
- "Probably written at Christ Church, Canterbury; dated by the script to the 1150s (T. Webber in Gibson et al. 1992: 24)."
- Named after the large illustration of the scribe Eadwine; associated dispersed prefatory picture leaves: New York Pierpont Morgan M. 521 and M. 724, London BL Add. 37472(1), London V&A MS 661 - likely removed before the manuscript came to Trinity College in the early 17th century.
- History includes William L'Isle (d. 1637), one-time fellow of King's College Cambridge; his edition preserved in Oxford Bodl. Laud Misc. 201 (Pulsiano 2000); full history discussion referenced to T. A. Heslop, D. McKitterick, and M. Gibson in Gibson et al. 1992: 193-213.

## Relevance

Authoritative capsule description confirming the trilingual architecture: each Latin psalter version paired with a different vernacular or scholarly apparatus (Latin Glossa Ordinaria / OE interlinear / Anglo-Norman interlinear), the English counterpart to the continental bilingual sacred texts in this loop.
