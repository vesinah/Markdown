## Excerpts / observed facts

- Date: "s. xiimed; perhaps c. 1160"; medieval provenance Canterbury, Christ Church; produced there and recorded in Prior Eastry's early-14th-century inventory; given to Trinity by Thomas Nevile, Dean of Canterbury, early 17th century.
- "The Eadwine Psalter is a trilingual, glossed psalterium triplex ... contains a calendar, triple Metrical Psalms 90:15-95:2, canticles, two continuous commentaries, two prognostications, a marginal image of Halley's Comet (recorded in 1147), a diagrammatic representation of Christ Church's waterworks, and a full page visual memorialisation of Eadwine."
- Arrangement: "The psalms (except Ps. 151) are in Hebrew with French glosses, the Roman version with Old English glosses, and the Gallican version in parallel columns."
- "At least 13 scribes appear to have been employed"; Webber's hand analysis: "the Old English gloss on Psalms 26-77, for example, is ascribed to one glossator - OE1 (Webber 19)."
- Sources: "the Latin gloss and commentary is derived from the Glossa Ordinaria, which was 'in the main stream of contemporary scholarship' (Gibson 1992, p. 109), but the question of sources for the Old English and Anglo-Norman glosses is still unresolved; both sets have multiple erasures and overwritings, illustrating that these are careful, dynamic and functional translations."
- Anglo-Norman gloss exemplar hypothesis: "the possible exemplar used for the Anglo-Norman gloss was a corrupt manuscript which was then corrected, further expanding the corruptness (Markey 1989, p. 150)" - one of two unproven hypotheses.
- Key literature listed: Gibson/Heslop/Pfaff eds., The Eadwine Psalter: Text, Image, and Monastic Culture in Twelfth-Century Canterbury (MHRA 14, 1992); Markey/Verfaillie-Markey PhD Ghent 1989 critical edition of the Hebraicum and its Anglo-Norman interlinear tradition including Paris BN lat. 8846; Harsley EETS OS 92 (1889); Pulsiano, Old English Glossed Psalters: Psalms 1-50 (Toronto 2001).
- QUB Omeka record: ca. 1150, parchment, 46 x 33 cm; "an illustrated copy of the Psalms in three Latin versions with translations into Old English and Anglo-Norman French."

## Relevance

The fullest open description of the Eadwine Psalter's trilingual interlinear system, scribal organization, and unresolved vernacular-gloss source questions; companion manuscript Paris BN lat. 8846 extends the Anglo-Norman gloss tradition.
