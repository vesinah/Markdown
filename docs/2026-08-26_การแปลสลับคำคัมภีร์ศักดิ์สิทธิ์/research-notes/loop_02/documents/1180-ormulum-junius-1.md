# Orm (Orrm) - The Ormulum, Bodleian MS Junius 1

- **Author:** Orrm (Orm/Ormin), Augustinian canon
- **Title:** The Ormulum (Orrmulum)
- **Year:** composed s. xii2; Bodleian catalogue dates the manuscript "(?)Bourne, Lincolnshire, 1170s x 1180s"
- **Type:** Autograph homiliary manuscript: verse Gospel paraphrases + expositions
- **URL:** https://medieval.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/catalog/manuscript_6317 ; https://em1060.stanford.edu/manuscript/oxford-bodleian-library-junius-1-5123 ; https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/objects/90a06f70-880a-4b5b-bd30-798710afff11 ; new edition project: https://su.diva-portal.nl/smash/record.jsf?pid=diva2:1803898 (Stockholm University record: "Ormulum: edited from Oxford, Bodleian library, MS Junius 1 ...")
- **Access date:** 2026-08-25
- **Status:** SEARCH-VERIFIED (Bodleian Medieval Manuscripts catalogue page for Junius 1 retrieved in extensive excerpts via search; EM1060 Stanford entry likewise; digital.bodleian object page snippet seen)

## Excerpts / observed facts

- Bodleian catalogue summary: "The surviving portions of the first volume of The Orrmulum. It contains biblical narratives and homilies. An incomplete list of pericopes, between Orrm's Dedication and Preface, shows that at least 242 biblical narratives, each followed by a homily, were planned. The manuscript is Orrm's autograph, and contains numerous insertions, deletions and revisions in his hand."
- Languages: "Middle English and Latin. The table of contents enumerates the Latin pericopes for the individual items."
- Structure observed in contents listing: each item = numbered 'Fit' opening with a metrical paraphrase of a Latin Gospel lection (e.g., Fit 1 double homily on Lk 1:5 and Lk 1:18, incipit "An preost wass onn Herodess da3h3"), followed by exposition; bridge passages between fits are separately demarcated by ordinatio.
- Hand C "added the Gospel pericopes at the beginning of individual homilies, using a small academic hand" and "uses the full compendium of Latin abbreviations in the pericopes."
- Metre: Bodleian Archives snippet describes "The Ormulum, in 20,000 lines of septenarian Middle English verse"; the homilies adapt the Latin iambic septenarius rhythm in unrhymed long lines.
- Only ca. 32 homilies survive of 242 planned; Lambeth Palace Library MS 783 is an early-modern transcript preserving text from leaves since lost (per em1060/Bodleian-related records seen this session).
- Stockholm University (Diva) record: new edition freshly transcribed from Junius 1 (Nils-Lennart Johannesson edition project); earlier standard editions Holt-White 1878 and Matthes.

## Relevance

English counterpart to continental interlinear sacred texts: Latin lections are retained as headings (added in Latin by Hand C) while the vernacular carries both a metrical source-adaptation of the pericope and its exposition - a bilingual sacred-text strategy operating at the level of layout and liturgy rather than between-the-lines glossing.
