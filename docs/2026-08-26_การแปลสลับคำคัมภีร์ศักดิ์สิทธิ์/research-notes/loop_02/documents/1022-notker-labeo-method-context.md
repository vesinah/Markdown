# Notker Labeo's translation method - Latin preserved with OHG renderings (St Gall context)

- **Subject:** Notker Labeo/Teutonicus (c. 950-1022), St Gall schoolmaster: Boethius Consolatio, Psalter, Psychomachia tradition context

## Excerpts / observed facts

- Cambridge frontmatter (Reading in Medieval St. Gall): "Learning to read in medieval Germany meant learning to read and understand Latin as well as the pupils' own language. The teaching methods used in the medieval Abbey of St. Gall survive in the translations and commentaries of the monk, scholar and teacher Notker Labeo (ca. 950-1022)."
- SPG Mitteilungen Nr. 67 (App 2022, on the Stiftsbibliothek exhibition): Notker "hilft diese Sprachhuerde [des Lateinischen] abzubauen, indem er lateinische Passagen ins Althochdeutsche uebersetzt und kommentiert"; called "der bedeutendste Uebersetzer ins Althochdeutsche vor Martin Luther"; first attested use of 'diutisk' as a language name attributed to him; his works written "fuer den Unterricht".
- Same source reports a St Gall discovery presented with the exhibition: a parchment bifolium where "dem lateinischen Text ein althochdeutscher folgt" for Notker's astronomical treatise De quatuor quaestionibus compoti - previously known only in Latin, now attested in OHG translation.
- Vienna page (Krotz) review note confirms existence of "Der Codex Vindobonensis 2681 aus dem bayerischen Kloster Wessobrunn um 1100. Diplomatische Textausgabe der Wiener Notker Psalmen, Cantica Wessobrunner Predigten und katechetischen Denkmaeler ... hg. von Evelyn Scherabon Firchow ..., Hildesheim 2009" - a second transmission branch of the Notker Psalter.
- Note on Psychomachia: no dedicated source on an OHG Notker-Psychomachia was retrieved this session; the topic remains UNVERIFIED beyond its appearance in the loop assignment. (Prudentius Psychomachia manuscripts glossed in Latin/Italian were encountered incidentally via Yale Beinecke catalog record.)

## Relevance

Frames Notker not as interlinear glossator but as translator-commentator who keeps the Latin visible (bilingual double texts such as De syllogismis; Latin text plus OHG rendering and commentary), bridging Carolingian interlinear practice and 12th-century mixed-text psalters like Einsiedeln-written Cod. Sang. 21.
