Provenance Route section:
"9th c. CE — The oldest dated Nepalese manuscript: a palm-leaf copy of the Skanda Purāṇa, dated to 810 CE. Nepal's Kathmandu Valley emerges as a uniquely favourable environment for manuscript preservation: the relatively dry mountain climate, combined with continuous institutional custodianship in monasteries, temples, and private collections, allows palm-leaf and paper manuscripts to survive for a millennium or more."

"When Islamic invasions destroy the great monastic universities of northern India — Nalanda (c. 1200), Vikramashila (c. 1193) — many Sanskrit and Buddhist texts survive only in Nepalese copies. [...] The oldest surviving manuscript of a Vedic text and the oldest surviving Pali manuscript are both found in Nepal."

"1970–2001 — Over thirty-one years, the NGMPP microfilms approximately 180,000 manuscripts. [...] Writing supports range from palm leaf to handmade Nepalese paper, birch bark, and nīlapatra (black paper with gold or silver ink). Book formats include pothi (loose-leaf stacks), accordion books, and Western codex forms. All microfilms are stored at the Nepal National Archives, with copies at the State Library in Berlin."

"1989 — Fire at the Tengboche Monastery [...] The manuscripts had been photographed by the NGMPP just months before the fire — 185 texts, 15,766 folios preserved on microfilm."

"An estimated 80–85% of manuscripts still extant in Nepal have been captured on microfilm." (under 2002)

"2013–present — HMML (Hill Museum & Manuscript Library) partners with the Hamburg Centre for the Study of Manuscript Cultures (CSMC) to digitise manuscripts at the Āśā Archives in Kathmandu. The collection — over 7,000 manuscripts dating from the 15th to 19th centuries — includes pothi-style palm-leaf and paper manuscripts, accordion books, ritual handbooks, and codices in Sanskrit, Newari, and Nepali."

Material Evidence section: "Palm-leaf manuscripts use the pothi format: rectangular leaves inscribed with a stylus, strung through central holes, stored between carved wooden boards. Handmade Nepalese paper (lokta paper) supports both pothi-format and accordion books. Nīlapatra (blue-black paper) manuscripts feature text in gold or silver ink, often in the elaborate Rañjanā script."
