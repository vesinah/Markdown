## A. RESOLVED: MS.SL.68 treatment sequence (Collège de France) - A18 gap D1 CLOSED

**A1. Full offprint retrieved.**
Source S01 (HAL halshs-04447688, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Publication verified: Care and Conservation of Manuscripts 17, Proc. 17th Int. Seminar, Copenhagen, 11-13 Apr 2018, ed. M.J. Driscoll, Museum Tusculanum Press 2021, pp. 511-534; peer-reviewed ("The articles in these Proceedings have all been subjected to peer-review").

Collection facts: "This corpus comprises 81 items from various palm-leaf manuscript traditions, dating from the eleventh century to the twentieth... It represents at least seven languages and twelve alphabets." Five groups: 8 Sanskrit nāgarī mss collected by Sylvain Lévi in Nepal 1897-1922 (incl. MS.SL.68); 10 south Indian; 26 Pāli collected by Louis Finot; 33 SE Asian; 4 Javanese.

MS.SL.68 condition and treatment (verbatim):
- Object: "comprises 208 palm leaves written on both recto and verso and gathered between two wooden boards painted on their inner sides. The whole item is about 55.5 cm long × 6.7 cm wide and 7 cm thick."
- Damage classes: "strong soiling, dust, concretions, mud blots, glued residues that had induced chemical alterations such as coloured halos and also a general browning"; transfers of material between stuck leaves "classified into three degrees of severity: transfer of ink only; transfer of ink and of the superficial layer; transfer of the whole palm leaf width"; plus insect excretion deposits and rodent activity.
- Tests: "A bathophenetroline test did not reveal the presence of free iron(II) ions in the ink. A solubility test revealed that the ink was soluble in water and in a mix of water and ethanol, but not soluble in ethanol or acetone... hypothesis of a vegetal binder, probably gum arabic." Water caused planar distortion; acetone none. Paint-layer best cleaning agent: saliva.
- Leaf treatment: dry cleaning (scalpel scratching of excretions, brushing); dust with "barely damped hydrophilic cotton"; fold flattening by humidification under light weights; reinforcement with "chamois Japanese paper (100 % kozo, 3.7 g/m2...) pre-coated with Klucel G... reactivated with water"; separation of stuck leaves done DRY to protect the 3D structure; delaminations stabilised with wheat starch glue (Zin Shofu) +/- Klucel-G-precoated kozo linings; "Planar distortion and lacunae were not treated because they were deemed acceptable for readability and digitisation" (stabilisation, not aesthetic).
- Boards (Emilie Masse): concretion scratching + brushing + suction through tulle; saliva cleaning; rinse 1:1 water:ethanol; flakes/lifts consolidated with "sturgeon glue (Kremer) prepared at 1 % in water".
- Housing: NOT rejoined - ribbed cardboard box (Klug) with three cradles (leaves wrapped in Tyvek; two separated board cradles), wedged with Plastazote, paint protected with tissue paper.
- Environment: new storage targets "temperature: 18/19 °C, relative humidity: 50 % [± 5 %]"; interim guidance "constant temperature of around 19/20 °C with a relative humidity of 50 %".
- Programme outcome: "Since this first restoration work in 2013, ten other palm-leaf manuscripts... have been treated"; digitised on Salamandre (salamandre.college-de-france.fr).
- Traditional-care critique: lemongrass oil "forms a thick layer on the surface of the leaf which may become hard and increase in weight"; solidification-temperature mismatch in temperate climates; oiling increases dust aggregation; original wrapping textiles should be stored separately.

## B. RESOLVED: Heritage Science 12:199 full text (A18 gap B1 CLOSED)
Source S02. Verified mechanism chain: "*Aspergillus niger* can secrete cellulase, lipase, and acidic substances after colonizing on the carriers of PLMs. These substances sequentially damage the carrier's epidermal cells, mesophyll cells, and leaf fibers, leading to the separation of different tissue structures." Sequence at molecular level: "the lipids on the surface of the leaves were degraded initially, and sequential depolymerization of hemicellulose, amorphous cellulose, and crystalline cellulose occurred."
Quantitative: O/C ratio fell 0.28 -> 0.17; hemicellulose content 22.5% -> 11.2%; extractives 10.6 -> 8.5 then 14.9%; cellulose 37.5 -> 39.1 then 33.6%; relative crystallinity 39.8% -> 56.0% (14 d) -> 38.8% (28 d); cell-wall hardness 0.30 -> 0.34 -> 0.29 GPa (rise-then-fall, tracking crystallinity); conidia ~3.2 µm cannot penetrate compact epidermis but secretions penetrate inward; fibre volume fraction ~23%, wall thickness ~4 µm.
Method: strains isolated from ancient PLMs, Xishuangbanna (ambient RH 82-85%; storage room held 21.9 °C / 42.0% RH); inoculation per GB/T 18261-2013 at 28±2 °C, 85±5% RH, 28 days, sampling every 7 days; first application of nanoindentation to PLM research (TI-950, 80 µN, Oliver-Pharr).
Assessment use: hardness/E-modulus rise-fall trend "provided a new reference for assessing the degree of deterioration of PLMs."

## C. RESOLVED: MDPI Polymers 17(1):83 full text (A18 corroboration upgraded)
Source S03. Same Beijing group (USTB Key Lab of Archaeomaterials / Chinese Academy of Cultural Heritage). Talipot samples from Yunnan, aged 200 d at 25 °C, RH 10/30/50/70/90%.
Key results: cracking at 10/30% RH (transverse cracks up to ~20 µm at 10%); mould at 70/90% (hyphae penetrating surface); none at 50%. After 200 d: cellulose degradation 19.82% (10% RH) vs 54.40% (90% RH); hemicellulose 34.78% vs 64.28%; crystallinity drop 8.01% vs 13.11%. At 50% RH degradation limited to 4.08% (cellulose) and 13.55% (hemicellulose), crystallinity -6.61%. Hemicellulose degrades faster than cellulose in all conditions. Cellulose I retained; extra XRD peaks at 26°/29° attributed to Ca/Si introduced during preparation. Conclusion: "50% RH is an optimal humidity condition for the preservation of Palm Leaf Manuscripts."

## D. NEW: npj HS s40494-025-01943-0 scientific restoration protocol (A18 Phase B item 6 CLOSED)
Source S04. Specimen: Dai-language engraved PLM "Bala Shanghaya", Jātaka text in Old Dai (Tai Lü) script, ~49.8 cm x 4.3-4.9 cm, 10 leaves (BY1-BY10), Xishuangbanna.
Verified protocol elements:
- Survey damage types: stains (incl. adhesive-tape residue), missing fragments, fraying fibers, tears, binding-hole wear; upper leaves worst.
- Non-invasive metrics per leaf: thickness 0.41-0.62 mm (ISO 534:2011); pH 5.7-6.3 = mild acidification, uniform front/back -> deacidification deliberately skipped ("potential dimensional changes that deacidification treatment might cause"); CIELAB ΔE* > 5 on backs of BY8-BY10 flagged for cleaning.
- Microbiology: 10 bacterial + 2 fungal isolates; Bacillus 58.33%, Paenibacillus 16.67%, plus Priestia, Phanerochaetella, Aspergillus (cellulose/lignin degraders noted).
- Principles stated: "restoration to original condition," "minimal intervention," "reversibility," "safety".
- Cleaning: dry (soft brushes, erasers) then wet - wiping with warm water to remove surface contamination, temperature controlled against warping.
- Filling: artificially aged palm-leaf patches; best match = patches dry-heated at 120 °C for 10 d (color match + moderate tensile strength + larger elongation at break; GB/T 464-2008 aging).
- Adhesive comparison: 20% wheat starch deformed leaves and cured >24 h (rejected); 5% methylcellulose cured 4-6 h, endotherm ~40 °C; 30% isinglass optimal - cured 2-4 h, best thermal stability (DSC 0-150 °C), highest tensile-strength stability under 80 °C/65% RH aging (GB/T 22894-2008); composite repair paste = 200-mesh palm-leaf powder + 20-30% w/v gelatin or isinglass at 1:4~1:4.5 mass ratio, inlaid at seams.
- Binding retained traditional form; acid-free storage boxes; stable T/RH environment.

## G. Sri Lanka NLDSB conservation network (A18 gap D4 expanded)
Source S05 (natlib.lk): services include island-wide public-library conservation centres "project was launched in 1992. Currently 126 conservation centres have been established"; training on ola-leaf conservation for all library staff; "Production of traditional herbal oil for the preservation of palm leaf manuscripts. This oil is known as 'PANHINDA'."; contact Document Conservator, NLDSB, Colombo 07.
Source S06 (IFLA PAC Sri Lanka): centre inaugurated 5 Aug 2015 at NLDSB; FAQ topics "Traditional paper and manuscript preservation (Palm Leaf Manuscripts)" and "Treating Fungal and Mould Infestation"; annual Goethe-Institut workshops; UNESCO MoW focal point; "'Panhida Herbal Oil'... produced through a machine produced by the NLDSB"; "131 library conservation centres in main public libraries throughout the island". Discrepancy note: natlib says 126 centres, IFLA page says 131 (both quoted verbatim; reconciliation needed).
Source S07 (IFLA FAQ, palm-leaf specific): tradition began 3rd c. BCE, faded 18th c. ACE after Dutch printing press; species Corypha umbraculifera (Ola) and Borassus [sic] "unbraculifera"; preparation boil with unripe papaya pulp + pineapple leaves, smoke-seasoning in kitchen stove hood, polished on areca-palm cylinder; stylus = Panhinda; typical problems: stains, fungal/mould growth, insect damage, brittleness/cracking, stuck-together leaves; NLSL treatment oil "made through the distillation of Vateria copallifera resins... mixed with charcoal powder and applied to the palm leaf surface by cotton cloth"; functions: contrast, flexibility, fungicide, anti-bacterial, deleterious to insect larvae; "twice a year has been seen to minimise the deterioration".
