## A. Gāndhārī Prakrit — ภาษาของต้นฉบับพุทธที่เก่าที่สุด

### F1. Gāndhārī + Kharoṣṭhī: birch bark → palm-leaf pothi (แกนคำตอบ "ภาษาใดอยู่บนวัสดุใด")
- "The earliest Buddhist manuscripts were written in the Kharoṣṭhī script and Gāndhārī language, initially on birchbark scrolls and later on palm-leaf pothi-format manuscripts (i.e., bound or wrapped palm-leaf folios)." [VERIFIED-PAGE — Baums 2022 PDF, p.1]
- "In the 3rd and 4th centuries CE, a Buddhist community at Bamiyan in Afghanistan produced Sanskrit manuscripts in the pan-Indian Brāhmī script in parallel with Gāndhārī manuscripts, using palm leaves and the pothi format or a bound or wrapped folio for both. By the 5th century CE, the pothi, Sanskrit, and Brāhmī had completely taken over from the Gāndhārī manuscript tradition." [VERIFIED-PAGE — Baums 2022, History of Research]
- "Altogether, approximately 150 birchbark scrolls and about the same number of small palm-leaf fragments in the Gāndhārī language are now known." [VERIFIED-PAGE — Baums 2022]
- Non-Buddhist counterexample: "a non-Buddhist treatise on statecraft written in Kharoṣṭhī script and Sanskrit language" → อักษรกับภาษาไม่จำเป็นต้องคู่กัน [VERIFIED-PAGE — Baums 2022, Miscellaneous Texts]
- การโต้เถียงทางวิชาการ: "Gāndhārī hypothesis" (Waldschmidt, Brough) — คำแปลจีนยุคแรกแปลจากต้นฉบับ Gāndhārī ไม่ใช่ Sanskrit; Baums/Salomon: "the existence of such a body of Gāndhārī literature is now a matter of fact rather than of hypothesis" [VERIFIED-PAGE — UW EBMP page]
- งาน Fussman "Gāndhārī écrite, gāndhārī parlée" (1989) = กรอบโต้เถียง Gāndhārī ลายลักษณ์ vs ปากเปล่า; Baums 2022 อ้างไว้ใน Review of Literature [VERIFIED-PAGE — Baums 2022]

### F2. BL Kharoṣṭhī fragments — ข้อมูลภาษา/สำนัก
- "twenty-nine fragments of manuscripts written on birch bark scrolls in the Gāndhārī (a dialect of Prakrit) language and in the Kharoṣṭhī script... dated from about the first century A.D., which would make them the oldest surviving substantial collection of Buddhist manuscripts, as well as of any kind of Indian manuscripts"; Gāndhārī/Kharoṣṭhī "current from about the third century B.C. to the fourth century A.D."; jar inscription dedicated to teachers of the Dharmaguptaka school; ข้อความเทียบกับ Pali, Sanskrit, Chinese versions [VERIFIED-PAGE — UW asian.washington.edu]

## B. Pali — ภาษาพิธีของเถรวาทบนใบลาน

### F3. ล้านนา/ล้านช้าง: ใบลานส่วนใหญ่สองภาษา บาลี+ vernacular; เก่าสุด = บาลีล้วน
- DLNTM (Thai page): "คัมภีร์ใบลานส่วนใหญ่เป็นแบบสองภาษาคือ ภาษาบาลีกับภาษาล้านนา และยังพบว่ามีภาษาถิ่นอื่น ๆ ด้วย (ส่วนใหญ่เป็นภาษาบาลีกับภาษาถิ่น) เช่น ภาษาไทยใหญ่ ไทยลื้อ ไทยเขิน ลาว พม่า และภาษาไทย คัมภีร์ใบลานอีกจำนวนหนึ่งรวมถึงคัมภีร์ใบลานที่เก่าแก่ที่สุดเป็นภาษาบาลีล้วน" + "ภาษาบาลีที่ใช้มักจะมีความแตกต่างจากภาษาดั้งเดิม เช่นที่มีในไวยากรณ์มูลกัจจายนะ" [VERIFIED-PAGE — CrossAsia DLNTM TH]

## D. Nissaya / bitexts — การแปลข้ามบรรทัด (interlinear practice)

### F8. Trent Walker JAOS 140.3 (2020) — แหล่งหลักของลูปนี้
- รายชื่อภาษา: "render Pali into local vernaculars, including Burmese, Khmer, Khün, Lanna, Lao, Lü, Mon, and Siamese"
- นิยาม bitext: "texts that are presented bilingually, with portions in one language mixed together with portions in another, typically in an interphrasal or interlinear arrangement"
- ชื่อเรียกตามท้องถิ่น: "nissaya (Burmese), naṃ/trā-ai (Mon), nisrai/nissăy (Lanna, Lao, Siamese), nāmaśăbd/săpº (Lanna, Lao, Siamese), cuṇṇiyapad (Siamese), ṕlè yak śăbd (Siamese), and prè lot prayog (Khmer)"
- Timeline: earliest mainland example = Pali-Mon inscription late 11th–early 12th c.; Pali-Burmese late 13th c.; Sanskrit-Burmese listed in 1442 inscription; earliest complete Pali-Siamese bitext 1482 Ayutthaya; earliest securely dated Pali-Lanna manuscript bitext 1552; Sanskrit-Lanna 1578; oldest surviving Pali-Lao bitext = Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha CS 948 = 1586/7 CE (PLMP 08040102007_04)
- Material link: "Most Pali manuscripts from the seventeenth through early twentieth centuries in Siam and Cambodia are inscribed on palm leaves"; interlinear annotations "khaam hvăt / t́vă kṣien" appear on certain Pali palm-leaf manuscripts of core curriculum works (Kaccāyanavyākaraṇa, Dhammapadaṭṭhakathā, Maṅgalatthadīpanī, Sāratthasaṅgaha, Samantapāsādikā, Visuddhimagga, Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha); inscribed (carved) annotations most common on palm-leaf copies of Kaccāyanavyākaraṇa
- โครง 3 ขั้นของ bitext composition: Selection (citation/invention), Analysis (parsing, amplification, rearrangement, annotation, gloss), Presentation (philological/exegetical/homiletic/poetic)
- ข้อจำกัดเฉพาะภาษา: "Pali-Sinhala bitexts omit the technique of grammatical annotation (2d), since what Burmese, Mon, Khmer, and various Tai languages express through particles may be signaled morphologically in Sinhala."
- First-millennium bilingual inscriptions pair Indic (Sanskrit or Pali) with Cham/Khmer/Mon/Pyu; interphrasal Sanskrit-Pyu inscription tentatively 6th c.
- Case study: Nissaya ṭīkā abhidhammatthasańgaha dated 1585 CE (cūḷasakarāja 947), now at Văt Hlai2 Hin Hlvaṅ (Wat Lai Hin Luang), Lampang [all VERIFIED-PAGE — JAOS PDF archived]

### F9. ข้อโต้เถียง McDaniel vs Walker (scholarly debate)
- Walker 2020: "Justin McDaniel, based on his readings of Pali-Lao and Pali-Lanna bitexts, claims that such bilingual compositions follow no strict conventions but rather reflect the idiosyncratic approaches of individual teachers. By contrast, my research... demonstrates not only that strict conventions were followed, but that many of these conventions were shared across mainland Southeast Asia from the eleventh century onward." [VERIFIED-PAGE]
- Pruitt 1994 Étude linguistique de nissaya birmans (EFEO); Tin Lwin 1961 SOAS PhD; Okell 1965 "Nissaya Burmese" Lingua 15:186–227 cited as foundation studies [VERIFIED-PAGE as cited; Okell DOI verified via Crossref]

## F. Khom/Khmer + misc collections

### F11. Khom script, Central Thailand
- DLLM: "The Khom form of this script was used for writing Buddhist texts and other treatises in Central Thailand until the early 20th century, when it was gradually replaced by Thai script. The DLLM collection contains a number of manuscripts in Pali, Lao or Thai languages, written in Khom script, from Southern Laos." [VERIFIED-PAGE]
- Rylands: "a Khmer Pali manuscript of extracts from the Buddhist Tripitaka, comprising seven large bundles of palm leaves with carefully incised letters" [SNIPPET — library.manchester.ac.uk]
- Academia bitexts paper snippet: "Pali-Khmer and Pali-Tai bitexts will often meticulously mark Pa[li] nouns in the nominative... and accusative... cases with specific technical particles. The formal or literary registers of all languages in Southeast Asian Theravada cultures bear similar imprints of the Pali-vernacular bitextual style." [SNIPPET]
