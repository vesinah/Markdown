- Article footer: "Received 4 December 2017; revised accepted 12 October 2019"
- Affiliations: National Museum Institute, Dept of Conservation, New Delhi; Institute of Conservation, University of Applied Arts Vienna; Dept of Culture, Government of Kerala

### F2. Material findings (VERIFIED-PAGE, full text)
- Sample set: FOUR painted palm-leaf manuscripts from an unknown artist in Odisha, owner-attributed late 18th–early 19th century CE; methods: digital microscope (Dino-Lite 200x), SEM-EDX (FEI QUANTA FEG 250 / EDAX Apollo-X), PLM; 24 measuring points
- Palette conclusions: blue = indigo (Indigofera tinctoria, C16H10N2O2); white = China clay (Al2O3·nSiO2·nH2O); yellow = ORPIMENT As2S3 ("contrary to earlier belief that yellow pigment was sourced from turmeric powder"); red = cinnabar/vermilion HgS (+ iron-oxide red, probably some red dye); black = carbon/lamp black; GREEN = no green pigment — mixture of indigo + orpiment + China clay ("as against the earlier belief that it was extracted from bean leaves")
- Overall: "in Indian palm leaf illustrations, mostly mineral colours have been used"
- Conservation notes: orpiment photosensitive; vermilion can blacken to elemental mercury (citing Spring & Grout 2004); bundles wrapped in red cloth against insects; leaves brittle from stains/discoloration/insect activity
- **Loop caveat:** this is Odisha 18th–19th-c. material — NOT a Pala-period study. It verifies the DOI and gives the Indian palm-leaf pigment baseline only.

## B. Met Museum Mahavihara Master group (A08 F14 upgraded to full-page evidence)

### F3. Met 74906 — attribution narrative (VERIFIED-PAGE)
- Object: Avalokitesvara expounding Dharma folio, Ashtasahasrika Prajnaparamita; early 12th c.; Pala period; "India, West Bengal or Bangladesh"; opaque watercolor on palm leaf; page 7 x 41.8 cm; miniature image area ~6.4 x 4.9 cm; Purchase Lila Acheson Wallace Gift 2001; acc. 2001.445f
- Artist bio: "Active in the early 12th century, in Bengal"; "known from one extant palm-leaf manuscript, now shared between New York and Lhasa"; deluxe Ashtasahasrika edition
- **Scriptorium hypothesis:** "This pious artistic venture to fulfill a royal commission was probably undertaken in the scriptorium of one of the great monasteries (mahaviharas) of eastern India at the height of Buddhist activity there."
- Donor colophon leaf preserved IN LHASA: edition identified as "the pious gift of the queen Vihunadevi" — queen otherwise unknown, "no means of constructing a provenance or reign date"
- Format: miniatures averaging 2½ by 3 inches (6.4 x 7.6 cm), "miniaturized compositions originated for large-scale mural painting programs into a book format"

## C. Cambridge Add.1464 / Add.1643 miniature cycles (loop's own raw archives)

### F5. CUDL MS-ADD-01464 (Pala tradition, Bengal/Bihar sphere)
- "This palm-leaf manuscript belongs to the Pāla tradition of illuminated manuscripts of the Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā in the ornamental Pāla script."
- Cycle: "originally had six illuminated folios, each with three miniatures" (=18); original first folio missing, replaced by later palm leaf without miniatures in different script
- Date controversial: colophon = 5th regnal year of a Mahīpāla → "equivalent to c. 985 or after ca 1070, and since Mahīpāla II most probably reigned only for three years, the earliest date is more probable"; donor = woman named Lāḍokā; two painted wooden covers bearing traces of worship materials [VERIFIED-PAGE]

### F6. CUDL MS-ADD-01643 (NEPALI counterpart, for contrast)
- Written by scribe Sujātabhadra, 1015 CE, joint reign Bhojadeva/Rudradeva/Lakṣmīkāmadeva, Hlāṃ vihāra; 85 miniatures at chapter ends (+ beginning; probably originally 88)
- Losty 1992:31 quote on CUDL page: subjects "'are in general different from their Indian Pāla counterparts... The majority of places designated are in eastern India, but there are some from far off places—China, Java, Ceylon, Gujarat, southern India'"; illuminator notes by first scribe; 1139 Karuṇavajra rescue colophon [VERIFIED-PAGE]
- Kathmandu Post 2019 (researcher Sanyukta Shrestha): Daniel Wright took ~450 documents after British Residency service 1866–76; CUL purchased the ms 1876; 85 illustrations across 42 of 222 folios (27 Loknath, ~21 Buddha, 11 Green Tara; 7 deities inside front cover); red outlines filled blue/grey/yellow/green/white/red; vajra painted at each string hole; captions Sanskrit in Nepal script [VERIFIED-PAGE — journalism/researcher opinion, treat as reported]

## D. Monastery-level production attributions (A08 F16 gap — RESOLVED at two anchors)

### F7. NALANDA — Asia Society 1987.1 (Rubin PHA essay, Pakhoutova & Helman-Ważny 2023)
- Object: five leaves, Ashtasahasrika Prajnaparamita; "Nalanda Monastery, Bihar, India; 1073, 1151; ink and opaque watercolor on palm leaf; each approx. 2 7/8 × 22 3/8 in. (7.3 × 56.8 cm)"; Mr. and Mrs. John D. Rockefeller 3rd Acquisitions Fund
- **Colophon evidence:** "The manuscript's Sanskrit and Tibetan colophons state that it was produced at Nalanda Monastery in 1073 CE and restored and rededicated in 1151 CE."
- Ownership chain: Kashmiri scholar Shakyashribhadra (1127/1145–1225; in Tibet 1204–1214) → Buton Rinchen Drub (1290–1364), founder of Zhalu; last inscription dedicates use for Kunga Gelek Wangchuk
- Miniature-cycle convention: "Manuscripts usually feature twelve figural images, as in this example, or eighteen [Losty 1982, 20]"; folio typically 3 panels (central bodhisattva/deity + two Buddha-life scenes); Eight Great Events cycle; illuminated folios placed beginning/middle/end
- Materials section: talipot used until 15th c. northern/western India and until 18th c. in BIHAR and Sri Lanka; palmyra used in eastern India 16th–19th c.; Ranjana script; reed pen; soot+oil/glue ink; "A few rare, dated examples are in the collections of the National Archives and the Kaiser Library in Kathmandu, Nepal, the University of Cambridge, and the British Museum, London."
- Scholarly anchors cited: Huntington & Huntington, *Leaves from the Bodhi Tree* (1990), 186–89 [fn12]; Jinah Kim, *Receptacle of the Sacred* (2013), 226–27 [fn14: high number of Nepalese/Tibetan patrons commissioned such books in India in first half 11th c.]; Steinkellner 2004; Bhattarai 2020 [VERIFIED-PAGE]

## F. Decline of manuscript culture under print/paper transition

### F11. What fetched sources actually support
- Guy & Britschgi, *Wonder of the Age* (Met/Yale 2011) publication page: book sections "examine manuscript painting as it evolved from palm-leaf to paper, the emergence of traditional painting as an independent art form, and its demise with the coming of photography"; also "refute the long-held view of anonymous authorship" via "connoisseurship and inscriptional evidence" — verifiable scholarly discussion exists at this level; body not fetchable (print book) [VERIFIED-PAGE description]
- Rubin Nalanda essay: talipot end-dates by region ("until the fifteenth century in northern and western India and until the eighteenth century in Bihar and Sri Lanka. The palmyra type was used in eastern India from the sixteenth to the nineteenth century") — material-availability side of decline [VERIFIED-PAGE]
- Nepal timeline (from loop_B02, Bendall/Shastri): palm leaf rare within ~a century after 1388/89; paper general use late 16th c.
- Modern institutional response: Lok Sabha Unstarred Q.2467 (30 Aug 2007), Minister Ambika Soni: preservation under National Mission for Manuscripts via 33 Manuscript Conservation Centres; spending 2004-05 Rs 3.47 cr → 2006-07 Rs 9.43 cr; since 2003 launch ">5,000 manuscripts containing 2.2 million manuscript pages digitized" [VERIFIED-PAGE, eparlib OCR archived]
- Crossref metadata-only anchors for decline/conservation literature: Restaurator "Repair and Conservation of Palm-leaf Manuscripts" doi:10.1515/rest.1970.1.2.105; Hikosaka, "On Holding an All-India Seminar on Palm Leaf Manuscripts and Old Documents," JIBS 44(2) doi:10.4259/ibk.44.444; Colas, "Palm-leaf Manuscript Libraries in Southern India Around the Thirteenth Century: The Sarasvatī Library in Chidambaram," *Libraries in the Manuscript Age* (De Gruyter 2023) doi:10.1515/9783110779653-007 pp.135–158 [CROSSREF-METADATA; De Gruyter body CAPTCHA-blocked]
