## A. UNESCO Memory of the World — the three target registrations

### F1. Kuthodaw Inscription Shrines (Myanmar) — VERIFIED 2013, but NOT palm leaf
- Register entry: "The Stone Inscription is a collection of 729 stone slabs on which are inscribed the whole of the Buddhist scriptures whose religious and social significance is important for Asia. It records the Fifth Great Synod convened by King Mindon"; **Registration Year: 2013; Submission Year: 2012; Submitted by: Myanmar; Document type: Drawings**; nomination form https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/myanmar_kuthodaw.pdf
- **Caveat for report framing:** Kuthodaw is marble/stone stelae, not palm-leaf manuscripts. It belongs in the MoW-Buddhist-canonical-text cluster but must not be presented as a palm-leaf registration.
- Britannica (SNIPPET): gold inlay "looted following the British annexation of Myanmar in 1885 and replaced with black ink beginning in 1892"; 2013 MoW listing.
- T&F AIC CMG abstract (SNIPPET): "729 marble stelae... inscribed with Theravāda Buddhist texts written in Pali... known as the 'World's Biggest Book'" DOI 10.1080/10344233.2018.1522779
- Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/maha-lawkamarazein-or-kuthodaw-inscription-shrines [VERIFIED-PAGE → documents\unesco_mow_kuthodaw_2013.raw.md]

### F2. Mahavamsa, the Great Chronicle of Sri Lanka (University of Peradeniya) — VERIFIED 2023
- Register entry: "One of the world's longest unbroken historical accounts... presenting Sri Lanka's history in a chronological order from the 6th century BCE... crucial information about lifetime of the Buddha, the Emperor Asoka and the rise of Buddhism as a world religion"; image credit "Mahawamsa - p.01 — University of Peradeniya"; **Registration Year: 2023; Submission Year: 2021; Submitted by: Sri Lanka; Document type: Books**; nomination form https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/53_131%2B.pdf

- ceylonhistory.com (SNIPPET): inscription date "May 24, 2023"; "Preserved as a rare palm-leaf manuscript at the University of Peradeniya". lakpura.com FAQ (SNIPPET): multiple ola copies survive; UNESCO representatives inspected several holdings before selecting Peradeniya's.
- Note: register page itself does not state "palm leaf"; palm-leaf support comes from press/institutional sources above.
- Source: https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/mahavamsa-great-chronicle-sri-lanka-covering-period-6th-century-bce-1815-ce [VERIFIED-PAGE → documents\unesco_mow_mahavamsa_2023.raw.md]

## B. Illicit trade / market dynamics

### F5. Fragile Palm Leaves origin story — VERIFIED (two concordant sources)
- BDRC blog (FULL READ): "In the early 1990s, [E. Gene Smith] and the scholar Peter Skilling heard reports of Buddhist manuscripts being sold in the markets of Northern Thailand and neighboring countries. Traveling to Kengtung, Burma, they were alarmed to find that the reports were true—baskets of palm-leaf manuscripts were being sold as souvenirs." FPL founded as result; 2003 Lumbini International Research Institute digitization funded by Reiyukai Foundation; 2016 BDRC digitization supported by Khyentse Foundation; collection includes ">100 Kammavācā manuscripts, dating from the later 18th century through the early 20th century" whose "delicate gold leaf and lacquer tend to flake." https://www.bdrc.io/blog/2018/02/12/the-gilt-manuscripts-of-the-fragile-palm-leaves-collection/ [VERIFIED-PAGE → documents\bdrc_gilt_fpl_origin_story.raw.md]
- Khyentse Foundation (SNIPPET): "...sold as souvenirs in the markets of Northern Thailand and neighboring countries... led to the creation of the Fragile Palm Leaves Foundation in 1994." https://khyentsefoundation.org/kf-projects/fragile-palm-leaves-foundation-fpl/
- Scale (BDRC Programs, SNIPPET): "10,896 Burmese-style palm-leaf manuscripts and approximately 5,000 manuscripts in a variety of other formats and languages" https://www.bdrc.io/programs/
- Theft risk (Culture in Crisis portal, SNIPPET): "Due to neglect, mishandling, and theft, these texts risk disappearing forever." https://cultureincrisis.org/projects/fragile-palm-leaves-digitisation-initiative
- BL EAP1150 (SNIPPET via DDG; site bot-walled): Skilling's private library of Pali/Burmese palm-leaf mss, "created/copied between the 17th-20th centuries CE" https://eap.bl.uk/collection/EAP1150-1?page=1

## C. Economics of manuscript production

### F7. Division of labour & merit-patronage economy (SNIPPET-level leads)
- JSTOR article "The Division of Labour in the Production of Sri Lankan Palm Leaf Manuscripts": "Most scholars who have written on palm leaf manuscripts have not discussed the technology, instruments and human labour involvement in the production process" — key economics-of-production source, paywalled. https://www.jstor.org/stable/43855214 [SNIPPET — Phase B target]
- Lakpura (commercial heritage site, use cautiously): "Many monastic scriptoriums (potgul) produced manuscripts on commission, their work funded by merit-seeking donors — an economic relationship that gave the tradition both institutional support and cultural prestige." https://www.lakpura.com/pages/ola-leave-manuscripts [SNIPPET]
- DREAMSEA (digitization project): "it was also believed that the copying of palm-leaf manuscripts give people the opportunity to make merit by sponsoring the making of manuscripts thereby enabling the scribes to earn some money." (Wat Ban Hong Luang, Lanna context) https://dreamsea.co/highlights-of-manuscripts-from-wat-ban-hong-luang-2/ [SNIPPET]

## D. Living scribal traditions today

### F8. China: beiyejing craft as national ICH + active restoration training — VERIFIED (partially)
- China Daily govt portal (FULL READ): _beiyejing_ at Potala Palace; "465 volumes of beiyejing (nearly 30,000 leaves), accounting for more than half of the total collection of type in Xizang"; dating mostly 7th-13th c.; "China currently has the largest existing collection of palm-leaf manuscripts in the world" (state-media claim); Potala Palace Management Office "created an experimental restoration team for palm-leaf manuscripts in 2019"; specialists Pema Dekyi and Tsering Wangdu describe case-by-case repair (fiber delamination, warping; Tibetan-paper powder/sheet patching); "At present, there is no technology or experience in the world to draw upon for palm-leaf restoration." https://govt.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202504/22/WS681c4c32498eec7e1f73635b/palm-leaf-scripture-experts-seek-to-master-restoration.html [VERIFIED-PAGE → documents\chinadaily_potala_restoration_2025.raw.md]
- IRCI research database record (FULL READ): documentary film "The Memory of Tanlan: The Craft and Inheritance of Dai Palm-leaf Manuscripts" (Zhang Hai, 2020, Yunnan University / Center for Ethnic and Folk Literature and Art Development) "documents the Dai Palm-leaf Manuscript Making Craft, **a national-level intangible cultural heritage**, including its technical process, inheritance status, and cultural significance." https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13944 [VERIFIED-PAGE]
- **ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน** from a primary government list this session: exact element number VIII-142 and June-2008 second-batch announcement date for the Dai craft (Baidu Baike hit says second batch announced by State Council June 2008 — wiki-class source, KEYWORDS ONLY). ## E. Digital repatriation / ethics of collecting

### F10. General digital-repatriation debate (SNIPPET-level; no Buddhist-manuscript-specific study found)
- itsartlaw.org (8 Dec 2025): critiques "digital repatriation... returning digital surrogates... as a modern form of restitution" https://itsartlaw.org/art-law/against-the-illusion-the-limits-of-digital-repatriation-in-restitution-debates/
- restitutionmatters.org (7 Jul 2025, Ahmad Mohammed): success "hinges not only on ethical intent or technological innovation but on the socio-technical infrastructure available to source communities" https://restitutionmatters.org/publication/after-the-return-readiness-and-responsibility-in-hosting-digitally-repatriated-heritage/
- Berkeley law PDF (Picci, Repatriation Handbook): digital restitution = "the providing of copies of documentation and/or images to communities of origin, explicitly excluding the return of original materials" https://www.law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/archive/2025/09/Virtual-Repatriation_G.-Picci.pdf [all SNIPPET]
