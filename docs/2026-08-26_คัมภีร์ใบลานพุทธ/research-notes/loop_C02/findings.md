### A4. Production-economics content of Alahakoon 2012 reconstructed from OA sources citing it (FULL READ of the citers)
Source: Nishanthi, H.D.M.; Wijayasundara, N.D.; Abhayasundera, P. (2025). "Variety of Palm Leaves Used for Writing Manuscripts in Ancient Sri Lanka," *Proceedings ICFSS* 2(1):37–51, DOI 10.33422/icfss.v2i1.1108 (OA PDF read in full).
[FULL READ -> documents\nishanthi_2025_icfss_variety_palm_leaves.pdf + _fulltext.raw.md]
- Raw-material economy: writing leaves from Talipot (*Corypha umbraculifera*, "pus-kola"; Alahakoon 2012 citing De Silva 1938: 205 varieties), Palmyra (*Borassus flabellifer*), Lonta (*Corypha utan*); selection criteria: ease of preparation, durability, handling, storage (Senadeera 1992; Deepanjani 2007; Alahakoon 2006).
- Ceremonial labour regime (Alahakoon 2012 via ICFFS): "labour activities involve a sense of ceremonial purity" — performer offers the tree flowers and lamps, formally requests "permission from the tree", wears white, observes Five Precepts, starts at astrologer-chosen auspicious time; Buddhist monks recite hymns and ceremonially treat the bud to music of daula, tammettama, magul bera. Key ritual-economic roles: **Buddhist monk, astrologer, drummer, layperson (gihiya)** (Lagamuwa 2006).
- Process chain with named specialists/tools: mark tree, select/cut bud (*thala gobaya*), remove ekels, roll into *vattu* (midrib removed); boil vattu in copper pot with unripe pineapple leaves, papaya fruit+leaves, beli leaves+fruits, kappetiya, pinna, bombu leaves, paddy, pure water, firewood (Alahakoon 2007, 2012); dry hung on thread 3+ days gentle sun then 3 days dew; polish on 6-ft poles with ~2-lb stones both sides, padi-gala polishing stone, hema-kanda wood; cut strips 8–32 in x 2–2¾ in (Kulasuriya 1996); singe edges with hot iron; punch 2 holes with hot iron rod.
- Writing labour: two styli — sharp **panhinda**, blunt **ulkatuwa** (Alahakoon 2012); steel tip, handle of metal/wood/ivory; beginners practice on ulkatuwa; "A head Buddhist monk at a temple mainly instructs trainees" — i.e., monastic master-apprentice training. Blackening (*kalumadeema*) with Gaduma charcoal + kekunu/resin oil, cleaned with millet flour/rice bran; merit-framing term *nethra pinkama* (Bandara 2020).
- Binding/finishing: folios strung; covers/kamba of wood, metal or ivory; monks sometimes chant during wrapping; insect-resistant wood boxes/cupboards (Dhammarathana 2019; Alahakoon 2019).
- Monastic child labour note: "Young monks in temples and monasteries enthusiastically participate in and support these activities (Alahakoon, 2023)" — polishing stage.
- Kotte period described as golden age when "scholars wrote a new set of palm leaf manuscripts and copied a large number of existing ones" (subject list incl. taxes and revenue, state administration).
