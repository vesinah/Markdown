### F2. Quantitative claims (all from full text) — VERIFIED
- First comprehensive analysis of PLM production/preservation literature: 1753–2024 (~270 years); ~300 publications screened; **311 references analyzed**, yielding a list of **165 plants** mentioned **931 times** (125 species, 137 genera, 61 families, 33 orders), in **16 use categories**; PRISMA-based systematic review; methods incl. DCA ordination + Sørensen similarity.
- Geography: 33 geographical categories; most-cited countries India (71 refs), Sri Lanka (32), Thailand (23); 61% of refs geographically unspecified. Plant-name richness: India overall up to 79 names ("78 plants from 41 families" in Results), Odisha 34–35 plants, Tamil Nadu ~22, Kerala 15, Bengal 15, Gujarat 14; Sri Lanka 24; as few as 3 in Nepal/Malaysia contexts.
- Uses: insecticides/repellents largest category (75 plants; 307 mentions); oil plants (73; 161 mentions); conservation agents (45; 125 mentions); writing-support palms = 6 species — *Borassus flabellifer* (6 regions, 20 pubs), *Corypha umbraculifera* (8 regions, 20 pubs), *C. taliera*, *C. utan*, *C. lecomtei*, *Nypa fruticans*.
- Field-state diagnosis (verbatim anchors): "we found a lack of publications with a botanical or ethnobotanical focus, and there are none that thoroughly document the detailed process of PLM production"; prior reviews limited to two publications (Nishanthi et al. 2023 bibliometric study of 101 pubs 2002–2021; Wiland 2022/2023 two-part conservation review).
- Research-frontier statement (Conclusions): knowledge "remains discrete, contradictory, and incomplete"; calls for direct documentation of craft practices, unambiguous botanical identification, plant-DNA/micro-fragment lab analysis of manuscripts, and ethnobotanical interviews "to fill remaining gaps and preserve disappearing traditions".
- Citation-chain goldmine: reference list includes Alahakoon 2012 (see F5), Van Dyke 2009 "Sacred leaves: the conservation and exhibition of early Buddhist manuscripts on palm leaves" (Book & Paper Group Annual 28:83–97), Riberaigua & Messager 2018 (Collège de France codicology, 17th Int. Seminar Copenhagen, pp. 511–534), Hinzler 1993 (Bali), Gunawardana 1997 (*Palm leaf manuscripts of Sri Lanka*, National Museum).

### F3. Southeast-Asia coverage gaps per the article itself — VERIFIED
- Thailand: only common-use plants named (unspecified palms; *Cymbopogon* spp.) across 23 pubs. Myanmar: 2 species in 4 pubs (*Cymbopogon nardus*, *Corypha lecomtei*). Vietnam: only *C. lecomtei*. Laos: 6 pubs name no plants. Cambodia: no literature found. Authors: field/archival work + material analysis needed for these countries.

## C. MoW nomination PDFs mined (Phase B candidate 1 EXECUTED)

### F6. Kuthodaw nomination form (2012-04, inscribed 2013) — FULL READ
- Nominator: Department of Archaeology, National Museum and Library, Ministry of Culture, Myanmar (contact U Kyaw Oo Lwin). Owner/custodian same department.
- Facts: pagoda built 1857 by King Mindon; work begun 1860, slabs opened 1868 (~8 yrs); inscribing took 7 years 6 months 14 days; each scribe chiselled only 10–12 lines/day; text "meticulously edited by many senior monks and lay officials consulting the Tipitaka kept in royal libraries in the form of palm-leaf manuscripts" (the explicit palm-leaf link sought in A20).
- 729 slabs = 410 Sutta + 111 Vinaya + 208 Abhidhamma; each slab 5'6"×3'6"×6", two faces; enclosures 42+168+519. Fifth Great Synod 1871, ~2,400 monks, five months. 1900 printed edition: 38 vols, ~400 pp each, Philip H. Ripley of Hanthawaddy Press. Protection history 1901/1910/1921/1960/1999 listed.
- Rarity section itself contrasts stone record with "wooden, palm-leaf, paper" Tipitaka documents held in Korea, India, Tibet, Japan, Sri Lanka.
- URL: https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/myanmar_kuthodaw.pdf [PDF → documents\unesco_mow_kuthodaw_nomination_2013.pdf]

### F8. Phra That Phanom nomination form — FULL READ (richest codicology + economics)
- Collection: 10 versions, all "Esan Thai-Pali languages", ancient "Esan Dharm Scripts", leaves 51–58 cm × 3–5 cm; versions distinguished by Chad/Dark Chad/lacquer/gold-lacquer treatments and plain wood covers; per-version registration numbers (462/1-7; 727/1-6; 3/4; 79/1…; 203/1:1A-1N; 414/3; 454/1; 460/1-3…; 491/1…; 565/1) and bundle counts 1–16.
- Custody chain (per-version provenance): donations from Wat Nong Vang & Wat Bo Non Tan/Wat That (Khon Kaen) & Wat Sri Pham/Wat Bosri (Loei) 1975; Luang Senapakdee 1916; Office of Cabinet Secretariat 1936; purchased from Ray W. Bugtang 1921; 1861 copy ordered by an Esan Prince Regent, presented to Kromluang Prajaksilpakom, donated to National Library 1921; royal-funeral transliteration edition 21 Apr 1940 (Wat Makutkasatriyaram).
- Merit-making copying economy (§7.1.2 verbatim): "They were traditionally and popularly produced out of local devotions, by copying… The copying and multiplying the contents of such manuscripts are considered a form of merit making since the contents deal with the coming of Buddhism into the area, and at the same time they serve as a means of preservation and wider transmission."
- Community structure: Kha Ogas (devotee families assigned to service the stupa), Look Phra That (child-vow devotees), academic communities. Wrapping custom: wooden boards + silk wrapping tied with 5 strings (Five Precepts), entwined 3× (Triple Refuge), ends untucked (freedom).
- Preservation: NLT descends from 1905 amalgamation (Mandira Dharma + Vajirayanana + Buddhasasana Sangaha libraries) under King Chulalongkorn; storage 22–24 °C, 50–65% RH 24h-controlled, iron compactus; handling only by trained gloved staff; microfilm access with Director permission; virtual access free at finearts.go.th and digital.nlt.go.th; future plan: digitize entire collection.
- Recent studies section: copies recorded at temples/libraries in Chiang Mai, Nan, Prae, Loei, Khon Kaen, Maha Sarakham, Nakhon Phanom, Roi Et, Ubon Ratchathani (TH) and Luang Prabang, Savannakhet, Khammouane, Vientiane, Bolikhamsai, Champasak (LA); scholarly 4-group typology (multi-bundle 4–16, single-bundle, split-bundles, combined).
- URL: https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/56_143%252B.pdf [PDF+text → documents\unesco_mow_phra_that_phanom_nomination_2023.pdf / _text.txt]
