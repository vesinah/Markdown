Title: BUDA - Buddhist Digital Archives. Khmer Manuscript Heritage Project.
"Cambodia's manuscript heritage is a priceless storehouse of Khmer cultural knowledge, Buddhist scholarship, and artistic achievement."

## Overview
In 2019, the Buddhist Digital Resource Center embarked on an ambitious project to digitize thousands of endangered manuscripts in Cambodia. More than 1.4 million pages of these precious texts are now digitally archived and available to the world for the first time.

## Background
Sadly, the vast majority of these texts - 95% to 98% of the country's traditional manuscripts - were destroyed during the tumultuous 1970s and 1980s.
[Image captions:] Filmed copies of manuscripts as viewed on the old microfilm machines at the FEMC office. / The digitization team at DDD during a visit by H.E. Siyonn Sophearith, Director-General of Techniques for Cultural Affairs, Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts.
Through the efforts of the Fonds pour l'Edition des Manuscrits du Cambodge (Fund for the Preservation of Cambodian Manuscripts, or FEMC), a project of the Ecole francaise d'Extreme-Orient from 1990-2012, tens of thousands of Cambodia's surviving manuscripts were located and cataloged. When possible, these manuscripts were photographed to create a record of their contents. Approximately thirty percent of the manuscripts microfilmed by FEMC have now disappeared from their original locations, meaning FEMC's film copies are the last records of their existence.

## The Khmer Manuscript Heritage Project
With generous funding from A Khmer Buddhist Foundation, BDRC launched an ambitious project to digitize and archive the surviving manuscripts. Thanks to the support of BDRC's local project partners, Digital Divide Data and the FEMC... in just two-and-a-half years the project created digital copies of 11,596 surviving manuscripts which are now freely accessible through this website.
We are especially indebted to: Kun Sopheap, Leng Kok-An, Olivier de Bernon, Mech Khoeun, Chhom Kunthea, Suon Kosal, Suong Sereykhosal, Vann Narith, Trent Walker, David Wharton, Jacqueline Vincent, Alexis Lecoq, ... ## The EFEO-FEMC Preservation of Cambodian Manuscripts
### The EFEO in Cambodia
EFEO founded 1898 in Saigon as Mission archeologique d'Indo-Chine. In Cambodia first established Conservation of Angkor 1907, then School of Pali, National Museum Phnom Penh, Royal Library, Buddhist Institute, Vat Poveal Museum Battambang. Ms. Suzanne Karpeles, the first female member of the EFEO, inaugural Curator of the Royal Library of Cambodia (established 1925; now National Library), instrumental in founding the Buddhist Institute in 1930 where she was Secretary General. In 1943, under Pierre Dupont (EFEO member), Royal Library made part of the Buddhist Institute, active until Khmer Rouge regime 1975. Mr. Thaong Yok (1921-2007), master of Pali studies and last living colleague of Ms. Karpeles, later key member of EFEO-FEMC team.

### Establishment of the EFEO-FEMC in 1990
Following the collapse of the Khmer Rouge regime in 1979, a number of outstanding individuals undertook the rescue and protection of the old collections of manuscripts in Phnom Penh. Among those of the former Buddhist Institute, the largest part was safeguarded by Venerable Preah Vanarat Ken Vong, one of the first six monks to be re-ordained in Cambodia.
The Fonds pour l'Edition des Manuscrits du Cambodge was established under the auspices of the EFEO (EFEO-FEMC) in 1990 with the aim to systematically inventory and photograph Cambodia's surviving manuscripts. Professor Leon Vandermeersch was Director of the EFEO at that time, and Professor Francois Bizot was in charge of its manuscript publication fund. Following preparatory missions, Prof. Bizot obtained permission from Khmer authorities for project based in small pavilion within compound of former Royal Palace Museum. Under direction of Prof. Olivier de Bernon, team of Cambodian staff began to restore, identify, inventory manuscripts located in Phnom Penh and later other provinces. King Norodom Sihanouk had been actively encouraging this mission since 1989; on his return 1991 invited project to continue at the Royal Palace; 1993 placed under senior royal patronage. In 1999 project moved to compound of Wat Ounalom at invitation of His Holiness Supreme Patriarch Samdech Tep Vong.
Funded since inception by French Ministry of Higher Education and Research and Ministry of Foreign Affairs; undertaken in concert with Cambodian Ministry of Cults and Religion with support of Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts which seconded staff members. Since 2012, work of FEMC has continued independently of the EFEO, with ongoing approval and financial support of the Cambodian government, and continued guidance from Prof. de Bernon. Key Cambodian members: Kun Sopheap, Leng Kok-An, Thaong Yok, Ly Sovy, Yun Polbo, Ros Soksambo, Vann Narith, Suon Kosal, Tom Saroeun.

### Achievements of the EFEO-FEMC
Since its inception, the EFEO-FEMC has visited over a thousand monasteries, mostly in Phnom Penh and the provinces of Kandal, Kompong Cham, and Siem Reap. Of these, just over one hundred still had a manuscript collection. Long-abandoned palm-leaf folios often found wrapped in orange cloth, sometimes hidden in places such as false ceilings unknown to current occupants, having survived due to faith and courage of people who took risk to protect them during Democratic Kampuchea. In Siem Reap province only a single large manuscript library remained, at Wat Thipadey (Vatt Dhipati); surveys conducted in 2020 revealed less-complete libraries still extant in Siem Reap. In Phnom Penh and Kandal province over 80 percent of monasteries surveyed no longer had a collection, and over two-thirds of surviving manuscripts were incomplete. Nevertheless 1991-1996 restored/inventoried/filmed almost 2,000 manuscripts in monasteries of capital city and Kandal province.
Project also worked on major collections formerly part of Buddhist Institute Library now held by other institutions in Phnom Penh. Largest: rescued and collated by Venerable Preah Vanarat Ken Vong. In 1992, just two years before his death, he entrusted restoration/inventory to EFEO-FEMC. Resulting collection of more than 125,000 pages is by far the largest in the country: EFEO-Preah Vanarat Ken Vong Library housed at Wat Ounalom (previously at Wat Saravan Techo where Ven. Ken Vong was abbot).
In 1996, EFEO-FEMC discovered the only important manuscript library in Cambodia spared destruction of war and Khmer Rouge: Wat Phum Thmei Serey Mongkol (Vatt Bhumi Thmi Sirimangal) in Kampong Cham province (story featured in 2021 Tricycle Magazine article). When the 50,000 scattered folios were fully restored in 1998, two-thirds of resulting 1,210 manuscripts were complete, making collection a key tool for identification of other texts. Kampong Cham province is by far the richest for surviving manuscripts in Cambodia.
Smaller parts of former Buddhist Institute collection in National Museum and National Library restored/inventoried 2002 and 2003 respectively. National Library with some 524 manuscripts holds second largest collection after EFEO-Preah Vanarat Ken Vong Library. Only other known remaining mss from former Buddhist Institute collection, housed in one of Silver Pagoda buildings within Royal Palace compound, restored/inventoried 2004.
During preservation work each palm-leaf folio dusted/cleaned, oil applied when necessary, collated to recompose fascicles/manuscripts/bundles; sometimes re-inked to obtain clear images. After photographing, fascicles tied using combined silk and cotton cords; bound between wooden covers encased in woven bamboo and fabric protective wrappings. Traditional cords/wrappings made by elderly women and men in workshops established by the project. EFEO-FEMC also donated glass cabinets for manuscript storage.
In parallel, EFEO-FEMC published first and second volumes of the Provisional Inventory of Cambodian Manuscripts, and filming of manuscripts between 1991-2004 produced some 400 strips of black-and-white film, documenting 757,507 pages of manuscripts. All photography done in situ using Nikon F3 camera with special back enabling 250 shots on Kodak Technical Pan and Kodalite black and white film. Copies of negative and positive films kept at Wat Ounalom originally digitized 2009 under joint EFEO-UNESCO program funded by Embassy of Singapore. Newly digitized by Digital Divide Data as part of collaborative project between BDRC and EFEO-FEMC in 2019-2021, now freely available via BUDA.
