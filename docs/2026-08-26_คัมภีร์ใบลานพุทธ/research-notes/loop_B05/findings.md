## 1. EFEO-FEMC inventory programme (full programme details now verified)

- VERIFIED-FULL (de Bernon chapter, Crossasia PDF): "As a result of the devastation of protracted war, the libraries of 83 percent of the monasteries of Cambodia, and an estimated 98 percent of the manuscripts, have been completely destroyed." EFEO-FEMC inaugurated fifteen years before writing (~1990): physical restoration then photographic replication. From 1990: visited ~1,000 temples ("one temple out of four"), inventoried nearly 10,000 bundles, took altogether 130,000 pictures; duplicate film sets prepared for Khmer authorities. Royal patronage of King Sihanouk since 1993. https://digital.crossasia.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/literaryheritageoflaos23_debernon_en.pdf
- VERIFIED-FULL (same): Field method sequence - locate monasteries with mss; reassemble dispersed leaves/bundles; physical restoration (re-inking, new cords, new wooden covers); identification; registration (alphanumerical inventory number encoding location + class); microfilming; repair of monastery libraries and cabinet installation. All operations on site, without displacing a single manuscript from its home monastery.
- VERIFIED-FULL (same): First nine years laboratory in Silver Pagoda, Phnom Penh (Royal Palace grounds); later hosted by the Maha Sangharaja at Vatt Unnalom (Wat Ounalom). Films 250 frames vs normal 36, black-and-white Kodak Technical Pan, Kodalithe duplication.
- VERIFIED-FULL (BDRC aboutkm page): FEMC established under EFEO auspices 1990 to systematically inventory and photograph surviving manuscripts; EFEO director Leon Vandermeersch, Francois Bizot in charge of manuscript publication fund; based first in pavilion of former Royal Palace Museum compound; moved to Wat Ounalom 1999 at invitation of Supreme Patriarch Samdech Tep Vong. Funded since inception by French Ministry of Higher Education and Research + Ministry of Foreign Affairs, in concert with Cambodian Ministry of Cults and Religion and Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts (which seconded staff). Since 2012 FEMC continues independently of EFEO with Cambodian government approval/financial support and guidance from de Bernon. http://khmer-manuscripts.bdrc.io/static/aboutkm
- VERIFIED-FULL (BDRC aboutkm): Visited over a thousand monasteries mostly Phnom Penh/Kandal/Kompong Cham/Siem Reap; just over one hundred still had collections. In Phnom Penh and Kandal over 80 percent of monasteries surveyed no longer had a collection; over two-thirds of surviving mss incomplete. Almost 2,000 mss restored/inventoried/filmed in capital+Kandal 1991-1996. Siem Reap: only single large library remained at Wat Thipadey (Vatt Dhipati); 2020 surveys found less-complete libraries still extant.
- VERIFIED-FULL (BDRC aboutkm): Filming 1991-2004 produced some 400 strips of black-and-white film documenting 757,507 pages of manuscripts; all photography in situ, Nikon F3 camera with special back enabling 250 shots on Kodak Technical Pan/Kodalite film. Copies at Wat Ounalom first digitized 2009 under joint EFEO-UNESCO programme funded by Embassy of Singapore; re-digitized by Digital Divide Data 2019-2021 for BUDA.
- VERIFIED-FULL (BDRC aboutkm): Approximately thirty percent of the manuscripts microfilmed by FEMC have now disappeared from their original locations - FEMC films are the last records of their existence.
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (GIS Reseau Asie via A14) + southeastasianarchaeology.com 2024 post: five-person team under Olivier de Bernon.

## 2. Preah Vanarat Ken Vong library and 1996 Kompong Cham discovery

- VERIFIED-FULL (de Bernon chapter): Bibliothèque Preah Vanarat Ken Vong organised/administered by FEMC since 1992; named after late Abbot of Vatt Saravan who in 1979 collected what was left of the ancient manuscript collection in Phnom Penh; died 1994 leaving 3,900 manuscripts - largest collection in Cambodia (National Library holds only 1,500 per this 2005-era text).
- VERIFIED-FULL (BDRC aboutkm): Ven. Preah Vanarat Ken Vong was one of the first six monks re-ordained in Cambodia after 1979; entrusted his rescued collection to EFEO-FEMC in 1992; resulting collection >125,000 pages = EFEO-Preah Vanarat Ken Vong Library, now housed at Wat Ounalom (previously Wat Saravan Techo). NOTE discrepancy vs de Bernon chapter figure (3,900 mss / >125,000 pages both plausible framings; National Library figure differs: de Bernon ~1,500 vs BDRC 524 mss restored/inventoried 2003 - different counting bases; do not merge).
- VERIFIED-FULL (both sources): November 1996 discovery at Kompong Cham province - only important monastic library spared KR destruction; hidden by Deputy Abbot after KR takeover; >6 months to transform ~50,000 jumbled palm leaves into bundles. de Bernon: 2,537 bundles. BDRC: fully restored 1998 as 1,210 manuscripts, two-thirds complete (Wat Phum Thmei Serey Mongkol / Vatt Bhumi Thmi Sirimangal). Kampong Cham richest province for surviving mss.

## 5. Ian Harris, Buddhism in a Dark Age - content on destruction/reconstruction (via full review)

- VERIFIED-FULL (Brawn review, SEAS 7(2) 2018, free-access PDF): Harris senior scholar at DC-Cam 2003-2007; used DC-Cam archive (unpublished materials, confession documents, Lon Nol government + foreign government reports, Party member/prisoner biographies, soldiers' notebooks, memoirs, 1979 Pol Pot trial information, Party magazines/films/instructional videos) plus 87 interviews he conducted/participated in and 34 conducted by others (67 with assistance 2003-2005; 20 alone 1997-2006; 34 by others in 1986). https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/seas/7/2/7_237/_pdf/-char/en
- VERIFIED-FULL (same): Harris quote: "although Buddhism was in mortal danger during Democratic Kampuchea, it was never totally extinguished, despite the claims of Angkar to the contrary" (p. 139). DK founded at Special National Congress April 25-27, 1975; twenty monks joined that delegation; thereafter monks/Buddhism never referenced in official communications: "From the official perspective, they had ceased to exist" (p. 67). Buddhism listed with imperialism and "reactionary capitalism" as one of three "mountains" needing eradication. 1976 White Khmer/White Scarf revolt executed; 1973 Patriotic Monks Association alternative sangha administration (pp. 69-72). Murals protected with charcoal + rice porridge mixture (p. 115). "by far the most common cause of violent death in the sangha was a refusal to disrobe" (p. 123). Monasteries turned into barracks/depots/offices; "good barracks and good prisons" (pp. 108-109). Final chapter devoted to post-1979 PRK re-establishment of sangha under Vietnamese control until 1989.
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (Cambridge JAS review): In 2003 Harris was contracted by DC-Cam to explore fate of Buddhists under CPK; Buddhism in a Dark Age expands the volume Harris produced for DC-Cam in 2007, adding pre-1975 and post-1979 material. (Connects DC-Cam Buddhist heritage documentation thread.) Cambridge URL in source-list.
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (OUP Hawaii Scholarship Online): book concludes with slow re-establishment and official supervision of the Buddhist order during PRK period; ch. 7 "Aftermath: Rebuilding the Sangha under Socialism" covers official/unofficial ordinations, Buddhism in late PRK. https://academic.oup.com/hawaii-scholarship-online/book/16521
- NOTE on destruction STATISTICS: neither the review nor OUP pages give manuscript-count statistics; the now-verified quantitative anchors are de Bernon (83% monastery libraries / ~98% mss destroyed) and BDRC (95%-98% destroyed during 1970s-1980s). Tricycle-attributed EFEO figures (358 of 433 monasteries without a single ms; Leng Kok-An ~10%) remain from A14.

## 7. Printed Khmer-script Tripitaka - volume count and completion year RESOLVED

- VERIFIED-FULL (Sovanratana PDF): On 14 December 1929 King Sisowath signed royal ordinance setting up the Tipitaka Commission (translating/editing Pali texts into Khmer); initial commission 37 members (President, two deputies, 34 members of whom 11 laymen); initiators incl. Résident Supérieur Fermant Laric and Suzanne Karpeles (Secretary General of Royal Library). Reorganized 1940 (Huot Tath vice-head after death of Oum Sou). By 1969 thirteen members remained incl. President Huot Tath. Corpus: 110 volumes, each 400-900 pages, entitled Preah Trai Pitak, bilingual: Vinaya 13 vols, Suttanta 64 vols, Abhidhamma 33 vols. Completion celebrated 1st-2nd April 1969 in the capital on grand scale. Six working groups (copying Pali from old texts; editing Pali; translating; Khmer proofreading; final proofreading; final editing/publishing). Variants footnoted against Ceylonese ('S'), Burmese ('M'), PTS ('E') editions. Pali printed on one page, Khmer opposite. Sets distributed to Burma, Ceylon, China, India, Japan, Laos, Thailand, Vietnam, England, France, Germany, US.
- VERIFIED-FULL (Cambodia Tribune, 2026-02-10): translation was 40-year process 1929-1969 by Tipitaka Congregation of the Buddhist Academy Phnom Penh; Cambodia first Buddhist country to translate Tipitaka into national language; 110 volumes - Vinaya 1-13, Sutta 14-77, Abhidhamma 78-110; 56th anniversary celebrated Feb 2026 with >2,500 participants at Oudong; government approved re-establishment of Tipitaka Congregation (Hun Manet policy decision "last year") to authenticate Khmer Tipitaka + five-yearly consecration ceremony at Angkor Wat or Oudong. https://cambodiatribune.com/cambodia-marks-56th-anniversary-of-khmer-tipitaka-translation/
- VERIFIED-FULL (cambodianview tripitaka.pdf): same structure (110 books: 13/64/33); "Totaling together consists of 36,630 pages in Pali language and 36,630 pages in Khmer language." Document bears BE 2548 (=2004/05) mark; author attribution garbled in OCR - provenance still secondary/unattributed. http://www.cambodianview.com/documents/tripitaka.pdf
- CONFLICT NOTES: (a) PPP/thefreelibrary snippet (A14) said Commission formed 1930, translated 1930-1968, 39 years - conflicts with Sovanratana ordinance date 14 Dec 1929 + completion celebration April 1969; Sovanratana + Cambodia Tribune + Grokipedia-independent agreement on 1929 start and 1969 completion preferred. (b) NLA holding (76 vols Vinaya+Sutra, produced 1931-1974) describes that library's partial set, not the complete edition - do not conflate. (c) LOC "1931 volume of larger 10-volume set possibly first printing of Tripitaka in Khmer script" refers to an early separate printing - keep distinct from the completed 110-vol edition.
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (Cambodianess 2025-03-14): Tripitaka Commission led by the Buddhist Institute; tasks include leading Khmer traditional group, researching religious theories, publishing Kampuchea Suriya magazine; Japanese organizational support sought for reprinting. https://cambodianess.com/article/tripitaka-commission-re-established

## 8. Post-1979 monk recopying networks

- VERIFIED-FULL (Sovanratana PDF): after 17 April 1975 all commission members except two (Oum Sum, Heng Leang Hor) killed; Commission never revived (as of paper's writing); individual translations of Atthakatha (Samantapasadika, Atthasalini) exist but never officially authorized for formal Pali schools.
- VERIFIED-FULL (BDRC aboutkm): post-1979 rescue by individuals - largest part of former Buddhist Institute mss safeguarded by Ven. Preah Vanarat Ken Vong, one of first six monks re-ordained in Cambodia.
- GAP: systematic monk-led RECOPYING campaigns of the early-to-mid 1980s remain undocumented in fetched sources. Harris ch.7 (PRK sangha rebuilding) and Sovanratana's Delhi thesis are the indicated next reads. Tricycle (A14) documents pre-1975 recopying tradition and village safekeeping, not 1980s campaigns.
