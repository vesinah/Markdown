### Chinese Buddhism

[_The following represents an unverified English translation. For all purposes consult the original Chinese text._]

貝葉經 [_bei ye jing_]—(Bèiyèjīng)—[Miscellany] Scriptures written on 貝多 [_bei duo_] (Bèiduō) tree leaves are therefore called (Bèiyèjīng).

The 酉陽雜俎 [_you yang za zu_] (Yǒuyáng Zázǔ) (Miscellany of Youyang) states: "The 貝多 [_bei duo_] (Bèiduō) tree originates from the 摩伽陀國 [_mo jia tuo guo_] (Mójiātuó Guó) (Magadha Kingdom). It grows six to seven zhang tall and does not wither in winter. There are three types of this tree: the first is 多羅婆力叉貝多 [_duo luo po li cha bei duo_] (Duōluóbólìchā Bèiduō) (Tarapatra), the second is 多梨婆力叉貝多 [_duo li po li cha bei duo_] (Duōlíbólìchā Bèiduō) (Talapatra), and the third is 部婆力叉多羅多梨 [_bu po li cha duo luo duo li_] (Bùbólìchā Duōluō Duōlí) (Bhurjapatra Talipat). All are written on their leaves, but the 部婆力叉多羅多梨 [_bu po li cha duo luo duo li_] (Bùbólìchā Duōluō Duōlí) type uses its bark for writing. 貝多 [_bei duo_] (Bèiduō) is a 梵語 [_fan yu_] (Fànyǔ) (Sanskrit) word, translated into Chinese as 'leaf'. As for 貝多婆力叉 [_bei duo po li cha_] (Bèiduō Bólìchā), it is translated into Chinese as 'tree leaf'. Scriptures from the 西域 [_xi yu_] (Xīyù) (Western Regions) use these three types of bark and leaves, and if well-preserved, they can last for five or six hundred years."

貝葉經—【雜語】以貝多樹葉書經文，故云貝葉經。酉陽雜俎曰：「貝多出摩伽陀國，長六七丈，經冬不凋。此樹有三種：一者多羅婆力叉貝多，二者多梨婆力叉貝多，三者部婆力叉多羅多梨。並書其葉部，闍一色，取其皮書之。貝多是梵語，漢翻為葉。貝多婆力叉者，漢言樹葉也。西域經書用此三種皮葉，若能保護，亦得五六百年。」

[zá yǔ] yǐ bèi duō shù yè shū jīng wén, gù yún bèi yè jīng. yǒu yáng zá zǔ yuē: “bèi duō chū mó jiā tuó guó, zhǎng liù qī zhàng, jīng dōng bù diāo. cǐ shù yǒu sān zhǒng: yī zhě duō luó pó lì chā bèi duō, èr zhě duō lí pó lì chā bèi duō, sān zhě bù pó lì chā duō luó duō lí. bìng shū qí yè bù, dū yī sè, qǔ qí pí shū zhī. bèi duō shì fàn yǔ, hàn fān wèi yè. bèi duō pó lì chā zhě, hàn yán shù yè yě. xī yù jīng shū yòng cǐ sān zhǒng pí yè, ruò néng bǎo hù, yì dé wǔ liù bǎi nián.”

[za yu] yi bei duo shu ye shu jing wen, gu yun bei ye jing. you yang za zu yue: "bei duo chu mo jia tuo guo, zhang liu qi zhang, jing dong bu diao. ci shu you san zhong: yi zhe duo luo po li cha bei duo, er zhe duo li po li cha bei duo, san zhe bu po li cha duo luo duo li. bing shu qi ye bu, du yi se, qu qi pi shu zhi. bei duo shi fan yu, han fan wei ye. bei duo po li cha zhe, han yan shu ye ye. xi yu jing shu yong ci san zhong pi ye, ruo neng bao hu, yi de wu liu bai nian."

Chinese Buddhism (漢傳佛教, _hanchuan fojiao_) is the form of Buddhism that developed in China, blending Mahayana teachings with Daoist and Confucian thought. Its texts are mainly in Classical Chinese, based on translations from Sanskrit. Major schools include Chan (Zen), Pure Land, Tiantai, and Huayan. Chinese Buddhism has greatly influenced East Asian religion and culture.
