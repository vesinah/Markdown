## A. Turfan / Berlin collection (Sanskrit Buddhist manuscripts, Tarim Basin)

1. Materials spectrum — palm leaves present but rare. [FULL-READ][ARCHIVED]
   Quote (Simone-Christiane Raschmann, "The Berlin Turfan-Collection", Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin):
   "Between 1902 and 1914 German scholars under the leadership of Albert Grünwedel and Albert von Le Coq made excavations in the area of Turfan, Hami, Kucha and Karashahr. ## C. Japan — Hōryūji palm leaves, Shōsōin/Shōgozō, pilgrim monks

9. Hōryūji Sanskrit palm-leaf Heart Sutra + Uṣṇīṣavijaya-dhāraṇī. [FULL-READ][ARCHIVED]
   Quotes: "The palm leaf manuscript held at Hōryū Gakumonji ... containing texts of the Prajñāpāramitā-hṛdaya, Uṣṇīṣavijaya-dhāraṇī, and a Sanskrit syllabary. ## D. China — beiye 貝葉 and Xuanzang's haul

13. beiyejing definition and etymology. [FULL-READ via proxy][ARCHIVED]
    Quotes (Wisdom Library aggregation of DILA Ding Fubao / NTI Reader / CC-CEDICT / DDB / Soothill):
    - NTI Reader: "貝葉經 bèi yè jīng refers to [noun] 'palm leaf sutra' ... Notes: A sutra written on leaves of pattra palm tree".
    - Ding Fubao quoting 酉陽雜俎 Youyang Zazu: "The 貝多 tree originates from the 摩伽陀國 Magadha Kingdom... There are three types of this tree... All are written on their leaves... Scriptures from the Western Regions use these three types of bark and leaves, and if well-preserved, they can last for five or six hundred years."
    - DILA DDB: 貝葉 refers to "*tāla"; Sanskrit patra; Korean 패엽 paeseop; Japanese バイヨウ baiyō.
    - Soothill, Dictionary of Chinese Buddhist Terms: "貝葉 — pattra; palm leaves from the borassus flabelliformis, used for writing material."
    - Sarvāstivāda Vinaya (Scroll 48, quoted moedict): "Birch bark (樺皮), palm leaves, pens, ink, and lamp light are all essential necessities; it is advisable to have plenty of them brought in."
    URLs: https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/bei-ye-jing ; https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/bei-ye

14. Xuanzang's returned corpus. [SNIPPET]
    Quote (Britannica): "Xuanzang spent the remainder of his life translating the Buddhist scriptures, numbering 657 items packed in 520 cases, that he brought back from India. He was able to translate only a small portion of this huge volume, about 75 items in 1,335 chapters..."

## E. Tibet/Mongolia — pothi FORMAT adopted on paper

16. Pecha defined as derived from Indian pothis. [SNIPPET][ARCHIVED]
    Quote (Rubin Museum, Project Himalayan Art glossary): "A pecha is a traditional form of Tibetan book, a format developed from Indian palm leaf manuscripts (pothis). Pechas usually have long, narrow, horizontal pages. The pages are not bound, but instead are placed between wood covers and then wrapped tightly with cloth."
    URL: https://rubinmuseum.org/projecthimalayanart/glossary/pecha/

17. Paper imitation of pothi reaching Tibet/Central Asia. [SNIPPET]
    Quote (Weston, Univ. of Glasgow, "Palm leaf and folding books of South Asia"): "Paper manuscripts in imitation this format were produced in the northern regions of the Indian sub-continent, a practice which was extended, principally by means of Buddhist culture, to Tibet and beyond, into Central Asia. In such manuscripts two drawn circles, often decorated, interrupt the text where the cord holes would have been present on a pothi book."
    URL: https://www.gla.ac.uk/media/Media_1144973_smxx.pdf

18. Mongolian paper sutras keep pothi shape. [SNIPPET]
    Quote (Princeton DPUL, "The pothī tradition"): "This form influenced book making even into northern regions without palms - within the large Tibetan and Mongolian paper sūtras the palm leaf origin remains discernable, as it does in the parabaik paper accordion-style books."
    URL: https://dpul.princeton.edu/global-book-forms/feature/the-pothi-tradition
    Corroboration (auction catalogue description, dealer text — lower weight): "The pothi format itself is inherited from the Indian palm-leaf manuscript and transmitted through Tibet into Mongolia, where it was retained for sacred texts long after paper had replaced palm leaf as the support."
    URL: https://www.liveauctioneers.com/item/235890954_three-religious-manuscripts-18th-century-mongolia-london-london
