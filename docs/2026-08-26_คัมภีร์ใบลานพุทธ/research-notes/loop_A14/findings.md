# Findings - Loop A14 - Cambodian palm-leaf manuscript culture (slaek trieang / sleuk rith)

Session date: 2026-08-26. All facts below are taken from sources fetched this session. Status legend: VERIFIED-FULL = source fully read and archived; VERIFIED-SNIPPET = only search-result snippet or partial page obtained; GAP = no verifiable confirmation this session.

Terminology note: the loop brief's romanization "slaek trieang" (Khmer ស្លឹកត្រែង) was not encountered in any fetched source. Fetched sources instead use "sleuk rith" / "sāstrā sleuk rith" (សាស្ត្រា ស្លឹករឹត, palm-leaf sastra) and, for Vietnam's Khmer community, kinh lá buông (buông-palm scriptures). The term question remains open (see Gaps).

## 1. Palm-leaf manuscript culture in Cambodia (Corypha leaves, Khmer script)

- VERIFIED-FULL (Tricycle/BDRC): Buddhist texts in Cambodia were "etched onto palm leaves or inked onto even more delicate bark paper"; scribes "would regularly recopy manuscripts" to protect texts from loss; most surviving manuscripts were created between 1850 and 1950 but their contents reach back to the sixteenth century or earlier because of recopying. https://tricycle.org/article/wat-phum-thmei-texts/
- VERIFIED-FULL (Tricycle, quoting Dr. Kunthea Chhom, Ministry of Culture epigraphist): "the manuscripts reflect both the continuity of the Angkorian intellectual tradition and the [flourishing] of the language." Same URL.
- VERIFIED-FULL (Tricycle): majority of Wat Phum Thmei library texts are "bitexts," bilingual sermons and treatises interweaving Pali and Khmer; collection includes Jataka tales, the Patham tras ("the oldest life narrative of the Buddha found in Cambodia"), the Cetanabheda ("scattering of the [32] minds"), and rare cosmological Trai Ta/Trai Bhed manuscripts (BDRC W1FEMC030198). Same URL.
- VERIFIED-FULL (LOC guide): LOC holds 60 Khmer and Pali-in-Khmer-script manuscripts including Suttapitaka and Abhidhamma selections, Jatakas, karma works, proverbs, prayers, and a Khmer-language Ramayana (Ramakerti) in multiple fascicles; some formerly owned by French colonial-era scholars Paul Mus and Suzanne Karpeles. https://guides.loc.gov/southeast-asian-rare-books-manuscripts/khmer
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (Cambodianess picture-of-the-day, 2026-03-05): Khmer palm-leaf manuscripts called "ollas"/lontar, Khmer sastra sleuk reut, used to preserve Buddhist texts, folklore. https://cambodianess.com/article/picture-of-the-day-palm-leaf-manuscript
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (hslb.org PDF, Cambodian-American Collection): "Satraa is the Khmer word for the palm leaf book... written in Khmer or Pali... The practice dates to before the Angkor Period (9th to 14th century)." https://hslb.org/wp-content/uploads/Cambodian-American-Collection/History-and-Culture/Arts/Literature/Palm-leaf-books.pdf

## 2. Royal/Khmer Pali Tripitaka: commissioning and printing

- VERIFIED-FULL (LOC guide): "Also significant is a 1931 volume, of a larger 10-volume set, of the Tripitaka... possibly the first printing of the Tripitaka in Khmer script." Also: Khmer rare books "created at the Buddhist Institute in Phnom Penh in the 1920s and 1930s," among the earliest Western-printed Khmer texts including a Khmer-language text printed in Cambodia in 1927; authors include Chhuon Nath, later Supreme Patriarch. https://guides.loc.gov/southeast-asian-rare-books-manuscripts/khmer
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (Phnom Penh Post article syndicated at thefreelibrary, dated 2025-03-14 in feed): "The Khmer Tripitaka was translated from Pali to Khmer by the Tripitaka Commission of Buddhist Studies in Phnom Penh from 1930 to 1968, taking 39 years to complete... considered a treasure of Cambodian Buddhism." Full page blocked (403); snippet retained from DDG results. https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Govt+to+re-establish+Tripitaka+Commission+formed+in+1930.-a0831106663
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (National Library of Australia catalogue record 1745562, Vinaipitak parivar...): "The NLA holds the complete Vinaya Pitaka and Sutra Pitaka series produced between 1931-1974. They consist of 76 (13, 63) volumes of parallel texts written in Pali and Khmer (in Khmer script). This rare Tripitaka edition is one of very few to survive the Cambodian civil war." Site blocked by Anubis wall; snippet retained. https://catalogue.nla.gov.au/catalog/1745562

## 3. Losses under Democratic Kampuchea (1975-1979)

- VERIFIED-FULL (Tricycle): "Scholars estimate that between 1975 and 1990 more than 95 percent of manuscripts in Cambodia disappeared, either through neglect or outright destruction at the hands of the Khmer Rouge." https://tricycle.org/article/wat-phum-thmei-texts/
- VERIFIED-FULL (Tricycle, citing Ian Harris, Buddhism in a Dark Age): In May 1975 Pol Pot and Nuon Chea announced a program that included a plan to "defrock all Buddhist monks and put them to work growing rice"; "Texts were burned, buried, or woven into hats for the military." In 1978 Yun Yat, minister of culture, information and propaganda, declared: "Buddhism is dead and the ground has been cleared for the foundations of a new revolutionary culture." Same URL.
- VERIFIED-FULL (Tricycle, EFEO figure): "358 of the 433 monasteries visited in Phnom Penh and Kandal provinces did not have even a single manuscript"; Leng Kok-An estimates only ten percent of monasteries visited by EFEO-FEMC between 1990 and 2012 still possessed manuscripts. Same URL.
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (angkordatabase film description, "Le fonds EFEO des manuscrits khmers"): "Initially collected by Abbot Joseph Guesdon, the EFEO Khmer manuscripts collection holds precious documents related to the Cambodian Royal Chronicles, juridical texts and Buddhist treatises. During the civil war, about 98% of these documents usually kept in pagoda libraries were destroyed." Presented by Olivier de Bernon and Lucie Labbe. https://angkordatabase.asia/films/le-fonds-efeo-des-manuscrits-khmers-efeo-collection-of-khmer-manuscripts
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (Georgetown Berkley Center post): by the regime's end in 1979 "nearly every monk and religious intellectual had been either murdered or driven into exile, and nearly every temple and library had been destroyed or used as a prison." https://berkleycenter.georgetown.edu/posts/buddhism-survived-the-khmer-rouge-to-give-hope-to-cambodia
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (USHMM Cambodia pages): monks labeled parasites, temples seized and converted; April 17 1975 to January 7 1979. https://www.ushmm.org/genocide-prevention/countries/cambodia/khmer-rouge-revolution and /cambodia-1975
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (JSTOR book record): Ian Harris, Buddhism in a Dark Age: Cambodian Monks under Pol Pot (University of Hawai'i Press) - first sustained analysis of whether the Khmer Rouge had a centralized plan to liquidate the sangha. https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt6wqf7r

## 4. Post-1979 restoration: FEMC/EFEO inventory and safekeeping stories

- VERIFIED-FULL (Tricycle): Wat Phum Thmei Serey Mongkol (Kampong Cham province) houses "the most complete library of palm leaf texts in the country"; in 1975 its head monk distributed manuscripts among devout villagers for safekeeping; after the fall of the Khmer Rouge the texts returned; EFEO-FEMC discovered them disorganized in 1996; restoration of approximately 2,500 manuscripts took two years; the library now holds 5,000-6,000 manuscripts including donations from nearby monasteries. https://tricycle.org/article/wat-phum-thmei-texts/
- VERIFIED-FULL (Tricycle): Between 1990 and 2012 the EFEO-FEMC team visited well over a thousand monasteries (focus: Phnom Penh, Siem Reap, Kandal, Kampong Cham), cleaning, reassembling, documenting contents in a two-volume catalog and photographing texts; led by Leng Kok-An and the late Kun Sopheap, assisted by older monks who had studied manuscripts between the 1940s and 1960s. Same URL.
- VERIFIED-FULL (Tricycle): concealed caches found in rafters and one false ceiling; leaves found chewed by insects and rats had to be separated, cleaned, re-inked with oil and traditional ink mixture before identification. Same URL.
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (GIS Reseau Asie, Inventaire des manuscrits khmers): since 1990, under Olivier de Bernon, "l'equipe du FEMC constituee de cinq personnes, procede methodiquement a la localisation, a la restauration physique, a l'identification et a l'inventaire microfilme des manuscrits subsistant dans les bibliotheques monastiques du Cambodge." https://www.gis-reseau-asie.org/en/publication/inventaire-des-manuscrits-khmers
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (southeastasianarchaeology.com, 2024-05-06): EFEO announced publication of "Khmer Manuscripts", an online database of Khmer documentary heritage built on FEMC work. https://www.southeastasianarchaeology.com/2024/05/06/khmer-manuscripts-resource/

## 5. Current digitization (BDRC, NIU/Luce, BL)

- VERIFIED-FULL (AKBF): A Khmer Buddhist Foundation provided over USD 1 million in grants to BDRC to source, preserve, restore and digitize "nearly 1.5 million pages of palm-leaf manuscripts - the largest collection of Cambodian Buddhist palm leaf manuscripts in the world"; digitization began 2019; images public via purl.bdrc.io/resource/PR1KDPP00 and the Khmer-community UI khmer-manuscripts.bdrc.io. https://www.akhmerbuddhistfoundation.org/manuscripts
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (archive.org bdrc-khmermanuscripts collection page): "more than 1.4 million images of manuscripts from Cambodia. 6,170 manuscripts were digitized directly in the field and 5,426 manuscripts were digitized from film copies made by the Ecole francaise d'Extreme-Orient and the Fonds pour l'Edition des Manuscrits du Cambodge between 1990 and 2004." https://archive.org/details/bdrc-khmermanuscripts
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (NIU SEADL Cambodia collection page, site now behind Turnstile): Henry Luce Foundation-funded project digitized 511 palm-leaf manuscripts at the National Library of Cambodia, freely available online in 2021; surviving NLC materials included about 500 palm-leaf manuscripts written in Pali and Khmer, some Thai. https://sea.lib.niu.edu/islandora/object/seadl:cambodia
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (khmer-manuscripts.bdrc.io about-page via search snippets): BDRC launched the Khmer Manuscript Heritage Project in 2019 with Digital Divide Data, The Brechin Group Inc., FEMC, and EFEO; also notes French colonial-era institutions (Conservation d'Angkor 1907, School of Pali, National Museum, Royal Library, Buddhist Institute). https://khmer-manuscripts.bdrc.io/ and https://khmer-manuscripts.bdrc.io/static/aboutkm?uilang=en
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (ebudinst.wordpress, Buddhist Institute blog, 2010): Buddhist Institute developed manuscript scanning/database digitization from 2003, judging scan quality superior to microfilm; separate National Library of Cambodia microfilm project exists. https://ebudinst.wordpress.com/2010/12/23/conservation-of-historical-khmer-handwritten-manuscripts-by-digitization/
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (BL Asian and African studies blog, Cambodia tag): BL posts on "traditional Khmer manuscripts - palm leaves (sleuk rith) and folding books (kraing)", plus documents in Khmer script made by foreigners. https://blogs.bl.uk/.m/asian-and-african/cambodia/

## 6. Khmer Krom (Mekong Delta, southern Vietnam) temple manuscripts

- VERIFIED-FULL (VOV): Khmer palm-leaf manuscripts in southern Vietnam are made from buong palm (Corypha) leaves; a small leaf holds about five lines of roughly 20 characters each; 2017 inscription of "Knowledge and techniques of writing on palm leaves of the Khmer people" as National Intangible Cultural Heritage by Vietnam's Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism; Khmer name for the palm is T'ran; stylus with sharp metal tip called Dek-cha; engraving technique called char; letters revealed by rubbing powdered charcoal and oil. https://english.vov.vn/en/your-vietnam/khmer-palm-leaf-manuscripts-a-living-cultural-heritage-post1274038.vov
- VERIFIED-FULL (Nhan Dan via vietnam.vn): palm-leaf scriptures appeared in southern Vietnam around the mid-19th century and are "the first written heritage of Theravada Buddhism in Vietnam"; texts mostly Buddhist scriptures transcribed in Pali using ancient Khmer script, plus literature, calendars, medicine; in An Giang province about 170 sets totaling over 900 volumes across Khmer temples in Tri Ton and Tinh Bien districts; each set has 4-10 volumes, each volume 20-60 leaves; Venerable Chau Ty (abbot, Soai So/Tom Nop pagoda, Nui To, Tri Ton) is described as the only person in An Giang still mastering the carving technique; trained under Venerable Chau Rieng of Xvayton Pagoda; named Outstanding Artisan 2015 and People's Artisan 2019; An Giang heritage project runs to 2030, with Phase 2 (2028-2030) preparing a UNESCO Memory of the World Asia-Pacific registration dossier. https://www.vietnam.vn/en/bau-vat-linh-thieng-cua-nguoi-khmer (source credited: https://nhandan.vn/bau-vat-linh-thieng-cua-nguoi-khmer-post831668.html)
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (buddhaworlds.com): palm-leaf manuscripts regarded as the earliest written heritage of Theravada Buddhism in Vietnam, appearing mid-19th century. https://buddhaworlds.com/en/palm-leaf-manuscripts-a-unique-heritage-of-khmer-theravada-buddhism-d54323.html
- NOTE: none of these Vietnamese-state-media sources mention Khmer Rouge-era losses inside Vietnam; the Khmer Krom war-loss angle is a gap.

## 7. EFEO / French scholarship

- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (angkordatabase author page): Olivier de Bernon (born Paris 1957), linguist and Buddhism researcher, EFEO scientific member since 1991, author of the first catalog of Cambodian liturgical and sacred texts within the FEMC under high patronage. https://angkordatabase.asia/authors/olivier-de-bernon
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (EFEO member page, FR/EN): biography - 1984 doctorate in philosophy; 1985 INALCO cambodgien diploma; a year in Khmer and Lao refugee camps in Thailand. https://www.efeo.fr/membre/olivier-de-bernon/?lang=en
- VERIFIED-FULL (Tricycle): FEMC = Fonds pour l'Edition des Manuscrits du Cambodge, an initiative run by local Cambodians backed by EFEO; two-volume catalog link points to EFEO Publications "Inventaire provisoire des manuscrits du Cambodge, premiere partie: bibliotheques monastiques de Phnom Penh et de la province de Kandal". https://tricycle.org/article/wat-phum-thmei-texts/ ; catalog: https://publications.efeo.fr/en/livres/787_inventaire-provisoire-des-manuscrits-du-cambodge-premiere-partie-bibliotheques-monastiques-de-phnom-penh-et-de-la-province-de-kandal
- VERIFIED-SNIPPET (aefek.fr de Bernon bibliography page): Corpus de 1891 (kamrangcpap khmer bipuran), diplomatic edition directed by Olivier de Bernon and Hel Chamroeun, preface by Samdech Hun Sen, Ministry of Economy and Finance, Phnom Penh. https://aefek.fr/debernon.html
- VERIFIED-FULL (LOC): Suzanne Karpeles "helped establish the Buddhist Institutes in Phnom Penh, Cambodia and Vientiane and Luang Prabang, Laos"; her and Paul Mus's libraries reached LOC via J. Thomas Rimer donations. https://guides.loc.gov/southeast-asian-rare-books-manuscripts/khmer
