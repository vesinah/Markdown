Palm leaf scriptures are a rare and valuable type of ancient book, recording all aspects of secular life and religious beliefs. Over the centuries, these palm leaf scriptures have survived and are considered sacred "treasures" by the Khmer people, requiring preservation and promotion.

Appearing in Southern Vietnam around the mid-19th century, palm leaf scriptures are the first written heritage of Theravada Buddhism in Vietnam. According to Khmer cultural researchers, the texts written on palm leaves are mostly Buddhist scriptures transcribed in Pali (ancient Khmer script). Besides Buddhist scriptures, these texts also record content on literature, calendars, medicine, stories about social phenomena, and more.

The reason the Khmer people choose palm leaves for carving is because palm leaves are very flexible, durable, and resistant to tearing and decay. After processing, palm leaves are dried in the sun and dew for about two weeks, then dipped in boiling water or briefly heated over a fire, making them resistant to time and insect infestation.

When carving letters on palm leaves, the carver uses a special pen with a wooden handle and a sharp iron nib called a Ðék-cha. The thumb controls the pen tip to ensure a steady hand, with strokes that are neither too shallow nor too deep, and are even and straight. Carving letters on palm leaves requires meticulousness and skill; even a slight mistake can tear the leaf, requiring the work to be redone.

[Caption] Venerable Chau Ty is the only person in An Giang who possesses the technique of carving characters on palm leaves.

After carving the characters, the carver would use a cloth soaked in ink (a mixture of finely ground charcoal powder mixed with turpentine and kerosene) to brush onto the surface of the leaf, then use another cloth to wipe away the ink. The carved characters would gradually appear and would be very difficult to fade.

Writing on palm leaves is a unique and creative artistic process that requires willpower and patience. Completing a single palm leaf scripture involves many steps, considerable effort, skill, and passion for the work. Furthermore, to learn how to engrave palm leaf scriptures, one must be proficient in Pali script and the content of each type of scripture, as most palm leaf scriptures are engraved using Pali characters.

In Khmer temples in Southern Vietnam, almost every temple has palm leaf scriptures for monks, achars, and Buddhist followers to study and research. In An Giang province, there are currently about 170 sets of palm leaf scriptures, totaling over 900 volumes, scattered across several Khmer temples in the two districts of Tri Ton and Tinh Bien. Each set contains 4 to 10 volumes, each volume with 20 to 60 palm leaves, each side of the leaf having 5 lines, with approximately 150 characters.

[Caption] Palm leaf scriptures are currently preserved in many Khmer temples in An Giang province.

Currently, most palm leaf scriptures are preserved in temples using very simple methods, such as wrapping the scriptures in cloth and placing them in glass cabinets. Over the years, due to the effects of time and environmental changes, these ancient palm leaf scriptures are gradually deteriorating. More notably, the artisans and senior monks who know how to engrave palm leaf scriptures in the Khmer community of Southern Vietnam are all elderly and frail, with only a few remaining.

Venerable Chau Ty, Vice Dharma Chief of the Vietnam Buddhist Association and Abbot of Soài So Tôm Nóp Pagoda in Núi Tô commune, Tri Tôn district, is perhaps the only person in An Giang province who still possesses the technique of writing Buddhist scriptures on palm leaves.

He said that at the age of 24, he studied under Venerable Chau Rieng, the abbot of Xvayton Pagoda (Tri Ton town), on how to write scriptures on palm leaves. Thanks to his diligence, hard work, and enthusiasm for learning, after only two years, he had mastered this very special technique of writing scriptures on leaves.

Venerable Chau Ty is renowned in the region for carving the most beautiful Pali script on palm leaves, and even now, at over 83 years old, he is the only person to possess the most complete set of palm leaf scriptures. For his contributions to preserving and promoting the nation's intangible cultural heritage, Venerable Chau Ty was awarded the title of Outstanding Artisan by the President of Vietnam in 2015 and the title of People's Artisan in 2019.

Venerable Chau Ty was always deeply concerned about how to teach as many people as possible about the scriptures carved on palm leaves, how to read and engrave the characters on palm leaves, especially in the context of recent years when palm leaves have become increasingly scarce, and the art of carving scriptures on palm leaves is facing the risk of disappearing over time.

Recognizing that palm leaf scriptures not only play a key role in preserving Buddhist scriptures but also hold significant meaning in safeguarding the precious cultural traditions of the Khmer people, in 2013, the Department of Culture, Sports and Tourism of An Giang province organized a class teaching Pali script carving on palm leaves for Khmer monks and Buddhists, led by Venerable Chau Ty.

Initially, the class had 14 people, but now only one or two know how to carve, and even those are not yet proficient. Ry Thi, 36, a student of Venerable Chau Ty and a passionate researcher of writing scriptures on palm leaves, said that learning to carve on palm leaves is very difficult, and finding a supply of high-quality palm leaves is also not easy. Without genuine passion and a desire to preserve the cultural values of our ancestors, it is very difficult to learn, and to carve as beautifully as Master Chau Ty, one also needs artistic talent and dexterity.

Therefore, in the future, Khmer Theravada Buddhist schools should consider developing a program to teach reading and carving Pali script on palm leaf scriptures, with the hope of reviving the art of script carving and training a new generation of monks who can read the ancient Khmer alphabet, in order to facilitate in-depth research on ancient documents, especially those written on palm leaf scriptures of the Khmer people.

In order to preserve and pass on to future generations the unique values of the palm leaf writing tradition, An Giang province has approved the Project on Preserving and Promoting the Value of the National Intangible Cultural Heritage "Knowledge and Techniques of Writing on Palm Leaves of the Khmer People in An Giang Province" until 2030.

Specifically, the project is divided into two phases. Phase 1 (2022-2026) will involve inventorying and classifying palm leaf scriptures throughout the province according to a systematic approach. Repair and restoration of damaged palm leaf scriptures will be carried out, and guidance on preservation methods will be provided to temples currently possessing these scriptures. Following this, the province will document and digitize the heritage of "Knowledge and techniques of writing on palm leaves of the Khmer people in An Giang province".

[Phase 1 continues] ... create additional versions of some palm leaf scriptures ... translate some representative palm leaf scriptures to serve as reference materials for research and dissemination through various media channels; and incorporate them into extracurricular programs and competitions in schools about heritage.

In Phase 2 (2028-2030), the province will develop a dossier to register the recognition of the documentary heritage of knowledge and techniques of writing on palm leaves of the Khmer people in An Giang province under the UNESCO Memory of the World Programme for the Asia-Pacific region.
