## A. Modern conservation science: humidity / RH thresholds

**A1. 50% RH identified as optimal storage condition (2025 experimental study).**
Source: Zhang W., Wang S., Han L., Guo H., "Aging effects of relative humidity on palm leaf manuscripts and optimal humidity conditions for preservation", npj Heritage Science 13, Article 218 (published 27 May 2025). https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-025-01794-9 [FULLY READ; raw archived]
Quote (abstract): "The results show that exposure to extreme dry or humid conditions negatively affects the manuscripts. In dry conditions, they experience bending, cracking, reduced mechanical strength, lower hygroscopicity, and chemical degradation. In humid conditions, fungal growth compromises the manuscripts' structure... Manuscripts stored at 50% RH showed no significant damage, suggesting that this level is optimal for their preservation."
Method detail (from Methods): samples aged 200 days at constant 25 C at RH levels 10%, 30%, 50%, 70%, 90%; analysis by SEM, DVS, TMA, FT-IR, XRD, TGA, color/gloss/contact-angle measurement.

**A2. Avoid RH above 70% for long-term preservation (2024 sorption-isotherm study).**
Source: Yu D., Li X., Sun S., Guo H., Luo H., Zhu J., Li L., Wang S., Han L., "The effect of traditional processing craft on the hygroscopicity of palm leaf manuscripts", npj Heritage Science 12, Article 280 (published 5 August 2024). https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-024-01402-2 [FULLY READ; raw archived]
Quotes:
- "According to the sorption isotherms, long-term preservation of palm leaf manuscripts should avoid relative humidity above 70%. The isotherms indicate that the moisture content of the samples increases significantly above 70% RH..."
- "traditional processing slightly increases the equilibrium moisture content (EMC) in environments with 0 to 60% relative humidity (RH), but significantly lowers EMC in high humidity environments, with reductions up to 19.01%. Additionally, hysteresis doubled post-processing, indicating enhanced stability under fluctuating humidity conditions."
- "FT-IR ... indicates that the relative contents of cellulose and hemicellulose were reduced by 39.90% and 3.97%" after processing, and "decreased by 57.52% and 19.83% after nature aging".
- EMC values at RH=95%: fresh leaf 40.53% vs processed SPL1 21.52% / SPL2 25.56%.

## B. Fungal and insect threats

**B1. Aspergillus niger deterioration mechanism on PLMs.**
Source: Chu S., Lin L., Tian X., "Analysis of Aspergillus niger isolated from ancient palm leaf manuscripts and its deterioration mechanisms", Heritage Science 12, 199 (2024). Article: https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-024-01320-3 ; DOAJ record: https://doaj.org/article/ff7805da6c0748bb92b5bd29525544d7 [SERP snippets + DDG summary; full text pending Phase B]
Quote (DDG snippet of RG mirror): "The study revealed the presence of the common fungus Aspergillus niger on ancient PLMs. Aspergillus niger can secrete cellulase, lipase, and acidic substances after colonizing on the carriers of PLMs."
DOAJ snippet: PLMs "are highly susceptible to microbial degradation during prolonged storage."

**B2. Fungal community composition on historical manuscript.**
Source: "Investigate the role of fungal communities associated with a historical manuscript" (npj Materials Degradation, 2022). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41529-022-00296-4 [SERP snippet]
Quote: "the most common fungal species associated with the historical manuscript were Aspergillus spp. with percentages of 61.5% of the total obtained strains".

**B3. Mold blocking/blocking-stick damage and insect/rodent activity.**
Source: Cornell University Library, Preservation Tutorial, "Palm Leaf Manuscripts". https://chinapreservationtutorial.library.cornell.edu/content/palm-leaf-manuscripts/ [FULLY READ; raw archived]
Quotes:
- "When exposed to high humidity or severely damaged by mold, palm leaves may stick together in blocks."
- "Damage to and deterioration of palm leaves are usually the result of staining, mechanical damage, splitting and cleavage, and insect and rodent activity."
- "Palm leaf is susceptible to desiccation, losing its flexibility and becoming brittle."
- Cord/hole wear: "Damage is also caused by friction between the cord and the edge of the binding hole."

## C. Traditional storage and care practices

**C1. Aromatic oils as insect repellents (traditional ink medium).**
Source: Cornell tutorial (URL as B3). [FULLY READ]
Quote: "Incised writing is made visible by applying a mixture of lampblack, bean plant or berry juice, and aromatic oil. The oils used have included camphor, citronella, castor, lemongrass, cedarwood, mustard, neem, eucalyptus, clove, and sesame. They are chosen for their insect repellent qualities."
Also: dryness treatment - "In many cases this dryness is treated by reapplying oil, which has a darkening effect if done too often."

**C2. Regional traditional processing incl. smoking, oiling, turmeric, pond-mud burial.**
Source: van Dyke Y., "Sacred Leaves: The Conservation and Exhibition of Early Buddhist Manuscripts on Palm Leaves", Book and Paper Group Annual 28 (2009), AIC. https://cool.culturalheritage.org/coolaic/sg/bpg/annual/v28/bpga28-17.pdf [FULLY READ via proxy; raw archived]
Quote: "the palm leaves can be processed by retting, soaking, and softening; boiling in water; smoking; wafting over a charcoal fire; and air, sun, or kiln drying... In Indian states, leaves are 1) dried in the shade and rubbed with gingili oil...; 2) hung in smoke for several days, then the surface smoke deposit is wiped off, sometimes followed by rubbing with turmeric paste; or 3) dried in the sun, buried in pond mud for ten to fifteen days... In Sri Lanka, fresh, young leaves are boiled in water or lime water for a few hours and dried in the shade. In Thailand, the golden leaf from the Lopburi region is preferred... Bundles of leaves are placed in a kiln for twenty-four hours..."

**C3. Wrapping cloths, chest design, wooden covers.**
Source: van Dyke 2009 (URL as C2) + Cornell tutorial.
Quotes:
- van Dyke: "Closed bundles were traditionally wrapped in several layers of cloth and stored in chests."
- Cornell: "Each 'book,' or bundle of leaves, is usually fastened together with braided cords threaded through two holes pierced through the entire manuscript about 4 centimeters from each end or by the insertion of bamboo splints. The resultant 'binding' is finished by the addition of heavy wooden covers at the front and back, also tied by the braided cords or wrapped with webbing or a textile cloth."
- Cornell: talipot "has excellent durability, reportedly lasting as long as 600 years"; average leaf ca. 48 cm x 4 cm.

**C4. Herbal repellents literature review (India).**
Source: Nishanthi M. et al., "Traditional Conservation Methods Used to Preserve Indian Palm Leaf Manuscripts: A Literature Review" (2024). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381553177 [SERP snippet only; full text pending Phase B]
Quote (snippet): "this study revealed that various traditional plants (herbs) were used to protect the Indian palm leaf manuscripts from insects, pests, and fungi."
Related: Saraswati Mahal Library seasoning techniques incl. turmeric paste: https://www.academia.edu/33860979/ [SERP snippet]

**D2. Metropolitan Museum of Art - treatment methodology (van Dyke 2009).**
Source: URL as C2. [FULLY READ]
Key quotes:
- Rediscovery: "During the summer of 2007, a collection numbering over a thousand leaves of rare and important Indian paintings on palm leaf and paper were rediscovered during a renovation and storage relocation project within The Metropolitan Museum of Art's Asian Art Department."
- Rehydration: "Humidification and rehydration of these desiccated leaves with the use of a Gore-Tex humidification package has many benefits..." ; "Humidification times varied between twenty to forty minutes for complete relaxation of the leaves".
- Consolidant testing: gelatin 1-2%, methyl cellulose 1-7%, isinglass 1-3% w/v; "Gelatin and methyl celluloses... proved unacceptable, visually creating tidelines and a glossy surface sheen"; chosen: "Consolidation of the paint layer was carried out with a warm solution of 1% brush-applied isinglass"; leaf consolidation 2-3% (up to 3-4%); reinforcement with toned Japanese tengujo/tenjugo paper attached with 2-3% isinglass.
- Ethics: "all agreed to an approach not to fill losses"; surface accretions "left alone and respected as evidence of their use and history" except insect deposits removed mechanically.
- Pigment ID: "Blacks were identified as carbon-based, flame carbon; blue was indigo; yellow was orpiment; red was vermilion; and white was calcium carbonate."

## F. Exhibition handling standards

**F1. Lux guidance (general sensitive objects).**
- CCAHA: "The standard recommendation for illuminance of sensitive objects is 50-100 lux." https://ccaha.org/resources/light-exposure-artifacts-exhibition [SERP snippet]
- NEDCC Preservation 101: "If originals must be exhibited, light levels should be no higher than 50 lux for very sensitive materials". https://www.nedcc.org/preservation101/session-3/3exhibits [SERP snippet]
- LOC limiting light damage overview: https://www.loc.gov/preservation/care/light.html [SERP snippet]

**F2. Palm-leaf-specific exhibition practice (Met).**
Source: van Dyke 2009. [FULLY READ]
Quote: "They were presented in the low and atmospheric lighting of 3-5 footcandles (30-50 lux). Simple, profiled wooden frames... outfitted with Optium glazing (a non-glare, anti-static, UV filtering, Plexiglas sheeting)". Storage after rotation: "unframed, interleaved, and stored in a standard-size Solander boxes"; un-exhibited folios "housed between two rectangular boards of acid-free corrugated board... wrapped, and tied with cotton twill cordage".
