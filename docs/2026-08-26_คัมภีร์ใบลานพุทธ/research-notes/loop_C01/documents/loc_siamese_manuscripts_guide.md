KEY EXCERPTS:
1. "There are twenty-eight Siamese manuscripts and an early printed book in the collection"
2. Khom norms: "several manuscripts were written in the Thai Khǭm script, which is closely related to the Khmer script... Thai Khǭm script was borrowed from Cambodia and used to write Buddhist texts. None of the manuscripts in the collection are written in the thick-lettered ornamental style of Thai Khǭm script typical of the eighteenth century, but are instead written in the thin-lettered Khǭm style, thus placing these texts firmly in the nineteenth century or later [citing Ginsburg, Thai Manuscript Painting, 1989, 11]. Thai Khǭm was also used for astrological and yantra texts, as this script was viewed as ancient and so it was an appropriate vehicle for material that was spiritually potent."
3. Material split (central Thailand): "nearly all these manuscripts are folding or concertina style manuscripts with paper made from the inner bark of the khoi (streblus asper) bush... Siamese palm leaf manuscripts were usually reserved for Buddhist texts often in Pali and that is true of the manuscripts in this part of the collection... Folding manuscripts on the other hand have been used for a variety of topics from government records to literature to astrology and medical texts."
4. Central-Thai PALM LEAF items w/ line counts (Buddhism tab): Phrakangkhā Witrani, 4 vol., 57 x 5 cm, palm leaf mss., 5 lines, Pāli in Thai Khǭm script; Phra Milinthapanhā, 517 ff./17 vol., 53 x 4 cm, palm leaf mss., 5 lines, Pāli in Thai Khǭm script.
