## 1. PLMP — Preservation of Lao Manuscripts Programme (German-funded)

- VERIFIED — "The Preservation of Lao Manuscripts Programme (PLMP) of the Lao Ministry of Information & Culture was supported by the German Ministry of Foreign Affairs through its cultural assistance programme from 1992 until 2004." Objectives included survey and in situ preservation in "all 17 provinces", microfilming, revitalisation of traditional literature, databases/textbooks/software, capacity building.
  URL: https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-lao-manuscripts-about/?lang=en (DLLM Background)
- VERIFIED — "Over the course of ten years until the cooperation project officially ended in December 2002, the manuscript holdings of over 800 monasteries had been surveyed, and approximately 86,000 texts (368,000 fascicles) preserved."
  URL: https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-lao-manuscripts-about/?lang=en
- VERIFIED — Microfilm collection "of some 12,000 selected texts ... comprises almost 500,000 frames, which on average contain about 6-8 palm-leaf pages, giving a total of some 3-4 million recorded manuscript pages." Master copy at Lao National Film Archive and Video Centre; working copy National Library of Laos; second copy Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin. Microfilming began Vientiane April 1994 with a Zeutschel OK102 camera on 35mm rolls.
  URL: https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-lao-manuscripts-about/?lang=en ; corroborated by Hundius & Wharton JLS 2011: https://www.laostudies.org/system/files/subscription/JLS-v2-i2-Nov2011-hundius-wharton.pdf
- VERIFIED — Follow-up phase Jan 2003–Dec 2004 set up provincial Manuscript Preservation Centres in monasteries in Luang Prabang, Vientiane, Savannakhet, Campasak provinces; they hold annual bun bai lan manuscript festivals ("imported" from the Lan Na PNTMP project, Chiang Mai University).
  URL: https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-lao-manuscripts-about/?lang=en
- VERIFIED — Outputs: 22 quarterly Lao-language newsletters Khao bai lan ("Palm-leaf News"), 14 booklets; programme nominated by German Government as contribution to UNESCO World Decade of Cultural Development (1987-1997); awarded gold medal at Expo 2000 Hannover; final international conference "The Literary Heritage of Laos" Vientiane Jan 2004 (>120 participants; papers published Nov 2005).
  URL: https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-lao-manuscripts-about/?lang=en
- VERIFIED — No material removed from sites during PLMP: "Throughout the programme, no materials, including the most damaged or decayed palm-leaves, wooden covers, manuscript wrappings and storage chests, or any other local artefacts, were removed from their original sites."
  URL: https://www.laostudies.org/system/files/subscription/JLS-v2-i2-Nov2011-hundius-wharton.pdf
- VERIFIED — Context/limits: "texts within the DLLM collection are in fact from only 380 locations"; footnote: Lao Buddhist Fellowship statistics for 2005-2006 count "3,656 monasteries with residing monks and 826 deserted monasteries".
  URL: https://www.laostudies.org/system/files/subscription/JLS-v2-i2-Nov2011-hundius-wharton.pdf

## 2. Pre-PLMP surveys / temple inventories before and after the war

- VERIFIED — Early inventories 1900–1973 by French/Lao scholars list "a total of 3,678 manuscripts from 94 monasteries in nine provinces". Finot 1917 Liste générale: Bibliothèque Royale de Luang Prabang (catalogued by M. Meiller, 1181 entries) + EFEO (338 entries). Chanthabouly Buddhist Council under Chao Phetsarat asked abbots nationwide to submit holdings lists 1934–36. Lafont's Inventaire des Manuscrits des Pagodes du Laos (1959) covered 83 monasteries: 13 Luang Prabang, 25 Vientiane, 45 Campasak.
  URL: https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-lao-manuscripts-about/?lang=en ; same text in JLS 2011 PDF above.
- VERIFIED — Wartime/1975 rupture: "During the Second Indochina War and the years immediately following the proclamation of the Lao PDR in 1975, the country met with extremely difficult conditions"; awareness returned mid-1980s; March 1988 Toyota Foundation-supported conference led to Inventory of Palm-leaf Manuscripts in Six Provinces (1988–1994): "about 128,000 fascicles were inventoried from some 250 selected monasteries in Vientiane Capital and the provinces of Luang Prabang, Vientiane, Bolikhamsai, Khammuan, Savannakhet, and Campasak."
  URL: https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-lao-manuscripts-about/?lang=en
- VERIFIED (post-war transfer case) — Grabowsky 2019: the Vat Si Bun Hüang corpus was transferred from Vat Si Mungkhun's library "at some time between 1976 and circa 1990", plausibly for safety when the library roof leaked and the monastery lacked an abbot — direct evidence of civil-war/early-LPDR disruption to temple repositories.
  URL: https://thesiamsociety.org/knowledge-hub/uploads/research/76/663c83092f582.pdf

## 3. DLLM — Digital Library of Lao Manuscripts

- VERIFIED — "The Digital Library of Lao Manuscripts builds on this previous work by providing detailed searches of inventory data for almost 12,000 manuscripts... unique in Southeast Asia in providing online access to such a large number and variety of primary literary sources." Implemented by National Library of Laos + University of Passau + Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin; funded by the German Research Foundation (DFG) and German Federal Ministry of Economic Cooperation and Development; Hundius Senior Advisor PLMP 1992-2004 and DLLM Project Supervisor 2007-2011.
  URL: https://www.laostudies.org/system/files/subscription/JLS-v2-i2-Nov2011-hundius-wharton.pdf
- SNIPPET — Current platform states: "makes images of about 13,000 texts from throughout Laos accessible for study" (iiif.crossasia.org); DLLM landing page says "over 12,000 texts"; laomanuscripts.net now redirects into CrossAsia infrastructure.
  URLs: https://iiif.crossasia.org/s/dllm ; https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-lao-manuscripts/?lang=en ; KPL 2020 (https://kpl.gov.la/En/Detail.aspx?id=56981) says "over 12,000 manuscript texts".
- VERIFIED — Collection profile: over 900 monolingual Pali texts; almost 75% bilingual Pali-vernacular; oldest dated DLLM manuscript = Parivāra, Tham Lao script, CS 882 = CE 1520, PLMP code 06018504078_00, Provincial Museum Luang Prabang.
  URL: https://www.laostudies.org/system/files/subscription/JLS-v2-i2-Nov2011-hundius-wharton.pdf
- VERIFIED — Inventory based on handwritten Lao-language PLMP datasheets produced over 10+ years; each text has unique 13-digit PLMP code (province-district-monastery-category-bundle + text no.); searchable fields include colophon yes/no and illustrated yes/no.
  URL: https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-lao-manuscripts-about-dllm-collection/?lang=en
- VERIFIED — Post-DLLM continuation: Dec 2020 National Library project "Documentation of Lao Traditional Recitation" (June–Dec 2020, supported by German Embassy Vientiane) added >150 audio/video recordings via DLLM and a YouTube channel.
  URL: https://kpl.gov.la/En/Detail.aspx?id=56981

## 4. Scripts and materials (Tham Lao vs Lao Buhan; nangsü bai lan)

- VERIFIED — "In Laos, the Tham script was generally reserved for religious writings, whereas texts which were considered secular were written in Lao Buhan, the precursor of the modern Lao script. A considerable number of works with identical titles are found in both Tham and Lao Buhan scripts." Oldest dated Tham-script document overall: bilingual gold-folio inscription, Sukhothai, CE 1376; oldest known dated Tham-script manuscript: Wat Lai Hin, Lampang, CS 833/CE 1471; Tham adapted for vernaculars not later than 15th c., most probably in Chiang Mai; 'Tham Script Domain' spans Laos, Upper North/Northeast Thailand, NE Myanmar, SW Yunnan.
  URL: https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-lao-manuscripts-resources-script/?lang=en
- VERIFIED — Codicology: typical Lao palm-leaf folio (bai lan) 45-55 cm x 4-5 cm; narrower lan kom 20-25 cm; iron stylus lek can; soot; yang-tree oil; phuk fascicle = avg 6-12 folios; bundle = mat; sa/khoi paper books also present.
  URL: https://digital.crossasia.org/digital-library-of-lao-manuscripts-about-dllm-collection/?lang=en
- VERIFIED — Grabowsky 2019: "Based on the data collected by the earlier mentioned Programme for the Preservation of Lao Manuscripts, it can be estimated that more than ninety percent of Lao manuscripts are palm-leaf manuscripts (nangsü bai lan)". Terminology sum/mat/phuk; blackening agent nam man nyang (Dipterocarpus wood oil); Dhamma script originated Lan Na ~14th c., reached Laos second half of 15th c.
  URL: https://thesiamsociety.org/knowledge-hub/uploads/research/76/663c83092f582.pdf

## 5. Luang Prabang monastic corpora

- VERIFIED (re-fetch as instructed) — CSMC RFH07 "Colophons in Lao Manuscripts from Luang Prabang: Exploring Production, Uses, and Interaction Between Sangha and Laity" (2023–2025, lead Silpsupa Jaengsawang): "The manuscript research corpus includes circa 10,200 manuscripts from four collections", produced between mid-16th century and present; >90% Buddhist texts; earliest dated = Parivāra 1520 CE.
  URL: https://www.csmc.uni-hamburg.de/uwa-i/cluster-projects/field-h/rfh07.html
- VERIFIED — Grabowsky 2019: "A total of thirty-three monasteries in a town of less than 50,000 inhabitants" in UNESCO World Heritage (since 1995) Luang Prabang; March 2016 discovery of >800 palm-leaf fascicles at Vat Si Bun Hüang; collection = 541 manuscripts / 861 fascicles (353 dated), spanning 320 years (oldest secure: Nagarakhaṇḍa fragment finished Thu 5 Sep 1799; possible 1783 and 1662 items; latest 1982); genre distribution: Jataka 291 fascicles (33.8%), Anisong 123 (14.3%), Suttanta 109 (12.7%), Vinaya 74 (8.6%); corpus originally the hò tai (library) of Vat Si Mungkhun, built up mainly by Somdet Rattanapanya/Sathu Phò Hung (1859–1945) and Sathu Phò Oun Hueane Rattanapanyo (1869–1955). Digitised under British Library EAP691 ("Rare manuscripts of great Buddhist thinkers of Laos", Feb 2015–Jul 2016); >35,000 historic photographs from 19 monasteries under EAP326.
  URL: https://thesiamsociety.org/knowledge-hub/uploads/research/76/663c83092f582.pdf
- SNIPPET — DREAMSEA digitisation at Vat Maha That Rasabovoravihan, Luang Prabang, Jan–May 2019: headline "Preserving 12,743 Palm Leaf Manuscripts in Luang Prabang"; body: "at least 12,700 palm-leaf manuscript pages"; includes Maha Nithan Phanya Chüang (CS 1154 = 5 July 1792); abbot Pha Phui Thirachitta Maha Thera (1925–2005) scribe of ≥64 manuscripts; mission led by Dr Khamvone Boulyaphonh (Director, Buddhist Archives since Sept 2018). NOTE: headline counts "manuscripts", body says "pages" — discrepancy unresolved.
  URL: https://dreamsea.co/preserving-12743-palm-leaf-manuscripts-in-luang-prabang/
- SNIPPET — Related DREAMSEA item: "Preserving 8,775 Palm Leaf Manuscripts in Lao PDR"; manuscripts sponsored by Kings/queens/aristocrats, scripts Tham Lao, Lao Buhan, Modern Lao.
  URL: https://dreamsea.co/preserving-8775-palm-leaf-manuscripts-in-lao-pdr/

## 6. Colophon networks (Grabowsky studies)

- VERIFIED — CSMC RFH07: colophons reflect sponsors' wishes (enlightenment; merit dedication to dead relatives; devotion to Buddhism; avoiding sickness/danger) and Sangha-laity collaboration; commissioning person identifiable only through colophons; multilingual/multiscriptual colophons (Dhamma script, modern Lao/Thai, Roman; Lao/Tai Lü/Pali/Thai/English/French).
  URL: https://www.csmc.uni-hamburg.de/uwa-i/cluster-projects/field-h/rfh07.html
- VERIFIED — Grabowsky 2019 case study: long colophon of BAD-21-1-0567 (CS 1280 = 15 Oct 1918) names sponsor Sathu Phò Hung, dedicates merit to parents, two wives, children; conclusion notes "relatively high percentage of women serving as principal lay supporters or the presence of royalty among the sponsors and donors".
  URL: https://thesiamsociety.org/knowledge-hub/uploads/research/76/663c83092f582.pdf
- SNIPPET — Grabowsky chapter "The Grammar and Function of Colophons in Lao Manuscripts: The Case of the Vat Maha That Collection, Luang Prabang", in The Syntax of Colophons (SMC 27, De Gruyter), pp. 229ff, DOI 10.1515/9783110795271-008; abstract: colophons almost exclusively vernacular Lao with short standardized Pali phrases; emphasis on scribes and sponsors; elaborate lunar-calendar dating.
  URL: https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110795271-008/html ; RG fulltext: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366457406 (direct download 403-blocked this session)
- SNIPPET — Grabowsky & colleagues, Tai Lue colophons/inscriptions note (Aséanie 33, 2014): corpus of 25 Tai Lue inscriptions from >160 pedestal inscriptions collected 1998-2005 plus 25 manuscript colophons, Mueang Sing area.
  URL: https://www.persee.fr/doc/asean_0859-9009_2014_num_33_1_2319
