## A. British Library Kharoṣṭhī scrolls (1994) + EBMP

### F1. การจัดซื้อปี 1994 และลักษณะชุดวัสดุ
- "In 1994, the British Library Oriental and India Office Collections acquired a collection of twenty-nine fragments of manuscripts written on birch bark scrolls in the Gāndhārī (a dialect of Prakrit) language and in the Kharoṣṭhī script."
- "They were contained inside a clay pot, also bearing an inscription in the same language, in which they had been buried in antiquity."
- "Preliminary analysis... dated from about the first century A.D., which would make them the oldest surviving substantial collection of Buddhist manuscripts, as well as of any kind of Indian manuscripts."
- 29 fragments = "portions of twenty or more originally separate scrolls, ranging in size from a few words to several hundred lines"
- Source: UW EBMP, The British Library Kharoṣṭhī Fragments page [VERIFIED-PAGE]

### F2. หลักฐานหาแหล่งที่มา (provenance) และการให้อายุ
- "The exact findspot of these manuscripts is unfortunately unknown. But in the past several manuscripts of the same type have been reported to have been found in or around Haḍḍa near Jalalabad in eastern Afghanistan... It is therefore likely that the new manuscripts came from the same region."
- อายุจากบุคคลประวัติศาสตร์: "references in two separate texts of the avadāna class to the Great Satrap Jihonika and to the Commander Aśpavarma"; แจกันจารชื่อ "Suhasoma and his wife Vasavadatta"
- Source: UW BL Kharoṣṭhī Fragments page [VERIFIED-PAGE]
- เชิงเทียบ: Salomon บทความ Lion's Roar เรียกวัสดุชุดแรกว่า "twenty-eight fragile birch bark scrolls" ที่ "predate the earliest Pali manuscripts by several centuries" — ตัวเลขต่างจาก UW (27 ม้วน / 29 fragments) → ใช้เลขของแหล่งสถาบันเป็นหลัก [VERIFIED-PAGE]

## E. ทำไม birch bark แทน palm leaf ในคันธาระ (climate rationale)

### F10. Monsoon boundary + ritual burial
- "The fact that extensive remnants have come to light in Gandhara is no coincidence but rather a result of particular climatic and cultural factors. Gandhara lies beyond the central monsoon zone, whose extremes of heat and humidity prevent the longterm survival of organic materials such as birch bark or palm leaf. Additionally, the Buddhists of ancient Gandhara had a practice of ritually interring their manuscripts in clay pots or other containers in the precincts of their monasteries, further promoting their preservation. It was likely due to these incidental factors that the oldest known Buddhist manuscripts were found in Gandhara, and not because such manuscripts were unique to the region. Similar texts must have existed elsewhere—perhaps everywhere—in the Buddhist cultures of the Indian heartland, but there is virtually no chance such manuscripts would have survived the deleterious effects of the monsoon climate."
- Author: Richard Salomon ("director of the University of Washington Early Buddhist Manuscripts Project and general editor of the Gandhāran Buddhist Texts series")
- Source: Lion's Roar, Salomon article [VERIFIED-PAGE]
- หมายเหตุ: แหล่งที่ fetch ไม่ได้ให้ statement ตรง ๆ ว่า "palm trees unavailable in Gandhara hence birch bark substituted"; สิ่งที่ยืนยันได้คือ monsoon/humidity ทำลายทั้ง birch bark และ palm leaf นอกโซนคันธาระ + การฝังใน pots ช่วยการเก็บรักษา → "ยังไม่พบข้อมูลยืนยัน" สำหรับ claim เรื่อง availability ของต้นปาล์มในท้องถิ่นโดยตรง

## F. Gilgit manuscripts (1931)

### F11. การค้นที่ Naupur
- "Credit goes to him [Sir Aurel Stein] for announcing the discovery of the famous Buddhist manuscripts from Naupur in Gilgit in 1931."
- "By 1931, Gilgit came to prominence as a famous archaeological site. It was due to the discovery of the world's oldest Buddhist manuscripts from Naupur that brought Gilgit to the centre stage... followed a systematic excavation of the Naupur site and discovery of another set of the Gilgit Manuscripts by M.K. Shastri in 1938."
- Tucci quote: "The discovery of Buddhist texts in Gilgit… indicates the presence of learned communities in Gilgit and Hunza."
- Source: The News on Sunday via Buddhist Art News (2015) [VERIFIED-PAGE]

### F12. เรื่องเล่าฉบับละเอียด: shepherd discovery + 1948 airlift to Delhi
- "Written on birch bark, the Manuscripts were discovered per chance by a shepherd at Naupur, a village in Gilgit, in 1931. He was grazing his sheep when he stumbled over the treasure which lay in several half-buried wooden boxes... The first news about the Gilgit Manuscripts was broken by the Statesman on 24 July 1931."
- "Dating back to 5th-6th century AD, the Gilgit Manuscripts hold 'the key to the exact evolution of Sanskrit, Buddhist, Chinese, Korean, Japanese and Tibetan literature' [SALT, April-June 2015]"
- 1948 transfer: "the transfer, on the pretext of safety, of the Gilgit Manuscripts from Srinagar to Delhi in 1948. As armed conflict was going on... Jawaharlal Nehru... convinced Sheikh Mohammad Abdullah... He sent a special plane to take the manuscripts out of Kashmir, only never to be returned."; RTI to National Archives of India unanswered since 20 November 2017; former National Museum director: "they are still kept in the National Museum"
- Research Library Hazratbal: 212 Sanskrit mss sent to National Archives New Delhi 1948, not returned
- บริบท Kashmir birch bark: Georg Buhler 1875 collected "more than 300 manuscripts, many of them in Sharda script written on birch bark leaves"; Stein deposited 350 mss with Bodleian 1911
