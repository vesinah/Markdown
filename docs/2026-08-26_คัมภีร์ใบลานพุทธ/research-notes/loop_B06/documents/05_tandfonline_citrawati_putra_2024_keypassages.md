## ABSTRACT (verbatim)
"Lontar manuscripts are an important part of Balinese cultural heritage, recording ancestral knowledge and spiritual practices. The majority of Balinese homes contain this traditional wealth. Nevertheless, many of them are damaged. ... This project has successfully collected Lontar identities as information in Wikidata and integrated them with the front and end page images and transcriptions in WikiCommons. WikiPustaka, a Wikisource platform that offers Balinese manuscripts and books, makes all material available."

## Introduction - epigraphy and early history (verbatim)
"The earliest reference to the word Lontar was discovered in the Trunyan A II inscription dated 971 saka. This is the same term as _ripta_, which refers to Lontar word. According to scholars, the Balinese community has been writing with Lontars for a long time, dating back to the 9th century. The inscription documents the people's requests to the king to have a damaged Lontar's inscription rewritten and to recreate it on copper (Ardika & Beratha, 1996)."

"The government established the foundation Kirtya in 1892 in an attempt to protect Lontar due to the enthusiastic buyers of Lontar that the Balinese were offering for sale (Hooykaas, 1979, p. 348)."

[Note by agent: this 1892 date for "foundation Kirtya" differs from Gedong Kirtya's commonly cited 1928 founding; kept verbatim with citation, flagged for Phase C verification.]

## Sacredness / reading taboo (verbatim)
"There is a very typical word at the beginning of the Lontars that demands further explanation, notably _aywa wera, aja wera,_ or _haywa wera_, which means don't spread it. According to in the Lontar quotation Tegesing Wisik Warak, mentioned that 'mwaḥ tgĕsiŋ wisik: hiki,o utama tmĕn·,haraŋ traŋ heŋ wwaŋ ṅawruhi, haywa wera,haywa cawuḥ,piṅitĕng juga iki.' To this day, the phrase Haywa wera is taken as a restriction on reading the Lontar and its contents since doing so will result in something horrible, such as ending up insane."

"On that special day, members of the Balinese community who own Lontars carry out ceremonies and propose special offerings to their Lontars." [day = commemoration of descent of knowledge from Goddess Sarasvati]

## Production (verbatim, condensed quotes)
"Lontars are made from the leaves of lontar trees (_Borassus Flabellifer_) ... In the Balinese language, these leaves are named '_rontal_'."
"...boiling the staple materials using specific spices, such as _gambir_ (_Uncarial_), candlenut (_Aleurites moluccana_...), _jebugarum_/nutmeg..., clove..., masoi..., pepper..., and additional materials, e.g. ... coconut tree barks and blackboard (_Alstonia scholaris_) tree barks. Each Lontar maker uses slightly different material compositions, espousing their family recipe."
"Writing on a sheet of Lontar entails a specific writing tool called pengrupak, a special-shaped knife used with a particular technique. ... the scratches are blackened with candlenuts, which are burned to char them."

## Classification into seven types (verbatim list)
"(1) Lontar Weda ...; (2) Lontar Agama ...; (3) Lontar Wariga ...; (4) Lontar Itihasa, containing traditional songs broadly known in Java and Bali (termed kakawin, kidung, or gaguritan); (5) Lontar Babad ...; (6) Lontar Tantri ...; (7) Lontar Lelampahan ..." (Purnama, 2020, p. 11)

## Corpus size (verbatim)
"There is believed to be a balanced distribution of Lontar manuscripts throughout Bali, particularly those in community collections. This is based on data collected by Penyuluh Bahasa Bali as early as 2016-2018, when they managed to record 25,106 Lontars across Bali (Ardiyasa, 2021, p. 81)."
"As previously mentioned, Lontars owned by the Balinese community numbered more than 25,000 cakep. These Lontars were evenly distributed in all Bali districts, which have eight districts and one municipality. The number did not include Lontars belonging to government, campus, and school libraries."

## WikiLontar 2021 outcomes (verbatim)
"This project rescued 606 Balinese community-owned Lontars from more severe damages. This project was undertaken in four districts in Bali: Klungkung, Gianyar, Buleleng and Denpasar."
"[T]he total number of Lontars documented and compiled for their metadata was 806 cakep of Lontars. This number was considered large for a project handled by 13 volunteers from Komunitas Wikipedia Denpasar, financially supported by the Wikimedia Foundation."
"Conserved and recorded Lontars were aged more than 100 years old on average."
"...the 161 red dots exhibit the titles of Lontar collection belonging to Sugi Lanus in Buleleng, who, in so doing, was identified as having 161 Lontars..."

## Kakawin transmission - Majapahit inheritance (verbatim; KEY PASSAGE for A16 gap 2)
"Following the Balinese literacy tradition, copying Lontar texts had been carried out for hundreds of years. This tradition sustained the viability of Ancient Javanese traditions in Bali. Bali, inheriting the traditions of Majapahit after its downfall at the end of the fourteenth Century, endeavoured to save the traditions, especially literature in the Ancient Javanese language. The tradition resulted in literary works from the Ancient Javanese era in Java to be found in Bali as Lontars as references for conducting ceremonies among the Balinese community. This project found a particular type of Lontars, i.e. Kakawin, e.g. Kakawin Bratha Yudha from Gria Kelodan Sawan (Buleleng), Jro Mangku Tojan (Klungkung), and Jro Mangku Sukawati (Gianyar)."

## Storage taxonomy (verbatim)
"In Balinese civilisation, there are three varieties of Lontar. Lontar cakepan, embat-embatan, and Lontar kropakan." + definitions: cakepan = wooden/bamboo covers ("Cakep means lid"); embat-embatan = sticky palm-frond draft leaves with one hole; kropakan = "a Lontar Cakepan kept in a wooden box".

## Conservation protocol (verbatim quotes)
"the first phase consisting of performing a ceremony to ask permission from the ancestors of Lontar owners."
"Some owners prefer burning them and spreading the ash around family temples." [unrestorable lontars]
"applying conservation liquid consisting of a mixture of citronella or clove oil with alcohol 95%."
"Lontars with faded and unreadable writings were re-blackened using candlenut charcoals."
"One volume of Lontars, which could comprise more than 50 sheets of Lontars, should be cleaned per sheet."
