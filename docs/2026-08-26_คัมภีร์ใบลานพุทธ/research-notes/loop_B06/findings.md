## 2. Sutasoma Bali survival - confirmed beyond Wikipedia

- VERIFIED (Palm Leaf Wiki item page, full read; raw copy archived at documents/03_palmleaf_org_kakawin_sutasoma_raw.md): a Balinese lontar of the Kakawin Sutasoma held under "Perpustakaan KTR Dokbud Bali Prop." with shelf data "[Perpustakaan KTR Dokbud Bali Prop. Page includes page-images and auto-transliteration of opening manggala verses invoking "śrī bajrājñāna śūnyātmaka paramasira... sangkaring boddhacītta" (Buddhist formulae) and narrative of Śrī Sutasoma as royal incarnation.
  URL: https://palmleaf.org/wiki/kakawin-sutasoma ; mirrored scans: https://archive.org/details/kakawin-sutasoma
- VERIFIED (British Library EAP280 project page, full read): "enabling scholars to study it from the perspective of a significantly earlier tradition than that of Bali, the island where 90% of the extant Old Javanese texts have been transmitted."
  URL: https://eap.bl.uk/project/EAP280
- VERIFIED (Citrawati & Putra 2024, New Review of Hypermedia and Multimedia, full text read): "Following the Balinese literacy tradition, copying Lontar texts had been carried out for hundreds of years. This tradition sustained the viability of Ancient Javanese traditions in Bali. Bali, inheriting the traditions of Majapahit after its downfall at the end of the fourteenth Century, endeavoured to save the traditions, especially literature in the Ancient Javanese language. The tradition resulted in literary works from the Ancient Javanese era in Java to be found in Bali as Lontars as references for conducting ceremonies among the Balinese community."

## 3. EAP projects - scope correction and verification (major correction to A16)

### EAP280 is NOT a Bali lontar project
- VERIFIED (full read): EAP280 = "Retrieving heritage: rare Old Javanese and Old Sundanese manuscripts from West Java (stage one)". Focus: "endangered collections of rare Old Javanese and Old Sundanese palm-leaf manuscripts, dating back to the pre-Islamic period of the 16th century and earlier". Two focal areas: (1) Garut regency kabuyutan collection: "approximately 30 fragile palm-leaf manuscripts that have miraculously survived since they were written at least five centuries ago", kept "inside three wooden boxes stored in a plain wooden hut, watched over by a keeper, who is in charge of periodically handling them for ritual purposes"; materials "Borassus Flabellifer, Nipa Fructicans"; languages "Old Sundanese, Old Javanese, Sanskrit"; contents "Hindu-Buddhist religious texts, epics". (2) private collections Garut/Cirebon.
- Outcomes (VERIFIED, same page): "The whole manuscript collection of Ciburuy has been digitally photographed"; surveys in Garut, Cikadu, Cirebon; "One previously undocumented palm-leaf manuscript has been digitised"; survey report PDF: https://eap.bl.uk/downloads/eap280_survey.pdf ; copies deposited with Perpustakaan Nasional RI, Universitas Padjadjaran, Pusat Studi Sunda (Bandung), British Library. Catalogued as "EAP280/1 Manuscript collection of the Situs Kabuyutan Ciburuy [14th century-17th century]" and "EAP280/2 Sample manuscript from the collection of Mr Kartani, Cirebon".
  URL: https://eap.bl.uk/project/EAP280

### The actual Bali/Lombok lontar EAP project is EAP1241
- VERIFIED (project page, full read): title "Survey, Preservation and Digitisation of Palm-leaf Manuscripts (lontar) in Private Collections of Bali and Lombok" (award year 2019 per eap.bl.uk search filter).
  URLs: https://eap.bl.uk/project/EAP1241 ; https://eap.bl.uk/search?f[0]=award_year:2019
- Numbers (VERIFIED quotes, outcomes section): "digitization and cataloguing of about 100 lontar manuscripts of the private collection of Balinese man of letters Ida Dewa Gede Catra, which until 2021 were stored in the premises of the Museum Pustaka Lontar Dukuh Penaban (Karangasem, East Bali...)"; "surveyed 9 private collections of manuscripts in Bali and 5 collections in Lombok"; "29 manuscripts from the Balinese collections and 7 manuscripts from the collections in Lombok were digitised and catalogued"; training of a Balinese team for independent digitisation/preservation/cataloguing. Project archive listing shows "(155)" archive files viewable.
- Sub-collections (VERIFIED list): EAP1241/1 Ida Dewa Gede Catra (1800-2022); /2 Drena, Selat Bangli; /3 Griya Darma Manuaba family; /4 Dewa Putu Semara Yana; /5 Ida Pedanda Gede Oka Kemenuh; /6 Ida Bagus Heri Juniawan (18th-20th c.); /7 Jro Mangku Made Sura Arthayana; /8 priestess Sang Mpu Istri Pramodawarddhanī; /9 Jero Tengah family; /10 I Wayan Gede Suryakusuma.

## 6. Ritual treatment and conservation of lontar (new verified detail)

All from Citrawati & Putra 2024 full text (URL above):
- Sacredness/restrictions: opening formula "aywa wera, aja wera, or haywa wera, which means don't spread it"; quoted lontar Tegesing Wisik Warak: "...haywa wera,haywa cawuḥ,piṅitĕng juga iki" - reading taboo backed by fear of insanity; owners "maintain them without having the courage to read them".
- Saraswati rites: community designates "a special day commemorating the descent of knowledge from the Goddess Sarasvati"; lontar owners "carry out ceremonies and propose special offerings to their Lontars".
- Handling protocol in WikiLontar 2021 rescue: first "a ceremony to ask permission from the ancestors of Lontar owners" before boxes may be opened; cleaning one-way sweeping; conservation liquid "mixture of citronella or clove oil with alcohol 95%"; faded writing "re-blackened using candlenut charcoals".
- End-of-life ritual: some owners burn unrestorable lontars and spread ash around family temples.
- Physical taxonomy (same source): three storage varieties - cakepan (wood/bamboo covers), embat-embatan (palm-frond draft leaves), kropakan (cakepan kept in wooden box).
- Production (same source): boiling leaves with gambir, candlenut, nutmeg, clove, masoi, pepper plus tree barks per family recipe; pressing 6-12 months.
- Earliest epigraphic mention (same source, citing Ardika & Beratha 1996): "The earliest reference to the word Lontar was discovered in the Trunyan A II inscription dated 971 saka"; inscription records villagers asking the king to have a damaged lontar inscription rewritten onto copper; scholars date Balinese lontar writing back to the 9th century.
