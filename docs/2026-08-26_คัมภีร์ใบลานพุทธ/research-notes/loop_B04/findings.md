## A. Thai-Mon palm-leaf collections in Thailand (closes A06 gap on "Mon" as collection language)

### F1. BL Endangered Archives Programme EAP1123 (pilot, 2018) — survey of Mon temple collections
- "The Mons of Thailand and Burma were regional cultural and religious intermediaries and supported a palm leaf manuscript tradition into the 1920s. No official Thai body has ever digitized these manuscripts. Among them are texts unknown in Burma..."
- Outcomes: Jan 6–Mar 5, 2019 team gathered names of 28 Mon temples/collections around Bangkok; 25 visited; 6 temples had no (or no longer had) Mon-language manuscripts; survey PDF produced
- Six collections catalogued: EAP1123/1 Wat Cet Riw วัดเจ็ดริ้ว; /2 Wat Sirimongkhon วัดสิริมงคล ဘာသိရိမင်္ဂလ; /3 Wat Saladaeng Neua วัดศาลาแดงเหนือ ဘာပြာန်; /4 Wat Metārāng วัดเมตารางค์; /5 Wat Makhām Ratburi วัดมะขาม ဘာမင်ဂၠန်; /6 Wat Makhām Pathumthānī — dates ranges late 19th–mid 20th c.
- Grant: Dr Patrick McCormick; host SEA Junction; archival partner Sirindhorn Anthropology Centre; award £11,463; DOI 10.15130/EAP1123; bundle anatomy stated: ~26 "faces" per bundle typical, secular up to 40–80 faces [VERIFIED-PAGE]

### F2. EAP1123/2 Wat Sirimongkhon (Samut Sakhon) catalogue record — bilingual Pali-Mon nissaya confirmed in situ
- Scope: "Most of the collection is the usual mixture of Mon and Pāḷi-language religious texts, including religious commentary, bilingual Pali-Mon nissaya texts, texts on the vinaya and abidhamma."
- Languages: Mon, Pali; Scripts: Burmese(!); Date range 1860–1970; extent 242 TIFF images
- Sub-series include: Kammathan (meditation), Pagoda History, Five Rich Men, Dreams of King Kosala, Milinda and Nāgasena Questions and Answers, unknown texts, random handwriting
- Copies also held by: Sirindhorn Anthropology Centre (Bangkok); **Mon Pitakattaik (Yangon)** — a Myanmar-side mirror institution [VERIFIED-PAGE]

### F3. EAP1432 Part 2 (Major grant, 2022) — the digitised corpus itself
- "The project produced far more material than originally projected: against an initial estimation of 10,000 images, the number was in reality around 38,000. The total number of texts was approximately 900." (902 archive records online)
- Dating: "most texts appeared to have been created in the mid- to late nineteenth century through to around 1920... range... from the late eighteenth century at the earliest through to the 1940s as the latest, which appears to have been the point at which the tradition ended"; parabaik/samut khoi typically undated; few printed books from the Pak Lat (modern Phra Pradaeng) Mon press set up early 20th c.
- Language/script profile: bulk Mon language + Mon script; Pali well represented; "mixed language Mon-Pāli nissaya or trāy texts"; significant number of Thai and Pali materials in Khom script ("a local version of the Khmer script"); smattering of Burmese and Thai
- Topics: Abhidhamma, vinaya, commentaries, literature, horoscopology, customs, music, history (history rare); priority given to secular Mon texts because rarer
- Significance claims: first large-scale high-quality online digitisation of Mon mss; "A very significant proportion of the texts digitised under this project appear to not exist inside Burma"; first Khom-script materials digitised online
- Grant £60,000, 12 months, CC BY-NC 4.0; DOI 10.15130/EAP1432; outputs include EAP1432 Methodology Report.pdf, Project Description in Mon.pdf, Burmese-Language Project Explanation.pdf [VERIFIED-PAGE]
- Continuation: EAP1668 part 3 (ongoing) photographs further Mon collections Central Thailand [VERIFIED-PAGE, related-project block]

### F4. SEA Junction announcement (McCormick, 2024-09-04) — community context
- "a collection of nearly one thousand Mon-language manuscripts have been digitized and placed online... can be downloaded for free"
- Origins: "The 'Thai Mon' or Thai Rāman community finds its origins in waves of migrations from the Mon-speaking areas of Lower Burma to Central and Northern Thailand, from the seventeenth to early nineteenth centuries"
- Scale: manuscripts kept "in around thirty to forty historically Mon temples, mostly in Central Thailand"
- Decline mechanism: 1940s Thai law + state control of monastic education ended Mon schooling; shift to Thai; now moribund in Thailand with very few manuscript-literate readers; "In Burma, of course, substantial numbers of Mon people are literate"
- Texts mid-19th–early 20th c., some late 18th c., some 1940s; subjects incl. "bilingual Pāli-Mon nissaya or trāy texts, vocabularies, and interlinear glossed texts" plus secular literature, astrology/divination, customs, music, medical treatment, history [VERIFIED-PAGE + raw HTML archived]

## B. Old Mon inscriptions & script history (closes part of A05-F6 gap)

### F5. STOU online exhibition คัมภีร์ใบลานมอญ (STOU Library) — Mon script + folio practice
- Script origin claim: "ตัวอักษรมอญรุ่นแรกสุด ปรากฏในราชอาณาจักรรามัญช่วงระหว่างพุทธศตวรรษที่ ๙ – ๑๐ ที่มีอิทธิพลของตัวอักษรอินเดียใต้ และได้พัฒนาต่อเนื่องกันมาจนถึงปัจจุบัน" (earliest Mon script in Rāmañña realm Buddhist centuries 9–10 ≈ 5th–6th c. CE, South Indian influence, continuous development)
- Mon script used for Mon + Pali + Sanskrit + Burmese; exhibition asserts it is origin of several other scripts (Tai Lue/Sipsongpanna, Lanna Tham, Isan-Lao Tham, Jinghong 'Chinese' script) — exhibition-level claim, needs scholarly corroboration
- Inscription materials listed: stone, fired clay, gold/silver/copper sheets, ivory, lacquer, cloth, samut khoi, bai lan/talipat/bai tey/lam chiak leaves
- Folio practice: incise both sides 4–6 lines/side; two cord holes per leaf; bundles ordered by Mon consonant sequence; leaf length 8–20 inches; bundles mostly even counts 24–32 leaves, long works >60
- Destruction event: พ.ศ. ๒๓๐๐ (B.E. 2300 = 1757 CE) fall of Hongsawadi (Pegu) — many Mon palm-leaf burned; survivors hidden then carried into Siam with Mon migrations; large-scale recopying in Thailand "สมัยธนบุรีถึงสมัยรัชกาล...พระบาทสมเด็จพระปกเกล้าฯ" (Thonburi period → reign of Rama VII)
- Current concentration: Mon wats in Bangkok + Nonthaburi, Pathum Thani, Phra Nakhon Si Ayutthaya, Nakhon Pathom, Samut Sakhon, Samut Prakan, Ratchaburi, Lopburi
- Genre inventory: Tripitaka + ṭīkā/nissaya apparatus; jataka groups (nipata, Dasajati 13 kanda, Pannasa, non-canonical); Pali grammar textbooks for monks sitting เปรียญรามัญ (Mon pariyan) exams; chants (paritta, patimokkha); sermons/practice (Visuddhimagga, Milindapanha, Abhidhamma, Pathamasambodhi); dhammathat law codes (claimed source of Thai and Burmese dhammathat); chronicles/histories (Tamnan Phra That, Sudhammavati founding, Hongsawadi founding, Rajadhiraj, Dhammazedi, Mon royal chronicles); tales; treatises (medicine, astrology, chan lakorn, stupa proportions, kham khoi rituals)
- Technique: majority iron-stylus incising; ink and gold lines minority; some illustrated lacquer-gilt volumes [VERIFIED-PAGE, raw md archived]

### F6. Christian Bauer, "Notes on Mon Epigraphy", JSS 79.1 (1991)
- Opening: "Since the publication of H.L. Shorto's Dictionary of the Mon Inscriptions [DMI] in 1971, over thirty inscriptions in Old Mon have been discovered in Thailand and Burma"
- New corpus "redresses not only a regional but also a chronological and dialectological bias in Mon epigraphy"; raises "early Khmer-Mon contacts" and provides "contact-induced language-change and language-shift in Northeastern Thailand and the Chao Phraya basin"
- Inventory entries read include: Lp.14/Lp.19 Lopburi 6c Pali rims; Lp.1 Lopburi pillar 7c–8c Old Mon (Bangkok National Library, 145 cm, 8 sides); Sap Champa Lopburi 7c Pali pillar; Lamphun 13c inscriptions (Lb.7); Dharmacakra from Chainat (Jn.14/15); NE Thailand reassessment (Ban Kut Ngong, Ban Kaeng, Ban Fai Hin); Dvaravati-era context (Quaritch Wales cited)
- Sections: seals/impressed designs; spatial deixis; pragata; titles (man, tralti, kurun, kammraten pdai karom, ksmun); 'at; glossary addenda to DMI [VERIFIED-PDF, extracted text archived]
- Bauer JSS 79.2 (1991) Notes on Mon Epigraphy II continues (Saraphi images Dw.38 Sp.1 etc.) [VERIFIED-PDF archived, partial read]

## F. Threats/conservation specific to Thai-Mon collections (new, supports Phase A conservation threads)

### F12. SPAFA "Mon Palm Leaf Manuscripts: Compilation, Conservation and Digitization" (1 Jul 2011–30 Sep 2012)
- "There is a large number of old Mon palm-leaf Manuscripts in Buddhist temples of Mon communities and villages in Thailand... most of them are kept without proper conservation and compilation"
- Distinct destruction vectors named: termites, theft, natural decay, "the enshrining in the relic-chamber when a new Chedi is built, or the practice of burning the leaf manuscripts for ashes to use in making lacquered Buddha images"
- Outputs planned: per-temple catalogues, English translations, digitised data sheets, community workshops [VERIFIED-PAGE]
