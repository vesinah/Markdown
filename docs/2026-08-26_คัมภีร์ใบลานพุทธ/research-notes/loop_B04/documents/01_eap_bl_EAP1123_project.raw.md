# Title: Recalling a Translocal Past: Digitising Thai-Mon palm-leaf manuscripts (EAP1123)

## Aims and objectives
The Mons of Thailand and Burma were regional cultural and religious intermediaries and supported a palm leaf manuscript tradition into the 1920s. No official Thai body has ever digitized these manuscripts. Among them are texts unknown in Burma, which are key to understanding recent history and the Mon role in intellectual history. The collections have been exposed to vermin and flooding. Disinterest has also led to their destruction.

Plan: survey about 10-15 manuscript collections over the preliminary grant. Collections vary from fifty to several hundred manuscript "bundles," which may or may not contain more than one text; estimated ~15 bundles of interest per collection. Bundles screened for complete/undamaged/easy-to-read. Conservative estimate ~100 bundles to be digitised. A typical bundle contains approximately 26 "faces" — the front and back of the palm leaf — although some secular texts can have 40 or even 80 faces. Images estimated ~3,500.

## Outcomes
Over two months (January 6 - March 5 2019), the research team gathered names of some 28 Mon temples/collections in and around Bangkok, by word of mouth and conversations with local experts. Of the 28 temples, 25 visited; remaining three far from Bangkok (e.g., Chiang Mai, ~400 miles north). Six temples did not have, or no longer had, Mon-language manuscripts. A survey was produced: [EAP 1123 Survey.pdf](https://eap.bl.uk/index.php/sites/default/files/EAP%201123%20Survey.pdf)
