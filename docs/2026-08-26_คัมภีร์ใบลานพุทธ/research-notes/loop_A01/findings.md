## S01 - Pali Text Society, About page
- Citation as printed: "The Pali Text Society" / site tagline "Founded in 1881 to foster and promote the study of Pali texts"
- URL: https://palitextsociety.org/about-the-pali-text-society/
- Status: HARVESTED (raw copy: documents/2023-palitextsociety-about.html)
- Key facts with exact quotes:
  - Founding: "The Pali Text Society (PTS) was founded in 1881 by T. W. Rhys Davids 'to foster and promote the study of Pali texts'."
  - Output scope: "Since its foundation the Society has published editions and translations of nearly all the Pali canonical texts, as well as editions of most of the principal commentaries and translations of some."
  - Publishing model: it "publishes editions of Pali texs in Roman script, English translations and ancillary works, including dictionaries, grammars, a Pali primer and a journal." [sic "texs" as printed]
  - Institutional archive: "The Faculty of Asian and Middle Eastern Studies at the University of Cambridge holds archives relating to the Pali Text Society."
  - Charity status: "Registered charity number: 262216"
- Relevance to Chapter 1: anchors the founding moment (1881) of Western Pali philology and its publication infrastructure; starting point of the historiography narrative.
- Phase B candidates raised here: Cambridge FAMES PTS archive page (https://www.ames.cam.ac.uk/faculty-library/archive/pali-text-society); JPTS archived volumes.

## S03 - The Fragile Palm Leaves Foundation (hosted on PTS site)
- Citation as printed: "The Fragile Palm Leaves Foundation"
- URL: https://palitextsociety.org/the-fragile-palm-leaves-foundation/
- Status: HARVESTED (documents/2023-palitextsociety-fragilepalmleaves.html)
- Key facts with exact quotes:
  - Origin: "The Fragile Palm Leaves Foundation evolved from the Fragile Palm Leaves manuscript preservation project, which began in Bangkok in 1994. The project became a registered foundation under Thai law on 21 February 2001 (Registration No. To. 66/2544)"
  - Mission: "dedicated to the study of the Buddhist literature of South-East Asia. This literature, preserved in inscriptions and manuscripts in diverse writing systems and languages, has not been adequately described or studied."
  - Dating context: "Chinese records suggest that Indian writing systems were in use by the second century CE, if not earlier, but no early manuscripts survive." And "by the fifth century or earlier this was one of the media for the recording of literature."
  - Production peak: "The bulk of the existing manuscripts date from the nineteenth century, a period of intense production."
  - Collection size: "contains more 5,000 manuscripts containing more than 10,000 titles."
  - Partners: "scanned in cooperation with the Lumbini International Research Institute (Lumbini, Nepal)"; database entry supported by the Pali Text Society; experimental DB on Max Planck ECHO site (echo.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/content/buddhism); scans at BUDA library.bdrc.io
- Relevance to Chapter 1: Thailand-based node of the international preservation network; demonstrates the shift from colonial-era philology to NGO/foundation model in Bangkok (1994/2001).

## S04 - Project to Digitize Myanmar Manuscripts (PTS)
- Citation as printed: "Project to Digitize Myanmar Manuscripts"
- URL: https://palitextsociety.org/project-to-digitize-myanmar-manuscripts/
- Status: HARVESTED (documents/2023-palitextsociety-myanmar-digitization.html)
- Key facts with exact quotes:
  - Start date: "In February 2013, work began on taking photos of palm-leaf manuscripts and parabaiks in Myanmar monasteries."
  - Funding: JSPS Scientific Research B (2011-2014, Head Investigator Ousaka), Challenging Exploratory Research (2013-2015, Kasamatsu), KDDI Foundation, Mitsubishi Foundation, second SRB 2016-2019, CARI of Rissho Kosei-kai (2017).
  - U Pho Thi Library, Thaton: "in August and September of 2013, work continued on taking photos of manuscripts in the U Pho Thi Library in the Saddhamma-jotikā-rāma Monastery in Thaton... Some 130 manuscripts were photographed."
  - Completion: "By August 2016, all the manuscripts had been photographed."
  - Rare material: "U Nyunt Maung identified approximately 50 manuscripts that contain rare Pāli texts, most of which have not been published."
  - Preservation practice quote (local expertise): lemon-grass oil "with powdered carbon added to help preserve the leaves and bring out the letters inscribed in the leaves using a metal stylus."
  - Outputs: PDFs downloadable via Pariyatti website catalogue; "100 PDFs of manuscripts from the Bagaya Monastery are being hosted on the University of Toronto website" (mmdl.utoronto.ca).
- Relevance to Chapter 1: exemplifies current Japan-funded, UK-charity-coordinated digitization fieldwork; shows collaboration between Japanese academia (Ousaka/Kasamatsu), PTS, and Burmese monastic libraries.

## S06 - About the NGMPP (Hamburg history subpage)
- Citation as printed: "About the NGMPP : The Nepalese-German Manuscript Cataloguing Project"
- URL: https://www.aai.uni-hamburg.de/en/forschung/ngmcp/history/about-ngmpp.html
- Status: HARVESTED (documents/2016-uni-hamburg-about-ngmpp.html; page footer says Last update: 23 March 2016)
- Key facts with exact quotes:
  - Foundation and funding: "The Nepal-German Manuscript Preservation Project (NGMPP) was founded in 1970 under an agreement between His Majesty's Government of Nepal and the German Oriental Society, and was financed by the German Research Council (Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft)."
  - Scale and significance: "Among the approximately 180,000 manuscripts that have been microfilmed by the NGMPP are found the oldest preserved manuscript of a Vedic text (dating from the 11th century), the oldest preserved Pali manuscript, and a manuscript of the Skanda Purana which is possibly the oldest dated Nepalese manuscript (810 AD)."
  - Microfilm deposit: "A further copy of every film has been sent to the German Oriental Society, which has forwarded it on permanent loan to the State Library in Berlin."
  - World record claim: "the National Archives houses the world's largest microfilm collection of South Asian manuscripts."
  - Rescue example: 1989 Tengboche monastery fire; "(185 texts, 15,766 folios) had been photographed by the NGMPP just a few months prior to the fire".
- Data conflict note: this page's intro line states the project "came to an end in March 2002", while S05 states March 2001. Both dates recorded as printed; verify in Phase B.
- Relevance to Chapter 1: the largest single microfilming program touching Buddhist (and Hindu) palm-leaf/pothi manuscripts; key to the German school of Indological philology infrastructure.

## S07 - Early Buddhist Manuscripts Project (EBMP), University of Washington
- Citation as printed: "The Early Buddhist Manuscripts Project | Asian Languages & Literature | University of Washington"
- URL: https://asian.washington.edu/early-buddhist-manuscripts-project
- Status: HARVESTED (documents/2025-uw-ebmp.html)
- Key facts with exact quotes:
  - Founding: "The Early Buddhist Manuscripts Project was founded at the University of Washington in September 1996 to promote the study, edition and publication of twenty-seven unique birch-bark scrolls, written in the Kharoṣṭhī script and the Gāndhārī language, that had been acquired by the British Library in 1994."
  - Expansion: "the EBMP is currently involved in the study of seventy-six birch-bark scrolls (primarily in the British Library, the Senior Collection, the University of Washington Libraries and the Library of Congress)"
  - Antiquity claim: "These manuscripts date from the first century BCE to the third century CE, and as such are the oldest surviving Buddhist manuscripts as well as the oldest manuscripts from South Asia."
  - Publication venue: results published by University of Washington Press.
- Note: the legacy domain earlymanuscripts.washington.edu and depts.washington.edu/ebmp are dead (parked domain); current home is asian.washington.edu.
- Relevance to Chapter 1: marks the 1990s turn toward the earliest manuscript evidence and the birth of Gandharan studies as a codicological discipline.

- Citation as printed (from the article's own citation block): สนฺติธมฺโม พระอัมรินทร์, สุจริตธรรมกุล วิไลพร, และ ยอดสา พงษ์ศิริ. 2023. "คัมภีร์ใบลานยุคพระเจ้ากาวิละ: กรณีศึกษาจากบันทึกท้ายคัมภีร์ใบลานอักษรธรรมล้านนา สำรวจพบ ณ วัดดวงดี อ.เมืองเชียงใหม่ จ.เชียงใหม่". ธรรมธารา 9 (2):2-37.
