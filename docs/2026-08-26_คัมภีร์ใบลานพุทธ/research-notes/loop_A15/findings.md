## 2. Tai Khün of Kengtung (Chiang Tung)

## 3. Tai Lue of Sipsong Panna (Xishuangbanna) and northern Laos

- STATUS: VERIFIED. Source: Grabowsky, V., "Tai Manuscripts in the Dhamma Script Domain: Surveying, Preservation and Documentation, Part 3", Manuscript Cultures Newsletter No 3 (CSMC Hamburg), pp.25-33, https://www.csmc.uni-hamburg.de/publications/mc/files/articles/mc03-grabowsky.pdf (full text via r.jina.ai; raw PDF archived).
  - Demography: "nearly 400,000 Tai Lü currently living in Sipsòng Panna"; Chinese state folds them into the Dai minzu together with Tai Nüa of Dehong etc.; "Only the Tai Lü use the Dhamma script, whereas the writing system of the Tai Nüa (lik to ngòk or 'sprout letters') is much closer to the script of the Burmese Shan."
  - Kengtung link: "as many as 200,000 Tai Lü in the eastern sections of the Burmese Shan State, especially in the Chiang Tung (Kengtung) region, where they are inter-mingled with 150,000 Tai Khün"; slightly less than 200,000 Tai Lü in Northern Laos (Müang Sing, Phong Saly highest concentration); up to ~500,000 assimilated+unassimilated Tai Lü in Northern Thailand.
  - Script reform: new simplified Tai Lü alphabet "officially introduced in 1955"; abolished Pali consonants, ligatures, sub/superscript marks; used in Xishuang Banna baozhi newspaper founded 1957; monastic education halted during Cultural Revolution 1966-1976; "up to ninety per cent of Tai Lü manuscripts were destroyed during this period".
  - Oldest dated ms recorded in Kato/Isra list: CS 1249 (=1887/88 AD), astrological text Pakkatün.
  - Survey: Kumiko Kato & Isra Yanantan initiated first survey of Tai Lü mss in Sipsòng Panna 1998 with Ai Kham and Cao Maha Khanthawong (Dao Jinxiang); surveys 1999-2000; catalogue 2001 located "more than 6,500 manuscripts" (table total 6,559); "the bulk of extant manuscripts are written on mulberry paper and not on palm-leaf".
  - Largest collection stored "in the Cultural Bureau of the Political Council of the Prefecture of Sipsòng Panna in Jinghong"; after borders reopened early 1990s, mss from Chiang Tung, Moeng Yòng, Moeng Luai entered southern Sipsòng Panna districts.
  - Dehong: 2001-2003 Yunnan Provincial Archives (Yunnan University) surveyed/catalogued/microfilmed Tai Nüa mss — 2,000 documents surveyed, ~900 catalogued, ~57,500 microfilm frames; Gengma County follow-up spring 2004; 2005 catalogue synopses of almost 200 mss, 22 written in Tai-Lü Dhamma script (Yin Shaoting & Daniels).
  - DLLM: among 8,349 mss online Oct 2010, "604 manuscripts written in Tham Lü"; Luang Namtha 198 Tham Lü mss of which 135 on mulberry paper; Sainyabuli 227 of which 221 on palm leaf.
  - Codicology note: "An average palm-leaf fascicle (phuk) comprises 6 to 12 folios... fascicles traditionally held between wooden covers. Larger codicological units consist of a number of fascicles held together by a cloth wrapping. Such 'bundles' (mat)... may contain single texts or... unrelated texts sponsored by the same donor" (per DLLM glossary).
  - BL/EFEO miscataloguing: "A considerable number of manuscripts classified as Lao, kept in the archives of the British Library (London) and the École française d'Extrême-Orient (Paris), are actually written in the Tai Lü language and script, as the author realised during research conducted at these institutions in 2002 and 2003."
- STATUS: VERIFIED. Source: Techasiriwan, A. & Grabowsky, V., "Note on Tai Lue Wooden Buddha Image Inscriptions and Buddhist Manuscript Colophons from Northern Laos", Aséanie 33 (2014), pp.61-81, DOI:10.3406/asean.2014.2319, https://www.persee.fr/doc/asean_0859-9009_2014_num_33_1_2319 (first page + core paragraph read; raw HTML archived).
  - Quote: "The inscriptions and colophons analysed in this study all come from Tai Lue areas in northern Laos, mostly from the district of Mueang Sing (Luang Namtha province) near the Chinese border. They consist of a corpus of twenty-five Tai Lue inscriptions selected from more than 160 pedestal inscriptions on wooden Buddha images which Volker Grabowsky collected from 1998-2005, and of twentyfive colophons from Tai Lue mulberry manuscripts of Buddhist texts. As a rule, these inscriptions and colophons are written in the Tai Lue language using the Tham (Dhamma) script."
  - Multiscript example: manuscript Tamnan Phraya Tham Ha Phra Ong "written in the Tham script but contains two short passages in the Shan and Lao scripts."
- PARTIAL (search capture): Digital Library of Lao Manuscripts analysis paper (academia.edu 40578466): script/language tables show e.g. Tai Lue category: Tham Lue 588 texts / 20,706 digital images; most recent dated ms in DLLM = CE 2000, "Kham khap ka long, in Tai Lue language and script... from Mueang Sing... dated CE 2000"; "36 illustrated texts..., of which 29 are Tai Lue or Tai Nuea sa-paper manuscripts"; PLMP surveyed holdings of over 800 monasteries, ~86,000 texts preserved.

## 4. beiye jing 贝叶经 — Chinese preservation programmes (Xishuangbanna/Dai)

- STATUS: VERIFIED. Source: yunnanexploration.com, "Pattra-leaf Scriptures Making Craft of Dai Ethnic Minority in Xishuangbanna" (2019), https://www.yunnanexploration.com/pattra-leaf-scriptures-making-craft-of-dai-ethnic-minority-in-xishuangbanna.html (raw copy archived).
  - Craft listed in China's Second National Intangible Cultural Heritage List, 2008, State Council, item number VIII-142; Dai names: leaves "Guo Lan" (郭懒), manuscripts "Tan Lan" (坦懒); Corypha umbraculifera.
  - "Approximately 4,000-5,000 volumes of Dai-language palm-leaf manuscripts have been discovered in Xishuangbanna, Pu'er, Lincang, and Dehong regions of Yunnan, with over 2,000 volumes officially cataloged."
  - Folk tradition: ancient mss numbered over 80,000 volumes; Xishuangbanna has collected more than 3,000.
  - Inheritor Bokonglun 波空论 (male, b. Jan 1948), 4th batch National ICH Representative Inheritor; timeline: Nov 2019 listed in national ICH Representative Project Catalog with Xishuangbanna Prefecture Cultural Center as protection unit; re-evaluated 31 Oct 2023.
  - Concept quoted: Dai + Thai + Lao + Shan share Baiyue ancestry and Theravada faith → "Southeast Asian Palm-Leaf Manuscript Cultural Sphere".
- STATUS: VERIFIED. Source: 中国非物质文化遗产网 ihchina.cn, 树叶上的傣族文化 走近神秘贝叶经 (2010, orig. 东方网 闫钰), https://www.ihchina.cn/project_details/10740.html (long read; raw HTML archived).
  - 傣语称"坦兰"; iron stylus on specially prepared 拜兰 (Corypha) leaves; transmission route India → Sri Lanka → via Myanmar/Thailand → SW Yunnan around 7th c.
  - 《中国贝叶经全集》100卷 project: launched 2002 jointly by Xishuangbanna Prefecture Government and Yunnan University Beiye Culture Research Center ("抢救树叶上的傣族文化"); at time of writing vols 1-40 published, full set due following year; investment by 昆明汉慧经贸有限公司.
  - Researchers bought ~100 beiye jing volumes in Myanmar for at least 50万元 because Cultural Revolution destruction left local holdings insufficient; oldest ms in institute collection ~600 years old; >1,300 Dai beiye works already translated into Chinese.
  - Temple management: every monastery had 藏经阁 "林坦" (lintan); Buddhist canonical texts not allowed out of temple; only mulberry-paper copies of practical knowledge kept in villages.
  - Artisan 康朗教 (Kang Lang Jiao, Gelao Ba/Mantan area): pigment recipe diesel + rose petals + battery carbon ink, wiped with sawdust; edges coated gold powder or red/black lacquer.
- STATUS: VERIFIED. Source: DMC.tv DIRI Journal p.89, "Preservation of Dai Buddhist Manuscripts in Sipsong Panna", https://www.dmc.tv/ebook/544/75563 (page incl. footnotes read; raw HTML archived).
  - Quote: "More than 400 copies in total have been collected from different locations. Of these, 152 are palm-leaf manuscripts, 211 are Chinese paper ones and 40 are 'Zanha Libretto.'"
  - Quote: "According to Yanxiang [Zai], the head of Xishuangbanna State Ethnic Research Institute and director of the State Palm-Leaf Culture Research Center, there are about 1,000 copies of palm-leaf scriptures in the Yunnan, and countless sa-paper manuscripts." Footnote: alternative figure of 3,000 scriptures comes from propaganda materials for The Complete Collection of Chinese Palm-Leaf Scripture.
  - Footnote citation (verified bibliographic): Yanxiang Zai, "On Compilation, Translation and Collation of Dai's Palm-Leaf Scriptures In Sipsongbanna", in Guo Shan, Zhou Ya, Yanxiang Zai (gen. ed.), Palm-Leaf Culture and Construction of a Harmonious Society of the Dai, Yunnan University Press, July 2008.
  - 1990 census data quoted: Dai population 1,115,900; 98.55% in Yunnan; Xishuangbanna 296,930; sa-paper = Broussonetia papyrifera.

## 5. Interconnections with Lanna/Laos corpora

- Grabowsky mc03 (VERIFIED): Tai Lü mss misclassified as "Lao" at BL and EFEO (see above); influence of Tai Lü orthography on Northern Thai mss flagged via Hundius 1990 fn (higher frequency of /ʉa/-/əə/ confusions in districts with many Khün/Lü descendants).
- Persée 2014 (VERIFIED): transcription system chosen to allow "easy comparisons with the Lan Na corpus which makes use of a similar transcription system."
