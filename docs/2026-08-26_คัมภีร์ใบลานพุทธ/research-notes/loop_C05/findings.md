### F11. Paper arrives 13th c.; print adopted only late 18th c. (VERIFIED-PAGE)
BnF / Patrimoines Partagés, Jérôme Petit (Head of South and Southeast Asia Collections, BnF Manuscripts Department), "Printing India's texts", written July 2024:
- "India initially committed its texts to memory... India then inscribed its texts on palm leaves, inventing a particular form of book known as a pothi. Scribes would etch the texts down the length of the leaf, turning the pages from the bottom up. This method endured even after paper arrived in the 13th century."
- First movable-metal-type presses in South India by Portuguese Jesuits settled in Goa (second half of 16th c.), followed by Danish missionaries of Tranquebar; "But the Indians did not adopt this technology to publish or disseminate their literature."
- Turning point: Bengal late 18th c.; Halhed *A Grammar of the Bengal Language* 1778; Serampore Mission Press >200,000 texts 1800-1835; Wilkins Devanāgarī type; "The Sanskrit works were printed in Devanāgarī type using the pothi format, thus preserving the reading habits of Indian scholars."
- URL: https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-southasia/en/printing-in-india-article (raw html archived)
