# Isan Manuscripts — Tai Manuscripts in the Southeast Asia Rare Book Collection at the Library of Congress

"There are sixty-four Isan manuscripts in the collection."

"The Isan or northeast region of Thailand is home to a large ethnic Lao community. Since the 1920s printers in the region have been making modern palm leaf manuscripts." [note 1: Peter Koret, "Books of Search...", in Grant Evans (ed.), Laos: Culture and Society, Chiang Mai: Silkworm Books, 1999]

"The library holds 58 manuscripts from the Lūk Sǭ Thammaphakdī publisher" (addresses Tanāo Rd. / Khāosān Rd., Bangkok). Earliest texts 1921 (different publisher); many from 1960s and 1980s; publishers Nāi Som Phūangphakdī in the '60s, Nāi Bunchūai Hiranawat since the '80s. Note 2: "According to the current owner, Surapan Puangpakdee, Lūk Sǭ Thammaphakdī opened in 2472 [1929] and started printing palm leaf manuscripts in 2480 [1937]. Postcards followed in 2496 [1953]."

"The texts are no longer hand-written by individual scribes, but are now machine-printed directly onto the palm leaf. The texts are written in the modern Thai script..." Many now list compiler/author — break from anonymous scribal tradition.

"Nearly all the Isan texts in the collection (28 of 35) are revisions by the head monk of Ubonrātchathānī province (พระราชรัตโนบลเจ้าคณะจังหวัดอุบลราชธานี)". Also two revisions by Mahā Sīlā Vīravong (born Rǭi Et); Siamese texts revised by Mahā Pui Sǣngchāi of Anongkhārām temple.

"the edges of many manuscripts are marked by red lacquer with gold leaf... Some manuscripts actually contain advertisements on the front cover... Some also claim to be 'copyrighted'..."
