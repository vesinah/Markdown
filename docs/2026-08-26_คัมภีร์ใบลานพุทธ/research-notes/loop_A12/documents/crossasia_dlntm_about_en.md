## Northern Thai Literary Tradition — Background
"Northern Thailand possesses distinct cultural and literary traditions... its rich manuscript collections have remained severely under-researched due to a lack of accessibility, despite an abundance of primary sources." Texts: Theravada Buddhist tradition plus "social relations, customary law, astrology, magic, mythology and rituals, grammar and lexicography, as well as poetry and epic stories, folk tales and romances"; many extra-canonical narrative works, especially Jataka stories. Languages: "Northern Thai (Lan Na), Tai Khuen, Tai Lue, Lao, Shan, Burmese, and Pali", various scripts.

"The oldest extant manuscripts are monolingual Pali texts dating from the late 15th century CE when Chiang Mai was a regional centre for the study and dissemination of Buddhist literature, and the earliest dated manuscript, of the Tiṁsati nipāta section of the Jātaka aṭṭhakathā vaṇṇanā, was copied in 1471 CE (CS 833)."

Few manuscripts from 17th–early 18th c. (Burmese suzerainty). Early 19th c.: Khruba Kancana developed Wat Sung Men, Phrae, as centre of Pali/Buddhist studies; mobilised copying across Northern Thailand and Luang Prabang. "The entire library collection at Wat Sung Men, which contains over 1,700 manuscripts and is the largest in Northern Thailand, was microfilmed as part of the Preservation of Northern Thai Manuscripts Project (PNTMP)."

Preservation history: early 20th c. central Bangkok reforms "supressed the use of the local Lan Na script". The Siam Society supported survey at Wat Lai Hin, Lampang Province by Ajarn Singkha Wannasai completed 1966. DELMN project by Harald Hundius early 1970s. A separate project taken over in 1981 by Social Research Institute (SRI) of Chiang Mai University "catalogued and microfilmed over 4,000 manuscripts", mostly secular fields (law, customs, astrology, history, medicine). CMU Center for Promotion of Arts and Culture implemented PNTMP from 1987 to 1991 — largest survey to date; over 400 rolls of microfilm held by Chiang Mai University Library (CMUL). Since 2005 EFEO cataloguing/digitisation (Lanna Manuscripts website); Palm Leaf Studies Centre and Institute of Lanna Studies at Chiang Mai Rajabhat University also active incl. Tai Khuen (Shan State) and Tai Lue (Sipsongpanna).

## DLNTM Collections (four collections; figures as shown on page)
1. Preservation of Northern Thai Manuscripts Project (PNTMP): 3,914 manuscripts, 159,564 images. Greyscale digitised from microfilm. Initiated by Harald Hundius (CMU Thai Dept Lan Na MA programme 1983–1992); implemented 1987–1991 by CMU Center for the Promotion of Arts and Culture; funded by German Federal Foreign Office Cultural Assistance Programme; M.R. Rujaya Abhakorn Project Leader. Over 400 rolls microfilm incl. entire Wat Sung Men collection (over 150 rolls). Copies kept at National Archives of Thailand, CMUL, Akademie der Wissenschaften und der Literatur Mainz. Microfilms digitised Germany 2012-2013 (Herrmann und Kraemer GmbH), CC BY-NC 4.0 (except medical treatises). "Contains possibly the oldest dated Pali manuscripts in Southeast Asia."
2. DLNTM direct digitisation: 1,051 manuscripts, 39,547 images. Colour, directly digitised 2013–2017 in 39 temples (German Federal Foreign Office funded). Note: PNTMP selected >6,000 texts but >2,000 not microfilmed; no microfilming in Chiang Rai and Lamphun provinces; only one temple in Chiang Mai Province (90 mss at Wat Chiang Man).
3. DELMN: 991 manuscripts, 13,419 images. First comprehensive survey of Lan Na manuscripts in eight northernmost provinces, Harald Hundius 1971–1974, funded by German Research Foundation (DFG); Ajarn Singkha Wannasai instrumental; ~100 locations, ~25 rolls microfilm.
4. HHHWC (Harald Hundius Handwritten Collection): 181 manuscripts, 21,222 images. Notebook copies by local scribes (mostly monks from Lamphun temples chosen by Singkha Wannasai) early 1970s.

Total = 6,137 manuscripts / 233,752 images (sum of the four figures above; front page states "digital library of 6,137 manuscripts from Northern Thailand").

## Technical Details
Zeutschel OM 1600 roll-film scanner (PNTMP/DELMN), Zeutschel OS 12000 book scanner (HHHWC); direct temple digitisation Nikon D610 + AF-S NIKKOR 50mm f/1.4G, tethered MacBook Pro, LED lights; "All photographic work took place in the temples, and no manuscripts were removed." 300ppi TIFF masters + 72ppi JPEG derivatives; EXIF metadata with GPS; images archived redundantly at Berlin State Library, CMUL, UPenn, National Library of Laos. Naming scheme PNTMPaaa_bbb_ccccccccc_cc_ddd etc.; survey datasheets (DS1) included as PDFs. Terms: CC BY-NC 4.0 (exception traditional medicine).
