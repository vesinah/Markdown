# Findings — LOOP A03: Writing/Incising Techniques on Palm-Leaf Manuscripts Across Regions

Project: palm_leaf_buddhist_manuscripts | Loop A03 | 2026-08-26

## S01. Cornell University Library — Preservation Tutorial, "Palm Leaf Manuscripts" [A]

- URL: http://preservationtutorial.library.cornell.edu/librarypreservation/mee/preservation/palmleaf.html
- Raw copy: documents/cornell_preservation_tutorial_palmleaf.html

Key quotes:
- "Two techniques are generally used for writing on palm leaf manuscripts: incising with a pointed metal stylus and writing directly with a pen or brush. Most palm leaf manuscripts are incised, with occasional additions or emendations written with pen on the surface."
- "Incised writing is made visible by applying a mixture of lampblack, bean plant or berry juice, and aromatic oil. The oils used have included camphor, citronella, castor, lemongrass, cedarwood, mustard, neem, eucalyptus, clove, and sesame. They are chosen for their insect repellent qualities."
- "The leaf tends to split along longitudinal veins, especially where previously incised with a stylus." / dryness "is treated by reapplying oil, which has a darkening effect if done too often."
- Method of writing detectable "through magnified examination, helped considerably by raking light."

Notes: canonical conservation-tutorial formulation of the two-technique dichotomy; names chemical agents (lampblack carbon + plant juice binder + aromatic oils as insecticides).

## S02. Yu, C., Zhang, M., Li, B. & Lian, X. (2025), "Unraveling the ink composition of ancient palm leaf manuscript from Tibet: A multi-analytical study", npj Heritage Science 13:73 [A]

- URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-025-01543-y
- Raw copy: documents/yu2025_npj_heritsci_tibet_palmleaf_ink.html

Key quotes:
- "ancient ink samples from 12th–13th-century palm-leaf manuscripts in Tibet were examined... The analysis revealed that the ink used in the palm-leaf manuscript derived from oil combustion, providing direct evidence of lamp soot and adhered to the surface of the palm leaves using adhesives without penetrating into their interior."
- TEM: "the particle size was relatively uniform spherical, with all particles within about 50 nm range... consistent with the particle morphology and size of lamp soot reported in the literature, thus confirming its identification as lamp soot."
- Adhesives: "animal glue such as bone glue and leather glue, pectin, and polysaccharides were used as adhesives in the ink" (pyrrole ← proline evidence for bone/leather glue; benzofuran/maltol markers for plant gum).
- "Lipids were also detected in both the ink and the support, suggesting the addition of oils such as sesame or almond oil to prepare an oil-based ink."
- Historical corpus context: "nearly 30,000 pieces of ancient palm-leaf manuscripts are still preserved in Tibetan monasteries... Most... date from the 11th to the 14th centuries CE, with few originating from before the 11th century CE, which were written with ink directly on both sides using tools such as reed."
- Historical records: "ink preparation involved materials with a wide range of origins, notably including ashes from burned wood and soot from cooking pots or lamps."
- Ink loss occurs "along the horizontal fiber bundles" — flaking mechanism.

Notes: strongest scientific source this loop; confirms Nepal/India-origin Sanskrit pothis were INK-WRITTEN (reed pen), not incised — key contrast to mainland SEA incise-and-blacken.

## S03. Ratwatte, F. (1990), "The Ancient Art of Ola Writing", Explore Sri Lanka, Aug 1990 [A]

- URL: https://exploresrilanka.lk/the-ancient-art-of-ola-writing/
- Raw copy: documents/ratwatte_1990_exploresrilanka_ola_writing.html

Key quotes:
- Tool name: "written on with a pointed stylus called a panhinda. Traditionally these styluses had handles elaborately carved with Buddhist symbols."
- Blackening step named: "the application of the ink which will bring the pale, incised script into bold relief. This process is called kalu-medima, 'the application of black polish.'"
- Agents: "A paste of lamp black (the soot obtained from the flame of a natural oil lamp), black resin and kekuna (Kokoona zeylanica) oil is rubbed on the manuscript."
- Finish: "The manuscript is wiped clean with a soft cloth and given a final polish with kurakkan (brown millet; Eleusine coracana)."
- Script morphology: "Three distinct styles of writing were used: hasthi, a large-lettered script; sinha, a rounded script and hansa, an elegant, small-lettered script." / "To avoid damaging the leaf, dots are not used and stops at the end of a sentence are indicated with a feather symbol."
- Training: student learns "with a long strip of untrimmed ola and a blunt writing stylus... A student was usually in his late teens before he became completely competent in ola writing."

## S04. Michael Backman Ltd — "Three Sri Lankan Palm Leaf Manuscript Styluses (Panhinda)" (19th c.), object essay citing Coomaraswamy 1908 [A]

- URL: https://www.michaelbackmanltd.com/object/three-sri-lankan-palm-leaf-manuscript-styluses-panhinda/
- Raw copy: documents/michaelbackman_panhinda_styluses.html

Key quotes:
- Coomaraswamy part names: "Coomaraswamy gives the traditional names for each part of the stylus: chatra, patra, nala, ganda and lekhaniya. Each part has a very precise proportion relative to the others."
- "Writing on each page was done with a stylus... that had a steel point which was sharpened from time to time on an oiled stone."
- Sri Lankan blackening recipe: "ink made from combining fine dust made from charcoal from leaves, commonly cotton plant leaves, with an alluvial resin. The mixture was heated until a black resinous oil was produced. This was rubbed over the manuscript pages with a cloth... then rubbed away with sifted rice bran, leaving the black resin in the scratched depressions."
- Preparation chain: boil leaflets, sun-dry 3 days/3 nights, polish over smoothed areca trunk, punch holes with steel punch, singe edges with hot iron.

## S06. Schuyler, M. (1908), "Notes on the Making of Palm-Leaf Manuscripts in Siam", Journal of the American Oriental Society 29: 281–283, DOI 10.2307/592633 [A]

- URL (full text): https://archive.org/stream/jstor-592633/592633_djvu.txt ; metadata https://philpapers.org/rec/SCHNOT-7
- Raw copy: documents/schuyler_1908_jaos29_palmleaf_siam.txt

Key quotes:
- Stylus form: "The pen or rather stylus is a needle point like the needle of a sewing machine, inserted in a wooden handle like an enormous cigar about eight inches in length."
- Ruling: "strings are then blackened with soot from the bottom of a rice pot and the strings are placed in position over the palm leaf and then snapped" — usually five guide lines per leaf.
- Incision then blacken+scour: "with the needle point scratches the letters... In order to render the writing clearer the entire surface of the leaf is smeared with soot and then wiped off and scoured with clean sand. The black adheres to the scratches and is removed from the rest of the surface by the sand."
- Format: each leaf two pages of five lines; book = 12 leaves; edges trimmed in press, "sometimes gilded".

## Cross-regional synthesis (all points sourced above)

1. Dominant technique (Sri Lanka, South India, mainland SEA, Bali): incision with pointed metal (occasionally wooden/bamboo) stylus → rubbing/blackening with soot-carbon suspended in oily/resinous binder → wiping/scouring excess (cloth, rice bran, hot sand).
2. Ink-and-pen traditions coexist: Nepal/Newar Sanskrit pothis written with reed-pen ink (S02, S12); eastern/northern India sometimes pen-and-ink (S14 LIS Academy); Kerala ORI holds exactly two ink-written palm-leaf mss incl. Aryamañjuśrīmūlakalpa (S11).
3. Named tools captured verbatim: panhinda (SL stylus; parts chatra, patra, nala, ganda, lekhaniya); ezhuthani/narayam (Kerala/Tamil stylus — see S14/gaps); min mo pot soot + nam sao kao rice water (Lanna); ลูกประคบ, เขม่าก้นหม้อดินเผา, น้ำมันยาง, ทรายร้อน (Thai/National Library); pe-za (Burmese book); pengerupak + candlenut soot + kincu (Bali); kalu-medima (SL blackening step); masi/masit (Indian carbon ink).
4. Chemical agents verified: lampblack/lamp soot (TEM ~50 nm spheres), animal glue (bone/leather), plant gum/pectin, sesame/almond lipids (S02); charcoal-dust + alluvial resin (S04); candlenut-oil soot (S08); camphor/citronella/castor/lemongrass/cedarwood/mustard/neem/eucalyptus/clove/sesame oils as insect-repellent additives (S01).
5. Script-morphology consequences documented: rounded letters to avoid splitting fibres (S14 Suriyakantha; S08 Bali); dot rendered as tiny circle in Burmese because jabbing splits the leaf (S09); Sinhala dot avoided, feather-symbol sentence stop (S03); even-pressure uniform incision depth (S03).
