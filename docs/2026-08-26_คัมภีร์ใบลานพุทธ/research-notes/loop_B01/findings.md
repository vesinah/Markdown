### F3. Chiang Mai News (2020) — Lanna vs central forms, belief rationale
- จักรพงษ์ คำบุญเรือง, "รูปแบบของ 'หอไตรในล้านนา'", Chiang Mai News, 21 Feb 2020, https://www.chiangmainews.co.th/100lanna/1270898/ [VERIFIED-PAGE — full text read; HTML archived]
- Central Thailand standard type: single-storey small wooden building, high open stilts, isolated in middle of pond, no bridge/stairs; rationale = ants/termites/insects + fire protection
- Lanna term: "หอไตรแถบล้านนาหรือชาวล้านนาเรียกว่า 'หอธรรม' นั้นกลับมีรูปแบบทางสถาปัตยกรรมที่ผิดแผกไปจากหอไตรในภาคกลางโดยสิ้นเชิง"
- Lanna hallmark: two storeys on rectangular plan; lower storey solid brick/mortar with NO stairs inside or outside; upper storey carved wood; access by leaning a special ladder through a narrow door. Belief basis: scriptures sacred, kept high, must be carried on the shoulder ("เทินแบกเหนือบ่า ห้ามถือในระดับต่ำ"), secured from outsiders
- Chronology claim: tall-tower type oldest, from พุทธศตวรรษที่ 21, era of King Tilokaraj who performed the first sangayana on Thai soil BE 2020 (CE 1477); masterpiece examples named: หอไตรวัดพระสิงห์วรมหาวิหาร (Chiang Mai), วัดพระธาตุหริภุญชัยฯ, วัดช่างฆ้อง (Lamphun), วัดพระธาตุลำปางหลวง (Lampang)
- Group 2 (~BE 24xx): central-style pond ho trai adopted with Lanna ornament (หน้าบัน ช่อฟ้า ใบระกา หางหงส์ คันทวย); examples วัดป่าเหียง, วัดสันกำแพง (Lamphun); ponds now filled/concrete bridges added
- Group 3 (BE 25th c., newer special forms): square plan; single-storey masonry like a small ubosot, or two-storey open ground floor with four-sided veranda; stacked mandapa roofs influenced by Ava Burmese style; examples วัดศรีบุญโยง & วัดพระแก้วดอนเต้า (Lampang), วัดฉางข้าวน้อย, วัดสันดอนรอม, วัดมหาวัน (Lamphun)
- Exterior decoration across all types: stucco, carved wood, gilt black lacquer ("ลายรดน้ำ ปิดทองล่องชาด"); three motif groups: (1) standing deva with lotus in añjali = guardians; (2) flower-vase (หม้อดอก) = altar vases; (3) Himavanta animals esp. lion/singha = symbol of the Buddha's Sakyavamsa lineage

## B. Thai/Lanna palm-leaf manuscript conventions

### F6. phuk/mat bundle anatomy (major upgrade over A04/A12)
- Jaengsawang, Silpsupa. "The Use of Anisong Manuscripts at Funerals in Northern Thailand and Laos", in De Gruyter open-access volume, ©2024, DOI https://doi.org/10.1515/9783111343556-011, pp. 303–334 (32 pp.). Full text read via r.jina.ai from https://iris.unive.it/retrieve/47e86e77-50db-4613-8f01-60874dab7b10/Manuscripts%20at%20Funerals.pdf [VERIFIED-PAGE — MD archived as jaengsawang_2024_anisong_funerals_fulltext.md]
- Units defined: "There are two different manuscript units in Northern Thai and Lao manuscript culture: fascicles (Thai: phuk) and bundles (Thai: mat)." "A unit consisting of several folios combined in a single binding, with one or two strings through one or two holes in the folios, is called a phuk ('fascicle'), no matter how many texts it contains. Several fascicles can be combined to make a larger unit – a bundle (mat)... a fascicle is defined as a sub-unit of a bundle."
- Line counts per leaf side: "anisong texts were written on four or five lines on both the recto and verso sides"; footnote: "There are generally four lines in those from Laos, but five lines in those from northern Thailand."
- Reading orientation: "The texts inscribed on the two sides are upside down in relation to each other, making it easy for preachers to turn over each leaf..."
- Corpus stats (funeral anisong subset): 23 manuscripts dated 1838–2004 CE = 19 palm-leaf fascicles + 4 mulberry-paper volumes; related paper snippet: NT corpus "207 palm-leaf manuscript bundles (Thai: mat) in which 339 fascicles (Thai: phuk) are contained" (Jaengsawang, academia.edu related item)
- Storage trace phenomenon: long-unused tightly bound fascicles leave "pale, mirror-image imprints of the ink on adjacent leaves that look like a faint second layer of writing"
- Colophon purpose quote (via Veidlinger 2007 cited therein): Lan Na colophons "stating explicitly that they were made in order to preserve the teachings of the Buddha for 5,000 years"
- Ho trai tie-in: 1945 CE colophon records copy made "สำหรับหอพระไตรปิฎกวัดบ้านเอื้อม" (for the monastic Tripitaka library of Wat Ban Üam, Lampang)
- unifont.org Hariphunchai Tai Tham project (translating Shoppingrome photo-essay 2009), https://unifont.org/hariphunchai/th/node/7 [VERIFIED-PAGE]: "Twenty-four leaves are strung together into a bundle called a 'phuk'"; stringing process = "saay sanong" (สายสนอง); covers = "mai prakap tham" (ไม้ประกับธรรม); "the bundles of a manuscript are wrapped in cloth, bound with five bands of cord, and then the name of the manuscript is written on the cloth." NOTE: 24-leaves figure = single secondary source, needs corroboration (BL A12 says bundles fastened with two cotton cords without leaf counts)

### F7. Foliation/numbering systems
- Jaengsawang 2024 (same source as F6):
  - Numerals: "Nai Tham numerals and Hora numerals are found in northern Thai and Lao manuscripts... Nai Tham numerals are used in religious texts, while Hora numerals, which originated in Burma, are used in manuscripts containing secular texts" (citing Jaengsawang 2022, 138)
  - Palm-leaf manuscripts use foliation counting leaves; oblong mulberry-paper books use pagination counting pages, in Nai Tham numerals
  - Dual foliation (Lao, Venerable Sathu Nyai Khamchan Virachitto collection): "foliated using two systems, numerals and words... 'The numeral was mostly written in the left-hand margin of the first page of each leaf, whereas the numerated word was placed in the traditional manner'" (Khamvone Boulyaphonh & Grabowsky quoted)
  - Alphabetic pagination (Luang Prabang, quoting Bounleuth Sengsoulin via Khamvone & Grabowsky 2017, 20–21): first twelve folios paginated ka, kā, ki, kī, ku, kū... corresponding 1–12; longer texts continue kh, g, gh, ṅ, c...
  - Practical foliation remark in Central Thai script on Tham-script manuscript: "หน้าต้นอานิสงส์เผาผี" ('first page of Anisong phao phi') — for lay helpers who re-order leaves after sermons
- Penn Museum 51-9-2 "Vohara manuscript", Nan Province, 1740s (Lanna/Pali), catalog notes (find.library.upenn.edu + openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0016/html/51_9_2.html, identical data): "Written in 4-6 lines per leaf"; "Foliated with numerals, 1-15"; "Leaves of palm leaf with two central holes approximately 16 cm apart"; original dimensions 5.5 x 44.5 cm [SNIPPET-RICH — both catalog excerpts agree]
- LOC guide (Lao/NT section): e.g. Khamphi phlat tæk phūk, palm leaf, 225 ff., 58 x 5 cm, 4 lines [SNIPPET-RICH]

### F8. Title/explicit placement practice

### F9. Lacquer/gilt decoration taxonomy of bai lan (resolves A04 gap partially)
- สารานุกรมไทยสำหรับเยาวชน เล่ม 32 "หนังสือโบราณของไทย", มูลนิธิโครงการสารานุกรมไทยสำหรับเยาวชน, https://saranukromthai.or.th/middlechild/1019 [VERIFIED-PAGE — core passages read; HTML archived]; mirror: trueplookpanya.com/knowledge/content/61810 [HTML archived]
  - Incision craft: เหล็กจาร sharpened constantly; weight control critical (light = broken strokes, heavy = pierced leaf); after incising rub with ลูกประคบ soaked in lampblack + resin-oil, then sprinkle hot fine sand and polish until letters stand out black on cleaned surface
  - Decoration applied to ปกหน้า ปกหลัง ขอบคัมภีร์ ไม้ประกับหน้า-หลัง: "ลายรดน้ำ ประดับมุก ประดับกระจก หรือปิดทอง"; decorated manuscripts named after technique: "คัมภีร์ใบลานฉบับทองทึบ ฉบับล่องชาด ฉบับล่องชาดรดน้ำดำ"; covers likewise named (ไม้ประกับประดับมุก, ไม้ประกับลายรดน้ำ)
  - Reading furniture: กากะเยีย = 8 round wooden rods strung on cord, unfolds into square reading table for supporting leaves
- Cross-confirm A12 F2 (Wat Bencha registry: ฉบับหลวง/ฉบับเฉลยศักดิ์ categories ลานดิบ, รักทึบ, ล่อรัก, ล่อชาด, ทองทึบ) — consistent vocabulary
- SLAM object 267:1952 "Palm Leaf Manuscript (bai lan)", Thai, Chakri dynasty, early 20th c.: h 7 cm x w 58.4 cm x d 5.4 cm, palm leaves + wood covers https://www.slam.org/collection/objects/646 [SNIPPET-RICH — museum record]
