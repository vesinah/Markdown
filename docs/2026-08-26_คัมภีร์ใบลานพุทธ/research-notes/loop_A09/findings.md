## A. Newar Buddhism — ความต่อเนื่องและธรรมเนียมต้นฉบับสันสกฤต

### F1. "Sanskrit Buddhism" รูปแบบที่ยังมีชีวิต + การหลอมรวม ศตวรรษที่ 9–13
- "The special, characteristic peculiarity of Newar Buddhism is that its ritual and its sacred literature are written in the Sanskrit language, because of which we can call Newar Buddhism the only surviving form of 'Sanskrit Buddhism'."
- "With the collapse of Buddhism in India, some Buddhists escaped from suppression and fled to Nepal... The two groups intermarried and their religions and cultures merged to become Newar Buddhism. This happened during a period from the 9th to the 13th century A.D. The Newars have continued to copy Sanskrit manuscripts up to the present day."
- Source: DSBC, Min Bahadur Shakya, https://dsbcproject.org/pages/a-short-history-of-sanskrit-buddhist-manuscripts [VERIFIED-PAGE]

### F2. คำยืนยันอิสระว่าด้วย "last surviving tradition"
- "The first two hundred manuscripts scanned by the project have been sourced from private collections in the Kathmandu Valley, where the last surviving tradition of South Asian Mahayana, practiced by Buddhist Newars, still keeps and uses these texts." — คอลเลกชันหลัก Akṣeśvara Mahāvihāra, Patan; นักสะสม Dīpak/Puṣparāja Vajrācārya,  late Ratnakājī Vajrācārya
- Banner caption: "Aṣṭasāhasrikā prajñāpāramitā Manuscript (Dated 1215 CE), Hiranyavarna Mahavihara, Lalitpur, Nepal"
- Source: DSBC Introduction, https://dsbcproject.org/pages/introduction [VERIFIED-PAGE]
- Crossref เสริม: Lewis, Todd T., *Popular Buddhist Texts from Nepal: Narratives and Rituals of Newar Buddhism*, SUNY Press, doi:10.1515/9780791492437 (foreword by Gregory Schopen) [CROSSREF-METADATA]

### F3. Brian Hodgson 1824 — จุดเริ่มการรู้จักโลกภายนอก
- "In 1824, Mr. Brian Hodgson, a British diplomat, discovered a great number of Sanskrit Buddhist manuscripts in Nepal and reported their existence to the modern world... Copies of these works, totaling 381 bundles of manuscripts, were distributed by Hodgson so as to render them accessible to European scholars."
- ตัวเลขกระจาย: Asiatic Society of Bengal / RAS London 85 / India Office 30 / Bodleian 7 / Société Asiatique 174 → BnF (ร่วมกับชุด Burnouf); fn1 อ้าง R.L. Mitra, *The Sanskrit Buddhist Literature of Nepal*
- Source: DSBC Short History [VERIFIED-PAGE] — ข้อความเดิมมีความกำกวมเลข 86 mss/179 works vs 381 bundles; จดไว้เป็นข้อสังเกต

## B. วัสดุและการเปลี่ยนสื่อ

### F4. ชนิดวัสดุเนปาล 5 แบบ + ขอบเขตเวลาใบลาน
- "From the first millenium up to the 16th centuries manuscripts were written on palm leaves called Tādapatra."
- "Rolled palmleaf manuscripts are called tāmsuks and have been used mainly for legal deeds. At least 1084 rolled palmleaf manuscripts are held by the National Archives of Nepal alone. These were written from the 14th century onwards. One of the oldest among these dates from 1334 CE."
- "Most of these manuscripts extant in Nepal are written in Bhujimol script."
- Haritalika paper: orpiment coating on handmade Nepali paper from Loktā bark; pH 7–9
- Thyasaphu (leporello), scroll (Vaṃśāvalī), bound-book (imitation Western)
- fn3–4 อ้างบทความ Griha Man Singh ใน *Abhilekha* (Nepal Archives) 1996, 1994
- Source: DSBC Short History [VERIFIED-PAGE]

## C. อักษร (Bhujimol/Ranjana/Kutila/Golmol/Newa)

### F6. ระบบอักษรนิวาร์ ≥6 ชุด
- "Newa is one of at least 6 scripts used for writing Nepali languages, which include Ranjana, Bhujimol, Kutila, Golmol, and Litumol."
- Newa (Prachalit/Nepaalalipi): Brahmi-derived; emerged 10th c.; decline after Gorkha conquest 1769; banned by Rana government 1905; ban lifted 1951; revival standard by Nepal Lipi Guthi 1989; ใช้เขียน Sanskrit/Bengali/Maithili/Hindi ด้วย
- Source: Ishida, Newa Orthography Notes, updated 28-Apr-2026, https://r12a.github.io/scripts/newa/new.html [VERIFIED-PAGE]

### F7. Ranjana/Lantsa + **ข้อขัดแย้งเชิงศัพท์ที่ต้องจด**
- Omniglot: "The Ranjana script, which is also known as Kutila or Lantsa... developed during the 11th century AD and was used until the mid-20th century in India and Nepal by the Newar people"; Tibetans call it Lantsa ใช้เขียนชื่อเรื่องสันสกฤตของคัมภีร์แปล + ตกแต่งวัด/mandala; ใช้ตกแต่งใน China/Mongolia/Japan
- **CONFLICT:** r12a แสดง Ranjana และ Kutila เป็นอักษรคนละตัวในลิสต์ ≥6 scripts; Omniglot ระบุ "Ranjana = Kutila" — ห้ามสรุปฝ่ายใดฝ่ายหนึ่งจนกว่าจะได้แหล่ง palaeography หลัก (Phase B)
- Sources: https://www.omniglot.com/writing/ranjana.htm [VERIFIED-PAGE]; r12a [VERIFIED-PAGE]

## F. NGMPP / NGMCP (microfilming & cataloguing)

### F14. โครงการอนุรักษ์ด้วยไมโครฟิล์ม
- "The Nepal-German Manuscript Preservation Project was founded in 1970 under an agreement between His Majesty's Government of Nepal and the German Oriental Society, and was financed by the German Research Council (Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft)."
- Conceived by Wolfgang Voigt (Staatsbibliothek zu Berlin Orientabteilung / DMG / KOHD) + Klaus Ludwig Janert (Cologne); agreement signed **February 16, 1970**; DMG agreement extended seven times (1975, 1982, 1986, 1990, 1993, 1996, 1999); Albrecht Wezler Director General from 1982
- "**more than 180,000 manuscripts containing some five million folios**. Approximately one fifth of these are Tibetan manuscripts"; "more than 47,000 historical documents in Sanskrit, Newari, Nepali, and Tibetan"
- สำเนาฟิล์ม: original+1 copy เนปาล; อีกชุด → German Oriental Society → **permanent loan to the State Library in Berlin**; "the National Archives houses the world's largest microfilm collection of South Asian manuscripts"
- Stations: National Archives 1970; Nepal Research Centre 1976; Bhaktapur 1976–78; Banepa 1979–80; Kesar Library 1975–76; Tribhuvan Library 1982; Janakpur 1984–85; ทั่วประเทศได้รับอนุญาต 1982; Tibetological section 1978; expeditions L1–L1013 (Langtang 1983 → Manang/Mugu-Dolpo 1999)
- ไฟไหม้ Tengboche 1989: "these rare manuscripts (185 texts, 15,766 folios) had been photographed by the NGMPP just a few months prior to the fire"
- Sources: About the NGMPP + History of the NGMPP pages [VERIFIED-PAGE, both archived]

### F15. NGMCP successor + ตัวเลข + วันสิ้นสุด (**NOTE date discrepancy**)
- "The Nepalese-German Manuscript Cataloguing Project was launched in April 2002. Its main purpose is to prepare a comprehensive descriptive catalogue of the more than 180,000 Nepalese manuscripts microfilmed under the Nepal-German Manuscript Preservation Project."
