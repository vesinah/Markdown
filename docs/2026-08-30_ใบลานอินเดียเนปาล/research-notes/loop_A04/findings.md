  - (Abstract) "The analysis revealed that the ink used in the palm-leaf manuscript derived from oil combustion, providing direct evidence of lamp soot and adhered to the surface of the palm leaves using adhesives without penetrating into their interior as determined by Raman spectroscopy, SEM–EDS, and TEM."
  - (Abstract) "Py-GC/MS and FTIR analysis identified the presence of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), proteins, pectin, fats, and polysaccharides in the ink sample. These findings indicated that the ink likely contained adhesives such as animal glue (e.g., bone glue and leather glue), as well as plant gum."
  - (Introduction) "Nearly 30,000 pieces of ancient palm-leaf manuscripts are still preserved in Tibetan monasteries. These palm-leaf manuscripts originated from ancient India... Most palm-leaf manuscripts predominantly date from the 11th to the 14th centuries CE... which were written with ink directly on both sides using tools such as reed."
  - (Results/Discussion) "The abundant pyrrole compounds are associated with proline in bone glue and leather glue, further suggesting that the adhesive may contain materials such as bone glue or leather glue. Lipids were also detected in both the ink and the support, suggesting the addition of oils such as sesame or almond oil to prepare an oil-based ink."
