# **NATIONAL MUSEUM OF THE PHILIPPINES - CEBU** **EXHIBITION HIGHLIGHTS**

Gallery 1: **<u>KINAIYAHAN:</u>** **<u>CEBU’S</u>** **<u>NATURAL</u>** **<u>WONDERS</u>** - This exhibition features the unique geological, botanical, and zoological heritage of
Cebu.





The **Cebu** **tamaraw** _(Bubalus cebuensis)_, is an extinct species
of dwarf water buffalo. This herbivore lived on the island of
Cebu probably between 10,000 and 100,000 years
ago—during the Pleistocene (Last Ice Age)—or perhaps as
recently as a few thousand years ago. Standing only 75 cm at
the shoulder and weighing about 150-165 kg., it was even
smaller than another Philippine dwarf species, the living
tamaraw _(Bubalus_ _mindorensis)_, which is endemic to the
island of Mindoro. Despite being miniature, B. cebuensis was
robust in stature and had large teeth and feet relative to the
size of the body.

Cebu was once the home to diverse tall hardwood trees
(dipterocarp) found in dipterocarp or lowland evergreen
rainforests. However, during Spanish rule, many hardwood
trees like the **white** **lauan** were cut down to build ships for
the Manila galleon trade which resulted in the abrupt
denudation of its forest cover. With less than 1% forest land,
it is considered one of the most denuded islands in the
country primarily as a result of land-use conversion, mining,
agriculture, human habitation, timber harvesting, and
charcoal making. It is considered a vulnerable species.

Cebu’s Endemics

_(Cinnamomum_

_cebuense_ and
_Lepeostegeres_

_cebuensis)_



**_Cinnamomum_** **_cebuense_** is an endemic species that is
abundant in the forest of Cantipla and is considered one of
three locally important cinnamon species in the country.
Barks and leaves are used as flavorings, spices, and
condiments. There are also studies identifying its potential
use in stomach aches.


Moreover, **_Lepeostegeres_** **_cebuensis_** is one of the many
endemic species in Cebu. It belongs to the family of mistletoe
and is considered an aerial stem parasitic plant. With its
discovery in the province in 2016, it brings the total species of
Lepeostegeres in the Philippines from two to three!

Mobile Museum
Boxes (Box #1 and

Box #4)



The **Mobile** **Museum** **Boxes** project, in partnership with the
Western Visayas Association of Museums and the National
Museum of the Philippines with the Forest Foundation
Philippines, is a traveling exhibition that features the rich
natural heritage of the Visayas Region. The 12 boxes feature
endemic and significant geological specimens, and flora and
fauna species found in various islands in the Visayas. The
boxes had 8 stops across the Visayas, from Eastern Samar,
Leyte, Bohol, Iloilo, and now here in Cebu!


**Box** **#1** features Cebu and Bohol’s 200-100 million-year-old
metamorphic basement. These basements are covered by
marine sandstone, shale, and limestone above which the
Visayas islands stand today.

Cebu flowerpecker

_(Dicaeum_
_quadricolor)_



Meanwhile, **Box** **#4** features the balicassiao _(Dicrurus_
_balicassius_ _mirabilis),_ which is an endemic bird in the
Philippines found also in Cebu. This bird is able to imitate calls
from other birds such as the Philippine Hawk Eagle, the
Bar-bellied cuckoo-shrike, and the Philippine bulbul.


The **Cebu flowerpecker** is a very rare and local resident in the
remaining forests in Cebu. It has a moderately short, deep or
broad beak, with the male and female birds having distinct
plumage colors. The males have four distinct vibrant colors of
red, black, greenish-yellow on the rump and greyish-white
below, from where the species name “quadricolor” come
from.Females have brownish olive-green above and greyish
white below. This species has been considered extinct since
1906 but was rediscovered after nearly 10 years. To date, no
photo taken from the wild has been published for the species.

Glory-of-the-sea

cone
_(Conus gloriamaris)_



Commonly known as the **“Glory-of-the-Sea”** **cone**, this
marine species of snail thrives in deep waters ranging from
250-300 meters. It is a relatively large, slender cone shell with
a tall spire, with finely reticulated orange-brown to brown
lines forming tiny triangles. This species has a special
significance to shell collectors because it was once regarded
as the rarest shell in the world. The fine specimen in our
exhibit measures 145-mm from Sogod, Cebu waters, a gift to
the nation by Guido T. Poppe.

Gallery 4: **<u>THE PHILIPPINE CENTER NEW YORK CORE COLLECTION OF 1974</u>** - The Philippine Center Core Collection of 1974 is an assemblage of
artworks meticulously selected for the Philippine Center New York to embody the Filipino national identity, in line with the dominant style of
the global art scene during the 1970s.


**_Procession_**
1974
Oil on canvas
On loan from the Philippine Center New York Collection


Cesar T. Legaspi was one of the Thirteen Moderns considered
forerunners and pioneering advocates of modern art in the
country. The group was formed before World War II and led
by National Artist Victorio C. Edades (1895-1985). After the
war, Legaspi joined the Neo-realist movement as one of its
core members. The group produced works that feature
Philippine themes in a style that is representational in form
but more open to various degrees of abstraction.

**_Serenade 7/25_**
1974
Etching
On loan from the Philippine Center New York Collection


Referred to as the Father of Philippine Printmaking, Mang
Maning, as he was fondly called, was born in Cebu City. He
studied Fine Arts at the University of the Philippines and
architecture studies at Mapua University from 1940-1941.
From 1945-1946, he obtained his Architectural Draughtsman
Certificate at the Central Institute of Technology Foundation
in Manila. After the war, he introduced serigraphy or
silkscreening to the local art scene.



_Shocking Pink,_



Lilian Hwang
**_Shocking Pink, Orange, Maroon_**
1973
Acrylic on textile
On loan from the Philippine Center New York Collection


Hwang labels her works as ‘calligrams’ which she identifies as
geometric patterns painted in green, blue, and yellow and
other colors. She learned cartoons under Pol Galvez and
Rodolfo Paras-Perez, the latter being her mentor, and whose
works are also part of this exhibition. The artist exhibited at
the Galerie Bleue and the Cultural Center of the Philippines
(CCP) in the seventies and eighties. In 1984, she participated
in the inter-gallery exhibition at the CCP entitled “Ugat Suri”
organized by the ASEAN Institute of Art in Manila

_The_ _Presentation_ _of_
_the Santo Niño in Cebu_



Carlos Francisco
**The Presentation of the Santo Niño in Cebu**
1965
Oil on canvas


On the same day of the erection of the cross in Cebu by
Ferdinand Magellan on April 14, 1521, Father Pedro de
Valderrama baptized its ruler Rajah Humabon, his wife, and at
least 500 natives. Magellan presented an image of the Santo
Niño as a baptismal gift to the king and queen who were given the
Christian names Carlos and Juana.


This painting was commissioned by the Philippine Government in
commemoration of the 400th anniversary of the Christianization of
the Philippines in 1965. This was featured in the _400_ _Years_ _of_
_Christian_ _Culture_ _Exhibition_ in Cebu Normal School (now Cebu
Normal University) from April 27 to May 2, 1965.
