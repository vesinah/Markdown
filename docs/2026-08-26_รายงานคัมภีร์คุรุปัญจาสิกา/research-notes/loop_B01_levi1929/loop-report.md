## PART 3: WHAT LÉVI SAID — MANUSCRIPT, AUTHORSHIP, SURVIVING VERSES

All statements below transcribed from the archived article extract (OCR, accents partially restored by context):

### 3.1 The manuscript and its discovery

- Lévi saw the original briefly in September 1928 during a rapid visit to Nepal, in "la bibliothèque du Durbar" (the Durbar library = today's National Archives, Kathmandu), among "les dernières acquisitions" (the most recent acquisitions); he "n'avais pu que jeter les yeux sur le manuscrit" (could only glance at it) and had no time to take notes.
- Physical description verbatim (OCR normalized): "le manuscrit était tracé sur des feuilles de palmier de très petit format, en écriture népalaise médiévale, et qu'il était formé de fragments incomplets de plusieurs ouvrages" — palm leaves, very small format, medieval Nepalese script, a composite codex of incomplete fragments of several works.
- A complete copy was procured thanks to "la bienveillance toujours active du Maharaja Chandra Sham Shere Jung" and arrived "fin novembre 1928"; Lévi adds that Chandra Shamsher died on the 26th of November [1929], and he pays tribute to his memory.
- Contents of the codex as listed by Lévi: 1° the first thirty-three stanzas of the treatise on honours due to the Guru [= Gurupañcāśikā]; 2° last folio of Samyaksambuddhabhāṣita-sattvaradhanam; 3° Mūlapāṭisaṃgraha; 4° Śuddhapatti; 5° Daśakuśalakarmapatha (first folio); 6° Āryasaddharmasmṛtyupasthāna...Sadgatikārikā; 7° beginning of a treatise in verse.
- On the codex's purpose: "Le scribe a sans doute prétendu réunir une série de petits traités qu'il tenait pour des œuvres d'Açvaghoṣa mahāpandita" (the scribe evidently intended to gather small treatises he held to be works of Aśvaghoṣa mahāpandita).

### 3.2 Verses surviving — text breaks at v.33

Verbatim key sentence: **"1° Les trente-trois premières stances d'un traité sur les honneurs dus au Guru par le disciple. C'est, comme on le verra, un fragment de la Gurupañcāçikâ «la Cinquantaine (de stances) du Guru», que les traductions en chinois et en tibétain attribuent à Açvaghoṣa"**

- The Sanskrit text edition printed by Lévi runs v.1 to v.33ab only; it ends mid-stanza 33 with "vidadhyād vandanāt pūrvaṃ tathā|" and no colophon (his edition heading: "Gumpancaiçikâ" [sic, OCR of Gurupañcāśikā]; opening "Om Namo Baddhāya").
- His French translation is titled: "La Cinquantaine (de stances en l'honneur) du Guru".
- Cross-confirmation, Szántó 2013 p. 444 (archived extract): "This ms., which breaks off halfway in the last quarter of verse 33"; and Szántó's diplomatic transcript footnote 9: "Lévi's ms. breaks off here".

### 3.3 Authorship statements by Lévi

- Attribution rests on the translations: the Chinese and Tibetan translations attribute the work to Aśvaghoṣa. No Sanskrit colophon exists in this witness (fragment breaks at v.33).
- Authorities cited by Lévi for the attribution:
  - Tibetan: Tanjur, Rgyud, XXVI, 72 — the commentary "ŚrīMahākālatantrarudrakalpamahāśmaśāna nāma ṭīkā Gurupañcāçikâ" (i.e. Tsongkhapa's Bla ma lnga bcu pa'i rnam bshad).
  - Chinese canon: "Trip. chinois, Tok., XXVII, 16; Nanj., 1080" (= Taishō T1687; Nanjio catalogue no. 1080).
- Lévi's editorial stance: "Je ne fais pas œuvre d'éditeur; je laisse à d'autres le soin de reprendre ces textes imparfaits avec l'aide des versions chinoises ou tibétaines" (he publishes with apparatus and French rendering but does not claim a critical edition).

### 3.4 Lévi's readings vs the DSBC/CIHTS recension (preview of Loop B02 diff)

Lévi's Sanskrit text differs materially from the CIHTS 1997 / DSBC / GRETIL text in several verses, e.g.: v.5d "tyaktva saddharmavandanam" vs DSBC "tyaktvā cārcanavandanam"; v.22ab "tasmaç chiṣyaḥ kriyatya[gī] śīlakṣāntiguṇānvitaḥ" vs DSBC "te śiṣyāḥ karuṇotsargaśīlakṣāntiguṇānvitāḥ"; v.26 "jīvam eva guro-r dravyaṃ..." vs DSBC "svātmavac ca guror dravyaṃ..."; v.30 "na saṃgāyanam anyonyaṃ gāyanaṃ na ca vādanaṃ na nṛtyaṃ na kathālāpaṃ" vs DSBC "saṃvāhanaṃ nartanaṃ ca..."; v.33 "caraṇakṣālanābhyaṅgodvartanāmardanādikam" vs DSBC "pādayoḥ kṣālanaṃ cāṅgaproñchābhyañjanamardanam". Full verse-by-verse collation is the task of loop_B02.

## PART 4: EARLY NOTICES — HARAPRASAD SHASTRI (NEGATIVE FINDING, DOCUMENTED)

- Hara Prasad Shastri (with Cecil Bendall), *A Catalogue of Palm-Leaf & Selected Paper MSS. Belonging to the Durbar Library, Nepal*: Vol. I (Calcutta, Baptist Mission Press, 1905); Vol. II (1915). Both volumes public domain on archive.org.
- Full-text search performed on both volumes' OCR (Vol I item `un-catalogo-de-manuscritos-en-hoja-de-palma-y-en-papel-seleccionado-pertenecient`; Vol II item `in.ernet.dli.2015.502082`): **zero occurrences** of "Gurupañcāśikā"/"Panchasika" or any matching entry.
- Consistency check: Lévi states he found the codex among the Durbar library's "dernières acquisitions" in September 1928 — i.e. it entered the collection AFTER both Shastri cataloguing campaigns (1898-99 expedition → Vol I; 1907 visit → Vol II). The negative finding is therefore expected and documented, not an anomaly.
- Other early notices: none located between 1929 and NGMPP. The manuscript remained unpublished as a catalogued NAK item until the NGMPP microfilming era; its current siglum NAK 3-715 = NGMPP B 23/8 is reported by Szántó 2013 (below).
