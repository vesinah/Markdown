Title: The Fifty Verses of Guru Devotion - By Geshe Ngawang Dhargyey
From the Introduction of Geshe Ngawang Dhargyey's short commentary:

"The Fifty Verses of Guru Devotion [Skt: Gurupancashika; Tib: Lama Nga-chu-pa] was written in about
the first century B.C. by Ashvagosha. This Indian poet was known by many names—such as Aryashura,
Matriceta, Patriceta, Matichitra, and Bhavideva—and was a contemporary of King Kaniska of the Kusan
Dynasty."

[Note for report: this traditional attribution/dating is now superseded by manuscript evidence;
Szanto 2013 shows the Sanskrit ms. names Vapilladatta as author.]

Further key statements:
- Success in sutra or tantra depends solely upon guru devotion; cites Lotus Sutra and Hevajra-related
  explanatory tantra ("Kyedor Shagyui Dorje'i Gur").
- Guru devotion involves both thought and action; total conviction guru is a buddha is prerequisite.
- "Ashvagosha's Fifty Verses is the most comprehensive summary of devoting yourself to your guru by
  means of action."
- Scriptural sources per verse are given in Tsongkhapa's Fulfillment of All Hopes.
- Source tantras listed: Guhyasamaja, Kalachakra, Chakrasamvara, Vajradakini, Vajrahridayalamkara.
- Text customarily taught before tantric initiation.
- Page offers two PDFs: "short commentary" and "long commentary" (the long commentary corresponds to
  the expanded LTWA book version).
